The call to worship one God reaches into hope, repentance, hardship, intention, and everyday responsibility. These teachings show how spiritual devotion and moral conduct belong together.

{% include glossary-key.html %}

## Worship and conduct
{: #worship-and-conduct }

- <span id="src-0050"></span>Prophet Muhammad ﷺ’s teachings do not separate worship from ordinary conduct. Duties to God repeatedly appear beside duties to family, neighbors, the poor, strangers, workers, opponents, animals, and the wider community. The result is a moral vision in which devotion is tested by how people actually treat one another. Because this section gathers many examples, they are grouped by theme so that the larger pattern remains visible.[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[3](/messenger/sources#ref-3){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[14](/messenger/sources#ref-14){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}


## God, direct worship, and servanthood
{: #god-direct-worship-and-servanthood }

- <span id="src-0924"></span>Prophet Muhammad ﷺ called people to worship one God alone and did not ask them to worship him, his family, saints, tribes, natural forces, or intermediaries.

    > "Say, ‘He is Allah, [who is] One,’ Allah, the Eternal Refuge. He neither begets nor is born, nor is there to Him any equivalent." (**Sūrat al-Ikhlāṣ 112:1-4**), Saheeh International[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}
    >
    > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿قُلْ هُوَ ٱللَّهُ أَحَدٌ ۝١ ٱللَّهُ ٱلصَّمَدُ ۝٢ لَمْ يَلِدْ وَلَمْ يُولَدْ ۝٣ وَلَمْ يَكُن لَّهُۥ كُفُوًا أَحَدٌۢ ۝٤﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ الإِخۡلَاصِ</span></div>
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-0931"></span>The Qur’an says that God is near and responds directly to the sincere prayer of the person who calls on Him.

    > "When My servants ask you, [O Muhammad], concerning Me - indeed I am near. I respond to the invocation of the supplicant when he calls upon Me. So let them respond to Me [by obedience] and believe in Me that they may be [rightly] guided." (**Sūrat al-Baqarah 2:186**), Saheeh International[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}
    >
    > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿وَإِذَا سَأَلَكَ عِبَادِى عَنِّى فَإِنِّى قَرِيبٌ ۖ أُجِيبُ دَعْوَةَ ٱلدَّاعِ إِذَا دَعَانِ ۖ فَلْيَسْتَجِيبُوا۟ لِى وَلْيُؤْمِنُوا۟ بِى لَعَلَّهُمْ يَرْشُدُونَ ۝١٨٦﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ البَقَرَةِ</span></div>
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-0938"></span>In the Qur’an’s account of the Night Journey, Prophet Muhammad ﷺ is called God’s servant. The extraordinary event does not turn him into a divine figure, and authenticated reports connect the journey with the establishment of the five daily prayers.

    > "Glory be to the One Who took His servant ˹Muḥammad˺ by night from the Sacred Mosque to the Farthest Mosque whose surroundings We have blessed, so that We may show him some of Our signs. Indeed, He alone is the All-Hearing, All-Seeing." (**Sūrat al-Isrāʾ 17:1**), Dr. Mustafa Khattab, *The Clear Quran*[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}
    >
    > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿سُبْحَـٰنَ ٱلَّذِىٓ أَسْرَىٰ بِعَبْدِهِۦ لَيْلًا مِّنَ ٱلْمَسْجِدِ ٱلْحَرَامِ إِلَى ٱلْمَسْجِدِ ٱلْأَقْصَا ٱلَّذِى بَـٰرَكْنَا حَوْلَهُۥ لِنُرِيَهُۥ مِنْ ءَايَـٰتِنَآ ۚ إِنَّهُۥ هُوَ ٱلسَّمِيعُ ٱلْبَصِيرُ ۝١﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ الإِسۡرَاءِ</span></div>
    {: .quote }

    The Night Journey is also reported in the hadith collections *Sahih al-Bukhari* and *Sahih Muslim*.

- <span id="src-0947"></span>Prophet Muhammad ﷺ taught that a servant is especially near to God while prostrating and should increase supplication then.

    > "The closest a servant is to his Lord is while prostrating, so increase your supplication." — *Sahih Muslim* 482[3](/messenger/sources#ref-3){: .footnote}[14](/messenger/sources#ref-14){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-0952"></span>Prophet Muhammad ﷺ compared the five daily prayers to bathing repeatedly in a river, presenting them as a repeated return to God throughout the day.

    > "If a river were at the door of one of you and he bathed in it five times a day, would any dirt remain?" — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 528; *Sahih Muslim* 667[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[3](/messenger/sources#ref-3){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[14](/messenger/sources#ref-14){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-0957"></span>Prophet Muhammad ﷺ taught that fasting is not only abstaining from food and drink; lying and acting on falsehood undermine its ethical purpose.

    > "Whoever does not leave false speech and acting upon it, God has no need of his leaving food and drink." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 1903, 6057[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-0962"></span>The Qur’an describes Prophet Muhammad’s ﷺ mission as a mercy to the worlds, a claim that can be read alongside the concrete teachings on children, servants, debtors, captives, non-Muslims, defeated enemies, and animals collected in this library.

    > "And We have not sent you, [O Muhammad], except as a mercy to the worlds." (**Sūrat al-Anbiyāʾ 21:107**), Saheeh International[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}
    >
    > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿وَمَآ أَرْسَلْنَـٰكَ إِلَّا رَحْمَةً لِّلْعَـٰلَمِينَ ۝١٠٧﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ الأَنبِيَاءِ</span></div>
    {: .quote }

## Repentance and hope
{: #repentance-and-hope }

- <span id="src-0971"></span>The Qur’an tells people who have seriously sinned not to despair of God’s mercy.

    > "Say, ‘O My servants who have transgressed against themselves [by sinning], do not despair of the mercy of Allah. Indeed, Allah forgives all sins. Indeed, it is He who is the Forgiving, the Merciful.’" (**Sūrat al-Zumar 39:53**), Saheeh International[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}
    >
    > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿۞ قُلْ يَـٰعِبَادِىَ ٱلَّذِينَ أَسْرَفُوا۟ عَلَىٰٓ أَنفُسِهِمْ لَا تَقْنَطُوا۟ مِن رَّحْمَةِ ٱللَّهِ ۚ إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ يَغْفِرُ ٱلذُّنُوبَ جَمِيعًا ۚ إِنَّهُۥ هُوَ ٱلْغَفُورُ ٱلرَّحِيمُ ۝٥٣﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ الزُّمَرِ</span></div>
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-0978"></span>The Qur’an teaches that sincere repentance, faith, and reform can transform a person’s moral record.

    > "Except for those who repent, believe and do righteous work. For them Allah will replace their evil deeds with good. And ever is Allah Forgiving and Merciful." (**Sūrat al-Furqān 25:70**), Saheeh International[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}
    >
    > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿إِلَّا مَن تَابَ وَءَامَنَ وَعَمِلَ عَمَلًا صَـٰلِحًا فَأُو۟لَـٰٓئِكَ يُبَدِّلُ ٱللَّهُ سَيِّـَٔاتِهِمْ حَسَنَـٰتٍ ۗ وَكَانَ ٱللَّهُ غَفُورًا رَّحِيمًا ۝٧٠﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ الفُرۡقَانِ</span></div>
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-0985"></span>Prophet Muhammad ﷺ compared God’s joy at a servant’s repentance to the relief of a traveler in a deadly desert who unexpectedly recovers the animal carrying his food and water, saying God’s joy is greater still.

    > "God is more joyful at the repentance of His servant than one of you who finds his lost camel in a barren desert." — *Sahih Muslim* 2747a[3](/messenger/sources#ref-3){: .footnote}[14](/messenger/sources#ref-14){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-0990"></span>In a story Prophet Muhammad ﷺ told, even a man who had murdered one hundred people was told that repentance remained open to him and that he should leave the corrupt environment that had shaped him.

    > "Who can stand between you and repentance? Go to such-and-such a land, for there are people there who worship God." — *Sahih Muslim* 2766a[3](/messenger/sources#ref-3){: .footnote}[14](/messenger/sources#ref-14){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

## Hardship, trust, and inner steadiness
{: #hardship-trust-and-inner-steadiness }

- <span id="src-0997"></span>The Qur’an does not promise a life without hardship; it repeats that ease accompanies hardship.

    > "So, surely with hardship comes ease. Surely with ˹that˺ hardship comes ˹more˺ ease." (**Sūrat al-Sharḥ 94:5-6**), Dr. Mustafa Khattab, *The Clear Quran*[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}
    >
    > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿فَإِنَّ مَعَ ٱلْعُسْرِ يُسْرًا ۝٥ إِنَّ مَعَ ٱلْعُسْرِ يُسْرًا ۝٦﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ الشَّرۡحِ</span></div>
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-1004"></span>The Qur’an states that God does not burden a soul beyond its capacity.

    > "Allah does not charge a soul except [with that within] its capacity. It will have [the consequence of] what [good] it has gained, and it will bear [the consequence of] what [evil] it has earned. ‘Our Lord, do not impose blame upon us if we have forgotten or erred. Our Lord, and lay not upon us a burden like that which You laid upon those before us. Our Lord, and burden us not with that which we have no ability to bear. And pardon us; and forgive us; and have mercy upon us. You are our protector, so give us victory over the disbelieving people.’" (**Sūrat al-Baqarah 2:286**), Saheeh International[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}
    >
    > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿لَا يُكَلِّفُ ٱللَّهُ نَفْسًا إِلَّا وُسْعَهَا ۚ لَهَا مَا كَسَبَتْ وَعَلَيْهَا مَا ٱكْتَسَبَتْ ۗ رَبَّنَا لَا تُؤَاخِذْنَآ إِن نَّسِينَآ أَوْ أَخْطَأْنَا ۚ رَبَّنَا وَلَا تَحْمِلْ عَلَيْنَآ إِصْرًا كَمَا حَمَلْتَهُۥ عَلَى ٱلَّذِينَ مِن قَبْلِنَا ۚ رَبَّنَا وَلَا تُحَمِّلْنَا مَا لَا طَاقَةَ لَنَا بِهِۦ ۖ وَٱعْفُ عَنَّا وَٱغْفِرْ لَنَا وَٱرْحَمْنَآ ۚ أَنتَ مَوْلَىٰنَا فَٱنصُرْنَا عَلَى ٱلْقَوْمِ ٱلْكَـٰفِرِينَ ۝٢٨٦﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ البَقَرَةِ</span></div>
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-1011"></span>The Qur’an presents remembrance of God as a source of inward peace for the heart.

    > "Those who have believed and whose hearts are assured by the remembrance of Allah. Unquestionably, by the remembrance of Allah hearts are assured." (**Sūrat al-Raʿd 13:28**), Saheeh International[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}
    >
    > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ وَتَطْمَئِنُّ قُلُوبُهُم بِذِكْرِ ٱللَّهِ ۗ أَلَا بِذِكْرِ ٱللَّهِ تَطْمَئِنُّ ٱلْقُلُوبُ ۝٢٨﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ الرَّعۡدِ</span></div>
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-1018"></span>The Qur’an tells believers that when they remember God, God remembers them.

    > "So remember Me; I will remember you. And be grateful to Me and do not deny Me." (**Sūrat al-Baqarah 2:152**), Saheeh International[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}
    >
    > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿فَٱذْكُرُونِىٓ أَذْكُرْكُمْ وَٱشْكُرُوا۟ لِى وَلَا تَكْفُرُونِ ۝١٥٢﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ البَقَرَةِ</span></div>
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-1025"></span>Prophet Muhammad ﷺ told believers to pursue what benefits them, seek God’s help, and not give in to helplessness.

    > "Be eager for what benefits you, seek God’s help, and do not give up." — *Sahih Muslim* 2664[3](/messenger/sources#ref-3){: .footnote}[14](/messenger/sources#ref-14){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-1030"></span>Prophet Muhammad ﷺ told believers not to torment themselves with “if only” after a loss, but to accept what has happened as God’s decree.

    > "If something befalls you, do not say, ‘If only I had done such-and-such.’ Say: ‘God decreed, and He did what He willed.’" — *Sahih Muslim* 2664[3](/messenger/sources#ref-3){: .footnote}[14](/messenger/sources#ref-14){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-1035"></span>Prophet Muhammad ﷺ taught that fatigue, illness, grief, hurt, distress, and even the prick of a thorn can become occasions through which sins are erased.

    > "No fatigue, illness, sorrow, hurt, or distress afflicts a Muslim, even a thorn that pricks him, except that God removes sins through it." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 5641-5642[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-1040"></span>The Qur’an teaches gratitude in times of ease and warns against ingratitude.

    > "And [remember] when your Lord proclaimed, ‘If you are grateful, I will surely increase you [in favor]; but if you deny, indeed, My punishment is severe.’" (**Sūrat Ibrāhīm 14:7**), Saheeh International[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}
    >
    > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿وَإِذْ تَأَذَّنَ رَبُّكُمْ لَئِن شَكَرْتُمْ لَأَزِيدَنَّكُمْ ۖ وَلَئِن كَفَرْتُمْ إِنَّ عَذَابِى لَشَدِيدٌ ۝٧﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ إِبۡرَاهِيمَ</span></div>
    {: .quote }

## Intention, balance, humility, and accountability
{: #intention-balance-humility-and-accountability }

- <span id="src-1049"></span>Prophet Muhammad ﷺ taught that the moral value of an action depends on the intention behind it.

    > "Actions are only by intentions, and each person will have only what he intended." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 1[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-1054"></span>Prophet Muhammad ﷺ warned against making religion so difficult through excessive devotion that a person becomes overwhelmed.

    > "Religion is ease. No one makes the religion excessively hard upon himself except that it overwhelms him. So aim rightly and come close." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 39[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-1059"></span>When three men proposed perpetual fasting, all-night prayer, and celibacy as higher forms of devotion, Prophet Muhammad ﷺ pointed instead to his own balanced practice of fasting and eating, praying and sleeping, and marrying.

    > "I fast and I break my fast; I pray and I sleep; and I marry women. Whoever turns away from my way is not of me." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 5063[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-1064"></span>Prophet Muhammad ﷺ approved the principle that a person owes rights to God, to his own self, and to his family, and should give each its due.

    > "Your Lord has a right over you, your self has a right over you, and your family has a right over you. Give each its right." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 1968[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-1069"></span>Prophet Muhammad ﷺ taught that the deeds most loved by God are those done consistently, even when they are small.

    > "The deeds most loved by God are those done most consistently, even if they are small." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 6465[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-1074"></span>Prophet Muhammad ﷺ said that he himself sought God’s forgiveness and repented repeatedly every day.

    > "By God, I seek God’s forgiveness and turn to Him in repentance more than seventy times in a day." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 6307[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-1079"></span>Prophet Muhammad ﷺ taught that the mercy visible throughout creation is only a small part of God’s mercy.

    > "God made mercy into one hundred parts. He sent down one part among creation and retained ninety-nine parts." — *Sahih Muslim* 2752a[3](/messenger/sources#ref-3){: .footnote}[14](/messenger/sources#ref-14){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-1084"></span>After seeing a mother desperately search for her child and then nurse him, Prophet Muhammad ﷺ said that God is even more merciful to His servants than that mother is to her child.

    > "God is more merciful to His servants than this woman is to her child." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 5999[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-1089"></span>Prophet Muhammad ﷺ taught that each person will stand directly before God without an interpreter between them.

    > "Each of you will meet his Lord with no interpreter between him and God." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 3595[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-1094"></span>Prophet Muhammad ﷺ told people not to wish for death because of suffering, but to leave the timing of life and death to God.

    > "Do not wish for death because of harm that has befallen you. Say: ‘O God, keep me alive while life is better for me.’" — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 5671[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-1099"></span>Prophet Muhammad ﷺ rejected belief in evil omens and said that he preferred a hopeful good word.

    > "There is no evil omen, and I like a good omen." They asked what that was. He said, "A good word." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 5756[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-1104"></span>When Aisha asked why Prophet Muhammad ﷺ stood in night prayer until his feet swelled, he said that he wanted to be a grateful servant.

    > "Should I not be a grateful servant?" — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 4836[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-1109"></span>Prophet Muhammad ﷺ taught that no one earns Paradise as a wage owed for deeds, including himself, but enters through God’s mercy.

    > "No one’s deeds will admit him to Paradise." They asked, "Not even you?" He said, "Not even me, unless God envelops me in mercy." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 5673[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-1114"></span>Prophet Muhammad ﷺ told of a woman known for sexual sin who was forgiven after giving water to a thirsty dog, joining mercy to animals with the possibility of repentance from a stigmatized past.

    > "She gave the dog water, so God thanked her for it and forgave her." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 3321[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }
