Each prediction should be considered with its wording, reported outcome, and evidence for dating and transmission. These entries keep those elements together, from public events and named individuals to later signs whose written sources predate their proposed fulfillment.

{% include glossary-key.html %}

## How to evaluate a prediction
{: #how-to-evaluate-a-prediction }

- <span id="src-2679"></span>Predictions have evidential value only when their sources are handled carefully. They are most useful when the wording is specific, there is good reason to date it before the event, the outcome was outside the speaker’s control, and the match does not depend on stretching the language.


- <span id="src-2681"></span>Qur’anic predictions can be compared with early textual witnesses. Hadith require an additional caution because the canonical collections were compiled generations after Prophet Muhammad ﷺ, even though their compilers used named transmission chains and earlier material. A skeptic can therefore argue that an individual report was shaped after the event. The examples below should not be treated as though they all have the same degree of historical independence.


## Qur’anic predictions tied to public events
{: #quranic-predictions-tied-to-public-events }

- <span id="src-2686"></span>The Qur’an predicted a Byzantine recovery after a devastating defeat. The Eastern Roman, or Byzantine, Empire had suffered severe losses to the Sasanian Persians. The Qur’an nevertheless said that the Romans would reverse their defeat “within a few years.”[63](/messenger/sources#ref-63){: .footnote}[64](/messenger/sources#ref-64){: .footnote}

    > "The Byzantines have been defeated in the nearest land. But they, after their defeat, will overcome, within three to nine years. To Allah belongs the command before and after, and that day the believers will rejoice." (**Sūrat al-Rūm 30:2-4**), Saheeh International[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}
    >
    > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿غُلِبَتِ ٱلرُّومُ ۝٢ فِىٓ أَدْنَى ٱلْأَرْضِ وَهُم مِّنۢ بَعْدِ غَلَبِهِمْ سَيَغْلِبُونَ ۝٣ فِى بِضْعِ سِنِينَ ۗ لِلَّهِ ٱلْأَمْرُ مِن قَبْلُ وَمِنۢ بَعْدُ ۚ وَيَوْمَئِذٍ يَفْرَحُ ٱلْمُؤْمِنُونَ ۝٤﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ الرُّومِ</span></div>
    {: .quote }

    <span id="src-2693"></span>The historical reversal was substantial. After Persian advances that included the capture of Jerusalem in 614, Emperor Heraclius began a counteroffensive in 622. Byzantine victories followed, and the Battle of Nineveh in 627 broke Persian resistance. On the traditional Meccan dating of Qur’an 30, the recovery fits naturally within the prediction’s time frame. The strength of the argument still depends partly on how the passage is dated and on what stage of the recovery counts as “victory,” so it is better described as a strong historical correspondence than as an indisputable proof.[63](/messenger/sources#ref-63){: .footnote}[64](/messenger/sources#ref-64){: .footnote}

    > "But they, after their defeat, will overcome, within three to nine years. To Allah belongs the command before and after. And that day the believers will rejoice." (**Sūrat al-Rūm 30:3-4**), Saheeh International[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}; historical context: S. E. Dean, "Battle of Nineveh," 2016
    >
    > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿فِىٓ أَدْنَى ٱلْأَرْضِ وَهُم مِّنۢ بَعْدِ غَلَبِهِمْ سَيَغْلِبُونَ ۝٣ فِى بِضْعِ سِنِينَ ۗ لِلَّهِ ٱلْأَمْرُ مِن قَبْلُ وَمِنۢ بَعْدُ ۚ وَيَوْمَئِذٍ يَفْرَحُ ٱلْمُؤْمِنُونَ ۝٤﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ الرُّومِ</span></div>
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-2700"></span>A Meccan Qur’anic passage said that the hostile “assembly” would be routed. Qur’an 54:45 says the gathered force would be defeated and turn its back. Aisha’s report in Bukhari places the adjacent verse in Mecca while she was still a young girl, well before the Battle of Badr.

    > "[Their] assembly will be defeated, and they will turn their backs [in retreat]." (**Sūrat al-Qamar 54:45**), Saheeh International[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}; Meccan setting of the adjacent verse: *Sahih al-Bukhari* 4876[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    >
    > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿سَيُهْزَمُ ٱلْجَمْعُ وَيُوَلُّونَ ٱلدُّبُرَ ۝٤٥﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ القَمَرِ</span></div>
    {: .quote }

    <span id="src-2707"></span>At Badr, Prophet Muhammad ﷺ recited this prediction before facing the Meccan force, which was then defeated. Bukhari’s account of the battle places the verse on his lips before its outcome was known.

    > He went out in armor reciting, "The assembly will be defeated, and they will turn their backs." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 4875[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-2712"></span>At Hudaybiyyah, the Qur’an promised that the believers would enter the Sacred Mosque safely even though they had just been denied entry. The wording presents the entry as future rather than as something already accomplished.

    > "Indeed, Allah will fulfil His Messenger's vision in all truth: Allah willing, you will surely enter the Sacred Mosque, in security—˹some with˺ heads shaved and ˹others with˺ hair shortened—without fear. He knew what you did not know, so He first granted you the triumph at hand." (**Sūrat al-Fatḥ 48:27**), Dr. Mustafa Khattab, *The Clear Quran*[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}
    >
    > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿لَّقَدْ صَدَقَ ٱللَّهُ رَسُولَهُ ٱلرُّءْيَا بِٱلْحَقِّ ۖ لَتَدْخُلُنَّ ٱلْمَسْجِدَ ٱلْحَرَامَ إِن شَآءَ ٱللَّهُ ءَامِنِينَ مُحَلِّقِينَ رُءُوسَكُمْ وَمُقَصِّرِينَ لَا تَخَافُونَ ۖ فَعَلِمَ مَا لَمْ تَعْلَمُوا۟ فَجَعَلَ مِن دُونِ ذَٰلِكَ فَتْحًا قَرِيبًا ۝٢٧﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ الفَتۡحِ</span></div>
    {: .quote }

    <span id="src-2719"></span>The treaty itself set the conditions for the delayed fulfillment. Quraysh refused entry that year but agreed that the Muslims could return the following year. Bukhari’s Hudaybiyyah account records both the initial frustration and the later entry.

    > The negotiator said, in effect, "We will not allow you this year... but we will allow you next year." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 2731-2732[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

## Predictions concerning specific people
{: #predictions-concerning-specific-people }

- <span id="src-2727"></span>During his final illness, Prophet Muhammad ﷺ privately told Fatima that he expected his own death and that she would be the first member of his household to follow him.

    > "I think my death has drawn near, and you will be the first of my family to follow me." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 3623-3624[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

    <span id="src-2732"></span>The same hadith corpus separately records that Fatima outlived him by only about six months. The fulfillment is precise within the transmitted tradition, although the surviving canonical compilation postdates both events.

    > "She remained alive for six months after the death of the Prophet." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 4240-4241[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-2737"></span>Prophet Muhammad ﷺ predicted that his young grandson Hasan would one day reconcile two large Muslim groups.

    > "This son of mine is a noble leader, and perhaps God will reconcile through him two great groups of Muslims." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 2704[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

    <span id="src-2742"></span>The same Bukhari report also narrates the later reconciliation. In 661 Hasan reached an agreement with Muawiya and withdrew from a contest that could have prolonged a major Muslim civil war. The report is unusually self-contained because the narrator recounts both the reconciliation and the earlier saying.

    > The report describes Hasan concluding peace, then recalls Muhammad's ﷺ words that God would "reconcile through him two great groups of Muslims." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 2704[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-2747"></span>Prophet Muhammad ﷺ said that Ammar ibn Yasir would be killed by a rebellious group. The statement is specific about both Ammar’s death and the moral direction of the conflict in which it would occur.

    > "Ammar will be killed by a rebellious group. Ammar will call them toward God, while they will call him toward the Fire." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 2812[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

    <span id="src-2752"></span>Ammar was later killed while fighting on Ali’s side at the Battle of Siffin in 657. The event became a major point of interpretation because the saying was remembered within the Muslim tradition. Its value as independent prophetic evidence still depends on how early the transmission of the report can be established.[65](/messenger/sources#ref-65){: .footnote}[66](/messenger/sources#ref-66){: .footnote}

    > "Ammar will be killed by a rebellious group." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 2812[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}; historical context: Encyclopedia.com, "Ammar Bin Yasir"
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-2757"></span>Prophet Muhammad ﷺ called Umar and Uthman “two martyrs” while both men were still alive. In a report set on Mount Uhud, Prophet Muhammad ﷺ was with Abu Bakr, Umar, and Uthman and identified the last two as martyrs.

    > "Be firm, Uhud. Upon you are only a prophet, a truthful one, and two martyrs." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 3675[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

    <span id="src-2762"></span>Both Umar and Uthman later died violently rather than from ordinary illness. Umar was assassinated in 644, and Uthman was killed during a siege of his home in 656. As with the report about Ammar, the critical question concerns the age of the saying’s transmission, not whether the later deaths occurred.[65](/messenger/sources#ref-65){: .footnote}[66](/messenger/sources#ref-66){: .footnote}

    > "Upon you are... two martyrs." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 3675[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

## Political and historical developments
{: #political-and-historical-developments }

- <span id="src-2770"></span>Prophet Muhammad ﷺ told Adi ibn Hatim that safe travel through Arabia, access to the Persian king’s treasures, and unusual prosperity would come; Adi remembered privately doubting the first prediction because banditry was still widespread.

    > "You will surely see a woman travel from Hira to circle the Kaaba, fearing none but God... and the treasures of Khosrau will be opened." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 3595[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

    <span id="src-2775"></span>In the same report, Adi says that he personally witnessed the first two events come to pass. He lived to see a woman make the journey safely and says that he himself was among those who opened the treasures of Khosrau. The narrator’s claim to have witnessed the fulfillments is notable, even though the surviving written collection is later.

    > Adi said, "I saw a woman travel from Hira... and I was among those who opened the treasures of Khosrau." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 3595[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-2780"></span>During the difficult Tabuk period, he named his own death, the conquest of Jerusalem, and a deadly plague in a sequence of future events.

    > "Count six things before the Hour: my death, then the conquest of Jerusalem, then a widespread death among you like a disease among sheep..." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 3176[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

    <span id="src-2785"></span>Jerusalem came under Muslim rule only a few years after his death, and the plague of Amwas struck Syria in 638–639. The plague is especially interesting because the conquest and the epidemic are separate events within the predicted sequence. Again, the hadith’s later compilation means that this is not equivalent to a modern prediction preserved in a securely dated pre-event document.[65](/messenger/sources#ref-65){: .footnote}[67](/messenger/sources#ref-67){: .footnote}

    > "My death, then the conquest of Jerusalem, then a widespread death among you..." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 3176[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}; Fordham University's sourcebook on the Plague of Amwas
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-2790"></span>Prophet Muhammad ﷺ foretold that members of his community would undertake a sea expedition and named Umm Haram among the first participants. At the time, Arabian Muslims were not a maritime imperial power.

    > He described people of his community "riding upon the sea" and told Umm Haram, "You will be among the first." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 2788-2789[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

    <span id="src-2795"></span>The same report later describes Umm Haram sailing during Muawiya’s period and dying after she disembarked. The prediction and its reported fulfillment are therefore preserved within the same narrative.

    > "She sailed on the sea during the caliphate of Muawiya... and after she disembarked, she fell from her riding animal and died." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 2788-2789[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-2800"></span>During the period of Muslim weakness in Mecca, he forecast secure travel across southern Arabia. Khabbab’s report begins with persecuted believers asking for relief, making the promise of such security more striking in its original context.

    > "God will complete this matter until a traveler goes from Sana to Hadramawt fearing none but God, except a wolf for his sheep." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 3612[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

## Later signs and the importance of source dating
{: #later-signs-and-the-importance-of-source-dating }

- <span id="src-2808"></span>Prophet Muhammad ﷺ described once-poor shepherds competing in tall or magnificent buildings as a sign near the end of history. The modern skylines of the Arabian Peninsula may remind readers of the report, but the statement is broad, eschatological, and recorded after his lifetime, so the resemblance alone does not make it conclusive proof.

    > "You will see barefoot, destitute shepherds competing with one another in constructing magnificent buildings." — *Sahih Muslim* 8a[3](/messenger/sources#ref-3){: .footnote}[14](/messenger/sources#ref-14){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-2813"></span>A report about a later extremist faction contains a highly specific physical description. Prophet Muhammad ﷺ is reported to have said that among the dissidents would be a man with one malformed arm resembling a breast or fleshy appendage. The report then says that, after fighting the group, Ali ordered the bodies searched and the described man was found.

    > One man among them would have "a hand like a woman's breast or a lump of flesh." — *Sahih Muslim* 1066g[3](/messenger/sources#ref-3){: .footnote}[14](/messenger/sources#ref-14){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

    Other reports describe the same kind of violent religious faction in moral and verbal terms: they would recite the Qur’an intensely while its meaning “did not go beyond their throats” and would pass out of the religion with startling speed. Muslim historical memory identified these descriptions with the Khawarij who emerged during the first civil war. As with the physical description above, the later compilation date makes this transmitted prophetic evidence rather than a prediction preserved in a surviving pre-event manuscript.

    > See *Sahih al-Bukhari* 3610[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}; also *Sahih al-Bukhari* 6930[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}.
    {: .quote }

    <span id="src-2823"></span>This example shows why the date of the surviving source matters. A detailed match preserved only in later writing can be explained either as faithfully transmitted foreknowledge or as a tradition shaped after the event. It can contribute to a cumulative case, but it should not be presented as though a dated seventh-century manuscript of the saying had survived from before the battle.

    > Ali said during the search, "By God, I have not lied, nor was I lied to," and the body was then found according to the report. — *Sahih Muslim* 1066g[3](/messenger/sources#ref-3){: .footnote}[14](/messenger/sources#ref-14){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-2828"></span>The Qur’an promised a frightened and insecure believing community that it would eventually be established in security. The promise is broader than the Byzantine prediction and is conditional on faith and righteous conduct. Even so, it is worth comparing with the community’s rapid movement from threatened survival to political security in Arabia.

    > "Allah has promised those who have believed among you and done righteous deeds that He will surely grant them succession [to authority] upon the earth just as He granted it to those before them and that He will surely establish for them [therein] their religion which He has preferred for them and that He will surely substitute for them, after their fear, security, [for] they worship Me, not associating anything with Me. But whoever disbelieves after that - then those are the defiantly disobedient." (**Sūrat al-Nūr 24:55**), Saheeh International[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}
    >
    > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿وَعَدَ ٱللَّهُ ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ مِنكُمْ وَعَمِلُوا۟ ٱلصَّـٰلِحَـٰتِ لَيَسْتَخْلِفَنَّهُمْ فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ كَمَا ٱسْتَخْلَفَ ٱلَّذِينَ مِن قَبْلِهِمْ وَلَيُمَكِّنَنَّ لَهُمْ دِينَهُمُ ٱلَّذِى ٱرْتَضَىٰ لَهُمْ وَلَيُبَدِّلَنَّهُم مِّنۢ بَعْدِ خَوْفِهِمْ أَمْنًا ۚ يَعْبُدُونَنِى لَا يُشْرِكُونَ بِى شَيْـًٔا ۚ وَمَن كَفَرَ بَعْدَ ذَٰلِكَ فَأُو۟لَـٰٓئِكَ هُمُ ٱلْفَـٰسِقُونَ ۝٥٥﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ النُّورِ</span></div>
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-2835"></span>The Qur’an also forecast the public ascendancy of the religion in a chapter revealed around Hudaybiyyah, just after the Muslims had been kept out of Mecca. Within two years Mecca came under Prophet Muhammad’s ﷺ authority, and within the first Muslim generations Islam spread far beyond Arabia. Because the language of “prevail” can be understood in more than one way, the expansion is better treated as a strong historical correspondence than as a precisely dated prediction.

    > "He is the One Who has sent His Messenger with ˹right˺ guidance and the religion of truth, making it prevail over all others. And sufficient is Allah as a Witness." (**Sūrat al-Fatḥ 48:28**), Dr. Mustafa Khattab, *The Clear Quran*[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}
    >
    > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿هُوَ ٱلَّذِىٓ أَرْسَلَ رَسُولَهُۥ بِٱلْهُدَىٰ وَدِينِ ٱلْحَقِّ لِيُظْهِرَهُۥ عَلَى ٱلدِّينِ كُلِّهِۦ ۚ وَكَفَىٰ بِٱللَّهِ شَهِيدًا ۝٢٨﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ الفَتۡحِ</span></div>
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-2842"></span>One hadith prediction has an unusual advantage because its major written sources clearly predate the event commonly identified as its fulfillment. Prophet Muhammad ﷺ is reported to have said that before the end of history a great fire would emerge from the Hijaz, the western Arabian region containing Mecca and Medina, with light reaching as far as Busra in Syria. The prediction appears in both Bukhari and Muslim.

    > "The Hour will not come until a fire emerges from the land of the Hijaz, illuminating the necks of the camels at Busra." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 7118[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}; *Sahih Muslim* 2902[3](/messenger/sources#ref-3){: .footnote}[14](/messenger/sources#ref-14){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

    <span id="src-2847"></span>A major volcanic eruption occurred beside Medina in 1256 CE, centuries after Bukhari and Muslim were compiled. Modern geological work confirms that the Harrat Rahat eruption produced a lava flow about 23 kilometers long and brought it within roughly 4 kilometers of Medina. Medieval Muslim accounts also describe extraordinary nighttime visibility over long distances, though those descriptions may themselves have been influenced by knowledge of the hadith. The safest independent comparison is therefore the central one: a great “fire” did emerge in the Hijaz near Medina centuries after the canonical collections containing the prediction already existed.[68](/messenger/sources#ref-68){: .footnote}

    > "A fire emerges from the land of the Hijaz..." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 7118[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}; U.S. Geological Survey, eruptive history near Al Madinah; Smithsonian Global Volcanism Program, Harrat Rahat
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-2852"></span>The Qur’an publicly named Abu Lahab, Prophet Muhammad’s ﷺ fiercely hostile uncle, as a man headed for ruin and punishment while he still had years left to oppose the mission. The evidential interest is that the text commits itself publicly regarding a named living opponent rather than making only anonymous future claims. A common apologetic argument says that Abu Lahab could simply have pretended to convert and falsified the verse, but that goes too far because a false profession could have been described as hypocrisy. The narrower point is firmer: the Qur’an made a public claim about a living adversary, and Abu Lahab remained an opponent until his death.

    > "May the hands of Abu Lahab perish, and he ˹himself˺ perish. Neither his wealth nor ˹worldly˺ gains will benefit him. He will burn in a flaming Fire." (**Sūrat al-Masad 111:1-3**), Dr. Mustafa Khattab, *The Clear Quran*[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}
    >
    > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿تَبَّتْ يَدَآ أَبِى لَهَبٍ وَتَبَّ ۝١ مَآ أَغْنَىٰ عَنْهُ مَالُهُۥ وَمَا كَسَبَ ۝٢ سَيَصْلَىٰ نَارًا ذَاتَ لَهَبٍ ۝٣﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ المَسَدِ</span></div>
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-2859"></span>A hadith also speaks in sweeping terms about the great imperial rulers surrounding Arabia, Khosrau and Caesar. The Sasanian monarchy collapsed during the first generation of Muslim expansion. Byzantium survived for centuries but permanently lost its Near Eastern heartlands to the new polity. Because the phrase “no Caesar after him” has required interpretation, and because the hadith was canonically compiled after those early conquests, this example is less independent than the later Hijaz-fire report. It can still form part of the cumulative record, but it should be weighted accordingly.

    > "When Khosrau is ruined, there will be no Khosrau after him; and when Caesar is ruined, there will be no Caesar after him... and their treasures will be spent in God's cause." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 3027[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}; *Sahih al-Bukhari* 3028[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
