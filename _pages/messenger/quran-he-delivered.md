The Qur’an’s relationship with its messenger is itself part of the evidence. These entries cover its correction and restriction of him, the reception of revelation, the literary challenge, theological continuity, and the history of the text’s preservation.

{% include glossary-key.html %}

## Bound to deliver the revelation
{: #bound-to-deliver-the-revelation }

- <span id="src-2450"></span>The relationship between the Qur’an and the man who recited it is itself part of the case. The text does not simply enlarge Prophet Muhammad’s ﷺ personal authority: it corrects him, restricts him, burdens him, distinguishes him from God, and presents him as bound by the revelation he delivers.


- <span id="src-2452"></span>The Qur’an presents Prophet Muhammad ﷺ as subject to revelation rather than in control of it. When opponents asked him to produce a different recitation or alter the existing one, the response said that he had no authority to change it on his own.

    > "When Our clear revelations are recited to them, those who do not expect to meet Us say ˹to the Prophet˺, 'Bring us a different Quran or make some changes in it.' Say ˹to them˺, 'It is not for me to change it on my own. I only follow what is revealed to me. I fear, if I were to disobey my Lord, the punishment of a tremendous Day.'" (**Sūrat Yūnus 10:15**), Dr. Mustafa Khattab, *The Clear Quran*[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}
    >
    > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿وَإِذَا تُتْلَىٰ عَلَيْهِمْ ءَايَاتُنَا بَيِّنَـٰتٍ ۙ قَالَ ٱلَّذِينَ لَا يَرْجُونَ لِقَآءَنَا ٱئْتِ بِقُرْءَانٍ غَيْرِ هَـٰذَآ أَوْ بَدِّلْهُ ۚ قُلْ مَا يَكُونُ لِىٓ أَنْ أُبَدِّلَهُۥ مِن تِلْقَآئِ نَفْسِىٓ ۖ إِنْ أَتَّبِعُ إِلَّا مَا يُوحَىٰٓ إِلَىَّ ۖ إِنِّىٓ أَخَافُ إِنْ عَصَيْتُ رَبِّى عَذَابَ يَوْمٍ عَظِيمٍ ۝١٥﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ يُونُسَ</span></div>
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-2594"></span>The Qur’an not only forbids him to change revelation; it makes complete delivery an obligation. That removes another convenient form of authorial control: withholding material when delivering it becomes personally costly.

    > "O Messenger, announce that which has been revealed to you from your Lord, and if you do not, then you have not conveyed His message. And Allah will protect you from the people. Indeed, Allah does not guide the disbelieving people." (**Sūrat al-Māʾidah 5:67**), Saheeh International[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}
    >
    > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿۞ يَـٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلرَّسُولُ بَلِّغْ مَآ أُنزِلَ إِلَيْكَ مِن رَّبِّكَ ۖ وَإِن لَّمْ تَفْعَلْ فَمَا بَلَّغْتَ رِسَالَتَهُۥ ۚ وَٱللَّهُ يَعْصِمُكَ مِنَ ٱلنَّاسِ ۗ إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ لَا يَهْدِى ٱلْقَوْمَ ٱلْكَـٰفِرِينَ ۝٦٧﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ المَائـِدَةِ</span></div>
    {: .quote }

## Public correction and accountability
{: #public-correction-and-accountability }

- <span id="src-2459"></span>It publicly corrects him for turning from a blind man while giving attention to influential Meccans. Abdullah ibn Umm Maktum came seeking instruction while Prophet Muhammad ﷺ was speaking to Meccan notables, and Prophet Muhammad ﷺ frowned and turned away. The criticism became permanent scripture recited by his own followers.

    > "The Prophet frowned and turned away. Because there came to him the blind man, [interrupting]. But what would make you perceive, [O Muhammad], that perhaps he might be purified. Or be reminded and the remembrance would benefit him? As for he who thinks himself without need, To him you give attention. And not upon you [is any blame] if he will not be purified. But as for he who came to you striving [for knowledge]. While he fears [Allah], From him you are distracted." (**Sūrat ʿAbasa 80:1-10**), Saheeh International[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}
    >
    > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿ عَبَسَ وَتَوَلَّىٰٓ ۝١ أَن جَآءَهُ ٱلْأَعْمَىٰ ۝٢ وَمَا يُدْرِيكَ لَعَلَّهُۥ يَزَّكَّىٰٓ ۝٣ أَوْ يَذَّكَّرُ فَتَنفَعَهُ ٱلذِّكْرَىٰٓ ۝٤ أَمَّا مَنِ ٱسْتَغْنَىٰ ۝٥ فَأَنتَ لَهُۥ تَصَدَّىٰ ۝٦ وَمَا عَلَيْكَ أَلَّا يَزَّكَّىٰ ۝٧ وَأَمَّا مَن جَآءَكَ يَسْعَىٰ ۝٨ وَهُوَ يَخْشَىٰ ۝٩ فَأَنتَ عَنْهُ تَلَهَّىٰ ۝١٠﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ عَبَسَ</span></div>
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-2466"></span>The Qur’an corrects Prophet Muhammad ﷺ for being too ready to grant certain people permission.

    > "May Allah pardon you ˹O Prophet˺! Why did you give them permission ˹to stay behind˺ before those who told the truth were distinguished from those who were lying." (**Sūrat al-Tawbah 9:43**), Dr. Mustafa Khattab, *The Clear Quran*[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}
    >
    > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿عَفَا ٱللَّهُ عَنكَ لِمَ أَذِنتَ لَهُمْ حَتَّىٰ يَتَبَيَّنَ لَكَ ٱلَّذِينَ صَدَقُوا۟ وَتَعْلَمَ ٱلْكَـٰذِبِينَ ۝٤٣﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ التَّوۡبَةِ</span></div>
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-2473"></span>It corrects him for restricting something lawful to himself in an attempt to please members of his household.

    > "O Prophet! Why do you prohibit ˹yourself˺ from what Allah has made lawful to you, seeking to please your wives? And Allah is All-Forgiving, Most Merciful." (**Sūrat al-Taḥrīm 66:1**), Dr. Mustafa Khattab, *The Clear Quran*[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}
    >
    > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿ يَـٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلنَّبِىُّ لِمَ تُحَرِّمُ مَآ أَحَلَّ ٱللَّهُ لَكَ ۖ تَبْتَغِى مَرْضَاتَ أَزْوَٰجِكَ ۚ وَٱللَّهُ غَفُورٌ رَّحِيمٌ ۝١﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ التَّحۡرِيمِ</span></div>
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-2480"></span>It preserves personally uncomfortable criticism in the episode involving Zayd and Zaynab. Whatever interpretation one gives the event, a text written simply to protect Prophet Muhammad’s ﷺ image had no obvious reason to preserve the statement that he feared people’s reaction.

    > "And [remember, O Muhammad], when you said to the one on whom Allah bestowed favor and you bestowed favor, 'Keep your wife and fear Allah,' while you concealed within yourself that which Allah is to disclose. And you feared the people, while Allah has more right that you fear Him. So when Zayd had no longer any need for her, We married her to you in order that there not be upon the believers any discomfort concerning the wives of their adopted sons when they no longer have need of them. And ever is the command of Allah accomplished." (**Sūrat al-Aḥzāb 33:37**), Saheeh International[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}
    >
    > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿وَإِذْ تَقُولُ لِلَّذِىٓ أَنْعَمَ ٱللَّهُ عَلَيْهِ وَأَنْعَمْتَ عَلَيْهِ أَمْسِكْ عَلَيْكَ زَوْجَكَ وَٱتَّقِ ٱللَّهَ وَتُخْفِى فِى نَفْسِكَ مَا ٱللَّهُ مُبْدِيهِ وَتَخْشَى ٱلنَّاسَ وَٱللَّهُ أَحَقُّ أَن تَخْشَىٰهُ ۖ فَلَمَّا قَضَىٰ زَيْدٌ مِّنْهَا وَطَرًا زَوَّجْنَـٰكَهَا لِكَىْ لَا يَكُونَ عَلَى ٱلْمُؤْمِنِينَ حَرَجٌ فِىٓ أَزْوَٰجِ أَدْعِيَآئِهِمْ إِذَا قَضَوْا۟ مِنْهُنَّ وَطَرًا ۚ وَكَانَ أَمْرُ ٱللَّهِ مَفْعُولًا ۝٣٧﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ الأَحۡزَابِ</span></div>
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-2487"></span>It threatens Prophet Muhammad ﷺ himself with severe punishment if he were to invent words and attribute them to God. A human author could of course write such a threat, so the verse is not self-proving. But it places the supposed author under an unusually severe condemnation of the very fabrication of which a skeptic would accuse him.

    > "And if Muhammad had made up about Us some [false] sayings, We would have seized him by the right hand; Then We would have cut from him the aorta." (**Sūrat al-Ḥāqqah 69:44-46**), Saheeh International[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}
    >
    > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿وَلَوْ تَقَوَّلَ عَلَيْنَا بَعْضَ ٱلْأَقَاوِيلِ ۝٤٤ لَأَخَذْنَا مِنْهُ بِٱلْيَمِينِ ۝٤٥ ثُمَّ لَقَطَعْنَا مِنْهُ ٱلْوَتِينَ ۝٤٦﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ الحَاقَّةِ</span></div>
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-2494"></span>It warns that he himself would face multiplied punishment if he yielded to pressure and falsely attributed a compromise to God.

    > "And if We had not strengthened you, you would have almost inclined to them a little. Then [if you had], We would have made you taste double [punishment in] life and double [after] death. Then you would not find for yourself against Us a helper." (**Sūrat al-Isrāʾ 17:74-75**), Saheeh International[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}
    >
    > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿وَلَوْلَآ أَن ثَبَّتْنَـٰكَ لَقَدْ كِدتَّ تَرْكَنُ إِلَيْهِمْ شَيْـًٔا قَلِيلًا ۝٧٤ إِذًا لَّأَذَقْنَـٰكَ ضِعْفَ ٱلْحَيَوٰةِ وَضِعْفَ ٱلْمَمَاتِ ثُمَّ لَا تَجِدُ لَكَ عَلَيْنَا نَصِيرًا ۝٧٥﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ الإِسۡرَاءِ</span></div>
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-2501"></span>It corrects him over speaking too confidently about what he will do in the future without acknowledging God’s will.

    > "And never say of anything, 'Indeed, I will do that tomorrow,' Except [when adding], 'If Allah wills.' And remember your Lord when you forget [it] and say, 'Perhaps my Lord will guide me to what is nearer than this to right conduct.'" (**Sūrat al-Kahf 18:23-24**), Saheeh International[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}
    >
    > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿وَلَا تَقُولَنَّ لِشَا۟ىْءٍ إِنِّى فَاعِلٌ ذَٰلِكَ غَدًا ۝٢٣ إِلَّآ أَن يَشَآءَ ٱللَّهُ ۚ وَٱذْكُر رَّبَّكَ إِذَا نَسِيتَ وَقُلْ عَسَىٰٓ أَن يَهْدِيَنِ رَبِّى لِأَقْرَبَ مِنْ هَـٰذَا رَشَدًا ۝٢٤﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ الكَهۡفِ</span></div>
    {: .quote }

## Receiving revelation and the distinction from ordinary speech
{: #receiving-revelation-and-the-distinction-from-ordinary-speech }

- <span id="src-2508"></span>It tells him not to rush his recitation while revelation is being received. The passage portrays Prophet Muhammad ﷺ trying to retain words being delivered to him and then being told that their collection and recitation are God’s responsibility.

    > "Move not your tongue with it, [O Muhammad], to hasten with recitation of the Qur'an. Indeed, upon Us is its collection [in your heart] and [to make possible] its recitation." (**Sūrat al-Qiyāmah 75:16-17**), Saheeh International[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}
    >
    > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿لَا تُحَرِّكْ بِهِۦ لِسَانَكَ لِتَعْجَلَ بِهِۦٓ ۝١٦ إِنَّ عَلَيْنَا جَمْعَهُۥ وَقُرْءَانَهُۥ ۝١٧﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ القِيَامَةِ</span></div>
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-2515"></span>Another passage similarly tells him not to hurry with the Qur’an before its revelation to him is complete.

    > "So high [above all] is Allah, the Sovereign, the Truth. And, [O Muhammad], do not hasten with [recitation of] the Qur'an before its revelation is completed to you, and say, 'My Lord, increase me in knowledge.'" (**Sūrat Ṭāhā 20:114**), Saheeh International[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}
    >
    > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿فَتَعَـٰلَى ٱللَّهُ ٱلْمَلِكُ ٱلْحَقُّ ۗ وَلَا تَعْجَلْ بِٱلْقُرْءَانِ مِن قَبْلِ أَن يُقْضَىٰٓ إِلَيْكَ وَحْيُهُۥ ۖ وَقُل رَّبِّ زِدْنِى عِلْمًا ۝١١٤﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ طه</span></div>
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-2522"></span>It says explicitly that he did not possess this scripture before it was revealed to him.

    > "And you did not recite before it any scripture, nor did you inscribe one with your right hand. Otherwise the falsifiers would have had [cause for] doubt." (**Sūrat al-ʿAnkabūt 29:48**), Saheeh International[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}
    >
    > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿وَمَا كُنتَ تَتْلُوا۟ مِن قَبْلِهِۦ مِن كِتَـٰبٍ وَلَا تَخُطُّهُۥ بِيَمِينِكَ ۖ إِذًا لَّٱرْتَابَ ٱلْمُبْطِلُونَ ۝٤٨﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ العَنكَبُوتِ</span></div>
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-2529"></span>It presents the contents of the revealed narratives as knowledge Prophet Muhammad ﷺ did not previously possess.

    > "And thus We have revealed to you an inspiration of Our command. You did not know what is the Book or [what is] faith, but We have made it a light by which We guide whom We will of Our servants. And indeed, [O Muhammad], you guide to a straight path." (**Sūrat al-Shūrā 42:52**), Saheeh International[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}
    >
    > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿وَكَذَٰلِكَ أَوْحَيْنَآ إِلَيْكَ رُوحًا مِّنْ أَمْرِنَا ۚ مَا كُنتَ تَدْرِى مَا ٱلْكِتَـٰبُ وَلَا ٱلْإِيمَـٰنُ وَلَـٰكِن جَعَلْنَـٰهُ نُورًا نَّهْدِى بِهِۦ مَن نَّشَآءُ مِنْ عِبَادِنَا ۚ وَإِنَّكَ لَتَهْدِىٓ إِلَىٰ صِرَٰطٍ مُّسْتَقِيمٍ ۝٥٢﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ الشُّورَىٰ</span></div>
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-2642"></span>The Qur’an sounds recognizably different from the ordinary speech attributed to Prophet Muhammad ﷺ. Hadith preserve conversations, jokes, legal explanations, household remarks, and political instructions, while the Qur’an maintains a markedly different diction, cadence, structure, and scriptural voice. This is not a straightforward stylometric proof, especially because many hadith were transmitted by meaning. It does, however, reinforce the observation that revelation did not simply appear as ordinary speech whenever an answer was convenient. The month-long delay during Aisha’s accusation makes the same point through timing rather than style.[34](/messenger/sources#ref-34){: .footnote}[35](/messenger/sources#ref-35){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}[47](/messenger/sources#ref-47){: .footnote}[48](/messenger/sources#ref-48){: .footnote}[79](/messenger/sources#ref-79){: .footnote}


## The literary challenge and coherence
{: #the-literary-challenge-and-coherence }

- <span id="src-2536"></span>It invites readers to examine it for contradictions. Issuing the challenge does not itself make the text divine, but it shows that consistency across the revelation is one of the tests the Qur’an asks readers to consider.

    > "Then do they not reflect upon the Qur'an? If it had been from [any] other than Allah, they would have found within it much contradiction." (**Sūrat al-Nisāʾ 4:82**), Saheeh International[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}
    >
    > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿أَفَلَا يَتَدَبَّرُونَ ٱلْقُرْءَانَ ۚ وَلَوْ كَانَ مِنْ عِندِ غَيْرِ ٱللَّهِ لَوَجَدُوا۟ فِيهِ ٱخْتِلَـٰفًا كَثِيرًا ۝٨٢﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ النِّسَاءِ</span></div>
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-2543"></span>It issues a literary challenge to its first Arabic audience. The argument is not that an English translation possesses some objectively measurable magical quality. It concerns the Arabic itself: its form, rhetoric, structure, sound, compression of meaning, and the response of people competent in the language.[34](/messenger/sources#ref-34){: .footnote}[35](/messenger/sources#ref-35){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}[47](/messenger/sources#ref-47){: .footnote}[48](/messenger/sources#ref-48){: .footnote}[78](/messenger/sources#ref-78){: .footnote}[79](/messenger/sources#ref-79){: .footnote}

    > "And if you are in doubt about what We have sent down upon Our Servant [Muhammad], then produce a surah the like thereof and call upon your witnesses other than Allah, if you should be truthful." (**Sūrat al-Baqarah 2:23**), Saheeh International[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}
    >
    > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿وَإِن كُنتُمْ فِى رَيْبٍ مِّمَّا نَزَّلْنَا عَلَىٰ عَبْدِنَا فَأْتُوا۟ بِسُورَةٍ مِّن مِّثْلِهِۦ وَٱدْعُوا۟ شُهَدَآءَكُم مِّن دُونِ ٱللَّهِ إِن كُنتُمْ صَـٰدِقِينَ ۝٢٣﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ البَقَرَةِ</span></div>
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-2550"></span>The challenge appears at different scales, from producing one comparable chapter to confronting the recitation as a whole.

    > "Say, 'If mankind and the jinn gathered in order to produce the like of this Qur'an, they could not produce the like of it, even if they were to each other assistants.'" (**Sūrat al-Isrāʾ 17:88**), Saheeh International[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}
    >
    > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿قُل لَّئِنِ ٱجْتَمَعَتِ ٱلْإِنسُ وَٱلْجِنُّ عَلَىٰٓ أَن يَأْتُوا۟ بِمِثْلِ هَـٰذَا ٱلْقُرْءَانِ لَا يَأْتُونَ بِمِثْلِهِۦ وَلَوْ كَانَ بَعْضُهُمْ لِبَعْضٍ ظَهِيرًا ۝٨٨﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ الإِسۡرَاءِ</span></div>
    {: .quote }

## God, the messenger, and the limits of his authority
{: #god-the-messenger-and-the-limits-of-his-authority }

- <span id="src-2557"></span>God, not Prophet Muhammad ﷺ, is the center of the Qur’an. The text repeatedly recounts earlier prophets, corrects Prophet Muhammad ﷺ, limits his powers, and calls him a messenger. This does not prove divine authorship, but it does not read naturally as a work whose chief purpose is the human speaker’s self-glorification.

    > "Say, 'I am only a man like you, to whom has been revealed that your god is one God. So whoever would hope for the meeting with his Lord - let him do righteous work and not associate in the worship of his Lord anyone.'" (**Sūrat al-Kahf 18:110**), Saheeh International[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}
    >
    > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿قُلْ إِنَّمَآ أَنَا۠ بَشَرٌ مِّثْلُكُمْ يُوحَىٰٓ إِلَىَّ أَنَّمَآ إِلَـٰهُكُمْ إِلَـٰهٌ وَٰحِدٌ ۖ فَمَن كَانَ يَرْجُوا۟ لِقَآءَ رَبِّهِۦ فَلْيَعْمَلْ عَمَلًا صَـٰلِحًا وَلَا يُشْرِكْ بِعِبَادَةِ رَبِّهِۦٓ أَحَدًۢا ۝١١٠﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ الكَهۡفِ</span></div>
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-2564"></span>The message places itself in continuity with Abraham, Moses, Jesus, and earlier revelation rather than introducing a new tribal deity.

    > "Say, 'We have believed in Allah and in what was revealed to us and what was revealed to Abraham, Ishmael, Isaac, Jacob, and the Descendants, and in what was given to Moses and Jesus and to the prophets from their Lord. We make no distinction between any of them, and we are Muslims [submitting] to Him.'" (**Sūrat Āl ʿImrān 3:84**), Saheeh International[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}
    >
    > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿قُلْ ءَامَنَّا بِٱللَّهِ وَمَآ أُنزِلَ عَلَيْنَا وَمَآ أُنزِلَ عَلَىٰٓ إِبْرَٰهِيمَ وَإِسْمَـٰعِيلَ وَإِسْحَـٰقَ وَيَعْقُوبَ وَٱلْأَسْبَاطِ وَمَآ أُوتِىَ مُوسَىٰ وَعِيسَىٰ وَٱلنَّبِيُّونَ مِن رَّبِّهِمْ لَا نُفَرِّقُ بَيْنَ أَحَدٍ مِّنْهُمْ وَنَحْنُ لَهُۥ مُسْلِمُونَ ۝٨٤﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ آلِ عِمۡرَانَ</span></div>
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-2601"></span>When opponents demanded spectacular signs on their own terms, the Qur’an refused to turn Prophet Muhammad ﷺ into a supernatural performer. They asked for springs, gardens, a house of gold, angels, or a book physically brought down to them. The response was that he was a human messenger; elsewhere, the recited Book itself is presented as sufficient. He is also told to deny independent knowledge even of what will happen to him and his audience.

    > "Or you have a house of gold or you ascend into the sky. And [even then], we will not believe in your ascension until you bring down to us a book we may read." Say, "Exalted is my Lord! Was I ever but a human messenger?" (**Sūrat al-Isrāʾ 17:93**), Saheeh International[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}
    >
    > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿أَوْ يَكُونَ لَكَ بَيْتٌ مِّن زُخْرُفٍ أَوْ تَرْقَىٰ فِى ٱلسَّمَآءِ وَلَن نُّؤْمِنَ لِرُقِيِّكَ حَتَّىٰ تُنَزِّلَ عَلَيْنَا كِتَـٰبًا نَّقْرَؤُهُۥ ۗ قُلْ سُبْحَانَ رَبِّى هَلْ كُنتُ إِلَّا بَشَرًا رَّسُولًا ۝٩٣﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ الإِسۡرَاءِ</span></div>
    {: .quote }

    > "And is it not sufficient for them that We revealed to you the Book which is recited to them? Indeed in that is a mercy and reminder for a people who believe." (**Sūrat al-ʿAnkabūt 29:51**), Saheeh International[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}
    >
    > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿أَوَلَمْ يَكْفِهِمْ أَنَّآ أَنزَلْنَا عَلَيْكَ ٱلْكِتَـٰبَ يُتْلَىٰ عَلَيْهِمْ ۚ إِنَّ فِى ذَٰلِكَ لَرَحْمَةً وَذِكْرَىٰ لِقَوْمٍ يُؤْمِنُونَ ۝٥١﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ العَنكَبُوتِ</span></div>
    {: .quote }

    > "Say, 'I am not something original among the messengers, nor do I know what will be done with me or with you. I only follow that which is revealed to me, and I am not but a clear warner.'" (**Sūrat al-Aḥqāf 46:9**), Saheeh International[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}
    >
    > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿قُلْ مَا كُنتُ بِدْعًا مِّنَ ٱلرُّسُلِ وَمَآ أَدْرِى مَا يُفْعَلُ بِى وَلَا بِكُمْ ۖ إِنْ أَتَّبِعُ إِلَّا مَا يُوحَىٰٓ إِلَىَّ وَمَآ أَنَا۠ إِلَّا نَذِيرٌ مُّبِينٌ ۝٩﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ الأَحۡقَافِ</span></div>
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-2618"></span>The Qur’an places additional burdens on Prophet Muhammad ﷺ rather than simply granting him privileges. The earliest revelations command night worship with unusual force, and classical Muslim interpretation commonly treated the night vigil as a special obligation for him while it remained voluntary for the wider community. Whatever one concludes about the exact legal classification, the text points toward additional discipline rather than easy exemption.

    > "O you who wraps himself [in clothing], Arise [to pray] the night, except for a little - Half of it - or subtract from it a little, Or add to it, and recite the Qur'an with measured recitation." (**Sūrat al-Muzzammil 73:1-4**), Saheeh International[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}
    >
    > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿ يَـٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلْمُزَّمِّلُ ۝١ قُمِ ٱلَّيْلَ إِلَّا قَلِيلًا ۝٢ نِّصْفَهُۥٓ أَوِ ٱنقُصْ مِنْهُ قَلِيلًا ۝٣ أَوْ زِدْ عَلَيْهِ وَرَتِّلِ ٱلْقُرْءَانَ تَرْتِيلًا ۝٤﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ المُزَّمِّلِ</span></div>
    {: .quote }

    > "And from [part of] the night, pray with it as additional [worship] for you; it is expected that your Lord will resurrect you to a praised station." (**Sūrat al-Isrāʾ 17:79**), Saheeh International[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}
    >
    > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿وَمِنَ ٱلَّيْلِ فَتَهَجَّدْ بِهِۦ نَافِلَةً لَّكَ عَسَىٰٓ أَن يَبْعَثَكَ رَبُّكَ مَقَامًا مَّحْمُودًا ۝٧٩﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ الإِسۡرَاءِ</span></div>
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-2630"></span>It also places personal limits on him during the height of his Medinan authority. Qur’an 33:52 closes further marriages to him under the terms stated there, while an earlier passage tells his existing wives that if they prefer worldly comfort he is to release them graciously. Neither restriction is an obvious addition for a text supposedly written simply to maximize its author’s domestic options.

    > "Not lawful to you, [O Muhammad], are [any additional] women after [this], nor [is it] for you to exchange them for [other] wives, even if their beauty were to please you, except what your right hand possesses. And ever is Allah, over all things, an Observer." (**Sūrat al-Aḥzāb 33:52**), Saheeh International[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}
    >
    > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿لَّا يَحِلُّ لَكَ ٱلنِّسَآءُ مِنۢ بَعْدُ وَلَآ أَن تَبَدَّلَ بِهِنَّ مِنْ أَزْوَٰجٍ وَلَوْ أَعْجَبَكَ حُسْنُهُنَّ إِلَّا مَا مَلَكَتْ يَمِينُكَ ۗ وَكَانَ ٱللَّهُ عَلَىٰ كُلِّ شَىْءٍ رَّقِيبًا ۝٥٢﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ الأَحۡزَابِ</span></div>
    {: .quote }

    > "O Prophet, say to your wives, 'If you should desire the worldly life and its adornment, then come, I will provide for you and give you a gracious release. But if you should desire Allah and His Messenger and the home of the Hereafter - then indeed, Allah has prepared for the doers of good among you a great reward.'" (**Sūrat al-Aḥzāb 33:28-29**), Saheeh International[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}
    >
    > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿يَـٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلنَّبِىُّ قُل لِّأَزْوَٰجِكَ إِن كُنتُنَّ تُرِدْنَ ٱلْحَيَوٰةَ ٱلدُّنْيَا وَزِينَتَهَا فَتَعَالَيْنَ أُمَتِّعْكُنَّ وَأُسَرِّحْكُنَّ سَرَاحًا جَمِيلًا ۝٢٨ وَإِن كُنتُنَّ تُرِدْنَ ٱللَّهَ وَرَسُولَهُۥ وَٱلدَّارَ ٱلْـَٔاخِرَةَ فَإِنَّ ٱللَّهَ أَعَدَّ لِلْمُحْسِنَـٰتِ مِنكُنَّ أَجْرًا عَظِيمًا ۝٢٩﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ الأَحۡزَابِ</span></div>
    {: .quote }

## Preservation, manuscripts, and reading traditions
{: #preservation-manuscripts-and-reading-traditions }

- <span id="src-2578"></span>The Qur’an makes an explicit claim that God will preserve the revelation.

    > "It is certainly We Who have revealed the Reminder, and it is certainly We Who will preserve it." (**Sūrat al-Ḥijr 15:9**), Dr. Mustafa Khattab, *The Clear Quran*[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}
    >
    > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿إِنَّا نَحْنُ نَزَّلْنَا ٱلذِّكْرَ وَإِنَّا لَهُۥ لَحَـٰفِظُونَ ۝٩﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ الحِجۡرِ</span></div>
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-2585"></span>**Very early manuscript evidence** makes that preservation claim historically significant. The Birmingham Qur’an leaves contain portions of chapters 18–20 in an early Arabic script. Radiocarbon dating places the parchment between 568 and 645 CE with 95.4% probability, while palaeographic assessment dates the manuscript to the mid-seventh century. The carbon test dates the parchment rather than the ink, so the evidence should not be claimed to show more than it does.[47](/messenger/sources#ref-47){: .footnote}[49](/messenger/sources#ref-49){: .footnote}[50](/messenger/sources#ref-50){: .footnote}[51](/messenger/sources#ref-51){: .footnote}[52](/messenger/sources#ref-52){: .footnote}


- <span id="src-2587"></span>The case for Qur’anic preservation is strongest when it is stated without exaggeration. Early manuscripts, the early standard consonantal text, mass recitation, and regulated canonical reading traditions together provide a very strong transmission picture. Canonical reading variants and early differences in orthography also exist. They belong to the history of Qur’anic transmission and should be discussed openly rather than hidden.[47](/messenger/sources#ref-47){: .footnote}[49](/messenger/sources#ref-49){: .footnote}[50](/messenger/sources#ref-50){: .footnote}[51](/messenger/sources#ref-51){: .footnote}[52](/messenger/sources#ref-52){: .footnote}

    > "Indeed, upon Us is its collection [in your heart] and [to make possible] its recitation." (**Sūrat al-Qiyāmah 75:17**), Saheeh International[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}
    >
    > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿إِنَّ عَلَيْنَا جَمْعَهُۥ وَقُرْءَانَهُۥ ۝١٧﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ القِيَامَةِ</span></div>
    {: .quote }

## What this evidence contributes
{: #what-this-evidence-contributes }

- <span id="src-2644"></span>**What this establishes:** each point above can be given a skeptical explanation in isolation. A human author can write self-criticism, issue a challenge, or claim preservation. The cumulative question is why the text so consistently constrains and corrects the man who would supposedly be using it for his own interests, while keeping its theological focus beyond him across very different stages of his mission.
