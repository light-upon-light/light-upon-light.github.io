A teacher’s public message can be compared with conduct observed at close range. These reports preserve Muhammad ﷺ’s household life, patience under provocation, generosity, courage, grief, loyalty, humor, and attention to people easily overlooked.

## The man who lived the teachings
{: #the-man-who-lived-the-teachings }

- <span id="src-1640"></span>Moral teaching is easier to assess when it can be **compared with ordinary conduct**. Prophet Muhammad ﷺ was observed over many years by his wives, a young household servant, close companions, political opponents, poor petitioners, soldiers, and strangers. Reports of private habits, everyday interactions, and moments of pressure therefore provide an important test of the public teacher.


## What people closest to him actually saw
{: #what-people-closest-to-him-actually-saw }

- <span id="src-1645"></span>Khadija’s first response to his prophetic experience was to appeal to **the character she already knew**. When Prophet Muhammad ﷺ returned terrified from the first revelation, his wife did not reassure him with promises of power or fame. She listed the good she had personally watched him do.

    > "By God, God will never disgrace you. You keep family ties, carry the burden of the weak, help the destitute, honor the guest, and assist those struck by calamity." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 3[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-1650"></span>Anas served Prophet Muhammad ﷺ for ten years from a young age and remembered that he was **never harsh with him** or even asked why he had or had not done a task.

    > "I served the Prophet ﷺ for ten years. He never said 'uff' to me and never said, 'Why did you do that?' or 'Why did you not do that?'" — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 6038[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-1655"></span>Anas also preserved an ordinary example of **that gentleness**. Prophet Muhammad ﷺ sent him on an errand, but the boy became distracted playing. When Prophet Muhammad ﷺ found him, he smiled and gently reminded him of the errand instead of humiliating him.

    > He came up smiling and said, "Little Anas, did you go where I asked you to go?" — *Sahih Muslim* 2310a[3](/messenger/sources#ref-3){: .footnote}[14](/messenger/sources#ref-14){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-1660"></span>Aisha said that he **never struck a woman or a servant**. This is testimony from within his household, not a public claim Prophet Muhammad ﷺ made about himself.

    > "The Messenger of God ﷺ never struck anyone with his hand, neither a woman nor a servant, except in battle." — *Sahih Muslim* 2328a[3](/messenger/sources#ref-3){: .footnote}[14](/messenger/sources#ref-14){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-1665"></span>Aisha also said that he did not take **revenge for personal injuries**. She distinguished wrongs done to him personally from the enforcement of public moral limits.

    > "The Messenger of God ﷺ never took revenge for himself." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 3560[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-1670"></span>When faced with two morally permissible options, he **chose the easier one**. Aisha’s description gives no sense that he considered unnecessary difficulty a virtue in itself.

    > "Whenever the Messenger of God ﷺ was given a choice between two matters, he chose the easier one so long as it was not sinful." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 3560[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-1675"></span>Anas remembered him as both **generous and courageous**, and the record gives concrete examples of both. One appears below: when a frightening sound was heard at night in Medina, Prophet Muhammad ﷺ was the first person to ride toward the danger.

    > "The Prophet ﷺ was the best in character, the most generous of people, and the bravest of people." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 6033[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-1680"></span>The Qur’an itself connects **his gentleness** with people’s willingness to remain around him. It does not praise kindness in the abstract, but explicitly contrasts his manner with the alienating effect of harshness.

    > "It is out of Allah’s mercy that you ˹O Prophet˺ have been lenient with them. Had you been cruel or hard-hearted, they would have certainly abandoned you. So pardon them, ask Allah’s forgiveness for them, and consult with them in ˹conducting˺ matters. Once you make a decision, put your trust in Allah. Surely Allah loves those who trust in Him." (**Sūrat Āl ʿImrān 3:159**), Dr. Mustafa Khattab, *The Clear Quran*[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}
    >
    > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿فَبِمَا رَحْمَةٍ مِّنَ ٱللَّهِ لِنتَ لَهُمْ ۖ وَلَوْ كُنتَ فَظًّا غَلِيظَ ٱلْقَلْبِ لَٱنفَضُّوا۟ مِنْ حَوْلِكَ ۖ فَٱعْفُ عَنْهُمْ وَٱسْتَغْفِرْ لَهُمْ وَشَاوِرْهُمْ فِى ٱلْأَمْرِ ۖ فَإِذَا عَزَمْتَ فَتَوَكَّلْ عَلَى ٱللَّهِ ۚ إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ يُحِبُّ ٱلْمُتَوَكِّلِينَ ۝١٥٩﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ آلِ عِمۡرَانَ</span></div>
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-1687"></span>Aisha summarized his character by saying that **it embodied the Qur’an**. Asked what Prophet Muhammad ﷺ was like, she described a close relationship between the scripture he taught publicly and the conduct she observed privately at home.

    > "His character was the Qur’an." — *Sahih Muslim* 746a[3](/messenger/sources#ref-3){: .footnote}[14](/messenger/sources#ref-14){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

## Mercy when he had reason to be angry
{: #mercy-when-he-had-reason-to-be-angry }

- <span id="src-1695"></span>A desert Arab once yanked Prophet Muhammad’s ﷺ cloak **hard enough to leave a mark**, then demanded money. Prophet Muhammad ﷺ had political authority and companions around him. He responded by turning toward the man, smiling, and ordering that he be given something.

    > "The Prophet ﷺ turned toward him, smiled, and ordered that a gift be given to him." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 3149[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-1700"></span>When a stranger urinated inside the mosque, Prophet Muhammad ﷺ **stopped the crowd from attacking him**. He let the man finish, had the place washed with water, and taught his companions without turning correction into unnecessary humiliation.

    > "Leave him... You were sent to make things easy, not to make them difficult." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 6128[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-1705"></span>A man once stood over Prophet Muhammad ﷺ with Prophet Muhammad’s ﷺ own **unsheathed sword while he slept**. The man asked who would protect him, and Prophet Muhammad ﷺ answered, “God.” After the immediate danger passed, the report records that Prophet Muhammad ﷺ did not punish him.

    > "He said, 'Who will protect you from me?' I said, 'God'... The Prophet ﷺ did not punish him." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 2910[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-1710"></span>When he was personally insulted over a distribution of wealth, he **remembered Moses and chose patience**. He understood the insult within a longer prophetic history of being mistreated rather than treating his own pain as unique.

    > "May God have mercy on Moses. He was hurt with more than this, yet he was patient." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 4335[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-1715"></span>When Tufayl ibn Amr asked Prophet Muhammad ﷺ to pray against the tribe of Daws after they rejected the message, Prophet Muhammad ﷺ instead **prayed for their guidance**.

    > "O God, guide Daws and bring them." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 2937[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-1720"></span>Even when a leading internal opponent died, Prophet Muhammad’s ﷺ **first response was mercy**, until revelation set a limit. Abdullah ibn Ubayy had led serious opposition in Medina. Yet when his son asked Prophet Muhammad ﷺ for a shirt to use as his father’s shroud and for a funeral prayer, Prophet Muhammad ﷺ gave the shirt and prayed. Qur’an 9:84 then prohibited him from doing so in such cases in the future. The episode preserves both his personal inclination toward mercy and his submission when the revelation he claimed to receive overruled it.

    > His son asked for Muhammad’s ﷺ shirt and funeral prayer, "so the Prophet ﷺ gave him his shirt" and went to pray for him. — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 1269[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-1725"></span>Prophet Muhammad ﷺ explicitly said that **cursing people** was not the purpose of his prophetic mission. When companions asked him to curse hostile polytheists, he answered by describing himself as having been sent as a mercy.

    > "I was not sent as one who curses. I was sent only as a mercy." — *Sahih Muslim* 2599[3](/messenger/sources#ref-3){: .footnote}[14](/messenger/sources#ref-14){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-1730"></span>Prophet Muhammad ﷺ refused to kill Medina’s leading internal critic **for political convenience**. After repeated political harm from Abdullah ibn Ubayy, Umar asked permission to kill him. Prophet Muhammad ﷺ refused in part because he considered the wider moral consequence of becoming known as a leader who killed people counted among his own community.

    > "Leave him, lest people say Muhammad kills his companions." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 4905[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-1735"></span>Prophet Muhammad ﷺ did not turn the poisoning at Khaybar into **a personal vendetta**. Prophet Muhammad ﷺ survived poisoned food served to him there and later said, during his final illness, that he could still feel its effects. Reports differ over what ultimately happened to the woman who prepared it after another man died from the same food, but neither account makes punishment a matter of revenge for Prophet Muhammad’s ﷺ own injury.

    > During his final illness he said that he still felt the effect of the food he had eaten at Khaybar. — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 4428[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}; *Sahih al-Bukhari* 5777[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

## Humility and a strikingly unroyal life
{: #humility-and-a-strikingly-unroyal-life }

- <span id="src-1743"></span>Prophet Muhammad ﷺ continued to **help his family at home** while leading a growing community. The detail matters because it concerns repetitive domestic work rather than a ceremonial display of humility.

    > "He used to serve his family." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 676[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-1748"></span>Prophet Muhammad’s ﷺ household sometimes passed long periods **without cooked food**. Aisha remembered seeing three new moons over two months without a cooking fire being lit in Prophet Muhammad’s ﷺ homes. Dates and water formed their basic diet during such periods, supplemented by milk given by neighbors.

    > "We would see three crescents in two months, and no fire would be lit in the houses of the Messenger of God ﷺ." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 6459[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-1753"></span>Prophet Muhammad ﷺ died while **his armor was pledged** to a Jewish merchant in exchange for barley, rather than leaving behind a large private fortune.

    > "The Messenger of God ﷺ died while his iron armor was pledged to a Jewish man for thirty measures of barley." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 2916[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-1758"></span>Prophet Muhammad ﷺ slept on **bedding coarse enough to leave marks** on his body. Umar saw those marks and wept as he compared Prophet Muhammad’s ﷺ circumstances with those of the Byzantine and Persian rulers.

    > Umar said, "Caesar and Khosrau live as they do, while you are the Messenger of God." He replied, "Are you not content that they have this world and we the next?" — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 4913[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-1763"></span>Prophet Muhammad ﷺ avoided **ceremonial displays of self-importance**. He sat among his companions so simply that a stranger could enter and ask which of them was Prophet Muhammad ﷺ. His companions also reported that they did not routinely stand when he arrived because they knew he disliked it.

    > "Which of you is Muhammad?" — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 63; see also *Jami al-Tirmidhi* 2754, graded *hasan* [good][2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[5](/messenger/sources#ref-5){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[16](/messenger/sources#ref-16){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-1768"></span>Companions reported that they could often tell from Prophet Muhammad’s ﷺ face when he disliked something, without him turning that displeasure into **coarse or humiliating speech**.

    > "The Prophet ﷺ was more modest than a virgin in her private room." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 3562, 6102; *Sahih Muslim* 2320[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[3](/messenger/sources#ref-3){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[14](/messenger/sources#ref-14){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

## Generosity and courage when they cost something
{: #generosity-and-courage-when-they-cost-something }

- <span id="src-1776"></span>Prophet Muhammad’s ﷺ generosity became **especially pronounced during Ramadan**, the Muslim fasting month. Ibn Abbas compared the freedom and speed of Prophet Muhammad’s ﷺ giving to a freely blowing wind.

    > "The Messenger of God ﷺ was the most generous of people, and he was most generous in Ramadan." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 6[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-1781"></span>At times he gave on a scale that **astonished new converts**. Anas reported that a man who asked for material help received a flock filling the space between two mountains. The man returned to his people marveling that Prophet Muhammad ﷺ gave as someone unafraid of poverty.

    > "Muhammad gives like a man who does not fear poverty." — *Sahih Muslim* 2312a[3](/messenger/sources#ref-3){: .footnote}[14](/messenger/sources#ref-14){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-1786"></span>Prophet Muhammad ﷺ used wealth to **reconcile hostile people** rather than simply accumulate it. Safwan ibn Umayya later said that Prophet Muhammad ﷺ kept giving to him after the battle of Hunayn while Safwan still hated him, until Prophet Muhammad ﷺ became the person he loved most.

    > "He continued giving to me until he became the dearest of people to me, though before that he had been the most hateful of people to me." — *Sahih Muslim* 2313[3](/messenger/sources#ref-3){: .footnote}[14](/messenger/sources#ref-14){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-1791"></span>Prophet Muhammad’s ﷺ courage involved **personal physical risk**. When a frightening sound alarmed Medina one night, Prophet Muhammad ﷺ had already ridden alone toward it on an unsaddled horse while others were still gathering to investigate.

    > They met him returning, a sword at his neck, saying, "Do not be afraid. Do not be afraid." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 6033[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-1796"></span>At Hunayn, when a sudden volley caused many Muslim fighters to retreat, reports distinguish their retreat from Prophet Muhammad ﷺ himself, who **remained in place on his mule**.

    > Al-Bara said, "The Messenger of God ﷺ did not flee." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 4317[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-1801"></span>Even **aggressive demands** did not necessarily end his generosity. During one journey, Bedouins crowded around him and pulled away his cloak while demanding shares of wealth. He did not retaliate, but answered by describing how freely he would distribute wealth if he possessed it.

    > "If I had camels as numerous as these thorn trees, I would distribute them among you. You would not find me miserly, false, or cowardly." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 2821[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-1806"></span>Prophet Muhammad ﷺ disliked **retaining surplus wealth** for himself even when it could lawfully have remained in his hands. He told Abu Dharr that if he possessed gold equal to Mount Uhud, he would not want to keep it for even a few nights except what was needed to pay a debt.

    > "I would not like to have gold equal to this Mount Uhud unless nothing of it remained with me after three nights, except something I kept for a debt." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 6445[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-1811"></span>Prophet Muhammad ﷺ **shared the physical risks of battle** rather than insulating himself from the danger borne by his followers. At Uhud he was wounded in the face.

    > "On that day, the Prophet’s face was wounded and one of his teeth was broken." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 2911; *Sahih Muslim* 1790[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[3](/messenger/sources#ref-3){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[14](/messenger/sources#ref-14){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

    Another Bukhari account adds that pieces of his helmet were driven into his cheek. The bodily danger of the battlefield reached him as it reached the people he led. — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 4075[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}

- <span id="src-1818"></span>Prophet Muhammad’s ﷺ generosity was remembered as **a settled habit**, not merely a few dramatic gifts. Anas said that when Prophet Muhammad ﷺ was asked for something he could give, he did not ordinarily respond with a flat refusal. Read beside the reports of his sparse household life, the pattern is clear: resources that reached him were unusually easy for him to pass on.

    > "The Prophet ﷺ was never asked for something to be given for which his answer was 'no.'" — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 6034[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

## Tenderness, loyalty, grief, and human warmth
{: #tenderness-loyalty-grief-and-human-warmth }

- <span id="src-1826"></span>Prophet Muhammad ﷺ **wept when his infant son died**. His words distinguish grief from rebellion against God. Prophetic patience did not require pretending that loss did not hurt.

    > "The eyes shed tears and the heart grieves... O Ibrahim, we are truly grieved by your departure." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 1303[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-1831"></span>Prophet Muhammad’s ﷺ loyalty to Khadija continued **long after her death**. He still spoke of her years later and sent food to her friends.

    > "When he slaughtered a sheep, he would send portions to the friends of Khadija." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 6004[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-1836"></span>There was **room for play** in his marriage. Aisha’s memory of racing Prophet Muhammad ﷺ is valuable precisely because nothing grand is happening. It preserves ordinary humor and companionship between them.

    > After each had won one race, he said with humor, "This one is for that one." — *Sunan Abi Dawud* 2578[4](/messenger/sources#ref-4){: .footnote}[15](/messenger/sources#ref-15){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-1841"></span>Prophet Muhammad ﷺ gave his time to a socially vulnerable woman with an intellectual or mental difficulty **until her need was met**. Anas remembers the leader of Medina allowing her to choose where she wanted to speak with him, then remaining with her until she had finished.

    > She said, "Messenger of God, I have a need from you." He told her to choose a side of the road, and he stayed with her until her need was met. — *Sahih Muslim* 2326[3](/messenger/sources#ref-3){: .footnote}[14](/messenger/sources#ref-14){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-1846"></span>**A child’s turn** was not ignored merely because older men were present. After drinking, Prophet Muhammad ﷺ had a boy on his right and elders on his left. Since custom gave the person on the right the next turn, he asked the boy’s permission to serve the elders first. When the boy declined, Prophet Muhammad ﷺ honored his right and handed him the drink.

    > He asked the boy, "Will you permit me to give these elders first?" ... Then the Messenger of God ﷺ placed the cup in the boy’s hand. — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 2602[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-1851"></span>Prophet Muhammad ﷺ **did not insult food** simply because he disliked it. Abu Huraira preserves a small domestic courtesy: Prophet Muhammad ﷺ ate what appealed to him and quietly left what did not.

    > "The Prophet ﷺ never criticized food. If he wanted it, he ate it; otherwise, he left it." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 3563[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-1856"></span>Prophet Muhammad ﷺ **wept out of concern** for the people who would follow his message. In one report, he recited earlier prophets’ prayers for their peoples, then raised his hands and wept for his own community. His concern in the scene is religious rather than political: what would become of them before God.

    > He raised his hands and said, "O Lord, my community, my community," and wept. — *Sahih Muslim* 202[3](/messenger/sources#ref-3){: .footnote}[14](/messenger/sources#ref-14){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-1861"></span>Anas remembered Prophet Muhammad ﷺ noticing his little brother Abu Umayr’s sadness after **the child’s pet bird** died and speaking gently with him about it. The loss may have been small to an adult, but Prophet Muhammad ﷺ treated the child’s grief as worth noticing.

    > "O Abu Umayr, what happened to the little bird?" — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 6129[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-1866"></span>Prophet Muhammad ﷺ **stood to welcome his daughter Fatima**. Aisha reported that when Fatima entered, Prophet Muhammad ﷺ rose, took her hand, kissed her, and seated her where he had been sitting. Fatima did the same when he visited her.

    > "When she entered upon him, he stood for her, took her hand, kissed her, and seated her where he had been sitting." — *Sunan Abi Dawud* 5217[4](/messenger/sources#ref-4){: .footnote}[15](/messenger/sources#ref-15){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-1871"></span>Prophet Muhammad ﷺ noticed when a poor person who served the community was **suddenly absent**. A Black man or woman who cleaned the mosque died and was buried without Prophet Muhammad ﷺ being informed. He later asked after the person, objected that no one had told him, went to the grave, and prayed there.

    > "Why did you not inform me?" Then he went to the grave and prayed over it. — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 458[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-1876"></span>People remembered him for **smiling at them**. Jarir ibn Abdullah said that after he became Muslim, Prophet Muhammad ﷺ never shut him out and smiled whenever they met.

    > "Whenever he saw me, he received me with a smile." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 3822[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-1881"></span>Prophet Muhammad’s ﷺ speech was **neither obscene nor deliberately coarse**. The report connects his restraint in language with the wider standard of character he taught others.

    > "The Prophet ﷺ was neither obscene nor one who deliberately spoke obscenely... The best of you are those best in character." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 6029[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-1886"></span>Prophet Muhammad ﷺ spoke **carefully and clearly** enough to be understood. Aisha remembered his words as distinct, not a rapid stream that left listeners behind.

    > "He did not speak rapidly as you do. He spoke with clear words, separated from one another." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 3568[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

    Anas likewise remembered that Prophet Muhammad ﷺ sometimes repeated an important statement three times so that listeners would understand it. He took responsibility for making himself clear rather than assuming that the audience would keep up. — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 95[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}

- <span id="src-1893"></span>**Harmless recreation** had a place in his religious life. When Aisha wanted to watch an Abyssinian display in the mosque, Prophet Muhammad ﷺ screened her with his cloak and stayed until she had watched for as long as she wished.

    > "The Prophet ﷺ screened me with his cloak while I watched the Abyssinians playing... until I had enough." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 5236[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-1898"></span>Aisha reported giving **three dates to a poor mother**, who gave one to each daughter and divided her own date between them. Prophet Muhammad ﷺ used the incident to teach that caring generously for daughters can protect a person from the Fire.

    > "Whoever is tested with daughters and treats them generously, they will be a shield for him from the Fire." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 5995[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-1903"></span>Prophet Muhammad ﷺ remembered **a socially obscure man** whom others had overlooked even after his death. After a battle, the companions listed those killed but did not name Julaybib. Prophet Muhammad ﷺ noticed that he was missing and made them search for him.

    > "But I am missing Julaybib. Search for him." ... "He is of me and I am of him." — *Sahih Muslim* 2472[3](/messenger/sources#ref-3){: .footnote}[14](/messenger/sources#ref-14){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-1908"></span>Prophet Muhammad’s ﷺ humor **did not depend on falsehood**. Prophet Muhammad ﷺ joked with companions and children, but explicitly distinguished joking from lying.

    > "I do not say anything except the truth." — *Jami al-Tirmidhi* 1990, graded *hasan* [good][5](/messenger/sources#ref-5){: .footnote}[16](/messenger/sources#ref-16){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-1913"></span>When Prophet Muhammad ﷺ wept over another dying child, he described the tears as **mercy rather than weakness**. The scene extends the lesson beyond grief for his own son: tenderness in the face of another person’s suffering was itself something he regarded as valuable before God.

    > "This is mercy God has placed in the hearts of His servants, and God shows mercy to the merciful." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 7377[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-1918"></span>At a graveyard, Prophet Muhammad ﷺ told his companions that he longed to see believers who would come after them, whom he called his “brothers.”

    > "I would love to see my brothers... They are those who have not yet come." — *Sahih Muslim* 249a[3](/messenger/sources#ref-3){: .footnote}[14](/messenger/sources#ref-14){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }
