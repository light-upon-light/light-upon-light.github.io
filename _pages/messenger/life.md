The chronology places the evidence in changing circumstances: ordinary adult life, persecution, migration, community leadership, victory, and death. These episodes show where the mission’s teachings and claims were tested, while distinguishing hadith from early biography.

## A life tested by powerlessness and power
{: #a-life-tested-by-powerlessness-and-power }

- <span id="src-1927"></span>Prophet Muhammad ﷺ lived long enough after claiming prophethood for his character to be tested under **sharply different conditions**. For years he could not protect even his own followers; later he became the leader of a state and returned in victory to the city that had expelled him. That change in circumstance makes the chronology morally significant, not merely biographical.[8](/messenger/sources#ref-8){: .footnote}[28](/messenger/sources#ref-28){: .footnote}[37](/messenger/sources#ref-37){: .footnote}[38](/messenger/sources#ref-38){: .footnote}[41](/messenger/sources#ref-41){: .footnote}[42](/messenger/sources#ref-42){: .footnote}


## Before revelation: an orphan, worker, husband, and trusted arbitrator
{: #before-revelation-an-orphan-worker-husband-and-trusted-arbitrator }

- <span id="src-1932"></span>Prophet Muhammad ﷺ was born **without a living father**, and the Qur’an turns his own experience of vulnerability into a duty toward others. According to the early biographical tradition, his father died before he was born and his mother died while he was still a child. The same Qur’anic chapter that reminds him of being an orphan tells him not to mistreat the orphan or turn away the person who asks for help.

    > "Did He not find you an orphan and give [you] refuge?" (**Sūrat ad-Duḥā 93:6**), Saheeh International[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}
    >
    > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿أَلَمْ يَجِدْكَ يَتِيمًا فَـَٔاوَىٰ ۝٦﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ الضُّحَىٰ</span></div>
    >
    > "So as for the orphan, do not oppress [him]. And as for the petitioner, do not repel [him]." (**Sūrat ad-Duḥā 93:9-10**), Saheeh International[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}
    >
    > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿فَأَمَّا ٱلْيَتِيمَ فَلَا تَقْهَرْ ۝٩ وَأَمَّا ٱلسَّآئِلَ فَلَا تَنْهَرْ ۝١٠﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ الضُّحَىٰ</span></div>
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-1943"></span>Before leading a community, Prophet Muhammad ﷺ worked for a living: reports say that he **tended sheep** for people in Mecca and later worked in trade. When he eventually taught the dignity of lawful earning, he spoke as someone who had himself worked rather than only commanded others.

    > Asked whether he had tended sheep, he said, "Yes. I used to tend the sheep of the people of Mecca for small coins." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 2262[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-1948"></span>The early biography remembers him as **a trusted man** before he claimed revelation. During the rebuilding of the Kaaba, Mecca’s central sanctuary, the clans nearly fought over who would have the honor of replacing the Black Stone. Prophet Muhammad ﷺ proposed placing it on a cloth so representatives of the clans could lift it together, after which he set it in place himself.

    > The clans agreed that the next man to enter should arbitrate; when Muhammad came, they accepted him, and his solution shared the honor among them. — *Sīra*: Ibn Ishaq, trans. A. Guillaume, pp. 84–86[8](/messenger/sources#ref-8){: .footnote}[19](/messenger/sources#ref-19){: .footnote}[28](/messenger/sources#ref-28){: .footnote}[37](/messenger/sources#ref-37){: .footnote}[38](/messenger/sources#ref-38){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-1953"></span>At about forty, his prophetic life **began in seclusion** rather than from a position of power. Bukhari’s account of the first revelation says that before the angelic encounter, Prophet Muhammad ﷺ had begun withdrawing to the cave of Hira for periods of worship.

    > "The love of seclusion was given to him. He would go to the cave of Hira and devote himself to worship there for a number of nights." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 3[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-1958"></span>Prophet Muhammad’s ﷺ immediate response to the first revelation **was fear, not triumph**. He returned to Khadija with his heart trembling and asked to be covered. Whatever interpretation one gives the event, the canonical account does not read like a calculated announcement that an ambitious plan had begun.

    > He returned to Khadija "with his heart trembling" and said, "Cover me, cover me." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 3[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-1963"></span>Khadija based her reassurance on **the character she had already observed**. She cited Prophet Muhammad’s ﷺ loyalty to relatives, care for the weak, generosity, hospitality, and help in times of calamity, then took him to Waraqa ibn Nawfal, a learned monotheist familiar with earlier scripture.

    > "God will never disgrace you. You keep family ties, support the weak, help the destitute, honor guests, and assist those afflicted." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 3[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-1968"></span>Waraqa’s response promised **conflict rather than easy prestige**. He told Prophet Muhammad ﷺ that the bearer of revelation who had come to Moses had now come to him, and that his own people would drive him out. Prophet Muhammad ﷺ was surprised at the thought of being expelled.

    > Muhammad ﷺ asked, "Will they drive me out?" Waraqa replied that no one had brought a message like this without being opposed. — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 3[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-1973"></span>Before prophethood, he participated in a Meccan alliance formed to **restore the rights** of people who had been wronged. Early biography therefore places his concern for justice before he possessed political power. The pact committed its members to stand with victims until their rights were restored.

    > "They pledged that they would stand with any wronged person in Mecca until his right was restored." — *Sīra*: Ibn Hisham, *al-Sīrah al-Nabawiyyah* 1/133[8](/messenger/sources#ref-8){: .footnote}[19](/messenger/sources#ref-19){: .footnote}[20](/messenger/sources#ref-20){: .footnote}[28](/messenger/sources#ref-28){: .footnote}[29](/messenger/sources#ref-29){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-1978"></span>Khadija was Prophet Muhammad ﷺ’s **only wife** throughout their long marriage. Early biography describes her as a Meccan merchant who employed him in trade and later proposed marriage after observing his conduct in business. In the traditional chronology, he was about twenty-five and she was older, and they remained married for roughly twenty-five years. She knew him before prophethood, supported him at the beginning of revelation, and endured the Meccan years beside him. Although plural marriage was socially available, he did not marry another woman while she lived.

    > "The Messenger of God ﷺ did not marry another woman until Khadija died." — *Sahih Muslim* 2436[3](/messenger/sources#ref-3){: .footnote}[14](/messenger/sources#ref-14){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}; *Sīra*: Ibn Ishaq/Ibn Hisham, for the marriage narrative[8](/messenger/sources#ref-8){: .footnote}[19](/messenger/sources#ref-19){: .footnote}[28](/messenger/sources#ref-28){: .footnote}[37](/messenger/sources#ref-37){: .footnote}[38](/messenger/sources#ref-38){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-1983"></span>The name *Muhammad* means “the one repeatedly praised” or “much praised.” He bore the name before making any prophetic claim, spent long periods of his mission being mocked and reviled, and later became someone named in the daily prayers of Muslims throughout the world. This does not prove prophethood by itself, but the correspondence is worth noticing.[77](/messenger/sources#ref-77){: .footnote}[28](/messenger/sources#ref-28){: .footnote}[37](/messenger/sources#ref-37){: .footnote}


- <span id="src-1985"></span>The sources do not give him **a forty-year career** as a poet, priest, scripture scholar, or religious orator preparing for this role. Arabia highly valued poetry, yet Prophet Muhammad’s ﷺ prophetic claim appears after a long ordinary adult life rather than at the end of an established literary or priestly career. The Qur’an itself appeals to that discontinuity before the audience who had known his earlier life.

    > "You did not know what is the Book or [what is] faith, but We have made it a light by which We guide whom We will of Our servants." (**Sūrat ash-Shūrā 42:52**), Saheeh International[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}; compare **Sūrat al-‘Ankabūt 29:48**[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}
    >
    > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿وَكَذَٰلِكَ أَوْحَيْنَآ إِلَيْكَ رُوحًا مِّنْ أَمْرِنَا ۚ مَا كُنتَ تَدْرِى مَا ٱلْكِتَـٰبُ وَلَا ٱلْإِيمَـٰنُ وَلَـٰكِن جَعَلْنَـٰهُ نُورًا نَّهْدِى بِهِۦ مَن نَّشَآءُ مِنْ عِبَادِنَا ۚ وَإِنَّكَ لَتَهْدِىٓ إِلَىٰ صِرَٰطٍ مُّسْتَقِيمٍ ۝٥٢﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ الشُّورَىٰ</span></div>
    {: .quote }

## Mecca: choosing the message at great personal cost
{: #mecca-choosing-the-message-at-great-personal-cost }

- <span id="src-1995"></span>Prophet Muhammad’s ﷺ early public message **challenged rather than flattered** the foundations of Meccan status. The Qur’an called people away from inherited idols to the worship of one God, while condemning neglect of the poor, cheating, arrogance, and exploitation of the vulnerable.

    > "Say, 'He is Allah, [who is] One,'" (**Sūrat al-Ikhlāṣ 112:1**), Saheeh International[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}
    >
    > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿قُلْ هُوَ ٱللَّهُ أَحَدٌ ۝١﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ الإِخۡلَاصِ</span></div>
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-2002"></span>Prophet Muhammad ﷺ was commanded to **begin with his own relatives** rather than exempt his clan from the message’s moral demands.

    > "And warn, [O Muhammad], your closest kindred." (**Sūrat ash-Shu'arā' 26:214**), Saheeh International[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}
    >
    > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿وَأَنذِرْ عَشِيرَتَكَ ٱلْأَقْرَبِينَ ۝٢١٤﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ الشُّعَرَاءِ</span></div>
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-2009"></span>Prophet Muhammad’s ﷺ followers endured persecution while they had **no political power**. Khabbab ibn al-Aratt later remembered complaining to Prophet Muhammad ﷺ while he sat near the Kaaba. Prophet Muhammad’s ﷺ answer was not a promise of immediate comfort, but a call to endure together with confidence that their circumstances would eventually change.

    > "By God, God will complete this matter until a traveler goes safely... but you are being hasty." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 3612[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-2014"></span>Prophet Muhammad ﷺ **did not soften the central claim** of monotheism in order to reach a compromise with polytheism. A short Meccan chapter draws a clear boundary between his worship and that of his opponents.

    > "For you is your religion, and for me is my religion." (**Sūrat al-Kāfirūn 109:6**), Saheeh International[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}
    >
    > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿لَكُمْ دِينُكُمْ وَلِىَ دِينِ ۝٦﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ الكَافِرُونَ</span></div>
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-2021"></span>The Qur’an records pressure on him to compromise and warns him **not to yield**. Whatever view one takes of revelation, the text restricts the messenger rather than giving him pragmatic freedom to reshape the message for political survival.

    > "They wish that you would soften [in your position], so they would soften [toward you]." (**Sūrat al-Qalam 68:9**), Saheeh International[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}
    >
    > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿وَدُّوا۟ لَوْ تُدْهِنُ فَيُدْهِنُونَ ۝٩﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ القَلَمِ</span></div>
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-2028"></span>The deaths of Khadija and Abu Talib removed **his strongest personal and political supports**. Early biographies place both losses late in the Meccan period. Khadija had been his closest emotional supporter, while Abu Talib had provided crucial clan protection. Prophet Muhammad ﷺ continued the mission after losing both.

    > "God will never disgrace you," Khadija had told him at the beginning of the mission. — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 3[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}; *Sīra*: Ibn Ishaq, for the chronology of the Meccan losses[8](/messenger/sources#ref-8){: .footnote}[19](/messenger/sources#ref-19){: .footnote}[28](/messenger/sources#ref-28){: .footnote}[37](/messenger/sources#ref-37){: .footnote}[38](/messenger/sources#ref-38){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

    The bereavements continued beyond that year. Prophet Muhammad ﷺ outlived all of his children except Fatima, and she survived him by only about six months. His prophetic life was never insulated from ordinary human grief.

- <span id="src-2035"></span>At al-Ta’if, severe rejection did not lead him to choose **indiscriminate vengeance**. Bukhari’s account does not contain every dramatic detail found in popular retellings, but it clearly records Prophet Muhammad’s ﷺ deep sorrow and his refusal of the angel’s offer to crush the people who had rejected him.

    > "Rather, I hope that God will bring from their descendants people who worship God alone." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 3231[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-2040"></span>When he finally left Mecca, he did so as **a hunted migrant** rather than a conqueror. The Qur’an recalls Prophet Muhammad ﷺ and Abu Bakr hiding in a cave while pursuers searched for them.

    > "˹It does not matter˺ if you ˹believers˺ do not support him, for Allah did in fact support him when the disbelievers drove him out ˹of Mecca˺ and he was only one of two. While they both were in the cave, he reassured his companion, 'Do not worry; Allah is certainly with us.' So Allah sent down His serenity upon the Prophet, supported him with forces you ˹believers˺ did not see, and made the word of the disbelievers lowest, while the Word of Allah is supreme. And Allah is Almighty, All-Wise." (**Sūrat at-Tawbah 9:40**), Dr. Mustafa Khattab, *The Clear Quran*[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}
    >
    > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿إِلَّا تَنصُرُوهُ فَقَدْ نَصَرَهُ ٱللَّهُ إِذْ أَخْرَجَهُ ٱلَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا۟ ثَانِىَ ٱثْنَيْنِ إِذْ هُمَا فِى ٱلْغَارِ إِذْ يَقُولُ لِصَـٰحِبِهِۦ لَا تَحْزَنْ إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ مَعَنَا ۖ فَأَنزَلَ ٱللَّهُ سَكِينَتَهُۥ عَلَيْهِ وَأَيَّدَهُۥ بِجُنُودٍ لَّمْ تَرَوْهَا وَجَعَلَ كَلِمَةَ ٱلَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا۟ ٱلسُّفْلَىٰ ۗ وَكَلِمَةُ ٱللَّهِ هِىَ ٱلْعُلْيَا ۗ وَٱللَّهُ عَزِيزٌ حَكِيمٌ ۝٤٠﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ التَّوۡبَةِ</span></div>
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-2047"></span>Even one of the men pursuing him could receive **a guarantee of safety**. Suraqa pursued Prophet Muhammad ﷺ and Abu Bakr during the migration. After failing to capture them, he asked for security. The hadith account says that Prophet Muhammad ﷺ granted it and asked him not to reveal their route.

    > Suraqa asked for a written guarantee of security, and the Prophet ﷺ ordered that one be written for him. — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 3906[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-2052"></span>During the years of Muslim weakness, the message emphasized **patient restraint** rather than private revenge. Mockery and injury were not treated as permission for individuals to begin cycles of retaliation.

    > "And be patient over what they say and avoid them with gracious avoidance." (**Sūrat al-Muzzammil 73:10**), Saheeh International[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}
    >
    > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿وَٱصْبِرْ عَلَىٰ مَا يَقُولُونَ وَٱهْجُرْهُمْ هَجْرًا جَمِيلًا ۝١٠﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ المُزَّمِّلِ</span></div>
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-2059"></span>The Qur’an forbade Prophet Muhammad ﷺ from pushing away **sincere low-status followers** in order to attract wealthy or influential people. Many early believers were poor, enslaved, young, or socially unprotected. The Qur’an specifically protected such worshippers from being discarded for reasons of status.

    > "And do not send away those who call upon their Lord morning and afternoon, seeking His countenance." (**Sūrat al-An'ām 6:52**), Saheeh International[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}
    >
    > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿وَلَا تَطْرُدِ ٱلَّذِينَ يَدْعُونَ رَبَّهُم بِٱلْغَدَوٰةِ وَٱلْعَشِىِّ يُرِيدُونَ وَجْهَهُۥ ۖ مَا عَلَيْكَ مِنْ حِسَابِهِم مِّن شَىْءٍ وَمَا مِنْ حِسَابِكَ عَلَيْهِم مِّن شَىْءٍ فَتَطْرُدَهُمْ فَتَكُونَ مِنَ ٱلظَّـٰلِمِينَ ۝٥٢﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ الأَنۡعَامِ</span></div>
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-2066"></span>Prophet Muhammad ﷺ sent persecuted Muslims to seek protection under **a Christian ruler known for justice**. Before the Muslims had a state of their own, Prophet Muhammad ﷺ recognized that trustworthy protection could come from across a religious boundary.

    > "There is a king there under whom no one is wronged." — *Sīra*: Ibn Ishaq, preserved by Ibn Hisham, report of the first migration to Abyssinia[8](/messenger/sources#ref-8){: .footnote}[19](/messenger/sources#ref-19){: .footnote}[28](/messenger/sources#ref-28){: .footnote}[37](/messenger/sources#ref-37){: .footnote}[38](/messenger/sources#ref-38){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-2071"></span>Prophet Muhammad ﷺ endured **a clan boycott** without abandoning or diluting the message. The resulting social and economic isolation made his claim more costly rather than turning it into a route to comfort or prestige.

    > "Do the people think that they will be left to say, 'We believe' and they will not be tried?" (**Sūrat al-'Ankabūt 29:2**), Saheeh International[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}
    >
    > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿أَحَسِبَ ٱلنَّاسُ أَن يُتْرَكُوٓا۟ أَن يَقُولُوٓا۟ ءَامَنَّا وَهُمْ لَا يُفْتَنُونَ ۝٢﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ العَنكَبُوتِ</span></div>
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-2078"></span>The migration was carefully prepared with routes, provisions, information, concealment, and the help of **a skilled non-Muslim guide**. Reliance on God did not mean refusing ordinary practical means, and professional trustworthiness could be recognized across a religious boundary.

    > "The Prophet ﷺ and Abu Bakr hired a skilled guide... who followed the religion of Quraysh." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 2264; see also Qur'an 9:40[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-2083"></span>Early biography reports that Meccan leaders offered him the usual rewards that could **tempt an ambitious claimant** to stop preaching. They offered wealth, leadership or kingship, desirable marriage, and treatment if he believed himself afflicted. The same biographical tradition says that he refused. Because the details come from *sira* [early biography] rather than Bukhari or Muslim, they should be weighed at that level of evidence. As preserved, however, the episode makes the cost-benefit pattern of his Meccan mission unusually clear.

    > The early sira preserves negotiations in which Quraysh offered wealth and leadership if Muhammad ﷺ would abandon the message. — *Sīra*: Ibn Ishaq, as preserved by Ibn Hisham[8](/messenger/sources#ref-8){: .footnote}[19](/messenger/sources#ref-19){: .footnote}[28](/messenger/sources#ref-28){: .footnote}[37](/messenger/sources#ref-37){: .footnote}[38](/messenger/sources#ref-38){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-2088"></span>The persecution was **physical as well as social**. Bukhari records enemies placing the remains of a slaughtered camel on Prophet Muhammad ﷺ while he prostrated at the Kaaba, leaving his daughter to remove them. Another report describes a man tightening a cloth around his neck while he prayed until Abu Bakr intervened. These were public assaults and humiliations at a time when Prophet Muhammad ﷺ had no state through which to punish his attackers.

    > Reported in *Sahih al-Bukhari* 240[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote} and *Sahih al-Bukhari* 3856[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}.
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-2093"></span>Early biography reports that even while some Meccans were plotting to kill Prophet Muhammad ﷺ, they still had **property deposited with him for safekeeping**. Before leaving Mecca, he reportedly arranged for Ali to return those trusts. If the report is accepted, hostility to his religious claim had not erased confidence in his ordinary honesty.

    > Reported in the *Sīra* of Ibn Ishaq/Ibn Hisham, in the account of the migration[8](/messenger/sources#ref-8){: .footnote}[19](/messenger/sources#ref-19){: .footnote}[28](/messenger/sources#ref-28){: .footnote}[37](/messenger/sources#ref-37){: .footnote}[38](/messenger/sources#ref-38){: .footnote}.
    {: .quote }

## Medina: community, conflict, restraint, and the test of power
{: #medina-community-conflict-restraint-and-the-test-of-power }

- <span id="src-2101"></span>**The migration in 622** radically changed Prophet Muhammad’s ﷺ circumstances. In Medina he was no longer only a persecuted preacher, but the leader of an emerging multi-clan community. His conduct under authority can therefore be compared with the moral claims he had made while weak.

    > "And [also for] those who were settled in al-Madinah and [adopted] the faith before them. They love those who emigrated to them and find not any want in their breasts of what the emigrants were given but give [them] preference over themselves, even though they are in privation." (**Sūrat al-Ḥashr 59:9**), Saheeh International[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}
    >
    > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿وَٱلَّذِينَ تَبَوَّءُو ٱلدَّارَ وَٱلْإِيمَـٰنَ مِن قَبْلِهِمْ يُحِبُّونَ مَنْ هَاجَرَ إِلَيْهِمْ وَلَا يَجِدُونَ فِى صُدُورِهِمْ حَاجَةً مِّمَّآ أُوتُوا۟ وَيُؤْثِرُونَ عَلَىٰٓ أَنفُسِهِمْ وَلَوْ كَانَ بِهِمْ خَصَاصَةٌ ۚ وَمَن يُوقَ شُحَّ نَفْسِهِۦ فَأُو۟لَـٰٓئِكَ هُمُ ٱلْمُفْلِحُونَ ۝٩﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ الحَشۡرِ</span></div>
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-2108"></span>The early Medinan charter formed a political compact that **included Jewish groups** without erasing their religion. The document is preserved in early biography and other historical works, so its clauses are studied as historical material rather than as a canonical hadith.

    > "The Jews of Banu Awf are one community with the believers; the Jews have their religion and the Muslims have theirs." — *Sīra*: Ibn Ishaq, trans. A. Guillaume, pp. 231–233[8](/messenger/sources#ref-8){: .footnote}[19](/messenger/sources#ref-19){: .footnote}[28](/messenger/sources#ref-28){: .footnote}[30](/messenger/sources#ref-30){: .footnote}[37](/messenger/sources#ref-37){: .footnote}[38](/messenger/sources#ref-38){: .footnote}[53](/messenger/sources#ref-53){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-2113"></span>**The first major battle, Badr**, came only after years of expulsion and conflict. The reports portray Prophet Muhammad ﷺ praying intensely rather than behaving as though victory for his followers were automatic.

    > "O God, I ask You to fulfill Your promise and covenant." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 3953[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-2118"></span>After the painful defeat at Uhud, he was told to **remain gentle, pardon his followers**, and continue consulting them. Their military error had serious consequences, but those consequences did not become a reason to abandon consultation.

    > "So by mercy from Allah, [O Muhammad], you were lenient with them. And if you had been rude [in speech] and harsh in heart, they would have disbanded from about you. So pardon them and ask forgiveness for them and consult them in the matter. And when you have decided, then rely upon Allah. Indeed, Allah loves those who rely [upon Him]." (**Sūrat Āl 'Imrān 3:159**), Saheeh International[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}
    >
    > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿فَبِمَا رَحْمَةٍ مِّنَ ٱللَّهِ لِنتَ لَهُمْ ۖ وَلَوْ كُنتَ فَظًّا غَلِيظَ ٱلْقَلْبِ لَٱنفَضُّوا۟ مِنْ حَوْلِكَ ۖ فَٱعْفُ عَنْهُمْ وَٱسْتَغْفِرْ لَهُمْ وَشَاوِرْهُمْ فِى ٱلْأَمْرِ ۖ فَإِذَا عَزَمْتَ فَتَوَكَّلْ عَلَى ٱللَّهِ ۚ إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ يُحِبُّ ٱلْمُتَوَكِّلِينَ ۝١٥٩﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ آلِ عِمۡرَانَ</span></div>
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-2125"></span>During the Battle of the Trench, he **shared the community’s hunger**. Jabir noticed Prophet Muhammad’s ﷺ severe hunger and prepared a small amount of food for him. The leader of Medina was enduring the siege with the community, not from the security of a stocked palace.

    > Jabir reported seeing the Prophet ﷺ suffering from "severe hunger." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 4101[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-2130"></span>At Hudaybiyyah in 628, Prophet Muhammad ﷺ approached Mecca **intending pilgrimage rather than battle**. When Quraysh prevented entry, he repeatedly pursued a negotiated truce.

    > "We have not come to fight anyone. We have come to perform the pilgrimage." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 2731–2732[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-2135"></span>Prophet Muhammad ﷺ accepted treaty wording that **omitted his title** “Messenger of God.” The Meccan negotiator refused to recognize the title. Prophet Muhammad ﷺ maintained his claim to be God’s Messenger but accepted “Muhammad son of Abdullah” in the written agreement in order to secure peace.

    > "By God, I am the Messenger of God even if you do not believe me. Write: Muhammad son of Abdullah." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 2731–2732[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-2140"></span>Prophet Muhammad ﷺ accepted treaty terms so painful that **his own companions struggled** to bear them. One clause required returning certain Meccan escapees. When the persecuted Abu Jandal arrived in chains during the negotiations, Prophet Muhammad ﷺ honored the concluded commitment rather than using the outrage of the moment to abandon the agreement.

    > When Quraysh later demanded compliance, the report says: "Abide by the promise you gave us," and Muhammad ﷺ did so. — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 2731–2732[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-2145"></span>In the tense aftermath of Hudaybiyyah, he followed the practical advice of **his wife Umm Salama**. When discouraged companions did not immediately respond to his instruction to end the pilgrimage state, she advised him to begin the rites himself without further discussion. Prophet Muhammad ﷺ did so, and the companions followed his example.

    > Umm Salama advised him to go out, sacrifice, and shave without further argument; "the Prophet ﷺ went out" and did so. — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 2731–2732[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-2150"></span>The Qur’an called this apparently humiliating truce a “**clear victory**.” The opportunities created by the peace help explain why early Muslims later regarded Hudaybiyyah as a decisive turning point rather than a defeat.

    > "Indeed, We have given you, [O Muhammad], a clear conquest." (**Sūrat al-Fatḥ 48:1**), Saheeh International[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}
    >
    > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿إِنَّا فَتَحْنَا لَكَ فَتْحًا مُّبِينًا ۝١﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ الفَتۡحِ</span></div>
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-2157"></span>**Economic cooperation with Jewish cultivators** continued after the conflict at Khaybar. The hadith says that Prophet Muhammad ﷺ allowed the Jewish inhabitants to cultivate the land in return for a share of its produce.

    > "The Prophet ﷺ gave Khaybar to the Jews to work and cultivate it, and they would receive half of its produce." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 4248[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-2162"></span>When Mecca came under his control in 630, he **restrained bloodshed after victory** in the sanctuary. A commander reportedly spoke of a day of great fighting. Prophet Muhammad ﷺ rejected that description, and the next day publicly reaffirmed Mecca’s sacred protection from bloodshed.

    > "It is not lawful for one who believes in God and the Last Day to shed blood in it." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 4295[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-2167"></span>Early biography records **broad clemency** toward the defeated Quraysh. The familiar wording comes from biography rather than Bukhari or Muslim, so that difference in source strength should be kept clear. The account portrays Prophet Muhammad ﷺ asking his former persecutors what they expected from him and then releasing them.

    > "Go, for you are free." — *Sīra*: Ibn Hisham's recension of Ibn Ishaq, trans. A. Guillaume, p. 553[8](/messenger/sources#ref-8){: .footnote}[19](/messenger/sources#ref-19){: .footnote}[28](/messenger/sources#ref-28){: .footnote}[37](/messenger/sources#ref-37){: .footnote}[38](/messenger/sources#ref-38){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-2172"></span>Prophet Muhammad ﷺ **worked beside the community** during demanding collective labor. At the construction of the mosque in Medina, Prophet Muhammad ﷺ carried building materials with the others. Reports of the Trench likewise remember him digging rather than supervising hardship entirely from a distance.

    > "The Prophet ﷺ himself began carrying the unbaked bricks for its building." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 3906[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}; *Sahih al-Bukhari* 6414[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-2177"></span>Prophet Muhammad ﷺ **released a group of armed men** captured after attempting to attack him near Mecca. Sahih Muslim reports that eighty men descended on Prophet Muhammad ﷺ and his companions, were captured, and were then set free rather than executed in retaliation.

    > "Eighty men from the people of Mecca came down upon the Messenger of God armed... He captured them and spared them." — *Sahih Muslim* 1808[3](/messenger/sources#ref-3){: .footnote}[14](/messenger/sources#ref-14){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-2182"></span>When he entered Mecca with overwhelming force, civilians were given **simple ways to remain safe**. Staying inside one’s own home was enough. The city was not treated as though every household were an enemy position.

    > "Whoever enters Abu Sufyan's house is safe; whoever lays down his arms is safe; whoever closes his door is safe." — *Sahih Muslim* 1780c[3](/messenger/sources#ref-3){: .footnote}[14](/messenger/sources#ref-14){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-2187"></span>After Hunayn, he supported **returning captives** to the defeated Hawazin when a delegation came seeking their families. Prophet Muhammad ﷺ had delayed the final disposition long enough for them to arrive, offered a choice concerning what had been captured, and urged the Muslim army to return the people.

    > "Your brothers have come to you repentant, and I consider that their captives should be returned to them." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 4318–4319[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-2192"></span>When victory came, the Qur’an instructed Prophet Muhammad ﷺ to **praise God and seek forgiveness** rather than use success for self-glorification.

    > "When the victory of Allah has come and the conquest, and you see the people entering into the religion of Allah in multitudes, then exalt [Him] with praise of your Lord and ask forgiveness of Him. Indeed, He is ever Accepting of repentance." (**Sūrat an-Naṣr 110:1-3**), Saheeh International[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}
    >
    > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿إِذَا جَآءَ نَصْرُ ٱللَّهِ وَٱلْفَتْحُ ۝١ وَرَأَيْتَ ٱلنَّاسَ يَدْخُلُونَ فِى دِينِ ٱللَّهِ أَفْوَاجًا ۝٢ فَسَبِّحْ بِحَمْدِ رَبِّكَ وَٱسْتَغْفِرْهُ ۚ إِنَّهُۥ كَانَ تَوَّابًۢا ۝٣﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ النَّصۡرِ</span></div>
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-2199"></span>Early biography reports that **a Christian delegation from Najran** was received in Medina for sustained religious discussion, hosted in the mosque, and allowed to perform its own prayer there. Because the account belongs to *sira* [early biography], not Bukhari or Muslim, the source distinction matters. Its picture is nonetheless consistent with the wider record of treaty and interfaith dealings.

    > Reported in the *Sīra* of Ibn Ishaq/Ibn Hisham, and also by Ibn Sa'd, *al-Ṭabaqāt*[8](/messenger/sources#ref-8){: .footnote}[9](/messenger/sources#ref-9){: .footnote}[19](/messenger/sources#ref-19){: .footnote}[20](/messenger/sources#ref-20){: .footnote}[28](/messenger/sources#ref-28){: .footnote}[29](/messenger/sources#ref-29){: .footnote}[37](/messenger/sources#ref-37){: .footnote}[38](/messenger/sources#ref-38){: .footnote}.
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-2204"></span>Prophet Muhammad’s ﷺ claim was carried **beyond Arabia’s tribal audience**. Reports preserve letters to neighboring rulers, including the Byzantine emperor Heraclius, inviting them to the same worship of one God. Bukhari preserves the Heraclius letter within Abu Sufyan’s hostile-witness account, while early historical sources also preserve letters associated with the Persian ruler, the Negus of Abyssinia, and the ruler of Egypt.

    > "I invite you with the invitation of Islam..." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 7[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}; *Sīra*: early biography, for the wider correspondence[8](/messenger/sources#ref-8){: .footnote}[9](/messenger/sources#ref-9){: .footnote}[30](/messenger/sources#ref-30){: .footnote}[37](/messenger/sources#ref-37){: .footnote}[38](/messenger/sources#ref-38){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

## The final pilgrimage and death
{: #the-final-pilgrimage-and-death }

- <span id="src-2212"></span>In his final pilgrimage, he reaffirmed **the sanctity of human life** and property. The teaching was delivered to a large gathered community near the end of his life.

    > "Your blood and your property are sacred to one another, like the sacredness of this day, in this month, in this town." — *Sahih Muslim* 1218a[3](/messenger/sources#ref-3){: .footnote}[14](/messenger/sources#ref-14){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-2217"></span>When abolishing pre-Islamic usury claims, he began with a financial claim belonging to **his own family**. Reform did not begin by protecting his relatives from its cost.

    > "The usury of the pre-Islamic period is abolished, and the first usury I abolish is that of Abbas ibn Abd al-Muttalib." — *Sahih Muslim* 1218a[3](/messenger/sources#ref-3){: .footnote}[14](/messenger/sources#ref-14){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-2222"></span>Prophet Muhammad ﷺ likewise abolished **inherited cycles of blood revenge** by beginning with a claim connected to his own clan.

    > "The blood claims of the pre-Islamic period are abolished, and the first blood claim I abolish is one of ours." — *Sahih Muslim* 1218a[3](/messenger/sources#ref-3){: .footnote}[14](/messenger/sources#ref-14){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-2227"></span>The final pilgrimage included a direct reminder to men about **women’s rights to proper provision** and treatment.

    > "Be mindful of God concerning women... Their right over you is that you provide for them and clothe them in a fitting manner." — *Sahih Muslim* 1218a[3](/messenger/sources#ref-3){: .footnote}[14](/messenger/sources#ref-14){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-2232"></span>Prophet Muhammad ﷺ presented his task as **delivering a message**, not securing his own personal immortality. In the same sermon, he asked the people what testimony they would give about whether he had conveyed it.

    > They replied, "We bear witness that you conveyed, fulfilled, and gave sincere counsel." He said, "O God, bear witness." — *Sahih Muslim* 1218a[3](/messenger/sources#ref-3){: .footnote}[14](/messenger/sources#ref-14){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-2237"></span>Near his death, he privately told Fatima that he believed **his end was close**. The report’s significance as evidence for prophethood is examined in the [predictions collection](/messenger/predictions#src-2727). On a human level, it also preserves a father making his daughter weep with one confidence and then comforting her with another until she laughed.

    > "I think my death has drawn near, and you will be the first of my family to follow me." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 3623–3624[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-2242"></span>According to Aisha, his last words were **directed toward God**, not toward office, money, or succession.

    > "O God, with the Highest Companion." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 4463[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-2247"></span>Prophet Muhammad’s ﷺ followers were not allowed to turn his death into **a denial of his humanity**. The Qur’an had already warned the community not to make faith depend on Prophet Muhammad’s ﷺ continued earthly life.

    > "Muhammad is not but a messenger. [Other] messengers have passed on before him. So if he was to die or be killed, would you turn back on your heels [to unbelief]? And he who turns back on his heels will never harm Allah at all; but Allah will reward the grateful." (**Sūrat Āl 'Imrān 3:144**), Saheeh International[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}
    >
    > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿وَمَا مُحَمَّدٌ إِلَّا رَسُولٌ قَدْ خَلَتْ مِن قَبْلِهِ ٱلرُّسُلُ ۚ أَفَإِي۟ن مَّاتَ أَوْ قُتِلَ ٱنقَلَبْتُمْ عَلَىٰٓ أَعْقَـٰبِكُمْ ۚ وَمَن يَنقَلِبْ عَلَىٰ عَقِبَيْهِ فَلَن يَضُرَّ ٱللَّهَ شَيْـًٔا ۗ وَسَيَجْزِى ٱللَّهُ ٱلشَّـٰكِرِينَ ۝١٤٤﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ آلِ عِمۡرَانَ</span></div>
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-2254"></span>Prophet Muhammad ﷺ left **no royal treasury** to his descendants. His armor remained pledged for food, and his stated rule was that what prophets leave behind becomes charity rather than a dynastic inheritance.

    > "We prophets are not inherited from. What we leave is charity." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 4240–4241[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-2259"></span>Prophet Muhammad’s ﷺ final public teaching included a warning against his followers **shedding one another’s blood** after his death. Loyalty to his message was not made a license for communal violence once he was gone.

    > "Do not return after me to striking one another's necks." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 7078[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-2264"></span>Reports from near the end of Prophet Muhammad ﷺ’s life repeatedly remember him stressing **prayer and responsibility** toward people under one’s authority rather than focusing on preserving wealth or dynastic power.

    > "The prayer, the prayer, and those whom your right hands possess." — *Sunan Abi Dawud* 5156[4](/messenger/sources#ref-4){: .footnote}[15](/messenger/sources#ref-15){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}; *Sunan Ibn Majah* 2698, graded sound[6](/messenger/sources#ref-6){: .footnote}[17](/messenger/sources#ref-17){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

## The society and generation he left behind
{: #the-society-and-generation-he-left-behind }

- <span id="src-2272"></span>The scale of change during Prophet Muhammad’s ﷺ twenty-three-year mission is **itself part of what needs explaining**. He began preaching in a fragmented tribal society without a state and died leading a polity that covered most of Arabia. In between, his community developed institutions of law, worship, taxation, learning, treaty-making, public charity, and written administration, while directly challenging practices such as idolatrous worship, blood vengeance, female infanticide, and unrestricted usury. The speed of this transformation does not by itself prove revelation, but an explanation of his historical career has to account for it.

    > "It is He who has sent among the unlettered a Messenger from themselves reciting to them His verses and purifying them and teaching them the Book and wisdom - although they were before in clear error -" (**Sūrat al-Jumu'ah 62:2**), Saheeh International[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}
    >
    > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿هُوَ ٱلَّذِى بَعَثَ فِى ٱلْأُمِّيِّـۧنَ رَسُولًا مِّنْهُمْ يَتْلُوا۟ عَلَيْهِمْ ءَايَـٰتِهِۦ وَيُزَكِّيهِمْ وَيُعَلِّمُهُمُ ٱلْكِتَـٰبَ وَٱلْحِكْمَةَ وَإِن كَانُوا۟ مِن قَبْلُ لَفِى ضَلَـٰلٍ مُّبِينٍ ۝٢﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ الجُمُعَةِ</span></div>
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-2279"></span>Some costly reforms were **adopted quickly** despite the absence of a large enforcement bureaucracy. Anas remembered wine being poured away when its prohibition was announced. Near the end of the mission, the financial and blood claims abolished in the Farewell Pilgrimage were made concrete by beginning with claims involving Prophet Muhammad’s ﷺ own family. This does not mean that every social vice disappeared overnight. It does show how quickly demanding norms could acquire real communal force.

    > Anas reported that when the prohibition of wine was announced, the containers in Medina were emptied. — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 2464[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}; *Sahih al-Bukhari* 4620[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}; *Sahih Muslim* 1218a[3](/messenger/sources#ref-3){: .footnote}[14](/messenger/sources#ref-14){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-2284"></span>The mission helped reconcile communities whose feud had involved **real bloodshed**. The Medinan tribes Aws and Khazraj had fought for generations, most recently at Bu’ath shortly before Prophet Muhammad’s ﷺ arrival. The Qur’an explicitly appealed to their own memory of once being enemies whose hearts had been brought together.

    > "And hold firmly to the rope of Allah all together and do not become divided. And remember the favor of Allah upon you - when you were enemies and He brought your hearts together and you became, by His favor, brothers. And you were on the edge of a pit of the Fire, and He saved you from it. Thus does Allah make clear to you His verses that you may be guided." (**Sūrat Āl 'Imrān 3:103**), Saheeh International[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}; *Sīra*: early biography, for the Bu'ath background[8](/messenger/sources#ref-8){: .footnote}[19](/messenger/sources#ref-19){: .footnote}[28](/messenger/sources#ref-28){: .footnote}[37](/messenger/sources#ref-37){: .footnote}[38](/messenger/sources#ref-38){: .footnote}
    >
    > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿وَٱعْتَصِمُوا۟ بِحَبْلِ ٱللَّهِ جَمِيعًا وَلَا تَفَرَّقُوا۟ ۚ وَٱذْكُرُوا۟ نِعْمَتَ ٱللَّهِ عَلَيْكُمْ إِذْ كُنتُمْ أَعْدَآءً فَأَلَّفَ بَيْنَ قُلُوبِكُمْ فَأَصْبَحْتُم بِنِعْمَتِهِۦٓ إِخْوَٰنًا وَكُنتُمْ عَلَىٰ شَفَا حُفْرَةٍ مِّنَ ٱلنَّارِ فَأَنقَذَكُم مِّنْهَا ۗ كَذَٰلِكَ يُبَيِّنُ ٱللَّهُ لَكُمْ ءَايَـٰتِهِۦ لَعَلَّكُمْ تَهْتَدُونَ ۝١٠٣﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ آلِ عِمۡرَانَ</span></div>
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-2291"></span>The refugees from Mecca were not merely admitted to Medina; the new community deliberately created **bonds of practical responsibility** between households. The emigrants had left property and protection behind. Early biography remembers pairings between emigrants and Medinan supporters, while the Qur’an praises the hosts for preferring the newcomers even when they themselves were in need.

    > "They give [them] preference over themselves, even though they are in privation." (**Sūrat al-Ḥashr 59:9**), Saheeh International[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}; *Sīra*: early biography, for the pairing of emigrants and helpers[8](/messenger/sources#ref-8){: .footnote}[19](/messenger/sources#ref-19){: .footnote}[28](/messenger/sources#ref-28){: .footnote}[37](/messenger/sources#ref-37){: .footnote}[38](/messenger/sources#ref-38){: .footnote}
    >
    > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿وَٱلَّذِينَ تَبَوَّءُو ٱلدَّارَ وَٱلْإِيمَـٰنَ مِن قَبْلِهِمْ يُحِبُّونَ مَنْ هَاجَرَ إِلَيْهِمْ وَلَا يَجِدُونَ فِى صُدُورِهِمْ حَاجَةً مِّمَّآ أُوتُوا۟ وَيُؤْثِرُونَ عَلَىٰٓ أَنفُسِهِمْ وَلَوْ كَانَ بِهِمْ خَصَاصَةٌ ۚ وَمَن يُوقَ شُحَّ نَفْسِهِۦ فَأُو۟لَـٰٓئِكَ هُمُ ٱلْمُفْلِحُونَ ۝٩﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ الحَشۡرِ</span></div>
    {: .quote }

- <span id="src-2298"></span>The generation formed around him did not respond to his death **by making him divine**. They had been taught to distinguish the messenger from God. Early reports remember Abu Bakr calming the community by reciting the Qur’anic reminder that Prophet Muhammad ﷺ was a messenger like earlier messengers and could die. That generation then became administrators, judges, teachers, and transmitters of the religious record rather than custodians of a royal cult centered on his person.

    > "Muhammad is not but a messenger. [Other] messengers have passed on before him. So if he was to die or be killed, would you turn back on your heels [to unbelief]?" (**Sūrat Āl 'Imrān 3:144**), Saheeh International[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}; recited by Abu Bakr in his address after Muhammad's ﷺ death, reported in *Sahih al-Bukhari*
    >
    > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿وَمَا مُحَمَّدٌ إِلَّا رَسُولٌ قَدْ خَلَتْ مِن قَبْلِهِ ٱلرُّسُلُ ۚ أَفَإِي۟ن مَّاتَ أَوْ قُتِلَ ٱنقَلَبْتُمْ عَلَىٰٓ أَعْقَـٰبِكُمْ ۚ وَمَن يَنقَلِبْ عَلَىٰ عَقِبَيْهِ فَلَن يَضُرَّ ٱللَّهَ شَيْـًٔا ۗ وَسَيَجْزِى ٱللَّهُ ٱلشَّـٰكِرِينَ ۝١٤٤﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ آلِ عِمۡرَانَ</span></div>
    {: .quote }
