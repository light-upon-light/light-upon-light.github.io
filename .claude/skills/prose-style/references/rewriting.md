# Rewriting existing text

Everything in the skill applies, with these additions.

A rewrite changes how the text reads, not what it says. The author owns the content: their claims, examples, and argument. Keep all of it, add none of your own, and keep the structure unless asked to change it. Cutting padding will usually make the text shorter. If it grows by more than a sentence or so, check whether you have added content. When the original has a real problem (a claim that is false, a step that is missing), keep the rewrite faithful and point out the problem separately, unless you were asked to improve the substance.

A rewrite is not a summary. Preserve every distinct premise, inference, counterexample, concession, causal link, qualification, and response to an objection. When a sentence contains both meta-commentary and reasoning, remove the commentary and keep the inference in plain language. When unsure whether something is substantive, keep it.

Work on a small unit at a time. Passing over a whole section at once misses most of the problems and flattens the rest. If the text needs reorganizing across paragraphs, do that as a separate pass over the continuous run.

Change wording only where the change is an improvement. A near-synonym is not one.

**Never lower the register.**

| Keep | Not |
| --- | --- |
| was delivered | came |
| stated | said |
| simultaneously | at once |
| developed through stages | came in stages |
| God's oneness, transcendence, and nearness | God as one, transcendent, and near |

**Keep each claim exactly as strong as it was, and add no contrast, dash, or intensifier the original lacks.** These changes all overstated or padded:

- "including tribes that had fought hardest" became "even tribes"
- "and though they had both the skill" became "yet though"
- "Revealed over the span of 23 years" became "Revealed gradually over…"
- "unforeseen events" became "events that could not be foreseen"

**Keep the words and marks that carry the argument.** Do not flatten a contrast or payoff into a neutral list, an "and," or separate sentences. Do not split a colon that ties a claim to its evidence. Do not move a payoff to the front: where four rival theories are rejected and one short line then says that the Prophet's ﷺ own explanation remains, moving that line first destroys it.

**Do not force a subject.** "All of the above is done by a book that was revealed orally…" is better than "The Qur'an does all of the above as a book…".

These rewrites improved the text without changing its claim:

| Before | After | What it fixed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| a text regarded by admirers and opponents alike as the summit of Arabic eloquence, by a far margin | a text that admirers and opponents alike regarded as by far the summit of Arabic eloquence | an awkward trailing phrase |
| because the civilizations involved had long since disappeared and their own records could no longer be read | because they concern civilizations that had long since disappeared and whose own records could no longer be read | a vague noun phrase |
| compare it with evidence | compare these details with evidence | an unclear "it" |
| or earlier. That date falls within a generation | or earlier, a date within a generation | a needless sentence break |
