# Muhammad ﷺ: His Life, Character, and Teachings

Muhammad ibn Abdullah ﷺ was born in Mecca around 570 CE and died in Medina in 632 CE. Muslims believe that, beginning at the age of forty, he received the Qur'an as revelation from God and was the last in the line of prophets that includes Abraham, Moses, and Jesus. The symbol **ﷺ** means “may God bless him and grant him peace.”

This article is about a narrower question than whether Islam is true: **what kind of man do the Qur'an and the major Muslim hadith traditions describe, and what did he teach people to become?** The answer is best seen cumulatively. A saying about mercy matters more when the same concern appears in his home, his courtroom, his treatment of a defeated enemy, his rules for war, his teaching about debt, and his response to an animal in distress.

### A note on sources and method

- The Qur'an is the earliest Islamic text and frequently addresses Muhammad ﷺ directly. **Sahih al-Bukhari** and **Sahih Muslim** are ninth-century compilations of earlier transmitted reports and are the two most highly regarded hadith collections in Sunni Islam. A modern historical-critical study can assess individual reports differently from traditional hadith criticism; here, the aim is to represent the strongest and most widely received Muslim source record accurately. Reports from other collections are used more selectively, with a grading noted where useful.
- Many events normally called “sira” [prophetic biography] are also preserved in hadith. Those stronger reports are preferred here. A famous devotional story has not been included merely because it is famous.
- Quotations below are **concise English renderings** of the cited Arabic, written for clarity rather than borrowed wholesale from a particular modern translation. Readers can follow each link to compare published translations and the surrounding report.
- Admiration does not require anachronism. Muhammad ﷺ lived in seventh-century Arabia, in a society with warfare, slavery, patriarchy, tribal retaliation, poverty, and few state institutions. The useful question is what he did within that world, what he restrained, what he reformed, and what moral direction his teaching established.

## I. A life in which the teachings were tested

### Before and at the beginning of revelation

1. **He knew vulnerability before he ever held authority.** His father died before he was born, his mother died while he was a child, and he later lost the grandfather who had taken him in. The Qur'an itself reminds him of that dependent childhood before commanding care for the orphan and petitioner.

   > “Did He not find you an orphan and give you shelter?... So do not oppress the orphan, and do not repel the one who asks.”  
   > [Qur'an 93:6, 9–10](https://quran.com/93/6-10)

2. **He had done ordinary work and knew what it meant to earn a living.** Muhammad ﷺ told his companions that, like the prophets before him, he had worked as a shepherd for the people of Mecca for wages. He did not construct a biography in which spiritual stature exempted a person from ordinary labor.

   > “God did not send a prophet except that he shepherded sheep... I shepherded them for the people of Mecca for coins.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 2262](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2262)

3. **The first person to reassure him appealed to a character already visible before his mission.** When the first revelation frightened him, his wife Khadija did not comfort him with a theological argument. She listed what she already knew about him: family loyalty, truthfulness, help for people without means, hospitality, and assistance in hardship.

   > “By God, God will never disgrace you. You maintain family ties, speak truthfully, support the needy, honor the guest, and help in calamity.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 3](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3)

4. **Even a leading enemy could not manufacture a pre-prophetic reputation for lying.** Years later, while Abu Sufyan was still an opponent of Muhammad ﷺ, the Byzantine emperor Heraclius questioned him. Asked whether Muhammad had been accused of lying before claiming prophethood, Abu Sufyan answered no. Asked whether he broke agreements, he again answered no.

   > “Have you ever accused him of lying before this claim?” He said, “No.” ... “Does he break his covenants?” He said, “No.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 7](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:7)

5. **The moral program his enemy described was not self-glorification.** In the same interrogation, Abu Sufyan summarized what Muhammad ﷺ was asking people to do: worship one God, pray, tell the truth, remain sexually chaste, and maintain family bonds.

   > “He commands us to worship God alone... to pray, speak the truth, be chaste, and maintain ties of kinship.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 7](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:7)

6. **He did not claim divinity, omniscience, or supernatural control of the world.** The Qur'an repeatedly makes him deny powers that a religious founder could have used to enlarge his personal status.

   > “I do not tell you that I possess God's treasures, nor that I know the unseen, nor do I say that I am an angel.”  
   > [Qur'an 6:50](https://quran.com/6/50)

7. **He denied even independent power to secure benefit for himself.** His prophetic claim was presented as dependence upon God, not personal mastery over fate.

   > “I have no power to bring benefit or harm even to myself, except as God wills. Had I known the unseen, I would have acquired much good.”  
   > [Qur'an 7:188](https://quran.com/7/188)

8. **He repeatedly told people that his preaching was not a paid service.** The Qur'an tells him to say that he seeks no payment for delivering its message.

   > “I ask you no payment for it. It is only a reminder for all people.”  
   > [Qur'an 12:104](https://quran.com/12/104)

9. **His scripture preserves public corrections of him.** When he frowned and turned from the blind believer Abdullah ibn Umm Maktum while trying to persuade influential Meccan leaders, the Qur'an did not protect his image. It rebuked the choice and centered the sincere man who had come seeking guidance.

   > “He frowned and turned away because the blind man came to him... Yet the one who came to you eagerly, in reverence, you were distracted from him.”  
   > [Qur'an 80:1–10](https://quran.com/80/1-10)

10. **The Qur'an also corrected him when social pressure affected a difficult personal decision.** Whatever conclusion a reader reaches about revelation, the text Muslims say he proclaimed does not read like a continuous exercise in protecting its messenger from embarrassment.

   > “You concealed within yourself what God would disclose, and you feared the people, while God has more right that you should fear Him.”  
   > [Qur'an 33:37](https://quran.com/33/37)

11. **He explicitly blocked exaggerated religious praise of himself.** Muhammad ﷺ compared excessive praise to what Christians had come to say about Jesus and told Muslims to keep his human servanthood in view.

   > “Do not exaggerate in praising me as the Christians exaggerated in praising the son of Mary. I am only His servant.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 3445](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3445)

12. **He refused to exploit a cosmic coincidence at the most emotionally vulnerable moment.** A solar eclipse occurred on the day his young son Ibrahim died. Some people connected the eclipse to the child's death. Muhammad ﷺ could have allowed a story of cosmic mourning to enhance his aura. He immediately rejected it.

   > “The sun and moon do not eclipse because of anyone's death or birth.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 1043](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:1043)

### Perseverance without becoming cruel

13. **Years of rejection did not turn his mission into a prayer for revenge.** After the people of Ta'if rejected him and he suffered what Aisha later called the hardest day of his life, the Angel of the Mountains offered, in the report, to crush them. He refused and expressed hope for their descendants instead.

   > “Rather, I hope that God will bring from their descendants people who worship God alone.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 3231](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3231)

14. **He defined his prophetic role against cursing people.** When companions asked him to curse hostile polytheists, his answer was a definition of mission, not merely a tactical decision.

   > “I was not sent as one who curses. I was sent only as a mercy.”  
   > [Sahih Muslim 2599](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2599)

15. **He prayed for the guidance of a hostile tribe when a companion wanted its destruction.** Tufayl ibn Amr asked him to invoke God against the tribe of Daws because of their resistance. Muhammad ﷺ instead asked God to guide them and bring them peacefully.

   > “O God, guide Daws and bring them.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 2937](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2937)

16. **During the migration from Mecca, his language to a frightened companion was reassurance rather than panic.** With pursuers searching for them, the Qur'an depicts Muhammad ﷺ in the cave telling Abu Bakr that they were not abandoned.

   > “Do not grieve. God is with us.”  
   > [Qur'an 9:40](https://quran.com/9/40)

17. **In Medina he worked alongside the community rather than supervising all physical labor from a distance.** Reports of the mosque's construction remember him carrying building material with the others.

   > “The Prophet himself began carrying the unbaked bricks for its building.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 3906](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3906)

18. **During the siege of Medina, he shared the digging and hunger.** At the Battle of the Trench, companions found a rock they could not break. Muhammad ﷺ came down into the trench himself; the narrator adds that a stone had been tied to his stomach after days of hunger.

   > “He got up, with a stone tied to his belly, for we had eaten nothing for three days.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 4101](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:4101)

19. **After military disaster, he was commanded to forgive and consult the very followers whose mistakes had contributed to it.** After Uhud, the Qur'an connects his gentleness with keeping people together and tells him to pardon them, pray for their forgiveness, and continue consulting them.

   > “Pardon them, ask forgiveness for them, and consult them in affairs. Had you been harsh and hard-hearted, they would have dispersed from you.”  
   > [Qur'an 3:159](https://quran.com/3/159)

20. **At Hudaybiyya he prioritized a peace agreement over wounded pride.** Muhammad ﷺ told the Meccans that the Muslims had not come to fight and accepted treaty terms many companions found painful. The agreement even required him to remove the title “Messenger of God” from the written treaty when the Meccan negotiator refused it.

   > “By God, I am the Messenger of God even if you deny me... Write: Muhammad son of Abdullah.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 2731–2732](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2731)

21. **He accepted painful treaty terms even when their human cost appeared in front of him.** During the negotiations, Abu Jandal, a persecuted Meccan Muslim, arrived in chains. His father Suhayl, the Meccan negotiator, insisted that returning him be the first application of the new agreement. Muhammad ﷺ tried to obtain an exception, but did not collapse the peace when Suhayl refused.

   > Suhayl said, “This is the first term on which we make peace with you: you shall return Abu Jandal to me.” The Prophet asked him to make an exception, but Suhayl refused.  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 2731–2732](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2731)

22. **The Qur'an called that restraint a victory before its political results were obvious.** The opening of chapter 48 describes the truce, not a battlefield conquest, as a “clear victory.”

   > “Indeed, We have granted you a clear victory.”  
   > [Qur'an 48:1](https://quran.com/48/1)

### Power did not erase restraint

23. **When he returned to Mecca with overwhelming power, he created multiple routes to safety.** The city that had expelled him was not told that every household would be treated as an enemy position. In the conquest report, people were safe if they entered Abu Sufyan's house, laid down their weapons, or simply shut their doors.

   > “Whoever enters Abu Sufyan's house is safe; whoever lays down his arms is safe; whoever closes his door is safe.”  
   > [Sahih Muslim 1780c](https://sunnah.com/muslim:1780c)

24. **He spared a group captured after trying to attack him near Mecca.** Sahih Muslim reports that eighty armed men descended upon Muhammad ﷺ and his companions. They were captured, and he let them go.

   > “He captured them alive, and the Messenger of God spared them.”  
   > [Sahih Muslim 1808](https://sunnah.com/muslim:1808)

25. **He taught that victory did not cancel the sanctity of people or property.** In his Farewell Pilgrimage, when he had become the dominant leader of Arabia, he compared a person's blood, property, and honor to the sacredness his hearers attached to the holy day and sanctuary.

   > “Your blood, your property, and your honor are sacred to one another, like the sacredness of this day of yours.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 67](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:67)

26. **His last public teaching included a warning against communal bloodshed after him.** He did not frame loyalty to him as permission for his followers to turn violently on one another.

   > “Do not return after me to striking one another's necks.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 7078](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:7078)

27. **He died without converting religious rule into personal luxury.** Although he had led a state and received public revenues, he died with his armor held as security by a Jewish merchant for food purchased on credit.

   > “When the Messenger of God died, his armor was mortgaged to a Jew for thirty measures of barley.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 2916](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2916)

28. **Material scarcity was not an occasional staged performance.** Aisha recalled stretches in which no cooking fire was lit in the Prophet's homes and the household lived mainly on dates and water, with milk sometimes supplied by neighbors.

   > “Three new moons would be seen in two months, and no fire would be lit in the houses of the Messenger of God.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 6459](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6459)

29. **Even when wealth passed through his hands, he disliked retaining it for himself.** He told Abu Dharr that if he possessed gold equal to Mount Uhud, he would prefer not to keep it for even a few nights except what was needed to settle a debt.

   > “I would not like to have gold equal to Uhud and have any of it remain with me after three nights, except something kept for a debt.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 6445](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6445)

## II. The character he practiced when no public performance was needed

### At home

1. **His wife Aisha gave the shortest summary of his character: the Qur'an had become conduct.** Asked what Muhammad ﷺ was like in private life, she did not distinguish a polished public persona from a different private one.

   > “His character was the Qur'an.”  
   > [Sahih Muslim 746a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:746a)

2. **He did household work.** Aisha was asked what the Prophet ﷺ did at home. Her answer is strikingly ordinary: he served his family, and when it was time for prayer, he went to pray.

   > “He used to be in the service of his family; when prayer came, he went out to the prayer.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 676](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:676)

3. **Aisha said he never struck a woman or a servant.** Her recollection covered years of domestic life, travel, disagreement, and public stress.

   > “The Messenger of God never struck anything with his hand, neither a woman nor a servant.”  
   > [Sahih Muslim 2328a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2328a)

4. **He did not use personal injury as a reason for revenge.** The same report says that he did not retaliate merely because he had personally been wronged; punishment was connected to public or divine law rather than wounded ego.

   > “He never took revenge for himself because something had been done to him.”  
   > [Sahih Muslim 2328a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2328a)

5. **He chose the easier lawful path when two legitimate options existed.** Aisha's description is of a religion lived without a preference for unnecessary severity.

   > “Whenever he was given a choice between two matters, he chose the easier of the two so long as it was not sinful.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 3560](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3560)

6. **He did not make meals into a place for belittling the person who prepared them.** Abu Hurayra said he never criticized food. Preference did not become insult.

   > “The Prophet never criticized food. If he liked it, he ate it; if he disliked it, he left it.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 5409](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5409)

7. **He continued honoring Khadija after her death.** Aisha remembered how often he spoke well of his first wife and how he sent gifts to Khadija's old friends. Loyalty did not end when the person could no longer return it.

   > “I was granted her love.”  
   > [Sahih Muslim 2435b](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2435b)

8. **He treated tenderness to one's spouse as morally significant, not trivial.** He taught that even ordinary family expenditure, including the food a husband placed in his wife's mouth, could be rewarded when done with good intention.

   > “Whatever you spend seeking God's pleasure is rewarded, even the morsel you place in your wife's mouth.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 5354](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5354)

9. **His standard for male religious character included treatment of one's wife.** He did not define excellence only by prayer, scholarship, or public reputation.

   > “The best of you are those best to their families, and I am the best of you to my family.”  
   > [Jamiʿ al-Tirmidhi 3895, graded hasan sahih](https://sunnah.com/tirmidhi:3895)

10. **His final pilgrimage included explicit responsibility toward women.** In a long public address he told men to be conscious of God concerning women and affirmed reciprocal marital rights, including proper provision of food and clothing.

   > “Be mindful of God concerning women... You owe them their provision and clothing in a fitting manner.”  
   > [Sahih Muslim 1218a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:1218a)

### With children and young people

11. **He openly kissed and hugged children in a culture where a man could boast of emotional hardness.** When a Bedouin said he had ten children and had never kissed any of them, Muhammad ﷺ treated the absence of tenderness as a deficiency, not masculinity.

   > “Whoever does not show mercy will not be shown mercy.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 5997](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5997)

12. **He noticed a small child's grief over a pet.** Anas remembered Muhammad ﷺ joking gently with his little brother Abu Umayr about a small bird that had died. For the child, the loss mattered; the Prophet ﷺ entered his world rather than dismissing it.

   > “O Abu Umayr, what happened to the little bird?”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 6129](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6129)

13. **He carried his young granddaughter while leading communal prayer.** He would put Umamah down when he prostrated and lift her when he stood. Affection for a child was not treated as beneath the dignity of formal worship.

   > “When he stood he carried her, and when he prostrated he put her down.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 516](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:516)

14. **A crying baby could change the length of his prayer because he thought about the mother's distress.** This is a small but revealing example of how ritual leadership was adjusted around human need.

   > “I hear a child crying, so I shorten the prayer because I know the intensity of his mother's distress.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 710](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:710)

15. **He stood up to welcome his daughter Fatima.** Aisha reported that when Fatima entered, Muhammad ﷺ rose, took her hand, kissed her, and seated her in his own place; Fatima did the same for him when he visited her.

   > “When she entered upon him, he stood for her, took her hand, kissed her, and seated her where he had been sitting.”  
   > [Sunan Abi Dawud 5217, graded sahih](https://sunnah.com/abudawud:5217)

16. **He did not teach men to hide grief behind hardness.** When his infant son Ibrahim was dying, his eyes filled with tears. Asked about it, he distinguished merciful grief from speech that rebels against God.

   > “The eyes shed tears and the heart grieves, but we say only what pleases our Lord.”  
   > [Sunan Abi Dawud 3126, graded sahih](https://sunnah.com/abudawud:3126)

17. **He made the care of daughters a religious good in its own right.** After telling of a poor mother who divided her only date between two daughters, Aisha reported Muhammad ﷺ saying that generous care for daughters protects a person from the Fire.

   > “Whoever is tested with daughters and treats them generously, they will be a shield for him from the Fire.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 5995](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5995)

18. **He redirected a man from voluntary warfare to serving his living parents.** The point was not that public sacrifice was unimportant, but that dramatic religious action could not erase quiet obligations at home.

   > “Are your parents alive?” He said, “Yes.” He replied, “Then strive in their service.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 5972](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5972)

19. **He placed extraordinary weight on a mother's care.** Asked who most deserved a person's good companionship, he named the mother three times before the father.

   > “Your mother.” “Then who?” “Your mother.” “Then who?” “Your mother.” “Then who?” “Your father.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 5971](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5971)

### With servants, companions, and ordinary people

20. **A man who served him for ten years remembered no verbal humiliation.** Anas ibn Malik had unusually close access to Muhammad ﷺ from youth. His recollection is valuable precisely because service creates countless opportunities for impatience.

   > “I served the Prophet for ten years, and he never once said to me, ‘Uff.’”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 6038](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6038)

21. **Anas also remembered that Muhammad ﷺ did not keep interrogating him over mistakes.** A servant did not have to fear constant “Why did you do that?” or “Why did you fail to do this?”

   > “He never said about something I had done, ‘Why did you do that?’ nor about something I left, ‘Why did you leave it?’”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 6038](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6038)

22. **He was remembered as a man who smiled at people.** Jarir ibn Abdullah said that after he became Muslim, the Prophet ﷺ did not shut him out and met him with a smile whenever he saw him.

   > “Whenever he saw me, he received me with a smile.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 3822](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3822)

23. **His speech was neither obscene nor deliberately coarse.** The report links this restraint in language to his ethical standard for people generally.

   > “The Prophet was neither obscene nor one who deliberately spoke obscenely... The best of you are those best in character.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 6029](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6029)

24. **He spoke slowly and clearly enough to be understood.** Aisha said his words were distinct rather than a rapid stream of speech.

   > “He did not speak rapidly as you do. He spoke with clear words, separated from one another.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 3568](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3568)

25. **He could absorb crude behavior without answering in kind.** A Bedouin yanked his cloak so violently that it left a mark on his shoulder, then demanded wealth. Muhammad ﷺ turned, smiled, and ordered that he be given something.

   > “The Prophet turned toward him, laughed, and ordered that a gift be given to him.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 3149](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3149)

26. **He spared a man who had stood over him with his own sword.** A Bedouin found Muhammad ﷺ resting alone, took his sword, and asked who could save him. After the immediate danger passed, the Prophet did not have him punished.

   > “Then he did not punish him, but sat down.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 2910](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2910)

27. **He was accessible enough for a socially vulnerable woman to stop him in public.** Sahih Muslim tells of a woman described in the language of the time as having a mental impairment. She said she needed something from him. He invited her to choose any street in Medina and stayed with her until her need was met.

   > “Choose whichever road you wish, and I will stand with you until I take care of your need.”  
   > [Sahih Muslim 2326](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2326)

28. **He noticed when a poor person disappeared.** A Black man or woman who used to clean the mosque died and was buried without the Prophet ﷺ being informed. He later asked where the person was, objected that he had not been told, went to the grave, and prayed there.

   > “Why did you not inform me?” ... Then he went to the grave and prayed over it.  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 458](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:458)

29. **His generosity could survive aggressive entitlement.** During one journey Bedouins crowded him and pulled away his cloak while demanding shares. He did not retaliate; he told them that if he possessed livestock as numerous as the desert trees, he would distribute it and they would not find him miserly.

   > “If I had camels as numerous as these thorn trees, I would distribute them among you. You would not find me miserly, false, or cowardly.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 2821](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2821)

30. **Anas remembered generosity and courage together, not as rival virtues.** When a frightening sound alarmed Medina one night, Muhammad ﷺ rode out ahead of everyone to investigate and met the others on his way back, already reassuring them.

   > “He was the best of people, the most generous of people, and the bravest of people.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 2820](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2820)

### Warmth, leisure, and fairness inside family life

31. **He made room for harmless recreation rather than treating constant seriousness as piety.** Aisha wanted to watch an Abyssinian display in the mosque. Muhammad ﷺ screened her with his cloak and stayed there until she had watched as long as she wished.

   > “The Prophet screened me with his cloak while I watched the Abyssinians playing... until I had enough.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 5236](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5236)

32. **He told husbands not to reduce a wife to the trait they dislike most.** Long relationships contain frustrations; his advice was to hold disliked and valued qualities in view together.

   > “A believing man should not hate a believing woman. If he dislikes one quality in her, he will be pleased with another.”  
   > [Sahih Muslim 1468b](https://sunnah.com/muslim:1468b)

33. **When women came to his household complaining that their husbands were beating them, he said those husbands were not the best Muslims.** The report has a difficult sequence in which permission to strike had temporarily been given after men's complaints; the women then came with their own complaints, and Muhammad ﷺ publicly rejected the resulting behavior as a mark of excellence.

   > “Many women have gone around Muhammad's family complaining of their husbands. Those men are not the best among you.”  
   > [Sunan Abi Dawud 2146, graded sahih](https://sunnah.com/abudawud:2146)

34. **He required parents to be fair among their children in gifts.** When a father wanted Muhammad ﷺ to witness a gift given to one child but not the others, the Prophet refused to validate the favoritism.

   > “Be conscious of God and be just among your children.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 2587](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2587)

## III. Mercy was made a social obligation, not merely a feeling

### The poor, the orphaned, the sick, and the overlooked

1. **He located false religion in neglect of the orphan and hungry.** The Qur'an's criticism of a person who “denies the Judgment” immediately turns to social behavior, not abstract disbelief alone.

   > “Have you seen the one who denies the Judgment? That is the one who pushes away the orphan and does not urge the feeding of the poor.”  
   > [Qur'an 107:1–3](https://quran.com/107/1-3)

2. **He pictured care for an orphan as companionship with him in Paradise.** He held his index and middle finger close together to make the moral proximity concrete.

   > “I and the one who cares for an orphan will be in Paradise like these two.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 6005](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6005)

3. **He compared caring for a widow or poor person to conspicuously demanding acts of worship.** The caregiver, not only the warrior or ascetic, received heroic religious language.

   > “The one who works for a widow or a poor person is like one who strives in God's cause, or one who prays by night and fasts by day.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 5353](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5353)

4. **He reduced social duty to verbs ordinary people could act on.** Feed, visit, free. The ethic is deliberately practical.

   > “Free the captive, feed the hungry, and visit the sick.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 3046](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3046)

5. **He taught that relieving another person's distress brings divine relief.** The same long saying joins material help, leniency toward debtors, discretion about faults, mutual aid, learning, and the irrelevance of pedigree without good deeds.

   > “Whoever relieves a believer of a worldly distress, God will relieve him of a distress on the Day of Resurrection.”  
   > [Sahih Muslim 2699a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2699a)

6. **Lineage could not substitute for character and action.** In a tribal society, this closing sentence of the same teaching is deliberately leveling.

   > “Whoever is slowed by his deeds will not be accelerated by his lineage.”  
   > [Sahih Muslim 2699a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2699a)

7. **He taught people to make room for debtors in genuine hardship.** The Qur'an requires time for the person who cannot pay and commends forgiving the debt as charity.

   > “If the debtor is in hardship, then grant time until ease; and if you remit it as charity, that is better for you.”  
   > [Qur'an 2:280](https://quran.com/2/280)

8. **He made graciousness in ordinary commerce a religious virtue.** Mercy was expected at the marketplace and during collection, where power imbalances often become visible.

   > “May God have mercy on a person who is easygoing when he sells, when he buys, and when he seeks repayment.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 2076](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2076)

9. **He told of a creditor forgiven by God because the creditor had been lenient with struggling people.** The story makes merciful treatment of debtors a reason to hope for mercy oneself.

   > “Give time to one in ease, and overlook one in hardship.” ... So God overlooked him.  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 2077](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2077)

10. **He did not romanticize poverty by excusing exploitation from the other side.** A debtor who can pay but deliberately delays was described as committing injustice. Compassion and responsibility operate together.

   > “Delay in payment by a person able to pay is injustice.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 2400](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2400)

11. **He taught that feeding one's own dependents is also charity.** Social generosity was not meant to be performed at the expense of neglected family obligations.

   > “When a Muslim spends on his family seeking reward, it is charity for him.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 5351](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5351)

12. **He advised leaving heirs financially secure rather than making them dependent on others.** This is a practical ethic of responsibility, not praise of destitution for its own sake.

   > “To leave your heirs well provided for is better than leaving them poor, begging from people.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 5354](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5354)

### Women as moral agents, learners, and holders of rights

13. **The Qur'an established women's inheritance as an enforceable share, not a favor from male relatives.** In its own historical setting, the important point is the legal formulation: women are named as entitled heirs.

   > “For men there is a share of what parents and close relatives leave, and for women there is a share.”  
   > [Qur'an 4:7](https://quran.com/4/7)

14. **A woman's marriage gift was declared her property.** The Qur'an directs husbands to give women their dowries freely and only permits taking back what a woman voluntarily chooses to give.

   > “Give women their marriage gifts graciously; but if they willingly give up some of it to you, then enjoy it with a clear conscience.”  
   > [Qur'an 4:4](https://quran.com/4/4)

15. **He invalidated a marriage a woman said her father had forced upon her.** Khansa bint Khidam complained that her father married her against her wishes; Muhammad ﷺ annulled the marriage.

   > “Her father married her while she disliked it, so the Messenger of God annulled that marriage.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 5138](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5138)

16. **He required a woman's consent before marriage.** The rule distinguishes how consent is expressed depending on a woman's situation but makes consent, rather than a guardian's preference alone, necessary.

   > “A previously married woman must not be married until consulted, and a virgin must not be married until her permission is sought.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 5136](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5136)

17. **Women could demand direct access to his teaching, and he reorganized his time for them.** When women told him that men were taking most of his teaching time and asked for a day of their own, he appointed one and taught them.

   > “Set aside a day for us.” So he appointed a day on which he met them and instructed them.  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 101](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:101)

18. **He told men not to block women from communal worship.** Whatever later Muslim cultures have done, the instruction itself is direct.

   > “Do not prevent the female servants of God from the mosques of God.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 900](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:900)

19. **The Qur'an places believing women and men side by side as moral allies.** Their public ethical responsibility is reciprocal: both promote good and resist wrong.

   > “The believing men and believing women are allies of one another. They encourage what is right and discourage what is wrong.”  
   > [Qur'an 9:71](https://quran.com/9/71)

20. **Spiritual worth is stated in parallel male and female terms.** One verse deliberately repeats ten virtues for both sexes, then gives the same promise of forgiveness and reward.

   > “The Muslim men and Muslim women, the believing men and believing women... God has prepared for them forgiveness and a great reward.”  
   > [Qur'an 33:35](https://quran.com/33/35)

21. **Marriage was described in the language of tranquility, affection, and mercy.** The Qur'an does not reduce marriage to lineage or control.

   > “He made for you spouses from among yourselves so that you may find tranquility in them, and He placed affection and mercy between you.”  
   > [Qur'an 30:21](https://quran.com/30/21)

22. **Men were told to live with wives honorably even when feelings were difficult.** The Qur'an explicitly rejects inheriting women against their will and follows it with a command of decent companionship.

   > “Live with them honorably. If you dislike them, perhaps you dislike something in which God has placed much good.”  
   > [Qur'an 4:19](https://quran.com/4/19)

23. **A woman's political judgment could shape a major public decision.** At Hudaybiyya, when distressed companions hesitated to end their pilgrimage rites, Umm Salama advised Muhammad ﷺ to perform the rites himself without arguing further. He followed her counsel, and the crisis dissolved.

   > “Go out and do not speak to anyone until you sacrifice your animal and call your barber.” He followed her advice.  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 2731–2732](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2731)

24. **He defended his wife Safiyya from an ethnic insult rather than asking her to absorb it silently.** Safiyya, who was of Jewish ancestry, came to him crying after being disparaged for that ancestry. He affirmed her dignity through the prophetic ancestry of Moses and Aaron and rebuked the insult.

   > “You are the daughter of a prophet, your uncle is a prophet, and you are married to a prophet. So what can she boast over you about?”  
   > [Jamiʿ al-Tirmidhi 3894, graded hasan sahih](https://sunnah.com/tirmidhi:3894)

### Race, status, slavery, and people whom society could diminish

25. **He confronted a companion for a racist insult.** When Abu Dharr disparaged another man through his Black mother, Muhammad ﷺ did not dismiss it as banter. He identified it as a residue of pre-Islamic ignorance.

   > “Did you insult him through his mother? You are a man in whom there is still ignorance.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 30](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:30)

26. **In a society that still practiced slavery, he insisted that enslaved people be treated as human kin rather than a lower species.** The same rebuke required shared standards of food and clothing and forbade crushing workloads. This did not abolish the institution in one command, but it placed real moral duties on the person with power.

   > “Those under your authority are your brothers. Feed them from what you eat, clothe them from what you wear, and do not burden them beyond capacity.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 30](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:30)

27. **If an owner did overload an enslaved worker, Muhammad ﷺ required the owner to help with the work.** Authority did not turn another person's exhaustion into an irrelevant private matter.

   > “If you burden them with what overcomes them, then help them.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 30](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:30)

28. **Striking an enslaved person could obligate emancipation.** Muslim's collection records the instruction that a person who slapped or beat an enslaved person without right should free that person as expiation.

   > “Whoever slaps or beats his slave, the expiation for it is to set him free.”  
   > [Sahih Muslim 1657b](https://sunnah.com/muslim:1657b)

29. **The Qur'an repeatedly turned emancipation into worship and moral repair.** Freeing an enslaved person is praised as climbing the “steep path” of goodness and is also prescribed as expiation for several serious wrongs.

   > “And what will make you understand the steep path? It is freeing a person from bondage.”  
   > [Qur'an 90:12–13](https://quran.com/90/12-13)

30. **He condemned the sale of a free human being into slavery.** In a hadith qudsi [a saying in which the Prophet reports words from God], this is one of three abuses for which God declares Himself the adversary on the Day of Judgment.

   > “I will be the opponent of... a man who sells a free person and consumes the price.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 2270](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2270)

31. **He elevated people whose societies had placed low in the status hierarchy.** Bilal, a formerly enslaved Abyssinian Muslim, became the Prophet's best-known caller to prayer. Muhammad ﷺ told him that he had heard his footsteps before him in Paradise.

   > “Tell me of the deed you have done in Islam for which you most hope, for I heard your footsteps before me in Paradise.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 1149](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:1149)

32. **He entrusted young Usama ibn Zayd, the son of a formerly enslaved man, with military command and defended the appointment against status-conscious criticism.** The report preserves the criticism rather than pretending social prejudice had vanished.

   > “If you criticize his leadership, you used to criticize his father's leadership before him. By God, his father was fit for leadership, and he was among the dearest people to me.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 4469](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:4469)

33. **He noticed the socially forgotten even in death.** After a battle, companions listed the dead but did not initially name Julaybib, a man with little social standing. Muhammad ﷺ did.

   > “But I am missing Julaybib. Search for him.” ... “He is of me and I am of him.”  
   > [Sahih Muslim 2472](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2472)

34. **He rejected contempt as part of the definition of arrogance.** Pride was not simply liking good clothes; it was refusing truth and looking down on human beings.

   > “Arrogance is rejecting the truth and despising people.”  
   > [Sahih Muslim 91a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:91a)

35. **The Qur'an replaced tribe and ethnicity as measures of human worth.** Diversity is presented as a basis for mutual recognition, while moral worth is tied to God-consciousness rather than ancestry.

   > “We made you peoples and tribes so that you may know one another. The most honored of you before God is the most God-conscious.”  
   > [Qur'an 49:13](https://quran.com/49/13)

36. **Muhammad ﷺ taught that God judges inner moral reality rather than appearance and wealth.** The statement directly relocates worth away from visible status markers.

   > “God does not look at your bodies or your wealth; He looks at your hearts and your deeds.”  
   > [Sahih Muslim 2564c](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2564c)

### Further protections for people with little leverage

37. **The Qur'an gave the murdered infant girl a voice in its picture of the Last Judgment.** Female infanticide is not discussed as an unfortunate custom to be gently outgrown; the child herself becomes the moral accuser.

   > “When the girl buried alive is asked for what sin she was killed.”  
   > [Qur'an 81:8–9](https://quran.com/81/8-9)

38. **It prohibited killing children because of poverty.** Fear about household provision could not make a child's life expendable.

   > “Do not kill your children because of poverty. We provide for you and for them.”  
   > [Qur'an 6:151](https://quran.com/6/151)

39. **It treated consuming an orphan's property as a grave moral offense.** Protecting an orphan meant protecting the child's assets from the adults managing them, not only providing food.

   > “Those who consume the property of orphans unjustly are only consuming fire into their bellies.”  
   > [Qur'an 4:10](https://quran.com/4/10)

40. **Mandatory almsgiving created an institutional claim for people in need.** The Qur'an lists the poor, the needy, people in debt, stranded travelers, and emancipation among the designated destinations of obligatory charity.

   > “Charities are for the poor, the needy... for freeing those in bondage, those in debt... and the traveler in need.”  
   > [Qur'an 9:60](https://quran.com/9/60)

41. **He did not want charity used as a weapon of humiliation.** The Qur'an says a kind word and forgiveness are better than a gift followed by injury or reminders of one's generosity.

   > “A kind word and forgiveness are better than charity followed by hurt.”  
   > [Qur'an 2:263](https://quran.com/2/263)

42. **People with little money were not excluded from generosity.** Muhammad ﷺ told people to protect themselves through charity even if all they could give was half a date.

   > “Protect yourself from the Fire even with half a date.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 1417](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:1417)

43. **He expanded “charity” beyond money.** A good act itself could be charity, which means material poverty did not make someone morally useless to the community.

   > “Every good deed is charity.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 6021](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6021)

44. **When companions asked what someone with no money could give, he kept finding forms of contribution.** Work, helping a person in distress, promoting good, and finally simply refraining from harm were all placed within the moral economy of charity.

   > “Let him help one in desperate need... and if he does not, let him refrain from evil; that is charity for him.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 6022](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6022)

45. **He challenged a successful companion's sense of superiority over poorer people.** Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas thought his status distinguished him from people beneath him. Muhammad ﷺ told him that the community's provision and victory were connected to its weak members.

   > “Are you given victory and provision except through the weak among you?”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 2896](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2896)

46. **As the Medinan community gained resources, he assumed public responsibility for dependents and unpaid debts left by deceased Muslims.** Private wealth still went to heirs; unprovided dependents were not simply abandoned.

   > “Whoever leaves property, it belongs to his heirs; whoever leaves debt or dependents, we will take care of them.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 6763](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6763)

47. **He joined mercy to children with respect for older people.** The ethic was not to elevate one vulnerable age group by dismissing another.

   > “He is not of us who does not show mercy to our young and recognize the right of our elders.”  
   > [Jamiʿ al-Tirmidhi 1920, graded hasan](https://sunnah.com/tirmidhi:1920)

48. **The Qur'an gives a compact definition of righteousness that moves from belief to costly social responsibility.** It includes giving loved wealth to relatives, orphans, the poor, travelers, petitioners, and emancipation, then keeping promises and remaining patient in hardship.

   > “Righteousness is... to give wealth, despite loving it, to relatives, orphans, the needy, the traveler, those who ask, and for freeing those in bondage.”  
   > [Qur'an 2:177](https://quran.com/2/177)

## IV. The kind of person he taught his followers to become

### Character before performance

1. **He made intention morally serious.** One of the most famous teachings preserved from him says that an outwardly identical action can have a different moral value depending on why it was done. Religion was not meant to be a theater of appearances.

   > “Actions are only by intentions, and each person will have only what he intended.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 1](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:1)

2. **He made concern for another person's good part of faith itself.** Moral concern was not meant to stop at the boundary of the self.

   > “None of you truly believes until he loves for his brother what he loves for himself.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 13](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:13)

3. **He defined a Muslim partly by whether other people are safe from him.** The test is concrete: tongue and hand.

   > “A Muslim is one from whose tongue and hand the Muslims are safe.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 10](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:10)

4. **He tied faith to disciplined speech.** Not every true thought needs to become a cruel remark, and silence can be an ethical choice.

   > “Whoever believes in God and the Last Day should speak what is good or remain silent.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 6018](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6018)

5. **He redefined strength as mastery over anger.** Physical dominance was not the highest form of power.

   > “The strong person is not the one who defeats others in wrestling. The strong person is the one who controls himself when angry.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 6114](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6114)

6. **His simplest repeated counsel to a man was not to give anger control.** When a man asked him for advice, Muhammad ﷺ kept returning to the same instruction.

   > “Do not become angry.” He repeated it several times: “Do not become angry.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 6116](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6116)

7. **He taught that gentleness improves what it enters.** This was not merely advice to naturally mild personalities; it was a general moral principle.

   > “Gentleness is not in anything except that it beautifies it, and it is not removed from anything except that it disfigures it.”  
   > [Sahih Muslim 2594a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2594a)

8. **He taught that forgiveness is not humiliation.** The moral paradox is that giving up retaliation can increase, rather than diminish, a person's honor.

   > “No one forgives except that God increases him in honor, and no one humbles himself for God except that God raises him.”  
   > [Sahih Muslim 2588](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2588)

9. **He gave backbiting a precise definition that removes the usual excuse.** If the negative thing said behind someone's back is true, it can still be backbiting; if false, it is slander.

   > “Mentioning about your brother what he dislikes.” They asked, “What if it is true?” He replied, “If true, you backbit him; if false, you slandered him.”  
   > [Sahih Muslim 2589](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2589)

10. **The Qur'an attacks ridicule, insulting labels, suspicion, spying, and backbiting in one concentrated passage.** Social harm is treated as a cluster: how people name one another, imagine one another, investigate one another, and speak when the other is absent.

   > “Let no people ridicule another people... Do not insult one another, nor call one another by offensive names... Avoid much suspicion... do not spy or backbite.”  
   > [Qur'an 49:11–12](https://quran.com/49/11-12)

11. **Reconciliation between people was a positive duty.** The same chapter frames believers as a moral family precisely in order to require peacemaking when relationships fracture.

   > “The believers are but brothers, so make peace between your brothers.”  
   > [Qur'an 49:10](https://quran.com/49/10)

12. **Truthfulness was presented as a path that forms the person over time.** Muhammad ﷺ taught that repeated truth makes someone truthful in character, just as repeated lying forms a liar.

   > “Truthfulness leads to righteousness, and righteousness leads to Paradise... Beware of lying, for lying leads to wickedness.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 6094](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6094)

13. **He made the neighbor's safety a test of faith.** Religious identity was contradicted by making the person next door afraid of one's harm.

   > “By God, he does not believe... the one whose neighbor is not safe from his harm.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 6016](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6016)

14. **He described neighborly obligation as almost inheritance-level serious.** Gabriel's repeated counsel about neighbors was, Muhammad ﷺ said, so persistent that he wondered whether neighbors would be given inheritance rights.

   > “Gabriel kept instructing me about the neighbor until I thought he would make him an heir.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 6014](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6014)

15. **Hospitality was attached to belief, not social class.** The same teaching that regulates speech also requires honoring a guest and refusing harm to a neighbor.

   > “Whoever believes in God and the Last Day should honor his neighbor... and should honor his guest.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 6018](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6018)

16. **He distinguished genuine family loyalty from mere reciprocity.** Maintaining relatives only when they maintain you is exchange; he praised the person who keeps the bond when relatives sever it.

   > “The one who truly maintains kinship is the one who, when his relatives cut him off, maintains relations with them.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 5991](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5991)

17. **He instructed people to help an oppressor, but gave the phrase a moral reversal.** When listeners asked how an oppressor could be helped, Muhammad ﷺ said the help consists in stopping the oppression.

   > “Help your brother whether he is an oppressor or oppressed.” ... “You help the oppressor by preventing him from oppression.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 2444](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2444)

18. **He refused the idea that contemptuous pride is compatible with goodness.** A person could dress well without pride; the actual vice was rejection of truth and disdain for people.

   > “God is beautiful and loves beauty. Pride is rejecting truth and looking down on people.”  
   > [Sahih Muslim 91a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:91a)

### He taught without making people hate learning

19. **His principle for religious teachers was to make the path approachable.** “Easy” here does not mean erasing moral demands; it means not turning teaching itself into needless repulsion.

   > “Make things easy and do not make them difficult. Give glad news and do not drive people away.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 69](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:69)

20. **When a Bedouin urinated in the mosque, he stopped the crowd from attacking the man.** He let the man finish, had the area washed with water, and turned a humiliating incident into quiet instruction.

   > “Leave him, and pour a bucket of water over it. You were sent to make things easy, not to make them hard.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 220](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:220)

21. **A man who accidentally spoke during prayer remembered him as the best teacher he had ever seen.** Muawiya ibn al-Hakam said Muhammad ﷺ did not strike, scold, or insult him; after the prayer he simply explained the rule.

   > “May my father and mother be given for him. I never saw a teacher before or after him better in teaching than he was.”  
   > [Sahih Muslim 537a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:537a)

22. **He could answer a humiliating confession without humiliating the confessor.** A man admitted that he had broken the Ramadan fast through sexual intercourse and could not afford the prescribed forms of expiation. When food was eventually brought for charity, the man said his was the poorest household in Medina. Muhammad ﷺ laughed and told him to feed his family.

   > “Then feed it to your family.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 5368](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5368)

23. **He did not measure sincerity by how many voluntary practices a person could add.** A Bedouin promised to perform the obligatory prayers, fasting, and charity without adding voluntary acts. Muhammad ﷺ did not shame the modest commitment.

   > “He will succeed if he is truthful.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 46](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:46)

24. **He resisted religious maximalism when it harmed human balance.** Three men proposed extreme regimes of prayer, fasting, or celibacy. Muhammad ﷺ answered with his own practice: worship and sleep, fast and eat, marry and live family life.

   > “I fast and I break my fast; I pray and I sleep; and I marry women. Whoever turns away from my way is not of me.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 5063](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5063)

25. **He endorsed the principle that the body and family have rights alongside worship.** Salman told Abu al-Darda not to exhaust himself with continuous fasting and prayer. Muhammad ﷺ confirmed the principle.

   > “Your Lord has a right over you, your self has a right over you, and your family has a right over you. Give each its right.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 1968](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:1968)

26. **He said plainly that religion was not supposed to become a contest in self-exhaustion.** The report warns that a person who tries to overwhelm the religion by unsustainable severity will be overwhelmed.

   > “Religion is easy, and no one makes the religion excessively hard upon himself except that it overcomes him. So aim for what is right and draw near.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 39](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:39)

27. **He shortened congregational prayer for people with less stamina.** Leaders were told to remember that a congregation contains the weak, sick, old, and people with pressing needs.

   > “When one of you leads the people in prayer, let him shorten it, for among them are the weak, the sick, and the elderly.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 703](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:703)

28. **He rebuked a companion for making ordinary worship unnecessarily burdensome.** Muadh's very long prayer drove a working man to leave the congregation. Muhammad ﷺ took the complaint seriously and told Muadh not to put people to trial.

   > “Are you putting people to trial, Muadh?”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 705](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:705)

29. **He sometimes withheld a practice he loved because he feared burdening everyone else.** He said he would have required tooth-cleaning before every prayer if that would not impose hardship on his community.

   > “Were it not that I would cause hardship for my community, I would have ordered them to use the tooth-stick with every prayer.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 887](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:887)

30. **He stopped leading optional Ramadan night prayers in congregation because he feared they might become obligatory.** His own desire for worship gave way to concern about a future burden on others.

   > “Nothing prevented me from coming out to you except that I feared it might be made obligatory upon you.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 1129](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:1129)

### Worship, gratitude, hope, and repentance

31. **His own long worship was framed as gratitude, not spiritual vanity.** Aisha asked why he stood in night prayer until his feet became swollen when he believed God had forgiven him. His answer was gratitude.

   > “Should I not be a grateful servant?”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 4836](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:4836)

32. **He taught that salvation is ultimately a mercy, not a wage God owes for religious performance.** He included himself in the statement, preventing even prophetic worship from becoming grounds for entitlement before God.

   > “No one's deeds will admit him to Paradise.” They asked, “Not even you?” He said, “Not even me, unless God envelops me in mercy.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 5673](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5673)

33. **He described God's mercy with the image of a mother who has just found her child.** After captives arrived and a woman frantically searched until she found and nursed her baby, Muhammad ﷺ used the scene to teach hope in God.

   > “God is more merciful to His servants than this woman is to her child.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 5999](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5999)

34. **He made repentance sound like return, not permanent moral branding.** He compared God's joy at a person's repentance to a traveler in a deadly desert unexpectedly recovering the camel carrying all his provisions.

   > “God is more joyful at the repentance of His servant than one of you who suddenly finds his lost camel in a barren land.”  
   > [Sahih Muslim 2747a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2747a)

35. **He told a story in which a woman known for sexual sin was forgiven for one act of mercy to a thirsty dog.** The point is not that one can calculate salvation mechanically; it is that no person should imagine that the door to mercy has been closed by a despised past.

   > “She gave the dog water, so God thanked her for it and forgave her.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 3321](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3321)

36. **He taught people never to treat God's mercy as scarce.** One hadith depicts the mercy visible among all creatures as only one part of a much greater divine mercy.

   > “God made mercy into one hundred parts... He sent down one part to the earth, and through it creatures show mercy to one another.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 6000](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6000)

37. **The Qur'an tells people who have badly wronged themselves not to despair.** This is among the clearest expressions of the spiritual atmosphere Muhammad ﷺ preached.

   > “O My servants who have transgressed against themselves, do not despair of God's mercy. God forgives all sins.”  
   > [Qur'an 39:53](https://quran.com/39/53)

### Knowledge, thought, and useful action

38. **He treated the pursuit of knowledge as a road toward God.** The same hadith that praises relieving distress and helping debtors turns to the learner.

   > “Whoever travels a path seeking knowledge, God makes easy for him a path to Paradise.”  
   > [Sahih Muslim 2699a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2699a)

39. **He praised people who learn scripture and then teach it.** Knowledge was not meant to be accumulated as private prestige.

   > “The best of you are those who learn the Qur'an and teach it.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 5027](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5027)

40. **The Qur'an repeatedly invites reflection rather than treating nature as spiritually irrelevant.** Its signs include the alternation of night and day and the ordered world around the reader.

   > “In the creation of the heavens and earth and the alternation of night and day are signs for people of understanding.”  
   > [Qur'an 3:190](https://quran.com/3/190)

41. **He separated expertise in ordinary technical matters from prophetic authority.** After a comment about date-palm pollination proved unhelpful, Muhammad ﷺ told the growers that they knew their worldly craft better. The report is a rare, useful limit on religious overconfidence about technical expertise.

   > “You are more knowledgeable about the affairs of your worldly life.”  
   > [Sahih Muslim 2363](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2363)

42. **He treated removing a hazard from a public road as spiritually meaningful.** Religious merit could consist of noticing a small danger to strangers and quietly removing it.

   > “A man saw a thorny branch in the road and removed it, so God appreciated his act and forgave him.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 652](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:652)

43. **He taught that lasting productive work can remain charity after the worker leaves.** Planting and agriculture become ongoing good whenever people or animals benefit.

   > “No Muslim plants a tree or sows a crop from which a person, bird, or animal eats except that it is charity for him.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 2320](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2320)

44. **He defined true wealth as an inward condition rather than accumulation.** This protects both the poor from shame and the wealthy from imagining that possession cures greed.

   > “Richness is not having many possessions. True richness is richness of the soul.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 6446](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6446)

45. **He warned that health and free time are easily wasted forms of wealth.** The teaching asks people to notice ordinary blessings before they disappear.

   > “There are two blessings in which many people are deceived: health and free time.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 6412](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6412)

### Everyday integrity, privacy, and self-restraint

46. **He made keeping promises and trusts part of moral identity.** Lying, breaking promises, and betraying what is entrusted to a person were named signs of hypocrisy.

   > “The signs of a hypocrite are three: when he speaks he lies, when he promises he breaks it, and when entrusted he betrays.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 33](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:33)

47. **He prohibited the social habits that turn a community into rivals.** Grudge, contempt, humiliation, and oppression appear together in a teaching that locates God-consciousness in the heart rather than social rank.

   > “Do not hate one another or turn away from one another... A Muslim does not oppress, abandon, or despise his brother.”  
   > [Sahih Muslim 2564a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2564a)

48. **He treated another person's blood, wealth, and honor as a single field of inviolable rights.** Reputation is placed beside body and property rather than dismissed as socially harmless.

   > “All of a Muslim is inviolable to another Muslim: his blood, his wealth, and his honor.”  
   > [Sahih Muslim 2564a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2564a)

49. **He praised feeding people and greeting strangers, not only one's circle.** The small phrase “those you know and those you do not know” resists turning courtesy into networking.

   > “Feed people, and greet those you know and those you do not know.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 6236](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6236)

50. **He made small acts of attentiveness into charity.** Smiling, giving directions to a lost person, helping someone with poor eyesight, removing a hazard, and sharing water are all named in one report.

   > “Your smile in your brother's face is charity... and guiding a person with poor sight is charity for you.”  
   > [Jamiʿ al-Tirmidhi 1956, graded hasan](https://sunnah.com/tirmidhi:1956)

51. **The Qur'an protects the privacy of the home.** People were told not to enter someone else's house until they had sought permission and greeted its occupants.

   > “Do not enter houses other than your own until you have asked permission and greeted their people.”  
   > [Qur'an 24:27](https://quran.com/24/27)

52. **Sexual modesty was commanded to men as well as women.** The Qur'an first tells believing men to lower their gaze and guard their chastity, then gives believing women the corresponding command.

   > “Tell the believing men to lower their gaze and guard their chastity... And tell the believing women to lower their gaze and guard their chastity.”  
   > [Qur'an 24:30–31](https://quran.com/24/30-31)

53. **The Qur'an protects people against destructive sexual slander.** A serious accusation against a chaste person required evidence rather than gossip, suspicion, or reputation warfare.

   > “Those who accuse chaste women and then do not produce four witnesses, flog them... and do not accept their testimony afterward.”  
   > [Qur'an 24:4](https://quran.com/24/4)

54. **He taught that even reconciling two people or helping someone load a riding animal is charity.** Moral life was filled with small opportunities rather than restricted to large public acts.

   > “To act justly between two people is charity; to help a man onto his mount or lift his belongings onto it is charity.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 2989](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2989)

55. **The Qur'an confronted intoxicants and gambling as social as well as spiritual harms.** The following verse explains that they generate hostility and distract people from remembrance and prayer.

   > “Intoxicants, gambling... are filth from Satan's work, so avoid them that you may prosper.”  
   > [Qur'an 5:90–91](https://quran.com/5/90-91)

56. **Wastefulness itself was morally disapproved.** A hadith pairs wasting wealth with pointless talk and needlessly contentious questioning.

   > “God dislikes for you gossip, needless questioning, and wasting wealth.”  
   > [Sahih Muslim 1715a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:1715a)

## V. Justice, money, and leadership

### Justice even when it is personally costly

1. **The Qur'an commands testimony for justice even against oneself and one's closest family.** Tribal loyalty, family reputation, wealth, and poverty are all explicitly denied the right to bend the standard.

   > “Stand firmly for justice as witnesses for God, even against yourselves, your parents, or close relatives, whether one is rich or poor.”  
   > [Qur'an 4:135](https://quran.com/4/135)

2. **Hatred of an opposing group was not allowed to become permission for injustice.** This is one of the Qur'an's most demanding ethical rules because it applies precisely when impartiality feels least natural.

   > “Do not let hatred of a people cause you to depart from justice. Be just; that is nearer to God-consciousness.”  
   > [Qur'an 5:8](https://quran.com/5/8)

3. **Muhammad ﷺ publicly rejected a two-tier justice system for the elite and weak.** When a well-connected woman of Quraysh committed theft and people wanted Usama to intercede, the Prophet ﷺ said unequal enforcement had destroyed earlier peoples.

   > “Those before you were ruined because when a noble person stole they let him go, but when a weak person stole they applied the law.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 3475](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3475)

4. **He made the anti-nepotism point with the person dearest to him.** He said that even his own daughter Fatima would not be exempt if she committed the same offense. The severity of the historical punishment is a separate legal question; the principle being asserted here is that status cannot buy immunity.

   > “By God, if Fatima the daughter of Muhammad were to steal, I would apply the law to her.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 3475](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3475)

5. **He warned his own representative to fear the complaint of an oppressed person.** Before sending Muadh to Yemen, Muhammad ﷺ told him that official power carries spiritual danger because the wronged person's prayer has no barrier before God.

   > “Beware the supplication of the oppressed, for there is no barrier between it and God.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 2448](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2448)

6. **He taught that God has forbidden oppression even in the divine-human relationship and therefore humans must forbid it among themselves.** The hadith qudsi universalizes the principle.

   > “My servants, I have forbidden oppression for Myself and made it forbidden among you, so do not oppress one another.”  
   > [Sahih Muslim 2577a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2577a)

7. **He acknowledged that a judge can be persuaded by the more articulate litigant.** Muhammad ﷺ explicitly called himself human in judicial matters and warned that a favorable ruling cannot convert another person's right into one's own.

   > “I am only human... perhaps one of you is more eloquent in presenting his case. If I award him his brother's right, let him not take it.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 7169](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:7169)

8. **He prohibited judges from deciding cases while angry.** Justice required enough emotional self-control that power would not be exercised in a state of rage.

   > “No judge should judge between two people while he is angry.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 7158](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:7158)

### Work, trade, debt, and corruption

9. **He treated withholding a worker's earned wage as an offense for which God Himself is the adversary.** The worker in the report has already completed the agreed labor; taking the benefit and withholding the pay is singled out as a serious betrayal.

   > “I will be the opponent of... a man who hires a worker, receives the work from him in full, and does not pay his wage.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 2270](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2270)

10. **His own biography included paid manual work.** His history as a hired shepherd made work something he had experienced, not only regulated from political office.

   > “I used to shepherd them for the people of Mecca for coins.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 2262](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2262)

11. **He personally inspected a market and exposed concealed product defects.** When he put his hand into a pile of food and found wet grain hidden underneath, he told the seller to put the defect where buyers could see it.

   > “Why did you not put it on top so people could see it? Whoever deceives us is not of us.”  
   > [Sahih Muslim 102](https://sunnah.com/muslim:102)

12. **The Qur'an condemns shaving small amounts from measures and weights.** Economic morality includes mundane transactions where each individual loss might look too small to matter.

   > “Woe to those who give less than due, who demand full measure when they receive from people but give less when they measure or weigh for them.”  
   > [Qur'an 83:1–3](https://quran.com/83/1-3)

13. **It prohibits consuming another person's wealth through wrongful means and makes genuine consent central to trade.** Property rights are joined to the sanctity of life in the same verse.

   > “Do not consume one another's wealth unjustly, but only through trade by mutual consent. And do not kill yourselves.”  
   > [Qur'an 4:29](https://quran.com/4/29)

14. **Contracts were treated as moral commitments.** The opening of chapter 5 gives the command in unusually concise form.

   > “O you who believe, fulfill your contracts.”  
   > [Qur'an 5:1](https://quran.com/5/1)

15. **He rejected gifts that flowed to a public official because of the office.** A tax collector returned saying that some property was for the state and some had been given to him personally. Muhammad ﷺ exposed the conflict of interest by asking whether those gifts would have arrived if the man had simply stayed at home.

   > “Why did he not sit in his father's or mother's house and see whether gifts would be given to him?”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 7174](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:7174)

16. **He did not give public authority to people simply because they wanted status.** When men asked him for office, he stated a general rule against appointing someone who demands it or is eager for it.

   > “We do not appoint to this work anyone who asks for it or is eager for it.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 7149](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:7149)

17. **He described authority as a trust that can become disgrace if a person takes it without being able to fulfill its duties.** Abu Dharr was personally loved and morally sincere, yet Muhammad ﷺ told him he was not suited for political office.

   > “Abu Dharr, you are weak, and authority is a trust. On the Day of Resurrection it will be disgrace and regret except for one who takes it rightly.”  
   > [Sahih Muslim 1825](https://sunnah.com/muslim:1825)

18. **His treatment of Abu Dharr shows that goodness and competence are not identical.** Personal piety did not automatically qualify someone for every responsibility.

   > “I love for you what I love for myself. Do not be in charge of even two people, and do not administer an orphan's property.”  
   > [Sahih Muslim 1826](https://sunnah.com/muslim:1826)

### Leadership as responsibility rather than personal elevation

19. **The Qur'an made consultation part of Muhammad's own leadership.** Even after the serious errors at Uhud, he was told to continue consulting the community rather than conclude that only his own judgment mattered.

   > “Pardon them, ask forgiveness for them, and consult them in affairs.”  
   > [Qur'an 3:159](https://quran.com/3/159)

20. **Consultation is also named as a general trait of the believing community.** It is not limited to one episode.

   > “Their affairs are conducted by mutual consultation among them.”  
   > [Qur'an 42:38](https://quran.com/42/38)

21. **He could take advice from a wife at a moment of political crisis.** At Hudaybiyya he accepted Umm Salama's practical solution when the companions were emotionally frozen. Prophetic authority did not make receiving good counsel humiliating.

   > She advised him to perform the sacrifice and shaving himself. “He went out and did not speak to any of them until he had done that.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 2731–2732](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2731)

22. **He worked beside people during collective hardship.** Building the mosque and digging the trench were not tasks he reserved entirely for followers while he remained physically separate.

   > “We were with the Messenger of God at the Trench, and he was digging while we carried the earth away.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 6414](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6414)

23. **He did not confuse leadership with luxury.** Umar once entered Muhammad's small room and saw the marks that a rough sleeping mat had left on his side. The contrast with imperial rulers moved Umar to tears.

   > “I looked around his room, and by God I saw nothing of importance except three hides.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 2468](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2468)

24. **He made personal generosity compatible with stewardship rather than accumulation.** When asked for something he possessed and could give, Anas remembered that refusal was not his habit.

   > “The Messenger of God was never asked for something for the sake of Islam except that he gave it.”  
   > [Sahih Muslim 2312a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2312a)

25. **He taught leaders to fear making life needlessly hard for people.** In a prayer about those entrusted with his community's affairs, he contrasted harsh administration with gentleness.

   > “O God, whoever is put in charge of my community and is hard on them, be hard on him; whoever is gentle with them, be gentle with him.”  
   > [Sahih Muslim 1828a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:1828a)

### Further limits on economic and political power

26. **The Qur'an prohibited interest-based usury and distinguished it from ordinary trade.** Whatever later jurists debate about the boundary of riba [prohibited increase], the text's moral direction is clear: debt cannot be turned into an unlimited engine of extraction.

   > “Give up what remains of usury, if you are believers... You may have your principal: neither wronging nor being wronged.”  
   > [Qur'an 2:278–279](https://quran.com/2/278-279)

27. **He condemned hoarding staple goods for market advantage.** A food market was not morally neutral when scarcity could be manipulated.

   > “No one hoards except a sinner.”  
   > [Sahih Muslim 1605a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:1605a)

28. **The Qur'an prohibited using wealth to corrupt legal authorities.** It specifically links wrongful consumption of property with bribing those in power.

   > “Do not consume one another's wealth unjustly or offer it to authorities so that you may knowingly consume part of other people's wealth in sin.”  
   > [Qur'an 2:188](https://quran.com/2/188)

29. **A prophetic teaching was later invoked to stop abuse of non-Muslim taxpayers.** Hisham ibn Hakim saw farmers made to stand in the sun over the jizya tax and cited Muhammad ﷺ's warning against tormenting people. The report shows a principle capable of being used against officials after the Prophet's death.

   > “God will punish those who torment people in this world.”  
   > [Sahih Muslim 2613b](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2613b)

30. **He made the community responsible for people who died leaving dependents without support.** Public leadership carried liabilities, not only revenue.

   > “Whoever leaves debt or dependents, they are our responsibility.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 6763](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6763)

## VI. How he taught Muslims to treat people outside their faith

### Kindness, justice, and freedom of belief

1. **The Qur'an explicitly permits kindness and justice toward non-Muslims who are not fighting Muslims or expelling them from their homes.** The distinction is based on conduct, not simply religious identity.

   > “God does not forbid you from being kind and just toward those who did not fight you because of religion or drive you from your homes. God loves those who act justly.”  
   > [Qur'an 60:8](https://quran.com/60/8)

2. **It gives a direct rule against forced conversion.** Islamic history, like the history of every large civilization, contains coercion and political domination; the textual rule itself is nonetheless unambiguous.

   > “There is no compulsion in religion. Right guidance has become clear from error.”  
   > [Qur'an 2:256](https://quran.com/2/256)

3. **Religious disagreement was to be argued with wisdom and good counsel.** The command is about method as well as content.

   > “Invite to the way of your Lord with wisdom and good instruction, and debate with them in the best manner.”  
   > [Qur'an 16:125](https://quran.com/16/125)

4. **The Qur'an even tells Muslims not to insult the gods of other religions when that will provoke reciprocal abuse of God.** Restraint in religious speech is grounded in the consequences of needless provocation.

   > “Do not insult those they invoke besides God, lest they insult God in hostility and ignorance.”  
   > [Qur'an 6:108](https://quran.com/6/108)

5. **If an enemy polytheist asked for protection, the Qur'an required safe conduct, including delivery to a place of safety.** The opportunity to hear the Islamic message did not cancel the person's right to leave safely afterward.

   > “If any polytheist seeks your protection, grant him protection... then escort him to his place of safety.”  
   > [Qur'an 9:6](https://quran.com/9/6)

6. **He told Asma bint Abi Bakr to remain good to her non-Muslim mother.** Asma's mother visited while still a polytheist, and Asma asked whether she should maintain the relationship. Muhammad ﷺ said yes.

   > “Yes. Maintain good relations with your mother.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 2620](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2620)

7. **He stood when a Jewish funeral procession passed.** When someone pointed out that the deceased was Jewish, Muhammad ﷺ answered by drawing attention to shared humanity.

   > “Was he not a soul?”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 1312](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:1312)

8. **He visited a Jewish boy who was ill.** The boy had served him; when the boy became sick Muhammad ﷺ went to his home and sat near his head. The report also records his invitation to Islam, so the encounter combined religious conviction with personal visitation rather than indifference.

   > “A Jewish boy used to serve the Prophet. He became ill, so the Prophet went to visit him.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 1356](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:1356)

9. **He demanded gentleness even when he believed he had been deliberately insulted.** Some Jewish visitors greeted him with a pun meaning “death upon you.” Aisha answered angrily. Muhammad ﷺ stopped her and told her that God loves gentleness.

   > “Be calm, Aisha. God loves gentleness in all matters.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 6024](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6024)

10. **The life of a non-Muslim living under a Muslim covenant was declared inviolable.** The warning is among the strongest eschatological threats in the hadith literature.

   > “Whoever kills a person under covenant will not smell the fragrance of Paradise.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 3166](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3166)

11. **He refused to kill the leader of Medina's internal opposition for reputation or convenience.** Abdullah ibn Ubayy repeatedly caused political harm. When Umar asked permission to kill him, Muhammad ﷺ refused, explicitly considering the destructive public meaning such an action would carry.

   > “Leave him, lest people say that Muhammad kills his companions.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 4905](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:4905)

12. **He could release a dangerous captive without ransom.** Thumama ibn Uthal was a powerful hostile chief. After days of detention in Medina, Muhammad ﷺ simply ordered him freed. Thumama then chose Islam and described how his hatred had turned into love.

   > “Release Thumama.” ... “There was no face more hateful to me than your face; now your face has become the dearest of faces to me.”  
   > [Sahih Muslim 1764a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:1764a)

13. **The Qur'an denies inherited moral guilt.** No person carries another's sin merely because of family, tribe, or collective identity.

   > “No bearer of burdens will bear the burden of another.”  
   > [Qur'an 6:164](https://quran.com/6/164)

14. **It commands especially careful argument with Jews and Christians.** The verse acknowledges common ground while still preserving disagreement.

   > “Do not argue with the People of the Book except in the best manner... Say: We believe in what was revealed to us and what was revealed to you.”  
   > [Qur'an 29:46](https://quran.com/29/46)

15. **Muhammad ﷺ was not told to force belief even when unbelief caused him grief.** The Qur'an repeatedly separates his responsibility to convey from God's authority over hearts.

   > “Had your Lord willed, everyone on earth would have believed. Will you then compel people until they become believers?”  
   > [Qur'an 10:99](https://quran.com/10/99)

## VII. Restraint in conflict, war, and victory

War is part of Muhammad's ﷺ biography, and a serious account should not hide it. After the Muslim community fled persecution in Mecca and became a political community in Medina, it fought armed opponents, made treaties, besieged and was besieged, captured prisoners, and eventually conquered Mecca. What is morally important is not to pretend this did not happen, but to examine **the rules he gave violence, the people he excluded from it, the exits he left open, and what he did when he could punish an enemy.**

### War was bounded, not romanticized

1. **In the traditional Muslim reading, the Qur'an's earliest permission to fight is expressed in the language of having been wronged and driven from home.** The permission is not introduced as a celebration of conquest.

   > “Permission to fight is given to those who are fought because they have been wronged... those who were driven from their homes without right only for saying, ‘Our Lord is God.’”  
   > [Qur'an 22:39–40](https://quran.com/22/39-40)

2. **Its basic combat command includes an explicit prohibition on transgression.** Fighting an enemy did not make every act against that enemy permissible.

   > “Fight in God's cause those who fight you, but do not transgress. God does not love transgressors.”  
   > [Qur'an 2:190](https://quran.com/2/190)

3. **Muhammad ﷺ told soldiers not to desire battle.** Courage, when battle becomes unavoidable, is paired with a prior prayer for safety.

   > “Do not wish to meet the enemy; ask God for safety. But if you meet them, then be steadfast.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 3024–3025](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3024)

4. **The Qur'an requires taking a genuine opening for peace.** Peace is not treated as shameful merely because the opposing side initiates it.

   > “If they incline toward peace, then incline toward it, and put your trust in God.”  
   > [Qur'an 8:61](https://quran.com/8/61)

5. **If hostile people withdrew from fighting and offered peace, the Qur'an said there was no basis to attack them.** The rule ties armed action to an active state of hostility.

   > “If they withdraw from you, do not fight you, and offer you peace, then God gives you no way against them.”  
   > [Qur'an 4:90](https://quran.com/4/90)

6. **He explicitly forbade killing women and children.** After a woman was found dead in one military expedition, Muhammad ﷺ condemned the killing of women and children.

   > “The Messenger of God forbade the killing of women and children.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 3015](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3015)

7. **His instructions to commanders prohibited treachery and mutilation as well as killing children.** These were rules given before entering enemy territory, when discipline mattered most.

   > “Do not steal from the spoils, do not betray, do not mutilate, and do not kill a child.”  
   > [Sahih Muslim 1731a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:1731a)

8. **A report about a slain woman adds the principle that she was not a combatant.** Muhammad ﷺ then ordered that women and hired servants not be killed.

   > “She was not one who would have fought.” ... “Do not kill a woman or a hired servant.”  
   > [Sunan Abi Dawud 2669, graded hasan sahih](https://sunnah.com/abudawud:2669)

9. **He prohibited execution by burning.** He initially ordered two enemies burned, then reversed the order before it was carried out, saying punishment by fire belongs to God alone.

   > “If you capture them, kill them, but do not burn them, for no one should punish with fire except God.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 3016](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3016)

10. **The Qur'an makes care for captives part of righteousness.** It praises people who give food they themselves want to the poor, orphan, and captive.

   > “They give food, despite loving it, to the poor, the orphan, and the captive.”  
   > [Qur'an 76:8](https://quran.com/76/8)

11. **The Qur'an explicitly names gracious release or ransom as treatments of prisoners once battlefield control has been established.** This is the direction stated in the verse itself after its command about capture during fighting.

   > “Then afterward either release them graciously or for ransom, until the war lays down its burdens.”  
   > [Qur'an 47:4](https://quran.com/47/4)

12. **He treated covenant-breaking as a moral vice even in war.** His command to armies placed betrayal beside mutilation and killing children among the forbidden acts.

   > “Fight in the name of God... do not betray, do not act treacherously, do not mutilate.”  
   > [Sahih Muslim 1731a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:1731a)

13. **A protected non-Muslim's life remained sacred during Muslim political rule.** The severe warning against killing a person under covenant prevents “enemy religion” from becoming an unlimited category.

   > “Whoever kills a person under covenant will not smell the fragrance of Paradise.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 3166](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3166)

14. **The Qur'an states that even retaliation must remain proportionate, while patience and restraint are better.** It gives the injured party a right without making escalation a virtue.

   > “If you retaliate, retaliate only to the extent that you were harmed. But if you are patient, that is better for the patient.”  
   > [Qur'an 16:126](https://quran.com/16/126)

15. **It goes further by presenting forgiveness and reconciliation as a morally rewarded response to injury.** Justice and mercy are not collapsed into one another, but forgiveness is given special value.

   > “The recompense of an evil is an evil like it; but whoever forgives and reconciles, his reward is with God.”  
   > [Qur'an 42:40](https://quran.com/42/40)

### What he did when an enemy was in his power

16. **He refused vengeance against Ta'if at the moment of rejection.** The city had humiliated him when his movement was politically weak. In the report, his hope was that later generations would choose faith, not that the present generation be crushed.

   > “I hope that God will bring from their descendants people who worship God alone.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 3231](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3231)

17. **He released Thumama, an important enemy captive, without demanding the wealth Thumama offered.** The change in Thumama came after the order to release him, not as a condition for release.

   > “The Messenger of God said, ‘Release Thumama.’”  
   > [Sahih Muslim 1764a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:1764a)

18. **He spared the armed men captured after an attempted attack near Mecca.** The report explicitly connects this restraint with the Qur'anic memory that God held both sides back in the sacred valley.

   > “Eighty men from the people of Mecca came down upon the Messenger of God armed... He captured them and spared them.”  
   > [Sahih Muslim 1808](https://sunnah.com/muslim:1808)

19. **He spared the man who threatened him with a sword while he slept.** Once the weapon changed hands, the moment of danger did not become a pretext for personal punishment.

   > “Then he did not punish him.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 2910](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2910)

20. **He refused to have a corrosive internal critic killed.** Abdullah ibn Ubayy's conduct gave Muhammad ﷺ political reasons to be angry, but the Prophet considered the damage of becoming a ruler known for killing those counted among his followers.

   > “Leave him, lest people say Muhammad kills his companions.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 4905](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:4905)

21. **His wife said personal revenge simply was not his pattern.** This matters because it provides a general rule behind several individual episodes.

   > “He never took revenge for himself.”  
   > [Sahih Muslim 2328a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2328a)

22. **At Hudaybiyya he said explicitly that the Muslims had not come to fight.** He accepted postponement of the pilgrimage and difficult treaty terms in order to secure a truce.

   > “We have not come to fight anyone. We have only come to perform the pilgrimage.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 2731–2732](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2731)

23. **He accepted wording that denied him a cherished title rather than let a title destroy a peace agreement.** The text could say “Muhammad son of Abdullah” instead of “Muhammad, Messenger of God”; his conviction about being a prophet did not depend on winning the diplomatic phrasing.

   > “By God, I am God's Messenger even if you deny me... Write: Muhammad son of Abdullah.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 2731–2732](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2731)

24. **When he later entered Mecca with decisive power, civilians were given simple ways to be safe.** Remaining inside one's own home was enough.

   > “Whoever closes his door is safe.”  
   > [Sahih Muslim 1780c](https://sunnah.com/muslim:1780c)

25. **The Qur'an tells Muslims to protect houses of worship, not only mosques, when describing the moral purpose of checking aggression.** Monasteries, churches, synagogues, and mosques all appear in the verse.

   > “Had God not checked some people by means of others, monasteries, churches, synagogues, and mosques in which God's name is much mentioned would have been destroyed.”  
   > [Qur'an 22:40](https://quran.com/22/40)

26. **After the battle of Hunayn, he delayed distributing the captured people and property while waiting for the defeated Hawazin to come.** When their delegation arrived and asked for their families, he offered a choice and supported returning the captives; the Muslim army ultimately agreed to return them.

   > “Your brothers have come to you repentant, and I consider that their captives should be returned to them.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 4318–4319](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:4318)

## VIII. Mercy extended beyond the human community

### Animals were morally considerable creatures

1. **He taught that kindness to an animal can be a reason for divine forgiveness.** A man climbed into a well for a thirsty dog and gave it water. The companions then asked the natural question: can service to animals really carry reward?

   > “There is a reward for serving every living creature.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 2363](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2363)

2. **The same moral principle was told through a woman whose society would have despised her.** A prostitute saw a dog nearly dying of thirst, drew water with her shoe, and was forgiven. Mercy reaches the animal and reopens hope for the human being.

   > “She gave it water, and because of that she was forgiven.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 3321](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3321)

3. **Cruel neglect of an animal was treated as a serious sin.** Muhammad ﷺ told of a woman punished for confining a cat without feeding it or allowing it to find food.

   > “She neither fed it nor gave it water when she confined it, nor did she let it eat from the creatures of the earth.”  
   > [Sahih Muslim 2242a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2242a)

4. **He intervened when a camel was being underfed and overworked.** The report describes the animal making distressed sounds; Muhammad ﷺ stroked it, found its owner, and turned the moment into an ethical warning.

   > “Do you not fear God concerning this animal He has placed in your possession? It complained that you keep it hungry and exhaust it with work.”  
   > [Sunan Abi Dawud 2549](https://sunnah.com/abudawud:2549)

5. **He ordered companions to return a bird's chicks after the mother became distressed.** What might look like a child's small act of collecting birds became an issue of causing a parent animal grief.

   > “Who has distressed this bird over her young? Return her young to her.”  
   > [Sunan Abi Dawud 2675, graded sahih](https://sunnah.com/abudawud:2675)

6. **He objected when companions burned an ant colony.** His response again limited punishment by fire.

   > “It is not proper for anyone to punish with fire except the Lord of fire.”  
   > [Sunan Abi Dawud 2675, graded sahih](https://sunnah.com/abudawud:2675)

7. **He forbade using restrained animals as targets.** Recreation was not a justification for causing a living creature to suffer.

   > “The Prophet forbade that animals be kept tied up and used as targets.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 5513](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5513)

8. **He forbade striking an animal in the face.** Even when people used animals for transport and labor, there were bodily limits on how they could be treated.

   > “The Messenger of God forbade striking on the face.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 5541](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5541)

9. **Even lawful slaughter was governed by a command to minimize suffering.** The animal was not morally irrelevant merely because people were permitted to eat it.

   > “God has prescribed excellence in all things... When you slaughter, slaughter well. Let one of you sharpen his blade and spare the animal suffering.”  
   > [Sahih Muslim 1955a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:1955a)

10. **He condemned mutilation.** The prohibition applied to living creatures used as targets and sits within a broader ethic against disfigurement even during war.

   > “The Prophet cursed the one who mutilates an animal.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 5515](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5515)

### Shared spaces, cultivation, and public welfare

11. **Planting food that one never personally consumes still counts as charity.** The beneficiary may be a human being, bird, or animal. The ethic rewards creating life-sustaining benefit without knowing who will receive it.

   > “Whatever is eaten from it by a person, bird, or animal is charity for him.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 2320](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2320)

12. **He made removal of public hazards an act of faith and charity.** A road is morally shared space; leaving danger for strangers is not ethically neutral.

   > “Removing something harmful from the road is charity.”  
   > [Sahih Muslim 1009](https://sunnah.com/muslim:1009)

13. **He gave a memorable public-health rule for epidemics.** During plague, people were told neither to enter an affected land nor flee from it once there, limiting movement across the outbreak boundary.

   > “If you hear of plague in a land, do not enter it; if it occurs in a land while you are there, do not leave fleeing from it.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 5728](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5728)

14. **He prohibited contaminating stagnant shared water with urine.** Ritual cleanliness and public hygiene overlap in a very practical rule.

   > “None of you should urinate in stagnant water that does not flow and then bathe in it.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 239](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:239)

15. **The Qur'an teaches enjoyment without waste.** Food and drink are permitted, but excess is not treated as harmless simply because one can afford it.

   > “Eat and drink, but do not be wasteful. He does not love the wasteful.”  
   > [Qur'an 7:31](https://quran.com/7/31)

16. **Muhammad ﷺ taught that the earth itself can be a place of worship rather than sacredness being confined to elite buildings.** The saying helped make worship portable and ordinary.

   > “The earth has been made for me a place of prayer and a means of purification.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 335](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:335)

## IX. What the people with the closest access to him said

No witness is neutral simply because he or she knew Muhammad ﷺ. His followers believed in him. But close witnesses are still relevant to a question about **private consistency**: spouses saw the home, servants saw irritation and fatigue, companions saw danger and scarcity, enemies saw him before becoming followers, and political opponents had incentives to notice defects. Their testimony is worth reading in that light.

### Those who knew the private man

1. **Khadija's first reaction was confidence in his moral character, not surprise at a newly invented persona.** Before there was an Islamic state or a large Muslim community, she grounded her confidence in the way he already treated people.

   > “God will never disgrace you. You maintain family ties, speak truthfully, support the needy, honor the guest, and help in calamity.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 3](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3)

2. **Aisha's summary of his character was that it embodied the scripture he preached.** She also supplied some of the most candid details about his home, grief, choices, and disagreements.

   > “His character was the Qur'an.”  
   > [Sahih Muslim 746a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:746a)

3. **Anas had ten years in which to observe how Muhammad ﷺ treated someone in a subordinate role.** What stayed with him was the absence of contempt and fault-finding.

   > “I served him for ten years, and he never said to me, ‘Uff.’”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 6038](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6038)

4. **Anas's wider verdict joined goodness, generosity, and courage.** These traits were based on the accumulated memory of life with him, including the night Muhammad ﷺ rode alone toward a possible threat to Medina.

   > “He was the best of people, the most generous of people, and the bravest of people.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 2820](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2820)

### People who had once opposed him

5. **Abu Sufyan, while still an enemy, admitted that Muhammad ﷺ had not been known as a liar or covenant-breaker.** He later said that he looked for something negative to tell Heraclius and could find no opening except uncertainty about what might happen during the current truce.

   > “Have you ever accused him of lying?” “No.” ... “Does he break his covenants?” “No.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 7](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:7)

6. **Thumama's testimony is especially direct because the report shows a before and after.** He entered Medina as a hostile captive. Muhammad ﷺ released him, and only then did Thumama announce that the man, faith, and city he had hated most had become dearest to him.

   > “There was no face on earth more hateful to me than your face; now your face has become the dearest of all faces to me.”  
   > [Sahih Muslim 1764a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:1764a)

7. **Amr ibn al-As described an even longer moral reversal.** He said there had been a stage when no one was more hateful to him than Muhammad ﷺ, but after accepting Islam no one became dearer or more honored in his eyes.

   > “Then no one was dearer to me than the Messenger of God, and none was more exalted in my eyes.”  
   > [Sahih Muslim 121](https://sunnah.com/muslim:121)

### The standard he wanted to leave behind

8. **The Qur'an describes Muhammad ﷺ as deeply affected by other people's suffering.** Concern for his followers is not merely inferred from affectionate reports; it is written into the Qur'an's description of him.

   > “A messenger has come to you from among yourselves. Your suffering weighs heavily upon him; he is deeply concerned for you, and toward the believers he is compassionate and merciful.”  
   > [Qur'an 9:128](https://quran.com/9/128)

9. **The Qur'an also restrains his grief over people who reject the message.** The language suggests a man who could exhaust himself with concern, while reminding him that another person's belief cannot be forced.

   > “Perhaps you would consume yourself with grief because they do not believe in this message.”  
   > [Qur'an 18:6](https://quran.com/18/6)

10. **He repeatedly made good character the measure of excellence among his own followers.** This matters when trying to understand what he thought his mission should produce in actual human beings.

   > “The best among you are those who have the best character.”  
   > [Sahih al-Bukhari 6029](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6029)

11. **He taught that good character is among the weightiest things a person can bring before God.** Religious seriousness was therefore inseparable from the way one affects other people.

   > “Nothing is heavier on the believer's scale on the Day of Resurrection than good character.”  
   > [Jamiʿ al-Tirmidhi 2002, graded sahih](https://sunnah.com/tirmidhi:2002)

12. **A well-known early formulation says that moral refinement was itself part of why he was sent.** The wording is transmitted in early hadith collections, including al-Adab al-Mufrad and the Muwatta.

   > “I was sent to complete good character.”  
   > [Al-Adab al-Mufrad 273](https://sunnah.com/urn/2302710)

13. **The Qur'an's own broad description of his mission is mercy.** That claim is not adequately assessed by one isolated story. It is the pattern across the material above: the grieving child, the servant of ten years, the debtor, the widow, the racial insult, the worker's wage, the hostile tribe, the protected non-Muslim, the defeated enemy, the captive, the thirsty dog, and the overworked camel.

   > “We have sent you only as a mercy to all worlds.”  
   > [Qur'an 21:107](https://quran.com/21/107)

14. **He expressed affection for believers who would be born after his death.** At a graveyard he told the companions that he longed to see his “brothers.” When they asked whether they were not his brothers, he called them his companions and explained that he meant believers who had not yet come.

   > “I would love to see my brothers... They are those who have not yet come.”  
   > [Sahih Muslim 249a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:249a)

15. **A report in Sahih Muslim depicts him weeping in concern for his community.** After reciting prayers of Abraham and Jesus for their peoples, Muhammad ﷺ raised his hands and repeated his concern for his own followers.

   > “O my Lord, my community, my community,” and he wept.  
   > [Sahih Muslim 202](https://sunnah.com/muslim:202)

16. **He linked closeness to him in the afterlife with character rather than worldly prominence.** The people he described as dearest and nearest to him were those with the best conduct.

   > “The most beloved of you to me and nearest to me on the Day of Resurrection are those best in character.”  
   > [Jamiʿ al-Tirmidhi 2018, graded hasan](https://sunnah.com/tirmidhi:2018)

## Conclusion: why these details matter together

It is easy to admire a moral sentence in isolation. The more demanding question is whether a person's ethics survive **proximity, inconvenience, loss, insult, fear, money, and power**.

The sources above describe Muhammad ﷺ comforting children and shortening prayer for their mothers; serving his household and refusing to hit a woman or servant; standing for justice even against family prestige; ordering leniency for a debtor while condemning deliberate nonpayment; rejecting racist contempt while elevating formerly enslaved people; visiting and protecting non-Muslims; forbidding the killing of noncombatants and accepting costly peace; sparing men who had threatened or attacked him; noticing an unnamed mosque cleaner; stopping cruelty to birds and working animals; and dying with his armor pledged for food rather than surrounded by accumulated private wealth.

No single incident proves every religious claim Muslims make about him. That is not what a character study can do. But **the cumulative pattern is difficult to reduce to a slogan**. The man remembered by his family, servants, followers, former enemies, and scripture was not merely teaching people to perform religious rites. He was trying to form people who were truthful without cruelty, strong without arrogance, devout without extremism, just without favoritism, merciful without moral indifference, and conscious that the vulnerable person in front of them had a claim upon them.

For a reader meeting Muhammad ﷺ for the first time, that is the most useful place to begin: not with an idealized portrait detached from evidence, but with the repeated small decisions by which a human being's character becomes visible.
