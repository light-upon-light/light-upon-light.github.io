# Muhammad ﷺ: His Teachings, Character, Life, and the Case for His Prophethood

Many people first encounter Prophet Muhammad ﷺ as a name, a historical founder, or the subject of a debate. The Islamic sources preserve a much fuller picture. They describe a man who worshipped through the night and helped at home; who led a state yet died with his armor held as security for food; who wept over his child, played with his family, forgave personal injuries, restrained soldiers, protected treaty partners, listened to women and children, and repeatedly directed attention away from himself and toward God.[^2][^3][^12][^13][^36]

This article has two related but distinct aims: to examine **what Muhammad ﷺ actually taught and did**, and to consider **why his claim to prophethood deserves to be taken seriously**. A beautiful teaching does not by itself prove revelation, and a report of a miracle does not authenticate itself. Section IV therefore considers the case for prophethood cumulatively while distinguishing between different kinds of evidence.

At the center of that claim is the Qur’an, which Muhammad ﷺ recited publicly in portions over roughly twenty-three years. It describes him as unlettered, and his first audience had already watched him live an ordinary adult life among them. Whatever conclusion one reaches, **the text and the life have to be considered together**.[^1][^35][^36][^39][^40]

For someone claiming prophethood, **his life is itself relevant evidence**. Muhammad ﷺ had lived openly among his first audience for decades before revelation. They then watched him through years of weakness and persecution. His wives and household saw how he behaved in private, and later the same man acquired political and military power. Any explanation of his claim has to make sense of this entire course of life. The man known for truthfulness before the claim did not become a habitual liar after it. The teacher of mercy while weak continued to restrain retaliation when strong. The leader who demanded justice refused special treatment for his own family, while the head of state continued to serve at home and died without accumulating a private fortune.[^8][^27][^36][^37][^40][^41]

No single anecdote proves prophethood, and particular historical controversies should be examined on their own evidence. At the same time, one disputed episode should not be allowed to erase the wider record. **What matters is the coherence of the whole portrait across time, circumstance, public teaching, and private conduct.**

### A short note on sources

- **The Qur’an** is the primary text Muhammad ﷺ publicly recited as revelation.[^1][^35]
- **Hadith** are transmitted reports of his words, actions, and approvals. This article gives priority to Sahih al-Bukhari and Sahih Muslim, the two most highly regarded Sunni hadith collections. Other collections are identified explicitly when used.[^2][^3][^21][^22][^42][^43]
- **Early biography, often called *sira*,** preserves important narrative material that is not always found in the major hadith collections. Its reports vary in the strength of their transmission, so biographical material is identified as such rather than treated as equivalent to a rigorously authenticated hadith.[^8][^18][^27][^28][^36][^37][^38]
- Quotations from the Qur’an and hadith below are **close English renderings prepared for this article**. They are kept brief to make the context and main point easy to follow. The linked sources provide the fuller passages.
- **Admiration does not require anachronism.** Muhammad ﷺ lived in seventh-century Arabia, amid warfare, slavery, patriarchy, tribal retaliation, poverty, and limited state institutions. The useful historical question is what he did within that world: what he restrained, what he reformed, and what moral direction his teachings established.[^36][^39][^40][^41][^53][^54][^55]
- The symbol **ﷺ** means “may God’s peace and blessings be upon him.”

## I. The moral and spiritual vision he taught

### A. The poor, the vulnerable, neighbors, servants, and social dignity

1. **Care for the vulnerable is part of righteousness.** The Qur’an does not reduce goodness to ritual. It joins faith to the willingness to give cherished wealth to relatives, orphans, the poor, stranded travelers, those who ask for help, and people seeking freedom from bondage.[^55][^60]

   > “Righteousness is... to give wealth, despite loving it, to relatives, orphans, the needy, the traveler, those who ask, and for freeing people from bondage.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 2:177](https://quran.com/2/177)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

2. **Indifference to orphans and the hungry contradicts religion itself.**

   > “Have you seen the one who denies the Judgment? That is the one who pushes away the orphan and does not urge the feeding of the poor.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 107:1–3](https://quran.com/107/1-3)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

3. **Freeing an enslaved person and feeding people in a time of hunger are among the difficult forms of goodness the Qur’an praises.**[^53][^55]

   > “What will make you understand the steep path? It is freeing a person from bondage, or feeding on a day of hunger an orphaned relative or a poor person in misery.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 90:12–16](https://quran.com/90/12-16)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

4. **Caring for widows and the poor was given extraordinary spiritual value.**

   > “The one who works for the widow and the poor is like one striving in God’s cause, or like one who prays through the night and fasts through the day.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 5353](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5353)[^2][^12][^42]

5. **Caring for an orphan was described as a path to being near him in Paradise.**

   > “I and the caretaker of an orphan will be in Paradise like this,” and he brought his index and middle fingers together.
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 6005](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6005)[^2][^12][^42]

6. **Relieving another person’s hardship carries spiritual weight.** The same teaching joins practical acts to divine reward: lifting distress, easing a debtor’s difficulty, and protecting another person’s dignity.

   > “Whoever relieves a believer of a worldly hardship, God will relieve him of a hardship on the Day of Resurrection.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih Muslim 2699a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2699a)[^3][^13][^42]

7. **A debtor in genuine hardship must be given time.** Better still, the Qur’an encourages the creditor to forgive some or all of the debt as charity.

   > “If the debtor is in difficulty, then grant time until ease; and if you remit it as charity, that is better for you.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 2:280](https://quran.com/2/280)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

8. **A neighbor’s safety was made a matter of faith.**

   > “Whoever believes in God and the Last Day must not harm his neighbor.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 6018](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6018)[^2][^12][^42]

9. **The duty toward neighbors was emphasized with unusual force.** Gabriel spoke about it so often that Muhammad ﷺ thought neighbors might eventually be given inheritance rights.

   > “Gabriel kept instructing me concerning the neighbor until I thought he would make the neighbor an heir.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 6014](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6014)[^2][^12][^42]

10. **Hospitality was a religious duty, not merely a social courtesy.** In one short teaching, Muhammad ﷺ placed honoring guests beside protecting neighbors and disciplining one’s speech.

   > “Whoever believes in God and the Last Day should honor his guest.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 6018](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6018)[^2][^12][^42]

11. **Kindness was not to be reserved for one’s own circle.** He encouraged people to feed others and greet both those they knew and those they did not.

   > A man asked, “What part of Islam is best?” He said: “Feed people, and greet those you know and those you do not know.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 12](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:12)[^2][^12][^42]

12. **Believers were to feel one another’s suffering as members of a single body.**

   > “The believers in their mutual affection, mercy, and compassion are like one body: when one limb suffers, the whole body responds with sleeplessness and fever.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 6011](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6011)[^2][^12][^42]

13. **He confronted ethnic contempt even within his own community.** When Abu Dharr insulted another man through his mother, Muhammad ﷺ did not dismiss it as ordinary speech. He told Abu Dharr that this attitude was a remnant of the pre-Islamic age of ignorance.

   > “You are a man in whom there remains ignorance.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 30](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:30)[^2][^12][^42]

14. **People under one’s authority retained basic human dignity.** In the same rebuke to Abu Dharr, Muhammad ﷺ required masters to give enslaved people the same standard of food and clothing they gave themselves and prohibited overwhelming them with work.

   > “Your servants are your brothers whom God placed under your authority. Feed them from what you eat and clothe them from what you wear.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 30](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:30)[^2][^12][^42]

15. **If the work given to a servant was too heavy, the master was required to help.**

   > “Do not burden them with what overwhelms them; and if you burden them, then help them.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 30](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:30)[^2][^12][^42]

16. **Emancipation was made both a major good deed and a form of atonement.** The Qur’an repeatedly prescribes freeing enslaved people as a way to repair serious wrongs, while Muhammad ﷺ explicitly praised setting people free.

   > “Whoever frees a Muslim slave, God will save from the Fire a limb of his for every limb he freed.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 2517](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2517)[^2][^12][^42]

17. **Human beings share one origin, and inherited racial or tribal status does not determine worth before God.**

   > “O humanity, We created you from a male and a female and made you peoples and tribes so that you may know one another. The most honored before God is the most God-conscious.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 49:13](https://quran.com/49/13)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

18. **Human dignity comes before communal divisions.** The Qur’an describes God as having honored the children of Adam as such.

   > “We have certainly honored the children of Adam.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 17:70](https://quran.com/17/70)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

19. **Generosity was placed within everyone’s reach, including the poor.** Half a date, a good word, removing an obstacle, or helping another person can all carry moral value. Charity was not made the privilege of people with money.

   > “Do not belittle any good deed, even meeting your brother with a cheerful face.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih Muslim 2626](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2626)[^3][^13][^42]

20. **Giving in charity does not ultimately impoverish the giver.**

   > “Charity does not diminish wealth.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih Muslim 2588](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2588)[^3][^13][^42]

21. **The preservation of human life is given immense moral weight.** In recounting a command given to the Children of Israel, the Qur’an compares the unjust killing of one person to killing all humanity, and saving one life to saving all humanity. The verse has a specific historical context, but its valuation of human life is unmistakable.

   > “Whoever saves a life, it is as though he saved all humanity.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 5:32](https://quran.com/5/32)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

22. **Appearance and wealth are not what God ultimately evaluates.** Human worth is therefore not grounded in beauty, class display, or inherited social position, but in the heart and in conduct.

   > “God does not look at your faces and your wealth, but He looks at your hearts and your deeds.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih Muslim 2564c](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2564c)[^3][^13][^42]

23. **He entrusted leadership to people whose social background others looked down on.** Zayd ibn Haritha had formerly been enslaved and was freed by Muhammad ﷺ, who called him “our brother and our freedman” and appointed him to command. He later appointed Zayd’s son Usama. When people objected to Usama’s leadership, Muhammad ﷺ defended both father and son as fully worthy of command.

   > “If you criticize his leadership, you used to criticize his father’s leadership before. By God, he was worthy of leadership.”
   >
   > Sources: [Sahih al-Bukhari 3729–3730](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3729)[^2][^12][^42]; [Sahih al-Bukhari 4469](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:4469)[^2][^12][^42]

24. **Social responsibility was expressed in practical acts.** Care for the vulnerable was not left as a sentiment. Muhammad ﷺ told his followers plainly: free the captive, feed the hungry, and visit the sick.

   > “Free the captive, feed the hungry, and visit the sick.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 3046](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3046)[^2][^12][^42]

25. **Lineage could not compensate for a failure of character and action.** In a strongly tribal society, Muhammad ﷺ closed a teaching about mercy, mutual aid, and knowledge with a deliberately leveling principle.

   > “Whoever is slowed by his deeds will not be accelerated by his lineage.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih Muslim 2699a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2699a)[^3][^13][^42]

26. **Leniency toward debtors became a reason to hope for God’s own mercy.** Muhammad ﷺ told of a creditor whom God forgave because he had been gentle with people in financial difficulty.

   > “Give time to one in ease, and overlook one in hardship.” Then God overlooked him.
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 2077](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2077)[^2][^12][^42]

27. **Providing for one’s own family counts as charity when done sincerely.** Public generosity was not meant to come at the expense of obligations at home.

   > “When a Muslim spends on his family seeking reward, it is charity for him.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 5351](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5351)[^2][^12][^42]

28. **He preferred that a person leave heirs financially secure rather than deliberately dependent on others.** Poverty was not romanticized for its own sake.

   > “To leave your heirs well provided for is better than leaving them poor, begging from people.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 5354](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5354)[^2][^12][^42]

29. **Wrongfully striking an enslaved person could require setting that person free.** Although slavery still existed, legal authority over another human being did not create an unrestricted right to violence.

   > “Whoever slaps or beats his slave, the expiation for it is to set him free.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih Muslim 1657b](https://sunnah.com/muslim:1657b)[^3][^13][^42]

30. **Selling a free human being into slavery was condemned in the strongest terms.** In a hadith in which Muhammad ﷺ reports words from God, this is one of the abuses for which God Himself will be the offender’s adversary on the Day of Judgment.

   > “I will be the opponent of... a man who sells a free person and consumes the price.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 2270](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2270)[^2][^12][^42]

31. **People placed low in the old social hierarchy could receive the highest religious honor.** Bilal, a formerly enslaved Abyssinian Muslim, became the best-known caller to prayer in Muhammad’s ﷺ community. Muhammad ﷺ also told Bilal that he had heard his footsteps ahead of him in Paradise.

   > “Tell me of the deed you have done in Islam for which you most hope, for I heard your footsteps before me in Paradise.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 1149](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:1149)[^2][^12][^42]

32. **Poverty could never make a child’s life expendable.** The Qur’an expressly prohibited killing children out of fear over provision.

   > “Do not kill your children because of poverty. We provide for you and for them.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 6:151](https://quran.com/6/151)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

33. **An orphan’s property had to be protected as carefully as the orphan’s physical needs.** The Qur’an treats adults consuming an orphan’s wealth unjustly as a grave moral offense.

   > “Those who consume the property of orphans unjustly are only consuming fire into their bellies.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 4:10](https://quran.com/4/10)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

34. **Charity could not be used to humiliate its recipient.** The Qur’an says that a kind word and forgiveness are better than a gift followed by hurt or reminders of the giver’s generosity.

   > “A kind word and forgiveness are better than charity followed by hurt.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 2:263](https://quran.com/2/263)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

35. **A person without money could still contribute.** When his companions asked what someone with no material means could give, Muhammad ﷺ named working, helping a person in distress, encouraging good, and finally simply refraining from harming others.

   > “Let him help one in desperate need... and if he does not, let him refrain from evil; that is charity for him.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 6022](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6022)[^2][^12][^42]

36. **He challenged the assumption that social success made a person superior to the poor.** When Sa‘d ibn Abi Waqqas thought his status distinguished him from those beneath him, Muhammad ﷺ redirected his attention to the weak members of the community.

   > “Are you given victory and provision except through the weak among you?”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 2896](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2896)[^2][^12][^42]

37. **Mercy toward children was joined to respect for older people.** One age group was not to be honored by neglecting another.

   > “He is not of us who does not show mercy to our young and recognize the right of our elders.”
   >
   > Source: [Jami at-Tirmidhi 1920](https://sunnah.com/tirmidhi:1920)[^5][^15][^42]

38. **He praised charity given in private.** Public giving can encourage others, but concealed giving can better protect both the giver’s sincerity and the recipient’s dignity.

   > “If you give charity openly, that is good; but if you conceal it and give it to the poor, that is better for you.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 2:271](https://quran.com/2/271)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

39. **The Qur’an created a contractual route out of slavery and told owners to help finance it.** Slavery was not abolished in a single decree, but the law did more than regulate bondage: it also established routes by which enslaved people could become free.[^53][^55]

   > “If those under your authority seek a contract for freedom, make such a contract if you know good in them, and give them from God’s wealth.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 24:33](https://quran.com/24/33)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

40. **The same verse forbade forcing enslaved women into prostitution for profit.** Legal power over another person did not make sexual coercion for commercial gain acceptable.

   > “Do not compel your slave women into prostitution, if they desire chastity, seeking the temporary gains of worldly life.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 24:33](https://quran.com/24/33)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

41. **Social duty is placed immediately beside the worship of God.** In a single verse, the Qur’an names parents, relatives, orphans, the poor, near and distant neighbors, companions, travelers, and people under one’s authority within the same moral obligation.

   > “Worship God alone... and be good to parents, relatives, orphans, the needy, the near neighbor, the distant neighbor, the companion, the traveler, and those under your authority.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 4:36](https://quran.com/4/36)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

### B. Non-Muslims, religious difference, justice, peace, and war

1. **Hatred did not excuse injustice.** The Qur’an explicitly forbids hostility toward a people from distorting one’s judgment of what is just, placing a clear restraint on tribal and political bias.

   > “Do not let the hatred of a people cause you to depart from justice. Be just; that is nearer to God-consciousness.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 5:8](https://quran.com/5/8)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

2. **Justice was required even against oneself or one’s own family.**

   > “Stand firmly for justice as witnesses for God, even against yourselves, parents, or close relatives.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 4:135](https://quran.com/4/135)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

3. **He rejected special treatment for the powerful.** When people sought leniency for a woman from an influential family in a theft case, Muhammad ﷺ said earlier communities had been ruined by punishing the weak while sparing the powerful. He then invoked his own daughter to make clear that his family was not exempt.

   > “By God, if Fatima daughter of Muhammad stole, I would apply the law to her.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 6788](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6788)[^2][^12][^42]

4. **The Qur’an summarizes public morality in terms of justice, excellence, and generosity, while explicitly forbidding oppression.**

   > “God commands justice, excellence, and giving to relatives, and forbids indecency, wrongdoing, and transgression.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 16:90](https://quran.com/16/90)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

5. **Religious coercion was rejected as a matter of principle.** Faith cannot be made sincere by force.[^56][^59]

   > “There is no compulsion in religion. Right guidance has become distinct from error.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 2:256](https://quran.com/2/256)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

6. **Non-Muslims who were not fighting the Muslim community were to be treated with kindness and justice.**

   > “God does not forbid you from being kind and just toward those who did not fight you because of religion or drive you from your homes.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 60:8](https://quran.com/60/8)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

7. **Non-Muslims under covenant received serious religious protection.** Killing a protected non-Muslim was not treated as a lesser wrong because the victim followed another religion.[^56][^59]

   > “Whoever kills a person under covenant will not smell the fragrance of Paradise.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 3166](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3166)[^2][^12][^42]

8. **He stood for the funeral procession of a Jewish man.** When someone pointed out that the deceased was Jewish, Muhammad ﷺ answered by appealing to the man’s common humanity.

   > He said, “Was it not a soul?”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 1312](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:1312)[^2][^12][^42]

9. **He personally visited a Jewish boy who had served him when the boy became ill.** Muhammad ﷺ invited him to Islam during the visit, but the report also shows that religious difference did not prevent personal care for someone who was sick.

   > “A Jewish boy used to serve the Prophet ﷺ, and he became ill, so the Prophet ﷺ went to visit him.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 1356](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:1356)[^2][^12][^42]

10. **Muslims were forbidden to insult what other people worshipped.** The Qur’an restrains even offensive speech that could provoke retaliatory blasphemy and deepen hostility.

   > “Do not insult those they invoke besides God, lest they insult God in hostility out of ignorance.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 6:108](https://quran.com/6/108)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

11. **Dialogue with Jews and Christians was to be conducted in the best manner.**

   > “Do not argue with the People of the Book except in the best way, except with those of them who act unjustly.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 29:46](https://quran.com/29/46)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

12. **Religious invitation was to proceed through wisdom and good instruction, not abuse.**

   > “Invite to the way of your Lord with wisdom and good instruction, and debate with them in the best manner.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 16:125](https://quran.com/16/125)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

13. **The Qur’an recognizes the value of protecting several kinds of houses of worship.** In explaining why aggression may be resisted, it names monasteries, churches, synagogues, and mosques as places in which God’s name is much mentioned.

   > “Were God not to repel some people by means of others, monasteries, churches, synagogues, and mosques in which God’s name is much mentioned would have been demolished.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 22:40](https://quran.com/22/40)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

14. **Warfare was limited by an explicit prohibition on transgression.**[^57][^58]

   > “Fight in the way of God those who fight you, but do not transgress. God does not love transgressors.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 2:190](https://quran.com/2/190)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

15. **Women and children were not to be intentionally killed in war.** After a woman was found dead during a campaign, Muhammad ﷺ expressly forbade killing women and children.[^57][^58]

   > “The Messenger of God ﷺ forbade the killing of women and children.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 3015](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3015)[^2][^12][^42]

16. **His instructions to military commanders prohibited both treachery and mutilation.**

   > “Do not embezzle, do not act treacherously, do not mutilate, and do not kill a child.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih Muslim 1731a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:1731a)[^3][^13][^42]

17. **When the enemy genuinely inclined toward peace, Muslims were told to do the same.**

   > “If they incline to peace, then incline to it and trust in God.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 8:61](https://quran.com/8/61)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

18. **Feeding a captive is explicitly praised in the Qur’an.** The captive is placed beside the poor and the orphan as someone whose need is to be met.

   > “They give food, despite loving it, to the poor, the orphan, and the captive.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 76:8](https://quran.com/76/8)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

19. **Having the power to retaliate did not make full retaliation the highest moral choice.** The Qur’an repeatedly presents patience and forgiveness as better than taking everything one is legally entitled to take in return.

   > “Whoever is patient and forgives, that is truly among the matters requiring firm resolve.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 42:43](https://quran.com/42/43)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

20. **Cooperation was to be built around what is good, never around wrongdoing or aggression.**

   > “Cooperate with one another in righteousness and God-consciousness, and do not cooperate in sin and aggression.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 5:2](https://quran.com/5/2)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

21. **Religious difference did not cancel the duty to maintain family ties.** Asma bint Abu Bakr asked whether she should receive and care for her polytheist mother. Muhammad ﷺ told her to preserve the relationship rather than use religious difference as a reason to sever it.

   > He said, “Yes. Maintain good relations with your mother.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 2620](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2620)[^2][^12][^42]

22. **He warned his own representative to fear the prayer of anyone he might oppress.** Before sending Muadh to Yemen, Muhammad ﷺ reminded him that public authority carries spiritual danger because the supplication of the wronged person has no barrier before God.

   > “Beware the supplication of the oppressed, for there is no barrier between it and God.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 2448](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2448)[^2][^12][^42]

23. **The prohibition of oppression was made universal.** In a hadith in which Muhammad ﷺ reports words from God, oppression is forbidden among people as such, not merely within a particular tribe, court, or political dispute.

   > “My servants, I have forbidden oppression for Myself and made it forbidden among you, so do not oppress one another.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih Muslim 2577a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2577a)[^3][^13][^42]

24. **He openly acknowledged the limits of human judgment, including his own judgment of a case.** One litigant might present an argument more persuasively than another. A favorable ruling therefore could not make someone else’s property morally lawful to take.

   > “I am only human... perhaps one of you is more eloquent in presenting his case. If I award him his brother’s right, let him not take it.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 7169](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:7169)[^2][^12][^42]

25. **A judge was not to decide a case while angry.** The exercise of legal power required enough emotional self-control to keep rage from shaping a judgment.

   > “No judge should judge between two people while he is angry.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 7158](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:7158)[^2][^12][^42]

26. **Even an enemy polytheist who asked for protection had to be escorted to safety.** The Qur’an allowed the person to hear the Islamic message but still required safe passage afterward.

   > “If any polytheist seeks your protection, grant him protection... then escort him to his place of safety.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 9:6](https://quran.com/9/6)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

27. **He insisted on gentleness even in response to a deliberate insult.** When Jewish visitors used a hostile pun in greeting him and Aisha responded angrily, Muhammad ﷺ restrained her and appealed to the general value of gentleness.

   > “Be calm, Aisha. God loves gentleness in all matters.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 6024](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6024)[^2][^12][^42]

28. **The Qur’an rejects inherited or collective moral guilt.** No one bears another person’s sin merely because of family, tribe, religion, or group identity.

   > “No bearer of burdens will bear the burden of another.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 6:164](https://quran.com/6/164)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

29. **Muhammad ﷺ was not authorized to force belief, even when people’s unbelief grieved him.** The Qur’an repeatedly distinguishes his duty to convey the message from God’s authority over human hearts.

   > “Had your Lord willed, everyone on earth would have believed. Will you then compel people until they become believers?”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 10:99](https://quran.com/10/99)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

30. **In the traditional Muslim reading, the Qur’an’s first permission to fight is framed as a response to persecution.** It speaks of people who had been wronged and driven from their homes, rather than presenting warfare as an end in itself.[^57][^58]

   > “Permission to fight is given to those who are fought because they have been wronged... those who were driven from their homes without right only for saying, ‘Our Lord is God.’”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 22:39–40](https://quran.com/22/39-40)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

31. **He told soldiers not to wish for battle.** Courage when fighting became unavoidable was paired with a prior desire for peace and safety.

   > “Do not wish to meet the enemy; ask God for safety. But if you meet them, then be steadfast.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 3024–3025](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3024)[^2][^12][^42]

32. **Once hostile people withdrew from fighting and offered peace, there was no basis to attack them.** The Qur’an kept armed action tied to an active state of hostility.

   > “If they withdraw from you, do not fight you, and offer you peace, then God gives you no way against them.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 4:90](https://quran.com/4/90)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

33. **The protection of noncombatants is made explicit in a report about a woman found slain.** Muhammad ﷺ observed that she had not been fighting and then ordered that women and hired servants not be killed.[^57][^58]

   > “She was not one who would have fought.” ... “Do not kill a woman or a hired servant.”
   >
   > Source: [Sunan Abi Dawud 2669](https://sunnah.com/abudawud:2669)[^4][^14][^42]

34. **He prohibited killing captives by burning.** After initially ordering that two enemies be burned if captured, Muhammad ﷺ reversed the command before it was carried out and said that punishment by fire belongs to God alone.

   > “If you capture them, kill them, but do not burn them, for no one should punish with fire except God.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 3016](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3016)[^2][^12][^42]

35. **The Qur’an names gracious release and ransom as ways of dealing with prisoners after battlefield control has been established.** Capture was not presented as requiring permanent captivity.

   > “Then afterward either release them graciously or for ransom, until the war lays down its burdens.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 47:4](https://quran.com/47/4)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

36. **Even lawful retaliation had to remain proportionate, while patience was presented as better.** The injured party retained a right to respond without escalation being turned into a virtue.

   > “If you retaliate, retaliate only to the extent that you were harmed. But if you are patient, that is better for the patient.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 16:126](https://quran.com/16/126)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

37. **Treaties with religious opponents had to be honored as long as the other party honored them.** Religious disagreement did not make a solemn agreement disposable when breaking it became convenient.

   > “As for the polytheists with whom you made a treaty and who have not failed you in anything... fulfill your treaty with them to its term.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 9:4](https://quran.com/9/4)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

38. **Keeping covenants was a religious duty, not merely a diplomatic tactic.** The Qur’an places the obligation before God, making fidelity morally binding even when opportunism would be easier.

   > “Fulfill God’s covenant when you have made it, and do not break oaths after confirming them.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 16:91](https://quran.com/16/91)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

39. **Justice could require a ruling against Muslims and in favor of a non-Muslim.** In the traditional account behind Qur’an 4:105–112, members of a Muslim clan stole property and tried to blame a Jewish man. The revealed passage rejected communal favoritism and warned Muhammad ﷺ not to defend the deceitful.

   > “We have sent down the Book to you in truth so that you may judge between people by what God has shown you. Do not be an advocate for the deceitful.”
   >
   > Sources: [Qur’an 4:105–112](https://quran.com/4/105-112)[^1][^10][^11][^35]; *Jami at-Tirmidhi* 3036 (occasion report, graded *hasan*)

40. **Even an accusation that he had favored a relative was preserved in the record.** In an irrigation dispute involving his cousin al-Zubayr, Muhammad ﷺ first gave a conciliatory ruling that still allowed the other landowner to receive water. When the man angrily accused him of favoring his cousin, Muhammad ﷺ gave al-Zubayr his full legal entitlement. The report, including the accusation, was transmitted by al-Zubayr’s own son.

   > Muhammad ﷺ first told al-Zubayr to water and then release the water to his neighbor; after the objection, he ordered him to take his full right.
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 2359–2362](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2359)[^2][^12][^42]

41. **A captive mother was not to be separated from her child.** In a world where captives could be treated as property and families broken apart, the bond between parent and child was given a claim against the stronger party.

   > “Whoever separates a mother from her child, God will separate him from those he loves on the Day of Resurrection.”
   >
   > Source: [Jami at-Tirmidhi 1566](https://sunnah.com/tirmidhi:1566)[^5][^15][^42], graded *hasan*

### C. Money, work, knowledge, leadership, health, and the natural world

1. **Honest work carried its own dignity.** Muhammad ﷺ taught that the best food a person eats is earned through his own labor, and he cited the prophet David as an example.

   > “No one has eaten food better than that earned by the work of his own hands.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 2072](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2072)[^2][^12][^42]

2. **Commercial honesty was tied directly to blessing.** Concealing defects and lying in a transaction could strip that blessing away.

   > “If the buyer and seller speak truthfully and disclose defects, their transaction is blessed; if they lie and conceal, its blessing is erased.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 2079](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2079)[^2][^12][^42]

3. **Cheating people through weights and measures is explicitly condemned in the Qur’an.**

   > “Woe to those who give less than due, who demand full measure when receiving from people but give less when measuring or weighing for them.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 83:1–3](https://quran.com/83/1-3)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

4. **Withholding a worker’s earned wages was condemned in exceptionally severe terms.**

   > God says: “I will be the opponent on the Day of Resurrection of... a man who hires a worker, receives the full work from him, then does not pay his wage.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 2270](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2270)[^2][^12][^42]

5. **A wealthy debtor who deliberately delayed payment was committing an injustice.**

   > “Delay in payment by a wealthy person is injustice.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 2287](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2287)[^2][^12][^42]

6. **Leniency in ordinary buying, selling, and collecting debts was treated as a virtue.**

   > “May God have mercy on a person who is easygoing when he sells, when he buys, and when he asks for what is owed.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 2076](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2076)[^2][^12][^42]

7. **The Qur’an forbids both wrongful consumption of wealth and bribery used to obtain what belongs to others.**

   > “Do not consume one another’s wealth unjustly, nor offer it to authorities so that you may knowingly consume a portion of other people’s wealth sinfully.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 2:188](https://quran.com/2/188)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

8. **Support for vulnerable groups was built into public finance.** Qur’an 9:60 assigns mandatory alms to the poor, the needy, administrators, people in bondage, debtors, travelers, and other specified public religious purposes.

   > “Charitable dues are only for the poor, the needy... those in bondage, those in debt... and the stranded traveler.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 9:60](https://quran.com/9/60)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

9. **The pursuit of knowledge was itself a religious good.** The Qur’an even teaches Muhammad ﷺ to pray for an increase in knowledge.

   > “My Lord, increase me in knowledge.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 20:114](https://quran.com/20/114)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

10. **Knowledge appears at the very beginning of the message.** The first revealed command is to read or recite in God’s name, and the opening revelation immediately speaks of teaching and the pen.

   > “Read in the name of your Lord who created... Read, and your Lord is most generous, who taught by the pen, taught the human being what he did not know.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 96:1–5](https://quran.com/96/1-5)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

11. **Seeking knowledge was described as a path toward Paradise.**

   > “Whoever travels a path seeking knowledge, God makes easy for him a path to Paradise.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih Muslim 2699a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2699a)[^3][^13][^42]

12. **Reflection on the natural world was encouraged as part of faith.** The Qur’an repeatedly directs attention to the heavens, the earth, and the patterns within creation.

   > “In the creation of the heavens and the earth and the alternation of night and day are signs for people of understanding.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 3:190](https://quran.com/3/190)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

13. **He distinguished revelation from ordinary human judgment in technical matters.** After commenting on date-palm pollination and seeing that the harvest suffered, Muhammad ﷺ did not protect his prestige by pretending agricultural expertise.

   > “You know better about the technical affairs of your worldly life.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih Muslim 2363](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2363)[^3][^13][^42]

14. **Collective affairs were to be conducted through consultation.**

   > “Their affairs are conducted by mutual consultation.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 42:38](https://quran.com/42/38)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

15. **Muhammad ﷺ himself was commanded to consult his followers.** The command appears in a verse telling him to remain gentle, pardon them, and seek their counsel after Uhud, a painful military defeat outside Medina.

   > “Pardon them, seek forgiveness for them, and consult them in affairs.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 3:159](https://quran.com/3/159)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

   The early biography also records him accepting ideas that were not his own, including Salman al-Farisi’s unfamiliar proposal to defend Medina with a trench. At Hudaybiyyah, the canonical hadith record likewise preserves him acting on Umm Salama’s practical advice when the camp reached an impasse. **Consultation was something he practiced as well as taught.**

   > Sources: Ibn Ishaq/Ibn Hisham for Salman’s trench proposal[^8][^18][^27][^36][^37]; [Sahih al-Bukhari 2731–2732](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2731)[^2][^12][^42] for Umm Salama at Hudaybiyyah

16. **Leadership meant responsibility for others, not ownership of them.**

   > “Each of you is a shepherd, and each of you is responsible for those under his care.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 7138](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:7138)[^2][^12][^42]

17. **No ruler had an unlimited claim to obedience.** Political authority ended where disobedience to God began.

   > “Obedience is only in what is right.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 7257](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:7257)[^2][^12][^42]

18. **Food and consumption were governed by moderation rather than habitual excess.** The Qur’an gives the general rule, while a reported practical teaching advises leaving substantial room rather than routinely eating to complete fullness.

   > “Eat and drink, but do not be wasteful. He does not love the wasteful.”
   >
   > “If more is necessary, then a third for food, a third for drink, and a third for breath.”
   >
   > Sources: [Qur’an 7:31](https://quran.com/7/31)[^1][^10][^11][^35]; [Jami at-Tirmidhi 2380](https://sunnah.com/tirmidhi:2380)[^5][^15][^42]

19. **Planting could become an ongoing act of charity.** The benefit counted even when the food was eaten by an animal rather than a person.[^60][^61]

   > “No Muslim plants a tree or sows a crop from which a person, bird, or animal eats except that it is charity for him.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 2320](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2320)[^2][^12][^42]

20. **Compassion extended even to an animal many people would avoid.** Muhammad ﷺ told of a man whom God forgave after he gave water to a desperately thirsty dog. When asked whether kindness to animals is rewarded, he answered that it is.[^61]

   > “In every living creature there is a reward.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 2363](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2363)[^2][^12][^42]

21. **Cruelty to animals could be a grave sin.** In one of Muhammad’s ﷺ teachings, a woman was condemned for confining a cat until it died without either feeding it or allowing it to find food for itself.[^61]

   > “A woman was punished because of a cat she confined until it died.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 3318](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3318)[^2][^12][^42]

22. **Even lawful slaughter had to minimize the animal’s suffering.**

   > “God has prescribed excellence in all things... when you slaughter, slaughter well. Let one of you sharpen his blade and spare the animal suffering.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih Muslim 1955a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:1955a)[^3][^13][^42]

23. **Living creatures could not be used as targets for sport.**

   > “The Messenger of God ﷺ cursed whoever takes a living creature as a target.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih Muslim 1957a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:1957a)[^3][^13][^42]

24. **Cleanliness was treated as part of spiritual life.**

   > “Purity is half of faith.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih Muslim 223](https://sunnah.com/muslim:223)[^3][^13][^42]

25. **Illness was a reason to seek treatment, not an excuse for fatalistic passivity.**

   > “God has not sent down a disease except that He has also sent down its cure.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 5678](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5678)[^2][^12][^42]

26. **He gave a clear rule for containing plague.** People were not to enter a land where an outbreak was occurring, nor flee from a place once plague had broken out there. Whatever one thinks of premodern medicine more broadly, the rule plainly works against carrying an epidemic across boundaries.[^66]

   > “If you hear of plague in a land, do not enter it; and if it occurs in a land while you are there, do not leave it.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 5728](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5728)[^2][^12][^42]

27. **Ownership of a working animal created responsibility for its welfare.** When Muhammad ﷺ saw a severely emaciated camel, he rebuked its neglect rather than treating the animal as disposable equipment.

   > “Fear God regarding these animals. Ride them while they are in good condition, and feed them while they are in good condition.”
   >
   > Source: [Sunan Abi Dawud 2548](https://sunnah.com/abudawud:2548)[^4][^14][^42], graded sahih in the linked edition

28. **Serious debts were to be documented.** The Qur’an’s longest verse instructs believers to write down deferred financial obligations and use witnesses. Religious life therefore included ordinary safeguards against dispute and exploitation.

   > “When you contract a debt for a fixed term, write it down.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 2:282](https://quran.com/2/282)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

29. **Public office was a trust, not a reward for friendship.** When the beloved companion Abu Dharr asked for authority, Muhammad ﷺ refused to appoint him because he believed Abu Dharr was not suited to the responsibility.

   > “Abu Dharr, you are weak, and authority is a trust... except for the one who takes it with its right and fulfills its duties.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih Muslim 1825](https://sunnah.com/muslim:1825)[^3][^13][^42]

30. **Power was not something to chase for its own sake.** Muhammad ﷺ warned against seeking office simply because authority itself was desired.

   > “Do not seek authority. If it is given to you because you asked for it, you will be left to bear it; if it is given without your asking, you will be helped in it.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 7146](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:7146)[^2][^12][^42]

31. **He ordered his companions to return baby birds they had taken from their mother.** Seeing the mother flutter in distress, Muhammad ﷺ focused on the suffering they had caused her and required them to put her young back. The same report records him objecting to the burning of an ant colony.

   > “Who has distressed this bird by taking her young? Return her young to her.”
   >
   > Source: [Sunan Abi Dawud 2675](https://sunnah.com/abudawud:2675)[^4][^14][^42], graded sahih in the linked edition

32. **Religious leaders had to account for the weakest people in the congregation.** An imam leading communal prayer was not to impose the length he might choose in private because those behind him could include the elderly, the sick, and people with physical limitations.

   > “When one of you leads the people in prayer, let him shorten it, for among them are the weak, the sick, and the elderly.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 703](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:703)[^2][^12][^42]

33. **Learning scripture was meant to lead to teaching it.** Knowledge was not simply something to accumulate as private prestige.

   > “The best of you are those who learn the Qur’an and teach it.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 5027](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5027)[^2][^12][^42]

34. **True wealth was defined as an inward sufficiency, not an accumulation of possessions.** The poor are therefore not reduced to what they own, and the wealthy cannot assume that having more will itself cure greed.

   > “Richness is not having many possessions. True richness is richness of the soul.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 6446](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6446)[^2][^12][^42]

35. **Health and free time were described as blessings people often waste.** Ordinary capacities are easy to overlook until they are lost.

   > “There are two blessings in which many people are deceived: health and free time.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 6412](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6412)[^2][^12][^42]

36. **He personally inspected the market and exposed a concealed defect.** When he found wet grain hidden beneath dry grain, Muhammad ﷺ told the seller that buyers had to be able to see what was wrong with the product.

   > “Why did you not put it on top so people could see it? Whoever deceives us is not of us.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih Muslim 102](https://sunnah.com/muslim:102)[^3][^13][^42]

37. **Genuine consent is central to lawful trade.** In the same verse, the Qur’an joins consensual commerce to a prohibition on wrongful consumption of property and to the sanctity of life.

   > “Do not consume one another’s wealth unjustly, but only through trade by mutual consent.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 4:29](https://quran.com/4/29)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

38. **Contracts were moral commitments that had to be honored.** The Qur’an states the rule with unusual directness at the opening of a chapter.

   > “O you who believe, fulfill your contracts.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 5:1](https://quran.com/5/1)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

39. **A public official could not treat gifts generated by his office as personal favors.** When a tax collector claimed that some property had been given to him personally, Muhammad ﷺ exposed the conflict of interest by asking whether he would have received those gifts had he simply stayed at home.

   > “Why did he not sit in his father’s or mother’s house and see whether gifts would be given to him?”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 7174](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:7174)[^2][^12][^42]

40. **Leaders were warned against making life needlessly difficult for people.** In a prayer about those entrusted with his community’s affairs, Muhammad ﷺ contrasted harsh administration with gentleness.

   > “O God, whoever is put in charge of my community and is hard on them, be hard on him; whoever is gentle with them, be gentle with him.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih Muslim 1828a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:1828a)[^3][^13][^42]

41. **The Qur’an prohibited interest-based usury and distinguished it from ordinary trade.** Whatever later jurists debate about the exact legal boundary of *riba* [prohibited increase], the moral direction is clear: debt cannot become an unlimited means of extracting wealth from another person.

   > “Give up what remains of usury, if you are believers... You may have your principal: neither wronging nor being wronged.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 2:278–279](https://quran.com/2/278-279)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

42. **Hoarding essential goods to gain a market advantage was condemned.** Scarcity was not morally neutral when someone deliberately manipulated it for private profit.

   > “No one hoards except a sinner.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih Muslim 1605a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:1605a)[^3][^13][^42]

43. **One of his teachings was later invoked against the abuse of non-Muslim taxpayers.** Hisham ibn Hakim saw farmers forced to stand in the sun over the *jizya* [tax historically levied on certain non-Muslim subjects] and cited Muhammad’s ﷺ warning against tormenting people. The principle could therefore be turned against officials after his death.

   > “God will punish those who torment people in this world.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih Muslim 2613b](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2613b)[^3][^13][^42]

44. **Public leadership assumed responsibility for people who died leaving debts or unsupported dependents.** Government carried liabilities as well as receiving revenue, while the deceased person’s private property still passed to the heirs.

   > “Whoever leaves property, it belongs to his heirs; whoever leaves debt or dependents, we will take care of them.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 6763](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6763)[^2][^12][^42]

45. **Shared stagnant water could not be contaminated with urine.** A practical rule protecting a common resource joined ritual cleanliness to basic public hygiene.

   > “None of you should urinate in stagnant water that does not flow and then bathe in it.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 239](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:239)[^2][^12][^42]

46. **Worship was not confined to special or elite buildings.** Muhammad ﷺ taught that the earth itself could serve as a place of prayer, making worship portable and part of ordinary life.

   > “The earth has been made for me a place of prayer and a means of purification.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 335](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:335)[^2][^12][^42]

47. **Striking an animal in the face was forbidden.** Even when animals were lawfully used for transport or labor, there were physical limits on how they could be treated.

   > “The Messenger of God ﷺ forbade striking on the face.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 5541](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5541)[^2][^12][^42]

48. **Mutilating animals was also condemned.** The rule belongs to a wider ethic that rejects using living creatures as targets and likewise prohibits mutilation in war.

   > “The Prophet ﷺ cursed the one who mutilates an animal.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 5515](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5515)[^2][^12][^42]

49. **Wanting authority was not itself a qualification for receiving it.** Alongside warning individuals not to seek office for status, Muhammad ﷺ told those making appointments not to reward a person merely for being eager to rule.

   > “We do not appoint to this work anyone who asks for it or is eager for it.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 7149](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:7149)[^2][^12][^42]

50. **His treatment of Abu Dharr distinguishes personal goodness from practical competence.** Piety and affection did not automatically qualify a person for every responsibility.

   > “I love for you what I love for myself. Do not be in charge of even two people, and do not administer an orphan’s property.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih Muslim 1826](https://sunnah.com/muslim:1826)[^3][^13][^42]

51. **Understanding mattered more than merely possessing information.** Knowledge was meant to produce sound judgment and responsible worship, not function as intellectual display.

   > “When God intends good for someone, He gives him understanding of the religion.”
   >
   > Sources: *Sahih al-Bukhari* 71; *Sahih Muslim* 1037[^2][^3][^12][^13][^42]

52. **Studying revelation together was itself given spiritual value.** Muhammad ﷺ described communal reading and study as being surrounded by tranquility and mercy, so learning was not confined to isolated experts.

   > “No people gather in one of God’s houses, reciting God’s Book and studying it together, except that tranquility descends upon them.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih Muslim 2699a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2699a)[^3][^13][^42]

53. **He taught with attention to people’s capacity to concentrate.** Ibn Masud remembered that Muhammad ﷺ chose suitable times for instruction because he did not want listeners to become bored or exhausted by constant teaching.

   > “He used to choose suitable times to teach us, fearing that we would become bored.”
   >
   > Sources: *Sahih al-Bukhari* 68, 70[^2][^12][^42]

54. **Difficult questions were allowed.** The hadith literature preserves people asking Muhammad ﷺ about belief, doubt, intimacy, grief, worship, money, family problems, and death. The famous hadith of Gabriel teaches the foundations of religion through direct questions and answers.

   > “Tell me about Islam... Tell me about faith... Tell me about excellence.”
   >
   > Source: *Sahih Muslim* 8[^3][^13][^42]

55. **He severely warned against inventing teachings and attributing them to him.** Whatever questions a reader may have about later transmission, the warning itself created a strong religious reason to distinguish genuine reports from fabrications.

   > “Whoever deliberately lies about me, let him take his place in the Fire.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 107](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:107)[^2][^12][^42]

56. **Sham bidding used only to drive up another buyer’s price was prohibited.** Market freedom did not include manipulating someone into paying more through a false offer.

   > “The Prophet ﷺ forbade artificial bidding.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 2140](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2140)[^2][^12][^42]

57. **He encouraged charity designed to keep benefiting people beyond a single gift.** When Umar acquired valuable land, Muhammad ﷺ advised him to preserve the asset while dedicating its yield to the poor, relatives, travelers, and other beneficiaries. This became an important precedent for charitable endowment.

   > “Retain the property itself and give its produce in charity.”
   >
   > Sources: *Sahih al-Bukhari* 2737, 2764; *Sahih Muslim* 1632[^2][^3][^12][^13][^42][^60]

58. **Physical cleanliness was woven into ordinary worship.** Repeated acts of washing formed part of ritual life rather than being separated from spiritual practice.

   > “When you rise for prayer, wash your faces and your hands to the elbows, wipe your heads, and wash your feet to the ankles.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 5:6](https://quran.com/5/6)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

59. **Certain indispensable resources were treated as shared entitlements rather than things the powerful could simply monopolize.** A short report names water, pasture, and fire. Jurists differed over the rule’s exact legal scope, but its social direction is clear in a desert setting where control of water could amount to control over life itself.

   > “Muslims are partners in three: water, pasture, and fire.”
   >
   > Source: [Sunan Abi Dawud 3477](https://sunnah.com/abudawud:3477)[^4][^14][^42], graded sound

60. **He sometimes refused to exercise a power that people themselves asked him to use.** When prices rose and people asked Muhammad ﷺ to fix them, he declined because he feared meeting God responsible for an injustice involving another person’s property or life. Whatever one thinks of the economic rule in another context, the incident shows how he understood authority: **having power did not make every possible intervention morally his to exercise**.

   > “I hope to meet God with no one having a claim against me for an injustice concerning blood or property.”
   >
   > Source: [Sunan Abi Dawud 3451](https://sunnah.com/abudawud:3451)[^4][^14][^42], graded sound

61. **He answered a shocking moral request by making the questioner think from another person’s position.** A young man openly asked permission to commit adultery. Rather than letting the crowd attack him, Muhammad ﷺ called him close and asked whether he would accept the same act involving his own mother, daughter, sister, or aunt. The young man answered no each time, after which Muhammad ﷺ prayed for him.

   > The young man was asked whether he would accept the act for his own female relatives, and each time answered that he would not.
   >
   > Source: *Musnad Ahmad* 22211, graded sound by Shuayb al-Arnaut[^79]

62. **Even a voluntary confession to a grave sexual offense was not rushed into punishment.** When Ma’iz confessed to adultery, Muhammad ﷺ repeatedly turned him away and investigated whether he understood what he was saying and whether the legal conditions actually applied. The punishment described in the report is severe, but the report also shows a marked reluctance to impose it merely because a distressed person confessed.

   > The report describes Ma’iz returning repeatedly before the confession was finally accepted.
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 6825](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6825)[^2][^12][^42]

63. **He openly acknowledged that a court could reach the wrong result when the evidence was misleading.** Litigants differ in eloquence, so a persuasive person might receive a judgment for property that was not truly his. Muhammad ﷺ warned that the favorable ruling would not turn the underlying wrong into a moral right.

   > “I am only a human being. You bring me your disputes, and one of you may be more persuasive than another... If I award him something from his brother’s right, let him not take it.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 2680](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2680)[^2][^12][^42]

### D. God, worship, hope, repentance, and the inner life

1. **The worship of one God stood at the center of his message.** Muhammad ﷺ did not ask people to worship him, his family, saints, tribes, natural forces, or intermediaries. His central proclamation was uncompromising monotheism: ultimate love, fear, hope, gratitude, prayer, and surrender belong to God alone.

   > “Say: He is God, One; God, the One all depend upon. He neither begets nor is born, and none is comparable to Him.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 112:1–4](https://quran.com/112)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

2. **God is near and may be called upon directly.** Access to Him does not depend on a priestly class. The Qur’an does more than say that God hears prayer in the abstract; it places His response beside the sincere call of the person who prays.

   > “When My servants ask you about Me, I am near. I answer the call of the caller when he calls upon Me.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 2:186](https://quran.com/2/186)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

3. **Even serious sin did not justify despair of God.** A person who has badly damaged his life is not treated as spiritually disposable. The Qur’an explicitly tells sinners not to lose hope in divine mercy.

   > “O My servants who have transgressed against themselves, do not despair of God’s mercy. God forgives all sins.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 39:53](https://quran.com/39/53)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

4. **Sincere repentance can do more than erase the past.** The Qur’an describes repentance, faith, and reform as capable of transforming a person’s moral record.

   > “Except those who repent, believe, and do righteous deeds. For them God will replace their evil deeds with good.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 25:70](https://quran.com/25/70)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

5. **He described God as rejoicing in a servant’s return.** Muhammad ﷺ compared it to the overwhelming relief of a traveler in a deadly desert who unexpectedly recovers the animal carrying his food and water, then said God’s joy at a servant’s repentance is greater still.

   > “God is more joyful at the repentance of His servant than one of you who finds his lost camel in a barren desert.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih Muslim 2747a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2747a)[^3][^13][^42]

6. **Even a man who had murdered one hundred people was not beyond repentance.** In a story Muhammad ﷺ told, the killer asked whether return to God was still possible. A knowledgeable man told him that nothing stood between him and repentance, then instructed him to leave the corrupt environment that had shaped him.

   > “Who can stand between you and repentance? Go to such-and-such a land, for there are people there who worship God.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih Muslim 2766a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2766a)[^3][^13][^42]

7. **Hardship was not evidence that God had abandoned a person.** The Qur’an does not promise a life without pain. It teaches instead that difficulty and relief can coexist, repeating the assurance for emphasis.

   > “Surely with hardship comes ease. Surely with hardship comes ease.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 94:5–6](https://quran.com/94/5-6)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

8. **Moral responsibility is proportionate to human capacity.** Religious duty is therefore framed by the principle that God does not burden a soul beyond what it can bear.

   > “God does not burden any soul beyond its capacity.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 2:286](https://quran.com/2/286)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

9. **Spiritual life was tied to inward peace rather than spectacle.** The Qur’an presents remembrance of God as a source of settledness for the heart.

   > “Surely in the remembrance of God hearts find rest.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 13:28](https://quran.com/13/28)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

10. **Remembrance of God was described in strikingly personal terms.** The believer remembers God, and the Qur’an says that God remembers the believer.

   > “Remember Me, and I will remember you. Be grateful to Me and do not be ungrateful.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 2:152](https://quran.com/2/152)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

11. **Trust in God was joined to active effort.** Reliance was not meant to become helplessness. Muhammad ﷺ told believers to pursue what benefits them, seek God’s help, and not give in to incapacity.

   > “Be eager for what benefits you, seek God’s help, and do not give up.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih Muslim 2664](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2664)[^3][^13][^42]

12. **He warned against becoming trapped in counterfactual regret.** After a loss, a believer should learn what can be learned without endlessly tormenting himself with “if only.”

   > “If something befalls you, do not say, ‘If only I had done such-and-such.’ Say: ‘God decreed, and He did what He willed.’”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih Muslim 2664](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2664)[^3][^13][^42]

13. **Suffering could carry moral meaning without pain being romanticized.** Illness, grief, fatigue, and even the prick of a thorn were described as occasions through which sins may be erased.

   > “No fatigue, illness, sorrow, hurt, or distress afflicts a Muslim, even a thorn that pricks him, except that God removes sins through it.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 5641–5642](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5641)[^2][^12][^42]

14. **The spiritual life required gratitude in ease as well as endurance in hardship.**

   > “If you are grateful, I will surely increase you.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 14:7](https://quran.com/14/7)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

15. **Intention was morally central.** An outwardly impressive act can be hollow when its purpose is corrupt, while an ordinary act can carry great value through sincere intention.

   > “Actions are only by intentions, and each person will have only what he intended.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 1](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:1)[^2][^12][^42]

16. **He opposed forms of religious excess that break the person practicing them.** Muhammad ﷺ described the religion as workable and warned against trying to overpower it through unsustainable devotion.

   > “Religion is ease. No one makes the religion excessively hard upon himself except that it overwhelms him. So aim rightly and come close.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 39](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:39)[^2][^12][^42]

17. **Perpetual fasting, nightly all-night prayer, and celibacy were not ideals of superior holiness.** When three men proposed these extreme forms of devotion, Muhammad ﷺ pointed to his own balanced practice: he fasted and ate, prayed at night and slept, and married.

   > “I fast and I break my fast; I pray and I sleep; and I marry women. Whoever turns away from my way is not of me.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 5063](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5063)[^2][^12][^42]

18. **The body, the family, and the soul each had legitimate claims.** When Salman reminded an intensely ascetic companion that God, his own self, and his family each had rights, Muhammad ﷺ explicitly approved the principle.

   > “Your Lord has a right over you, your self has a right over you, and your family has a right over you. Give each its right.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 1968](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:1968)[^2][^12][^42]

19. **Sustainable goodness was preferred to brief bursts of exhausting devotion.**

   > “The deeds most loved by God are those done most consistently, even if they are small.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 6465](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6465)[^2][^12][^42]

20. **Muhammad ﷺ himself repeatedly sought God’s forgiveness.** His prophetic status did not lead him to present himself as beyond repentance or humility.

   > “By God, I seek God’s forgiveness and turn to Him in repentance more than seventy times in a day.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 6307](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6307)[^2][^12][^42]

21. **Divine mercy was described as vastly greater than all the mercy visible in creation.** In one report, Muhammad ﷺ said that every form of created tenderness, including an animal protecting its young, comes from one portion of mercy, while God has retained ninety-nine.

   > “God made mercy into one hundred parts. He sent down one part among creation and retained ninety-nine parts.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih Muslim 2752a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2752a)[^3][^13][^42]

22. **A mother’s fierce care became an illustration of God’s mercy.** After seeing a woman desperately search for her child and then nurse him, Muhammad ﷺ asked whether she would ever willingly throw that child into a fire.

   > “God is more merciful to His servants than this woman is to her child.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 5999](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5999)[^2][^12][^42]

23. **Each person is directly accountable before God.** Wealth, ancestry, and intermediaries cannot substitute for that individual moral encounter.

   > “Each of you will meet his Lord with no interpreter between him and God.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 3595](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3595)[^2][^12][^42]

24. **Despair was not a reason to wish life away.** Even under serious distress, Muhammad ﷺ told people not to ask for death, but to entrust the timing of life and death to God.

   > “Do not wish for death because of harm that has befallen you. Say: ‘O God, keep me alive while life is better for me.’”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 5671](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5671)[^2][^12][^42]

25. **He rejected bad omens and preferred hopeful speech.** People were directed away from fearful superstition and toward good expectation.

   > “There is no evil omen, and I like a good omen.” They asked what that was. He said, “A good word.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 5756](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5756)[^2][^12][^42]

26. **His own long worship was an expression of gratitude, not spiritual entitlement.** When Aisha asked why he stood in night prayer until his feet swelled despite believing that God had forgiven him, his answer was simply that he wanted to be a grateful servant.

   > “Should I not be a grateful servant?”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 4836](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:4836)[^2][^12][^42]

27. **Salvation was ultimately a mercy, not a wage owed for religious performance.** Muhammad ﷺ included himself in this teaching, so even prophetic worship could not become a basis for entitlement before God.

   > “No one’s deeds will admit him to Paradise.” They asked, “Not even you?” He said, “Not even me, unless God envelops me in mercy.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 5673](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5673)[^2][^12][^42]

28. **He told of a woman known for sexual sin who was forgiven after showing mercy to a thirsty dog.** The story joins two principles: kindness to animals matters morally, and a deeply stigmatized past does not close the door to God’s mercy.

   > “She gave the dog water, so God thanked her for it and forgave her.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 3321](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3321)[^2][^12][^42]

29. **Even at the beginning of the Qur’an’s account of the Night Journey, Muhammad ﷺ is called God’s servant.** The extraordinary nature of the event does not turn him into a divine figure. Servanthood remains the honor, while authenticated reports connect the journey with the establishment of the five daily prayers.

   > “Glory be to the One who took His servant by night from the Sacred Mosque to the Farthest Mosque.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 17:1](https://quran.com/17/1)[^1][^10][^11][^35]; see also the Night Journey reports in *Sahih al-Bukhari* and *Sahih Muslim*

30. **Prostration was described as a moment of exceptional nearness to God.** Muhammad’s ﷺ own prayer could be long and absorbed, while his leadership of others remained considerate of their limits.

   > “The closest a servant is to his Lord is while prostrating, so increase your supplication.”
   >
   > Source: *Sahih Muslim* 482[^3][^13][^42]

31. **The five daily prayers turn worship into a repeated return to God rather than an occasional performance.** Muhammad ﷺ compared their cleansing moral rhythm to bathing repeatedly in a river.

   > “If a river were at the door of one of you and he bathed in it five times a day, would any dirt remain?”
   >
   > Sources: *Sahih al-Bukhari* 528; *Sahih Muslim* 667[^2][^3][^12][^13][^42]

32. **Fasting was tied to truthfulness and conduct, not hunger alone.** Abstaining from food while continuing to lie and act on falsehood misses the ethical purpose of the discipline.

   > “Whoever does not leave false speech and acting upon it, God has no need of his leaving food and drink.”
   >
   > Sources: *Sahih al-Bukhari* 1903, 6057[^2][^12][^42]

33. **The Qur’an describes his mission in explicitly universal language of mercy.** That claim is best read beside the concrete teachings in this article concerning children, servants, debtors, captives, non-Muslims, defeated enemies, and animals.

   > “We have sent you only as a mercy to the worlds.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 21:107](https://quran.com/21/107)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

### E. Character, speech, anger, forgiveness, and everyday goodness

1. **Excellent character was central to the religion he taught.** The Qur’an itself describes Muhammad ﷺ in moral rather than imperial terms.

   > “You are truly upon tremendous character.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 68:4](https://quran.com/68/4)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

2. **Real strength was measured by self-control.** In a culture where physical strength carried obvious prestige, Muhammad ﷺ defined the stronger person as the one able to master his anger.

   > “The strong person is not the one who overcomes people in wrestling. The strong person is the one who controls himself when angry.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 6114](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6114)[^2][^12][^42]

3. **Restraining anger and forgiving others were marks of moral excellence.**

   > “Those who spend in ease and hardship, restrain anger, and pardon people. God loves those who do good.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 3:134](https://quran.com/3/134)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

4. **Forgiveness could increase a person’s honor rather than diminish it.**

   > “No one forgives another except that God increases him in honor, and no one humbles himself for God except that God raises him.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih Muslim 2588](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2588)[^3][^13][^42]

5. **Forgiveness did not require denying justice.** The Qur’an permits proportionate redress while placing reconciliation and pardon on a higher moral plane.

   > “The repayment of an evil is an evil like it; but whoever pardons and reconciles, his reward is with God.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 42:40](https://quran.com/42/40)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

6. **Evil was to be answered, where possible, with something better.** The Qur’an gives moral transformation as the aim, not merely the victim’s private serenity.

   > “Repel evil with what is better, and the one between whom and you was hostility may become like a devoted friend.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 41:34](https://quran.com/41/34)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

7. **Gentleness was a general moral principle.**

   > “Gentleness is not present in anything except that it beautifies it, and it is not removed from anything except that it disfigures it.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih Muslim 2594a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2594a)[^3][^13][^42]

8. **Teachers and leaders were told to make religion accessible rather than repellent.**

   > “Make things easy; do not make them hard. Give good news; do not drive people away.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 69](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:69)[^2][^12][^42]

9. **Control of the tongue was a mark of mature faith.** A person should contribute something good or have the discipline to remain silent.

   > “Whoever believes in God and the Last Day should speak good or remain silent.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 6018](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6018)[^2][^12][^42]

10. **Mockery and degrading labels were condemned.** Social humiliation was treated as a moral wrong, not harmless entertainment.

   > “Let not one people ridicule another; perhaps they are better than them... and do not insult one another with offensive nicknames.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 49:11](https://quran.com/49/11)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

11. **Spying, corrosive suspicion, and backbiting were all condemned.** To make the ugliness of backbiting vivid, the Qur’an compares it to eating the flesh of one’s dead sibling.

   > “Avoid much suspicion... do not spy, and do not backbite one another. Would one of you like to eat the flesh of his dead brother?”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 49:12](https://quran.com/49/12)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

12. **People were told to choose the best words, not merely words that were technically permissible.**

   > “Tell My servants to say that which is best.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 17:53](https://quran.com/17/53)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

13. **Humility was to shape both belief and outward manner.**

   > “Do not turn your cheek away from people in contempt, and do not walk the earth arrogantly... Be moderate in your walk and lower your voice.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 31:18–19](https://quran.com/31/18-19)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

14. **Mature servants of God were described as calm when verbally provoked.**

   > “The servants of the Most Merciful are those who walk humbly upon the earth, and when the ignorant address them, they say words of peace.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 25:63](https://quran.com/25/63)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

15. **Truthfulness was a path of character, not merely a rule against individual lies.**

   > “Truthfulness leads to righteousness, and righteousness leads to Paradise... Lying leads to wickedness, and wickedness leads to the Fire.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 6094](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6094)[^2][^12][^42]

16. **Believers were to desire good for others as sincerely as they desired it for themselves.**

   > “None of you truly believes until he loves for his brother what he loves for himself.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 13](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:13)[^2][^12][^42]

17. **Simply looking down on another person was itself treated as serious evil.**

   > “It is enough evil for a person to despise his Muslim brother.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih Muslim 2564a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2564a)[^3][^13][^42]

18. **Arrogance was defined morally, not by appearance.** Wearing good clothes was not the problem. Arrogance meant rejecting truth and despising other people.

   > “Arrogance is rejecting the truth and looking down on people.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih Muslim 91a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:91a)[^3][^13][^42]

19. **Small acts of generosity and attentiveness counted.** A person did not need wealth or status to live a morally significant life. Half a date, a good word, a smile, useful directions, or helping someone with poor sight could all matter.

   > “Protect yourself from the Fire even with half a date; if you cannot find that, then with a good word.”
   >
   > “Your smile in your brother’s face is charity... and guiding a person with poor sight is charity for you.”
   >
   > Sources: [Sahih al-Bukhari 1417](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:1417)[^2][^12][^42]; [Jami at-Tirmidhi 1956](https://sunnah.com/tirmidhi:1956)[^5][^15][^42]

20. **Ordinary helpfulness could become an act of worship.** Muhammad ﷺ counted reconciling people, helping someone with a mount, speaking a good word, walking to prayer, and removing harm from a road as forms of charity.

   > “Every good word is charity... and removing something harmful from the road is charity.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 2989](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2989)[^2][^12][^42]

21. **Another person’s private space could not be entered without permission.**

   > “Do not enter houses other than your own until you seek permission and greet their inhabitants.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 24:27](https://quran.com/24/27)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

22. **Consequential information had to be verified before it was spread.** The rule protects a community from harming people on the basis of unreliable reports.

   > “If an unreliable person brings you news, verify it, lest you harm people in ignorance and later regret what you did.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 49:6](https://quran.com/49/6)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

23. **People were forbidden to make confident claims about what they did not know.**

   > “Do not pursue that of which you have no knowledge. Hearing, sight, and heart, all of these will be questioned.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 17:36](https://quran.com/17/36)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

24. **The Qur’an called for forgiveness in a case where anger was entirely understandable.** Mistah, a poor relative whom Abu Bakr had supported financially, took part in spreading the slander against Abu Bakr’s daughter Aisha. Abu Bakr then swore to end his support. The revealed response called the injured father back to pardon, and Abu Bakr resumed helping him.

   > “Let them pardon and overlook. Do you not love that God should forgive you?”
   >
   > Sources: [Qur’an 24:22](https://quran.com/24/22)[^1][^10][^11][^35]; context in [Sahih al-Bukhari 4750](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:4750)[^2][^12][^42]

25. **Part of being a good Muslim was making other people safe from one’s harm.** The test is concrete rather than ceremonial: what comes from a person’s tongue and hand.

   > “A Muslim is one from whose tongue and hand the Muslims are safe.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 10](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:10)[^2][^12][^42]

26. **When a man repeatedly asked him for advice, Muhammad ﷺ kept returning to one instruction: do not give yourself over to anger.**

   > “Do not become angry.” He repeated it several times: “Do not become angry.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 6116](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6116)[^2][^12][^42]

27. **He defined backbiting in a way that removes a common excuse.** Saying something negative behind a person’s back is still backbiting when it is true. If it is false, the wrong becomes slander as well.

   > “Mentioning about your brother what he dislikes.” They asked, “What if it is true?” He replied, “If true, you backbit him; if false, you slandered him.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih Muslim 2589](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2589)[^3][^13][^42]

28. **Reconciliation was a positive duty, not merely an absence of open hostility.** The Qur’an invokes the moral kinship of believers precisely when it calls on them to repair broken relationships.

   > “The believers are but brothers, so make peace between your brothers.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 49:10](https://quran.com/49/10)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

29. **Genuine family loyalty went beyond reciprocity.** Maintaining ties only with relatives who maintain them in return is an exchange. Muhammad ﷺ praised the person who continues the relationship even when relatives sever it.

   > “The one who truly maintains kinship is the one who, when his relatives cut him off, maintains relations with them.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 5991](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5991)[^2][^12][^42]

30. **Helping an oppressor meant stopping the oppression.** Muhammad ﷺ took a familiar tribal slogan about supporting one’s own people and gave it the opposite moral force.

   > “Help your brother whether he is an oppressor or oppressed.” ... “You help the oppressor by preventing him from oppression.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 2444](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2444)[^2][^12][^42]

31. **A man who accidentally spoke during prayer remembered Muhammad ﷺ as the best teacher he had ever seen.** Afterward, Muhammad ﷺ neither struck nor scolded nor insulted him. He simply explained the rule.

   > “I never saw a teacher before or after him better in teaching than he was.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih Muslim 537a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:537a)[^3][^13][^42]

32. **He could receive a humiliating confession without humiliating the person making it.** A man admitted breaking the Ramadan fast through sexual intercourse, then explained that he could not afford the prescribed forms of expiation. When charitable food arrived and the man said his was the poorest household in Medina, Muhammad ﷺ laughed and told him to feed his own family.

   > “Then feed it to your family.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 5368](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5368)[^2][^12][^42]

33. **Sincerity was not measured by how many voluntary practices a person could add.** A Bedouin promised to fulfill the obligatory prayers, fasting, and charity without adding extra acts. Muhammad ﷺ did not shame this modest commitment.

   > “He will succeed if he is truthful.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 46](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:46)[^2][^12][^42]

34. **He sometimes declined to require a practice he valued because it would burden others.** He said that, were it not for the hardship it would impose on his community, he would have required tooth-cleaning before every prayer.

   > “Were it not that I would cause hardship for my community, I would have ordered them to use the tooth-stick with every prayer.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 887](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:887)[^2][^12][^42]

35. **He stopped leading optional Ramadan night prayers in congregation because he feared they might become obligatory.** His own desire for worship gave way to concern that others might later be burdened by it.

   > “Nothing prevented me from coming out to you except that I feared it might be made obligatory upon you.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 1129](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:1129)[^2][^12][^42]

36. **Keeping promises and trusts was part of moral integrity.** Lying, breaking promises, and betraying what had been entrusted to a person were identified as signs of hypocrisy.

   > “The signs of a hypocrite are three: when he speaks he lies, when he promises he breaks it, and when entrusted he betrays.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 33](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:33)[^2][^12][^42]

37. **He prohibited the habits that turn a community into hostile rivals.** Hatred, abandonment, contempt, and oppression appear together in a teaching that places human dignity above status competition.

   > “Do not hate one another or turn away from one another... A Muslim does not oppress, abandon, or despise his brother.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih Muslim 2564a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2564a)[^3][^13][^42]

38. **A person’s life, property, and honor were treated as inviolable rights.** Reputation is placed beside body and property rather than dismissed as something socially harmless to attack.

   > “All of a Muslim is inviolable to another Muslim: his blood, his wealth, and his honor.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih Muslim 2564a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2564a)[^3][^13][^42]

39. **Sexual modesty was required of men as well as women.** The Qur’an first tells believing men to lower their gaze and guard their chastity, then gives believing women a corresponding command.

   > “Tell the believing men to lower their gaze and guard their chastity... And tell the believing women to lower their gaze and guard their chastity.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 24:30–31](https://quran.com/24/30-31)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

40. **The Qur’an protects people from destructive sexual accusations.** A serious charge against a chaste person required evidence, not gossip, suspicion, or an attempt to destroy someone’s reputation.

   > “Those who accuse chaste women and then do not produce four witnesses... do not accept their testimony afterward.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 24:4](https://quran.com/24/4)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

41. **Intoxicants and gambling were treated as social as well as spiritual harms.** The Qur’an explains that they generate hostility between people and distract them from remembrance and prayer.

   > “Intoxicants, gambling... are filth from Satan’s work, so avoid them that you may prosper.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 5:90–91](https://quran.com/5/90-91)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

42. **Wastefulness was itself morally disapproved.** Alongside the Qur’an’s rule against excess in consumption, Muhammad ﷺ condemned wasting wealth together with empty talk and needless contentious questioning.

   > “God dislikes for you gossip, needless questioning, and wasting wealth.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih Muslim 1715a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:1715a)[^3][^13][^42]

43. **He rebuked a companion for making communal worship unnecessarily difficult.** When Muadh’s very long prayer caused a working man to leave the congregation, Muhammad ﷺ took the complaint seriously. Enduring a leader’s preferred level of devotion was not made a test of piety.

   > “Are you putting people to trial, Muadh?”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 705](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:705)[^2][^12][^42]

44. **Removing a danger from a public road could carry spiritual significance.** One story makes an anonymous act for unknown passersby a reason for divine forgiveness.

   > “A man saw a thorny branch in the road and removed it, so God appreciated his act and forgave him.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 652](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:652)[^2][^12][^42]

45. **Discipline of both speech and sexual conduct was tied to salvation.** Moral self-control concerned what a person said as well as how a person governed intimate desire.

   > “Whoever guarantees for me what is between his jaws and what is between his legs, I guarantee Paradise for him.”
   >
   > Source: *Sahih al-Bukhari* 6474[^2][^12][^42]

46. **He personally went to reconcile people when a dispute at Quba turned violent.** Peacemaking was not merely advice he delegated to others.

   > “Let us go and make peace between them.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 2693](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2693)[^2][^12][^42]

47. **Ordinary estrangement was not to harden into a permanent feud.** When two people had stopped speaking, Muhammad ﷺ said the better of them was the one willing to reopen the relationship first.

   > “The better of the two is the one who begins with peace.”
   >
   > Sources: *Sahih al-Bukhari* 6077; *Sahih Muslim* 2560[^2][^3][^12][^13][^42]

48. **Character was a measure of spiritual weight, not an ornament added to ritual religion.** The followers Muhammad ﷺ described as nearest to him in the next life were not the wealthiest or most prominent, but those with the best conduct.

   > “Nothing is heavier on the believer’s scale on the Day of Resurrection than good character.”
   >
   > “The most beloved of you to me and nearest to me on the Day of Resurrection are those best in character.”
   >
   > Sources: [Jami at-Tirmidhi 2002](https://sunnah.com/tirmidhi:2002)[^5][^15][^42]; [Jami at-Tirmidhi 2018](https://sunnah.com/tirmidhi:2018)[^5][^15][^42]

49. **An early report summarizes moral refinement as part of the purpose of his mission.** The statement is brief, but the many concrete teachings surrounding it show what that moral refinement meant in practice.

   > “I was sent to complete good character.”
   >
   > Source: [Al-Adab al-Mufrad 273](https://sunnah.com/urn/2302710)[^7][^17][^42]

50. **The Qur’an itself portrays Muhammad ﷺ as deeply affected by other people’s suffering.** His compassion is not inferred only from anecdotes. The scripture he recited describes their hardship as weighing heavily upon him.

   > “Your suffering weighs heavily upon him; he is deeply concerned for you, and toward the believers he is compassionate and merciful.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 9:128](https://quran.com/9/128)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

51. **His grief over people who rejected the message was intense enough that the Qur’an repeatedly restrained it.** His concern for them did not give him power to force belief; another person’s heart remained beyond his control.

   > “Perhaps you would consume yourself with grief because they do not believe in this message.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 18:6](https://quran.com/18/6)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

### F. Parents, spouses, women, children, and the home

1. **Kindness to parents was placed immediately after worship of God.** The Qur’an pays particular attention to parents in old age and forbids even the smallest expression of contempt toward them.

   > “Your Lord has decreed that you worship none but Him and show excellence to parents... do not even say ‘uff’ to them, and speak to them nobly.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 17:23](https://quran.com/17/23)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

2. **A mother was given exceptional priority in a child’s companionship.** When asked who most deserved good company, Muhammad ﷺ answered “your mother” three times before saying “your father.”

   > “Your mother.” He asked, “Then who?” He said, “Your mother.” He asked, “Then who?” He said, “Your mother.” Then, “Your father.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 5971](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5971)[^2][^12][^42]

3. **The Qur’an explicitly acknowledges the physical cost of motherhood.**

   > “We instructed the human being concerning his parents. His mother carried him in weakness upon weakness.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 31:14](https://quran.com/31/14)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

4. **Marriage required the woman’s consent.** A previously married woman could not be married without consulting her, and a virgin’s permission also had to be sought.[^53][^54]

   > “A previously married woman is not to be married until consulted, and a virgin is not to be married until her permission is sought.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 5136](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5136)[^2][^12][^42]

5. **He enforced that principle when a woman came to him with a complaint.** Khansa bint Khidam said that her father had married her against her wishes, and Muhammad ﷺ invalidated the marriage.

   > “Her father married her while she disliked it, so the Messenger of God ﷺ annulled that marriage.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 5138](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5138)[^2][^12][^42]

6. **Women could not be treated as property to be inherited.** The Qur’an directly addressed a pre-Islamic practice by telling men that they had no right to acquire women against their will.

   > “It is not lawful for you to inherit women against their will... and live with them honorably.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 4:19](https://quran.com/4/19)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

7. **Women were given legally defined shares of inheritance.** In seventh-century Arabia, the Qur’an stated the principle of inheritance explicitly for both men and women.[^53][^54]

   > “For men is a share of what parents and close relatives leave, and for women is a share of what parents and close relatives leave.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 4:7](https://quran.com/4/7)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

8. **Marriage was described in the language of tranquility, affection, and mercy.**

   > “He created for you spouses from among yourselves so that you may find tranquility in them, and He placed between you affection and mercy.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 30:21](https://quran.com/30/21)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

9. **The Qur’an uses clothing as an image of mutual intimacy and protection between spouses.**

   > “They are a garment for you, and you are a garment for them.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 2:187](https://quran.com/2/187)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

10. **A man’s character was measured in part by how he treated his family.** Public charisma did not compensate for private cruelty.

   > “The best of you are those best to their families, and I am the best of you to my family.”
   >
   > Source: [Jami at-Tirmidhi 3895](https://sunnah.com/tirmidhi:3895)[^5][^15][^42]

11. **Men were explicitly instructed to treat women well.**

   > “Treat women well.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih Muslim 1468a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:1468a)[^3][^13][^42]

12. **A spouse was not to be reduced to one disliked trait.** Muhammad ﷺ told husbands to remember that a quality they dislike can exist beside another they appreciate.

   > “A believing man should not hate a believing woman. If he dislikes one quality in her, he will be pleased with another.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih Muslim 1468b](https://sunnah.com/muslim:1468b)[^3][^13][^42]

13. **The Qur’an repeatedly presents believing women and men together as moral agents.**

   > “The believing men and believing women are allies of one another. They enjoin what is right and forbid what is wrong.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 9:71](https://quran.com/9/71)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

14. **Spiritual qualities and rewards were stated in parallel for women and men.** Qur’an 33:35 names believing, truthful, patient, humble, charitable, fasting, chaste, and God-remembering men and women side by side, then gives both the same promise.

   > “God has prepared for them forgiveness and an immense reward.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 33:35](https://quran.com/33/35)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

15. **Women asked for dedicated access to his teaching, and he provided it.** They told Muhammad ﷺ that men were taking most of his time and requested a day of their own. He appointed one and taught them directly.

   > “The women said, ‘The men have taken all your time from us, so appoint for us a day.’ He promised them a day and taught them.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 101](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:101)[^2][^12][^42]

16. **He was openly affectionate with children.** When a man expressed surprise that Muhammad ﷺ kissed his grandson Hasan, Muhammad ﷺ treated the man’s lack of tenderness as a moral issue rather than a matter of custom.

   > “Whoever does not show mercy will not be shown mercy.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 5997](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5997)[^2][^12][^42]

17. **He shortened a public prayer when he heard a baby crying.** Knowing that the child’s mother would be distressed, Muhammad ﷺ changed the length of his own intended worship to ease her burden.

   > “I begin the prayer intending to lengthen it, then I hear a child crying, so I shorten it because I know how his mother suffers from his crying.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 710](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:710)[^2][^12][^42]

18. **He carried his granddaughter while leading the prayer.** Muhammad ﷺ held Umama while standing and put her down when bowing or prostrating. Tenderness toward a child was not treated as beneath religious dignity.

   > “The Messenger of God ﷺ prayed while carrying Umama... when he stood he carried her, and when he prostrated he put her down.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 516](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:516)[^2][^12][^42]

   Children could interrupt him even in his most public and authoritative settings. On another occasion, he stopped a sermon, came down from the pulpit, picked up his stumbling grandsons Hasan and Husayn, and then resumed with them.

   > Source: [Sunan Abi Dawud 1109](https://sunnah.com/abudawud:1109)[^4][^14][^42], graded sound

19. **A woman’s marriage gift belonged to her.** The required bridal gift, often called *mahr* [the marriage gift owed to the bride], is given to the bride herself. The Qur’an addresses men directly and tells them to hand it over graciously rather than treat it as family property.

   > “Give women their bridal gifts graciously.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 4:4](https://quran.com/4/4)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

20. **Burying infant daughters alive was explicitly condemned.** Female infanticide is denounced both in Muhammad’s ﷺ reported teaching and in one of the Qur’an’s starkest images of judgment, where the murdered girl herself is called forward and the crime exposed.

   > “When the girl buried alive is asked for what sin she was killed.”
   >
   > Sources: [Qur’an 81:8–9](https://quran.com/81/8-9)[^1][^10][^11][^35]; [Sahih al-Bukhari 5975](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5975)[^2][^12][^42]

21. **He gave special honor to people who raised daughters well.** Protecting girls from violence was not enough; the years of care they required were themselves given religious value. Muhammad ﷺ linked that care with exceptional nearness to him in the next life.

   > “Whoever raises two girls properly until they grow up will come on the Day of Resurrection with me like this,” and he joined his fingers.
   >
   > Source: [Sahih Muslim 2631](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2631)[^3][^13][^42]

22. **Divorce could not be used to trap or injure a woman.** If a marriage was ending, the Qur’an told a husband either to retain his wife honorably or release her honorably, not keep her suspended in order to cause harm.[^53][^54]

   > “Retain them honorably or release them honorably. Do not retain them to harm them and thereby transgress.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 2:231](https://quran.com/2/231)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

23. **A woman’s marital complaint became the opening of a Qur’anic chapter.** She came disputing with Muhammad ﷺ about her husband and appealing to God, and the revealed passage begins by affirming that God heard her. Whatever one’s theology of revelation, the grievance of an ordinary woman was placed inside permanent scripture.

   > “God has certainly heard the words of the woman who was pleading with you concerning her husband and complaining to God.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 58:1](https://quran.com/58/1)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

24. **Even small acts of tenderness toward a spouse carried moral value.** Ordinary family spending could be rewarded through sincere intention, down to a morsel of food placed in one’s wife’s mouth.

   > “Whatever you spend seeking God’s pleasure is rewarded, even the morsel you place in your wife’s mouth.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 5354](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5354)[^2][^12][^42]

25. **Men were told not to prevent women from attending communal worship.** Whatever later Muslim societies have done, Muhammad’s ﷺ instruction itself is direct.

   > “Do not prevent the female servants of God from the mosques of God.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 900](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:900)[^2][^12][^42]

26. **Men were required to live honorably with their wives even when their feelings were difficult.** The Qur’an does not make affection a precondition for decent treatment.

   > “Live with them honorably. If you dislike them, perhaps you dislike something in which God has placed much good.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 4:19](https://quran.com/4/19)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

27. **He defended his wife Safiyya when she was insulted over her Jewish ancestry.** When Safiyya came to him crying after being disparaged, Muhammad ﷺ affirmed her dignity through the prophetic lineage of her people and rebuked the insult rather than asking her to absorb it silently.

   > “You are the daughter of a prophet, your uncle is a prophet, and you are married to a prophet. So what can she boast over you about?”
   >
   > Source: [Jami at-Tirmidhi 3894](https://sunnah.com/tirmidhi:3894)[^5][^15][^42]

28. **He redirected a man from voluntary warfare to caring for his living parents.** A dramatic religious sacrifice could not erase quieter obligations at home.

   > “Are your parents alive?” He said, “Yes.” He replied, “Then strive in their service.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 5972](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5972)[^2][^12][^42]

29. **When women complained that their husbands had beaten them, Muhammad ﷺ said those men were not the best Muslims.** The report preserves a difficult legal sequence, but his moral judgment on the conduct that resulted is explicit.

   > “Many women have gone around Muhammad’s family complaining of their husbands. Those men are not the best among you.”
   >
   > Source: [Sunan Abi Dawud 2146](https://sunnah.com/abudawud:2146)[^4][^14][^42]

30. **Parents were required to treat their children fairly in gifts.** When a father asked Muhammad ﷺ to witness a gift given to one child but not the others, he refused to validate the favoritism.

   > “Be conscious of God and be just among your children.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 2587](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2587)[^2][^12][^42]

31. **He accepted a woman’s refusal when his request was personal rather than a command.** When Muhammad ﷺ encouraged Barirah to return to her former husband, she first asked whether he was ordering her to do so. He explained that he was only interceding. She declined, and her decision stood.

   > “Are you ordering me?” He said, “No, I am only interceding.” She said, “I have no need of him.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 5283](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5283)[^2][^12][^42]

32. **His affection in marriage appeared in small, deliberate gestures.** Aisha remembered that he would drink from the same place on a cup where she had just drunk. The act is ordinary, but it shows that tenderness was not treated as incompatible with masculinity or religious seriousness.

   > “I would drink... then hand it to the Prophet ﷺ, and he would place his mouth where mine had been.”
   >
   > Source: *Sahih Muslim* 300[^3][^13][^42][^53][^54]

33. **He greeted children rather than treating them as beneath adult notice.** Anas preserved this habit by doing the same himself and explaining that Muhammad ﷺ used to greet them.

   > “Anas passed by a group of boys and greeted them, then said: ‘The Prophet ﷺ used to do so.’”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 6247](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6247)[^2][^12][^42]

## II. The man who lived the teachings

A teacher’s moral claims are easier to assess when we can compare them with his conduct away from formal teaching. Muhammad ﷺ was observed over many years by his wives, a young household servant, close friends, political opponents, poor petitioners, soldiers, and strangers. Their reports are particularly valuable when they describe ordinary behavior or moments in which he was under pressure.

### A. What people closest to him actually saw

1. **Khadija’s first response to his prophetic experience was to appeal to the character she already knew.** When Muhammad ﷺ returned terrified from the first revelation, his wife did not reassure him with promises of power or fame. She listed the good she had personally watched him do.

   > “By God, God will never disgrace you. You keep family ties, carry the burden of the weak, help the destitute, honor the guest, and assist those struck by calamity.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 3](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3)[^2][^12][^42]

2. **Anas served him for ten years and remembered remarkable personal gentleness.** He began serving Muhammad ﷺ while still young. A decade of daily household contact gave irritation with a young helper countless opportunities to appear, which gives his recollection particular weight.

   > “I served the Prophet ﷺ for ten years. He never said ‘uff’ to me and never said, ‘Why did you do that?’ or ‘Why did you not do that?’”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 6038](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6038)[^2][^12][^42]

3. **Anas also preserved an ordinary example of that gentleness.** Muhammad ﷺ sent him on an errand, but the boy became distracted playing. When Muhammad ﷺ found him, he smiled and gently reminded him of the errand instead of humiliating him.

   > He came up smiling and said, “Little Anas, did you go where I asked you to go?”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih Muslim 2310a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2310a)[^3][^13][^42]

4. **Aisha said that he never struck a woman or a servant.** This is testimony from within his household, not a public claim Muhammad ﷺ made about himself.

   > “The Messenger of God ﷺ never struck anyone with his hand, neither a woman nor a servant, except in battle.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih Muslim 2328a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2328a)[^3][^13][^42]

5. **Aisha also said that he did not take revenge for personal injuries.** She distinguished wrongs done to him personally from the enforcement of public moral limits.

   > “The Messenger of God ﷺ never took revenge for himself.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 3560](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3560)[^2][^12][^42]

6. **When faced with two morally permissible options, he chose the easier one.** Aisha’s description gives no sense that he considered unnecessary difficulty a virtue in itself.

   > “Whenever the Messenger of God ﷺ was given a choice between two matters, he chose the easier one so long as it was not sinful.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 3560](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3560)[^2][^12][^42]

7. **Anas remembered him as both generous and courageous, and the record gives concrete examples of both.** One appears below: when a frightening sound was heard at night in Medina, Muhammad ﷺ was the first person to ride toward the danger.

   > “The Prophet ﷺ was the best in character, the most generous of people, and the bravest of people.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 6033](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6033)[^2][^12][^42]

8. **The Qur’an itself connects his gentleness with people’s willingness to remain around him.** It does not praise kindness in the abstract, but explicitly contrasts his manner with the alienating effect of harshness.

   > “It was by mercy from God that you were gentle with them. Had you been harsh and hard-hearted, they would have dispersed from around you.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 3:159](https://quran.com/3/159)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

9. **Aisha summarized his character by saying that it embodied the Qur’an.** Asked what Muhammad ﷺ was like, she described a close relationship between the scripture he taught publicly and the conduct she observed privately at home.

   > “His character was the Qur’an.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih Muslim 746a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:746a)[^3][^13][^42]

### B. Mercy when he had reason to be angry

1. **A desert Arab once pulled Muhammad’s ﷺ cloak so violently that it left a mark, then demanded money.** Muhammad ﷺ had political authority and companions around him. He responded by turning toward the man, smiling, and ordering that he be given something.

   > “The Prophet ﷺ turned toward him, smiled, and ordered that a gift be given to him.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 3149](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3149)[^2][^12][^42]

2. **When a stranger urinated inside the mosque, Muhammad ﷺ stopped the crowd from attacking him.** He let the man finish, had the place washed with water, and taught his companions without turning correction into unnecessary humiliation.

   > “Leave him... You were sent to make things easy, not to make them difficult.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 6128](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6128)[^2][^12][^42]

3. **A man once stood over Muhammad ﷺ with Muhammad’s own unsheathed sword while he slept.** The man asked who would protect him, and Muhammad ﷺ answered, “God.” After the immediate danger passed, the report records that Muhammad ﷺ did not punish him.

   > “He said, ‘Who will protect you from me?’ I said, ‘God’... The Prophet ﷺ did not punish him.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 2910](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2910)[^2][^12][^42]

4. **When he was personally insulted over a distribution of wealth, he remembered Moses and chose patience.** He understood the insult within a longer prophetic history of being mistreated rather than treating his own pain as unique.

   > “May God have mercy on Moses. He was hurt with more than this, yet he was patient.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 4335](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:4335)[^2][^12][^42]

5. **When companions asked him to curse a tribe that had rejected his message, he prayed for the tribe’s guidance instead.** Tufayl ibn Amr reported that Daws had refused the message and asked Muhammad ﷺ to invoke God against them. Those around him expected a curse, but Muhammad ﷺ prayed for their guidance.

   > “O God, guide Daws and bring them.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 2937](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2937)[^2][^12][^42]

6. **Even when a leading internal opponent died, Muhammad’s ﷺ first response was merciful until revelation set a limit.** Abdullah ibn Ubayy had led serious opposition in Medina. Yet when his son asked Muhammad ﷺ for a shirt to use as his father’s shroud and for a funeral prayer, Muhammad gave the shirt and prayed. Qur’an 9:84 then prohibited him from doing so in such cases in the future. The episode preserves both his personal inclination toward mercy and his submission when the revelation he claimed to receive overruled it.

   > His son asked for Muhammad’s ﷺ shirt and funeral prayer, “so the Prophet ﷺ gave him his shirt” and went to pray for him.
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 1269](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:1269)[^2][^12][^42]

7. **He explicitly said that cursing people was not the purpose of his prophetic mission.** When companions asked him to curse hostile polytheists, he answered by describing himself as having been sent as a mercy.

   > “I was not sent as one who curses. I was sent only as a mercy.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih Muslim 2599](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2599)[^3][^13][^42]

8. **He refused to kill Medina’s leading internal critic for political convenience.** After repeated political harm from Abdullah ibn Ubayy, Umar asked permission to kill him. Muhammad ﷺ refused in part because he considered the wider moral consequence of becoming known as a leader who killed people counted among his own community.

   > “Leave him, lest people say Muhammad kills his companions.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 4905](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:4905)[^2][^12][^42]

9. **He did not turn the poisoning at Khaybar into a personal vendetta.** Muhammad ﷺ survived poisoned food served to him there and later said, during his final illness, that he could still feel its effects. Reports differ over what ultimately happened to the woman who prepared it after another man died from the same food, but neither account makes punishment a matter of revenge for Muhammad’s own injury.

   > During his final illness he said that he still felt the effect of the food he had eaten at Khaybar.
   >
   > Sources: [Sahih al-Bukhari 4428](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:4428)[^2][^12][^42]; [Sahih al-Bukhari 5777](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5777)[^2][^12][^42]

### C. Humility and a strikingly unroyal life

1. **He continued to help his family at home while leading a growing community.** The detail matters because it concerns repetitive domestic work rather than a ceremonial display of humility.

   > “He used to serve his family.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 676](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:676)[^2][^12][^42]

2. **His household sometimes passed long periods without cooked food.** Aisha remembered seeing three new moons over two months without a cooking fire being lit in Muhammad’s homes. Dates and water formed their basic diet during such periods, supplemented by milk given by neighbors.

   > “We would see three crescents in two months, and no fire would be lit in the houses of the Messenger of God ﷺ.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 6459](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6459)[^2][^12][^42]

3. **He died while his armor was pledged to a Jewish merchant for barley.** Whatever resources had passed through the hands of the leader of Medina, he did not end his life with the private fortune one would expect from a man using a prophetic claim for personal enrichment.

   > “The Messenger of God ﷺ died while his iron armor was pledged to a Jewish man for thirty measures of barley.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 2916](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2916)[^2][^12][^42]

4. **He slept on bedding coarse enough to leave marks on his body.** Umar saw those marks and wept as he compared Muhammad’s ﷺ circumstances with those of the Byzantine and Persian rulers.

   > Umar said, “Caesar and Khosrau live as they do, while you are the Messenger of God.” He replied, “Are you not content that they have this world and we the next?”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 4913](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:4913)[^2][^12][^42]

5. **He avoided ceremonial displays of self-importance.** He sat among his companions so simply that a stranger could enter and ask which of them was Muhammad ﷺ. His companions also reported that they did not routinely stand when he arrived because they knew he disliked it.

   > “Which of you is Muhammad?”
   >
   > Source: *Sahih al-Bukhari* 63; see also *Jami al-Tirmidhi* 2754, graded *hasan* [sound][^2][^5][^12][^15][^42]

6. **His modesty also appeared in the way he expressed displeasure.** Companions could often tell from his face that he disliked something without his turning that feeling into coarse or humiliating speech.

   > “The Prophet ﷺ was more modest than a virgin in her private room.”
   >
   > Sources: *Sahih al-Bukhari* 3562, 6102; *Sahih Muslim* 2320[^2][^3][^12][^13][^42]

### D. Generosity and courage when they cost something

1. **His generosity became especially pronounced during Ramadan, the Muslim fasting month.** Ibn Abbas compared the freedom and speed of Muhammad’s ﷺ giving to a freely blowing wind.

   > “The Messenger of God ﷺ was the most generous of people, and he was most generous in Ramadan.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 6](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6)[^2][^12][^42]

2. **At times he gave on a scale that astonished new converts.** Anas reported that a man who asked for material help received a flock filling the space between two mountains. The man returned to his people marveling that Muhammad ﷺ gave as someone unafraid of poverty.

   > “Muhammad gives like a man who does not fear poverty.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih Muslim 2312a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2312a)[^3][^13][^42]

3. **He used wealth to reconcile hostile people rather than simply accumulate it.** Safwan ibn Umayya later said that Muhammad ﷺ kept giving to him after the battle of Hunayn while Safwan still hated him, until Muhammad ﷺ became the person he loved most.

   > “He continued giving to me until he became the dearest of people to me, though before that he had been the most hateful of people to me.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih Muslim 2313](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2313)[^3][^13][^42]

4. **His courage involved personal physical risk.** When a frightening sound alarmed Medina one night, Muhammad ﷺ had already ridden alone toward it on an unsaddled horse while others were still gathering to investigate.

   > They met him returning, a sword at his neck, saying, “Do not be afraid. Do not be afraid.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 6033](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6033)[^2][^12][^42]

5. **At Hunayn, when a sudden volley drove many of his fighters backward, Muhammad ﷺ remained in place.** Al-Bara was directly asked whether the Muslims had fled and left him. He distinguished between some men retreating under the archers’ attack and Muhammad ﷺ himself remaining on his mule.

   > Al-Bara said, “The Messenger of God ﷺ did not flee.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 4317](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:4317)[^2][^12][^42]

6. **Aggressive demands did not necessarily end his generosity.** During one journey, Bedouins crowded around him and pulled away his cloak while demanding shares of wealth. He did not retaliate, but answered by describing how freely he would distribute wealth if he possessed it.

   > “If I had camels as numerous as these thorn trees, I would distribute them among you. You would not find me miserly, false, or cowardly.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 2821](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2821)[^2][^12][^42]

7. **He disliked retaining surplus wealth for himself even when it could lawfully have remained in his hands.** He told Abu Dharr that if he possessed gold equal to Mount Uhud, he would not want to keep it for even a few nights except what was needed to pay a debt.

   > “I would not like to have gold equal to this Mount Uhud unless nothing of it remained with me after three nights, except something I kept for a debt.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 6445](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6445)[^2][^12][^42]

8. **He shared the physical risks of battle rather than insulating himself from the danger borne by his followers.** At Uhud he was wounded in the face.

   > “On that day, the Prophet’s face was wounded and one of his teeth was broken.”
   >
   > Sources: *Sahih al-Bukhari* 2911; *Sahih Muslim* 1790[^2][^3][^12][^13][^42]

   Another Bukhari account adds that pieces of his helmet were driven into his cheek. The bodily danger of the battlefield reached him as it reached the people he led.

   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 4075](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:4075)[^2][^12][^42]

9. **His generosity was remembered as a settled habit, not merely a few spectacular gifts.** Anas said that when Muhammad ﷺ was asked for something he could give, he did not ordinarily respond with a flat refusal. Read beside the reports of his sparse household life, the pattern is clear: resources that reached him were unusually easy for him to pass on.

   > “The Prophet ﷺ was never asked for something to be given for which his answer was ‘no.’”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 6034](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6034)[^2][^12][^42]

### E. Tenderness, loyalty, grief, and human warmth

1. **He wept when his infant son died.** His words distinguish grief from rebellion against God. Prophetic patience did not require pretending that loss did not hurt.

   > “The eyes shed tears and the heart grieves... O Ibrahim, we are truly grieved by your departure.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 1303](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:1303)[^2][^12][^42]

2. **His loyalty to Khadija continued long after her death.** He still spoke of her years later and sent food to her friends.

   > “When he slaughtered a sheep, he would send portions to the friends of Khadija.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 6004](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6004)[^2][^12][^42]

3. **There was room for play in his marriage.** Aisha’s memory of racing Muhammad ﷺ is valuable precisely because nothing grand is happening. It preserves ordinary humor and companionship between them.

   > After each had won one race, he said with humor, “This one is for that one.”
   >
   > Source: [Sunan Abi Dawud 2578](https://sunnah.com/abudawud:2578)[^4][^14][^42]

4. **He gave his time to a socially vulnerable woman with an intellectual or mental difficulty until her need was met.** Anas remembers the leader of Medina allowing her to choose where she wanted to speak with him, then remaining with her until she had finished.

   > She said, “Messenger of God, I have a need from you.” He told her to choose a side of the road, and he stayed with her until her need was met.
   >
   > Source: [Sahih Muslim 2326](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2326)[^3][^13][^42]

5. **A child’s turn was not erased merely because older men were present.** After drinking, Muhammad ﷺ had a boy on his right and elders on his left. Since custom gave the person on the right the next turn, he asked the boy’s permission to serve the elders first. When the boy declined, Muhammad ﷺ honored his right and handed him the drink.

   > He asked the boy, “Will you permit me to give these elders first?” ... Then the Messenger of God ﷺ placed the cup in the boy’s hand.
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 2602](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2602)[^2][^12][^42]

6. **He did not insult food simply because he disliked it.** Abu Huraira preserves a small domestic courtesy: Muhammad ﷺ ate what appealed to him and quietly left what did not.

   > “The Prophet ﷺ never criticized food. If he wanted it, he ate it; otherwise, he left it.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 3563](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3563)[^2][^12][^42]

7. **He wept out of concern for the people who would follow his message.** In one report, he recited earlier prophets’ prayers for their peoples, then raised his hands and wept for his own community. His concern in the scene is religious rather than political: what would become of them before God.

   > He raised his hands and said, “O Lord, my community, my community,” and wept.
   >
   > Source: [Sahih Muslim 202](https://sunnah.com/muslim:202)[^3][^13][^42]

8. **He noticed a small child’s grief over a pet.** Anas remembered Muhammad ﷺ gently speaking with his little brother Abu Umayr about a small bird that had died. The loss may have been small to an adult, but Muhammad ﷺ treated the child’s grief as worth noticing.

   > “O Abu Umayr, what happened to the little bird?”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 6129](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6129)[^2][^12][^42]

9. **He stood to welcome his daughter Fatima.** Aisha reported that when Fatima entered, Muhammad ﷺ rose, took her hand, kissed her, and seated her where he had been sitting. Fatima did the same when he visited her.

   > “When she entered upon him, he stood for her, took her hand, kissed her, and seated her where he had been sitting.”
   >
   > Source: [Sunan Abi Dawud 5217](https://sunnah.com/abudawud:5217)[^4][^14][^42]

10. **He noticed when a poor person who served the community was suddenly absent.** A Black man or woman who cleaned the mosque died and was buried without Muhammad ﷺ being informed. He later asked after the person, objected that no one had told him, went to the grave, and prayed there.

   > “Why did you not inform me?” Then he went to the grave and prayed over it.
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 458](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:458)[^2][^12][^42]

11. **People remembered him for smiling at them.** Jarir ibn Abdullah said that after he became Muslim, Muhammad ﷺ never shut him out and smiled whenever they met.

   > “Whenever he saw me, he received me with a smile.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 3822](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3822)[^2][^12][^42]

12. **His speech was neither obscene nor deliberately coarse.** The report connects his restraint in language with the wider standard of character he taught others.

   > “The Prophet ﷺ was neither obscene nor one who deliberately spoke obscenely... The best of you are those best in character.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 6029](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6029)[^2][^12][^42]

13. **He spoke with enough care and clarity to be understood.** Aisha remembered his words as distinct, not a rapid stream that left listeners behind.

   > “He did not speak rapidly as you do. He spoke with clear words, separated from one another.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 3568](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3568)[^2][^12][^42]

   Anas likewise remembered that Muhammad ﷺ sometimes repeated an important statement three times so that listeners would understand it. He took responsibility for making himself clear rather than assuming that the audience would keep up.

   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 95](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:95)[^2][^12][^42]

14. **Harmless recreation had a place in his religious life.** When Aisha wanted to watch an Abyssinian display in the mosque, Muhammad ﷺ screened her with his cloak and stayed until she had watched for as long as she wished.

   > “The Prophet ﷺ screened me with his cloak while I watched the Abyssinians playing... until I had enough.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 5236](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5236)[^2][^12][^42]

15. **A poor mother’s care for her daughters became a lesson in the religious value of raising girls well.** Aisha gave a needy woman three dates. The mother gave one to each daughter, then divided her own date between them. Muhammad ﷺ used the incident to teach that generous care for daughters can protect a person from the Fire.

   > “Whoever is tested with daughters and treats them generously, they will be a shield for him from the Fire.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 5995](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5995)[^2][^12][^42]

16. **He remembered a socially obscure man whom others had forgotten even among the dead.** After a battle, the companions listed those killed but did not name Julaybib. Muhammad ﷺ noticed that he was missing and made them search for him.

   > “But I am missing Julaybib. Search for him.” ... “He is of me and I am of him.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih Muslim 2472](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2472)[^3][^13][^42]

17. **His humor did not depend on falsehood.** Muhammad ﷺ joked with companions and children, but explicitly distinguished joking from lying.

   > “I do not say anything except the truth.”
   >
   > Source: *Jami al-Tirmidhi* 1990, graded *hasan* [sound][^5][^15][^42]

18. **When he wept over another dying child, he called his tears mercy rather than weakness.** The scene extends the lesson beyond grief for his own son: tenderness in the face of another person’s suffering was itself something he regarded as valuable before God.

   > “This is mercy God has placed in the hearts of His servants, and God shows mercy to the merciful.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 7377](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:7377)[^2][^12][^42]

19. **He expressed affection for believers who would live after his death.** At a graveyard he told the companions that he longed to see his “brothers.” When they assumed he meant them, he explained that he meant believers who had not yet come.

   > “I would love to see my brothers... They are those who have not yet come.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih Muslim 249a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:249a)[^3][^13][^42]

## III. A life tested by weakness, persecution, authority, and victory

### A. Before revelation: an orphan, worker, husband, and trusted arbitrator

1. **Muhammad ﷺ began life without the protection of a living father, and the Qur’an turns his memory of vulnerability into a duty toward others.** According to the early biographical tradition, his father died before he was born and his mother died while he was still a child. The same Qur’anic chapter that reminds him of being an orphan tells him not to mistreat the orphan or turn away the person who asks for help.

   > “Did He not find you an orphan and give you shelter?... So do not oppress the orphan, and do not repel the one who asks.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 93:6, 9–10](https://quran.com/93/6-10)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

2. **He worked for a living before he ever led a community.** He tended sheep for the people of Mecca and later worked in trade. When he eventually taught the dignity of lawful earning, he spoke as someone who had himself worked rather than only commanded others.

   > Asked whether he had tended sheep, he said, “Yes. I used to tend the sheep of the people of Mecca for small coins.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 2262](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2262)[^2][^12][^42]

3. **The early biography remembers him as a trusted man before he claimed revelation.** During the rebuilding of the Kaaba, Mecca’s central sanctuary, the clans nearly fought over who would have the honor of replacing the Black Stone. Muhammad ﷺ proposed placing it on a cloth so representatives of the clans could lift it together, after which he set it in place himself.

   > The clans agreed that the next man to enter should arbitrate; when Muhammad came, they accepted him, and his solution shared the honor among them.
   >
   > Source: [Ibn Ishaq, early biography, trans. A. Guillaume, pp. 84–86](https://archive.org/details/TheLifeOfMohammedGuillaume)[^8][^18][^27][^36][^37] (biographical report)

4. **At about forty, his prophetic life began in seclusion rather than from a position of power.** Bukhari’s account of the first revelation says that before the angelic encounter, Muhammad ﷺ had begun withdrawing to the cave of Hira for periods of worship.

   > “The love of seclusion was given to him. He would go to the cave of Hira and devote himself to worship there for a number of nights.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 3](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3)[^2][^12][^42]

5. **His immediate response to the first revelation was fear, not triumph.** He returned to Khadija with his heart trembling and asked to be covered. Whatever interpretation one gives the event, the canonical account does not read like a calculated announcement that an ambitious plan had begun.

   > He returned to Khadija “with his heart trembling” and said, “Cover me, cover me.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 3](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3)[^2][^12][^42]

6. **Khadija based her reassurance on the character she had already observed.** She cited Muhammad’s ﷺ loyalty to relatives, care for the weak, generosity, hospitality, and help in times of calamity, then took him to Waraqa ibn Nawfal, a learned monotheist familiar with earlier scripture.

   > “God will never disgrace you. You keep family ties, support the weak, help the destitute, honor guests, and assist those afflicted.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 3](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3)[^2][^12][^42]

7. **Waraqa’s response promised conflict rather than easy prestige.** He told Muhammad ﷺ that the bearer of revelation who had come to Moses had now come to him, and that his own people would drive him out. Muhammad ﷺ was surprised at the thought of being expelled.

   > Muhammad ﷺ asked, “Will they drive me out?” Waraqa replied that no one had brought a message like this without being opposed.
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 3](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3)[^2][^12][^42]

8. **Before prophethood, he participated in a Meccan alliance formed to restore the rights of people who had been wronged.** Early biography therefore places his concern for justice before he possessed political power. The pact committed its members to stand with victims until their rights were restored.

   > “They pledged that they would stand with any wronged person in Mecca until his right was restored.”
   >
   > Source: Sira report, Ibn Hisham, *al-Sirah al-Nabawiyyah* 1/133[^8][^18][^19][^27][^28]

9. **Khadija was his only wife throughout their long marriage.** Early biography describes her as a Meccan merchant who employed Muhammad ﷺ in trade and later proposed marriage after observing his conduct in business. In the traditional chronology, he was about twenty-five and she was older, and they remained married for roughly twenty-five years. She knew him before prophethood, supported him at the beginning of revelation, and endured the Meccan years beside him. Although plural marriage was socially available, he did not marry another woman while she lived.

   > “The Messenger of God ﷺ did not marry another woman until Khadija died.”
   >
   > Sources: *Sahih Muslim* 2436[^3][^13][^42]; Ibn Ishaq/Ibn Hisham (biographical report) for the marriage narrative[^8][^18][^27][^36][^37]

10. **His birth name later came to fit his historical reception in a striking way.** *Muhammad* means “the one repeatedly praised” or “much praised.” He bore the name before making any prophetic claim, spent long periods of his mission being mocked and reviled, and later became someone named in the daily prayers of Muslims throughout the world. This does not prove prophethood by itself, but the correspondence is worth noticing.[^76][^27][^36]

11. **The sources do not give him a forty-year career as a poet, priest, scripture scholar, or religious orator preparing for this role.** Arabia highly valued poetry, yet Muhammad’s ﷺ prophetic claim appears after a long ordinary adult life rather than at the end of an established literary or priestly career. The Qur’an itself appeals to that discontinuity before the audience who had known his earlier life.

   > “You did not know what the Book was, nor the faith, but We made it a light by which We guide whom We will.”
   >
   > Sources: [Qur’an 42:52](https://quran.com/42/52)[^1][^10][^11][^35]; [Qur’an 29:48](https://quran.com/29/48)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

### B. Mecca: a message that cost him safety instead of buying it

1. **His early public message challenged rather than flattered the foundations of Meccan status.** The Qur’an called people away from inherited idols to the worship of one God, while condemning neglect of the poor, cheating, arrogance, and exploitation of the vulnerable.

   > “Say: He is God, One.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 112:1](https://quran.com/112/1)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

2. **He was commanded to begin with his own relatives rather than exempt his clan from the message’s moral demands.**

   > “Warn your closest relatives.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 26:214](https://quran.com/26/214)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

3. **His followers endured persecution while they had no political power.** Khabbab ibn al-Aratt later remembered complaining to Muhammad ﷺ while he sat near the Kaaba. Muhammad’s answer was not a promise of immediate comfort, but a call to endure together with confidence that their circumstances would eventually change.

   > “By God, God will complete this matter until a traveler goes safely... but you are being hasty.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 3612](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3612)[^2][^12][^42]

4. **He did not soften the central claim of monotheism in order to reach a compromise with polytheism.** A short Meccan chapter draws a clear boundary between his worship and that of his opponents.

   > “For you is your religion, and for me is my religion.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 109:6](https://quran.com/109/6)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

5. **The Qur’an records pressure on him to compromise and warns him not to yield.** Whatever view one takes of revelation, the text restricts the messenger rather than giving him pragmatic freedom to reshape the message for political survival.

   > “They wished that you would compromise, so they could compromise.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 68:9](https://quran.com/68/9)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

6. **The deaths of Khadija and Abu Talib removed his strongest personal and political supports.** Early biographies place both losses late in the Meccan period. Khadija had been his closest emotional supporter, while Abu Talib had provided crucial clan protection. Muhammad ﷺ continued the mission after losing both.

   > “God will never disgrace you,” Khadija had told him at the beginning of the mission.
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 3](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3)[^2][^12][^42]; see also [Ibn Ishaq, early biography](https://archive.org/details/TheLifeOfMohammedGuillaume)[^8][^18][^27][^36][^37] for the chronology of the Meccan losses.

   The bereavements continued beyond that year. Muhammad ﷺ outlived all of his children except Fatima, and she survived him by only about six months. His prophetic life was never insulated from ordinary human grief.

7. **At al-Ta’if, severe rejection did not lead him to choose indiscriminate vengeance.** Bukhari’s account does not contain every dramatic detail found in popular retellings, but it clearly records Muhammad’s ﷺ deep sorrow and his refusal of the angel’s offer to crush the people who had rejected him.

   > “Rather, I hope that God will bring from their descendants people who worship God alone.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 3231](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3231)[^2][^12][^42]

8. **When he finally left Mecca, he did so as a hunted migrant rather than a conqueror.** The Qur’an recalls Muhammad ﷺ and Abu Bakr hiding in a cave while pursuers searched for them.

   > “When the two were in the cave, he said to his companion: ‘Do not grieve. God is with us.’”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 9:40](https://quran.com/9/40)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

9. **Even one of the men pursuing him could receive a guarantee of safety.** Suraqa pursued Muhammad ﷺ and Abu Bakr during the migration. After failing to capture them, he asked for security. The hadith account says that Muhammad ﷺ granted it and asked him not to reveal their route.

   > Suraqa asked for a written guarantee of security, and the Prophet ﷺ ordered that one be written for him.
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 3906](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3906)[^2][^12][^42]

10. **During the years of Muslim weakness, the message emphasized patient restraint rather than private revenge.** Mockery and injury were not treated as permission for individuals to begin cycles of retaliation.

   > “Be patient over what they say, and leave them with a dignified withdrawal.”
   >
   > Source: Qur’an 73:10[^1][^10][^11][^35]

11. **He was forbidden to push aside sincere low-status followers in order to attract elites.** Many early believers were poor, enslaved, young, or socially unprotected. The Qur’an specifically protected such worshippers from being discarded for reasons of status.

   > “Do not drive away those who call upon their Lord morning and evening, seeking His face.”
   >
   > Source: Qur’an 6:52[^1][^10][^11][^35]

12. **He sent persecuted Muslims to seek protection under a Christian ruler known for justice.** Before the Muslims had a state of their own, Muhammad ﷺ recognized that trustworthy protection could come from across a religious boundary.

   > “There is a king there under whom no one is wronged.”
   >
   > Source: Report of the first migration to Abyssinia in the sira of Ibn Ishaq, preserved by Ibn Hisham[^8][^18][^27][^36][^37]

13. **He endured a clan boycott without abandoning or diluting the message.** The resulting social and economic isolation made his claim more costly rather than turning it into a route to comfort or prestige.

   > “Do people think they will be left to say, ‘We believe,’ without being tested?”
   >
   > Source: Qur’an 29:2[^1][^10][^11][^35]

14. **The migration joined trust in God with careful practical preparation, including help from a skilled non-Muslim guide.** Routes, provisions, information, concealment, and expertise were all arranged. Reliance on God did not mean refusing ordinary means, and professional trustworthiness could be recognized across a religious boundary.

   > “The Prophet ﷺ and Abu Bakr hired a skilled guide... who followed the religion of Quraysh.”
   >
   > Source: *Sahih al-Bukhari* 2264; see also [Qur’an 9:40](https://quran.com/9/40)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

15. **Early biography reports that Meccan leaders offered him the usual rewards that could tempt an ambitious claimant to stop preaching.** They offered wealth, leadership or kingship, desirable marriage, and treatment if he believed himself afflicted. The same biographical tradition says that he refused. Because the details come from *sira* [early biography] rather than Bukhari or Muslim, they should be weighed at that level of evidence. As preserved, however, the episode makes the cost-benefit pattern of his Meccan mission unusually clear.

   > The early sira preserves negotiations in which Quraysh offered wealth and leadership if Muhammad ﷺ would abandon the message.
   >
   > Source: Ibn Ishaq as preserved by Ibn Hisham (biographical report)[^8][^18][^27][^36][^37]

16. **The persecution was physical as well as social.** Bukhari records enemies placing the remains of a slaughtered camel on Muhammad ﷺ while he prostrated at the Kaaba, leaving his daughter to remove them. Another report describes a man tightening a cloth around his neck while he prayed until Abu Bakr intervened. These were public assaults and humiliations at a time when Muhammad ﷺ had no state through which to punish his attackers.

   > Sources: [Sahih al-Bukhari 240](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:240)[^2][^12][^42]; [Sahih al-Bukhari 3856](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3856)[^2][^12][^42]

17. **Early biography preserves a striking detail about the migration: people plotting his death still had property deposited with him for safekeeping.** Before leaving Mecca, Muhammad ﷺ reportedly arranged for Ali to remain behind and return those trusts. If the report is accepted, intense hostility to his religious claim had not erased confidence in his ordinary honesty.

   > Source: Ibn Ishaq/Ibn Hisham (biographical report on the migration)[^8][^18][^27][^36][^37]

### C. Medina: community, conflict, restraint, and the test of power

1. **The migration in 622 radically changed Muhammad’s ﷺ circumstances.** In Medina he was no longer only a persecuted preacher, but the leader of an emerging multi-clan community. His conduct under authority can therefore be compared with the moral claims he had made while weak.

   > “Those who were settled in the home and in faith before them love those who migrated to them... and give them preference over themselves even if they are in need.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 59:9](https://quran.com/59/9)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

2. **The early Medinan charter formed a political compact that included Jewish groups without erasing their religion.** The document is preserved in early biography and other historical works, so its clauses are studied as historical material rather than as a canonical hadith.

   > “The Jews of Banu Awf are one community with the believers; the Jews have their religion and the Muslims have theirs.”
   >
   > Source: [Ibn Ishaq, early biography, trans. A. Guillaume, pp. 231–233](https://archive.org/details/TheLifeOfMohammedGuillaume)[^8][^18][^27][^29][^36][^37][^52] (biographical document)

3. **The first major battle, Badr, came only after years of expulsion and conflict.** The reports portray Muhammad ﷺ praying intensely rather than behaving as though victory for his followers were automatic.

   > “O God, I ask You to fulfill Your promise and covenant.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 3953](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3953)[^2][^12][^42]

4. **After the painful defeat at Uhud, he was told to remain gentle, pardon his followers, and continue consulting them.** Their military error had serious consequences, but those consequences did not become a reason to abandon consultation.

   > “Had you been harsh and hard-hearted, they would have dispersed from around you. So pardon them... and consult them in affairs.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 3:159](https://quran.com/3/159)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

5. **During the Battle of the Trench, he shared the community’s hunger.** Jabir noticed Muhammad’s ﷺ severe hunger and prepared a small amount of food for him. The leader of Medina was enduring the siege with the community, not from the security of a stocked palace.

   > Jabir reported seeing the Prophet ﷺ suffering from “severe hunger.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 4101](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:4101)[^2][^12][^42]

6. **At Hudaybiyyah in 628, Muhammad ﷺ approached Mecca intending pilgrimage rather than battle.** When Quraysh prevented entry, he repeatedly pursued a negotiated truce.

   > “We have not come to fight anyone. We have come to perform the pilgrimage.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 2731–2732](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2731)[^2][^12][^42]

7. **He accepted treaty wording that omitted his title “Messenger of God.”** The Meccan negotiator refused to recognize the title. Muhammad ﷺ maintained his claim to be God’s Messenger but accepted “Muhammad son of Abdullah” in the written agreement in order to secure peace.

   > “By God, I am the Messenger of God even if you do not believe me. Write: Muhammad son of Abdullah.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 2731–2732](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2731)[^2][^12][^42]

8. **He accepted treaty terms so painful that his own companions struggled to bear them.** One clause required returning certain Meccan escapees. When the persecuted Abu Jandal arrived in chains during the negotiations, Muhammad ﷺ honored the concluded commitment rather than using the outrage of the moment to abandon the agreement.

   > When Quraysh later demanded compliance, the report says: “Abide by the promise you gave us,” and Muhammad ﷺ did so.
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 2731–2732](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2731)[^2][^12][^42]

9. **In the tense aftermath of Hudaybiyyah, he followed the practical advice of his wife Umm Salama.** When discouraged companions did not immediately respond to his instruction to end the pilgrimage state, she advised him to begin the rites himself without further discussion. Muhammad ﷺ did so, and the companions followed his example.

   > Umm Salama advised him to go out, sacrifice, and shave without further argument; “the Prophet ﷺ went out” and did so.
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 2731–2732](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2731)[^2][^12][^42]

10. **The Qur’an called this apparently humiliating truce a “clear victory.”** The opportunities created by the peace help explain why early Muslims later regarded Hudaybiyyah as a decisive turning point rather than a defeat.

   > “Indeed, We have granted you a clear victory.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 48:1](https://quran.com/48/1)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

11. **Economic cooperation with Jewish cultivators continued after the conflict at Khaybar.** The hadith says that Muhammad ﷺ allowed the Jewish inhabitants to cultivate the land in return for a share of its produce.

   > “The Prophet ﷺ gave Khaybar to the Jews to work and cultivate it, and they would receive half of its produce.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 4248](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:4248)[^2][^12][^42]

12. **When Mecca came under his control in 630, he restrained triumphalist bloodshed in the sanctuary.** A commander reportedly spoke of a day of great fighting. Muhammad ﷺ rejected that description, and the next day publicly reaffirmed Mecca’s sacred protection from bloodshed.

   > “It is not lawful for one who believes in God and the Last Day to shed blood in it.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 4295](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:4295)[^2][^12][^42]

13. **Early biography records broad clemency toward the defeated Quraysh.** The familiar wording comes from biography rather than Bukhari or Muslim, so that difference in source strength should be kept clear. The account portrays Muhammad ﷺ asking his former persecutors what they expected from him and then releasing them.

   > “Go, for you are free.”
   >
   > Source: [Ibn Hisham’s recension of Ibn Ishaq, trans. A. Guillaume, p. 553](https://archive.org/details/TheLifeOfMohammedGuillaume)[^8][^18][^27][^36][^37] (biographical report)

14. **He worked beside the community during demanding collective labor.** At the construction of the mosque in Medina, Muhammad ﷺ carried building materials with the others. Reports of the Trench likewise remember him digging rather than supervising hardship entirely from a distance.

   > “The Prophet ﷺ himself began carrying the unbaked bricks for its building.”
   >
   > Sources: [Sahih al-Bukhari 3906](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3906)[^2][^12][^42]; [Sahih al-Bukhari 6414](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6414)[^2][^12][^42]

15. **He released a group of armed men captured after attempting to attack him near Mecca.** Sahih Muslim reports that eighty men descended on Muhammad ﷺ and his companions, were captured, and were then set free rather than executed in retaliation.

   > “Eighty men from the people of Mecca came down upon the Messenger of God armed... He captured them and spared them.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih Muslim 1808](https://sunnah.com/muslim:1808)[^3][^13][^42]

16. **When he entered Mecca with overwhelming force, civilians were given simple ways to remain safe.** Staying inside one’s own home was enough. The city was not treated as though every household were an enemy position.

   > “Whoever enters Abu Sufyan’s house is safe; whoever lays down his arms is safe; whoever closes his door is safe.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih Muslim 1780c](https://sunnah.com/muslim:1780c)[^3][^13][^42]

17. **After Hunayn, he supported returning captives to the defeated Hawazin when a delegation came seeking their families.** Muhammad ﷺ had delayed the final disposition long enough for them to arrive, offered a choice concerning what had been captured, and urged the Muslim army to return the people.

   > “Your brothers have come to you repentant, and I consider that their captives should be returned to them.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 4318–4319](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:4318)[^2][^12][^42]

18. **The Qur’anic instruction attached to victory directed him toward worship and self-accounting, not self-glorification.** Success brought a command to praise God and seek forgiveness rather than to treat conquest as proof that the victor had become divine or beyond correction.

   > “When God’s help and victory come... glorify your Lord with praise and seek His forgiveness.”
   >
   > Source: Qur’an 110:1–3[^1][^10][^11][^35]

19. **A Christian delegation from Najran was received in Medina for sustained religious discussion rather than treated as an enemy simply for remaining Christian.** Early biography reports that the delegation was hosted in the mosque and allowed to perform its own prayer there. The account belongs to *sira* [early biography], not the two canonical Sahih collections, so the source distinction matters. Its picture is nonetheless consistent with the wider record of treaty and interfaith dealings.

   > Source: Ibn Ishaq/Ibn Hisham; also Ibn Sa’d, *al-Tabaqat* (biographical reports)[^8][^9][^18][^19][^27][^28][^36][^37]

20. **His claim was presented beyond Arabia’s tribal audience.** Reports preserve letters to neighboring rulers, including the Byzantine emperor Heraclius, inviting them to the same worship of one God. Bukhari preserves the Heraclius letter within Abu Sufyan’s hostile-witness account, while early historical sources also preserve letters associated with the Persian ruler, the Negus of Abyssinia, and the ruler of Egypt.

   > “I invite you with the invitation of Islam...”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 7](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:7)[^2][^12][^42]; early biography for the wider correspondence[^8][^9][^29][^36][^37]

### D. The final pilgrimage and death

1. **In his final pilgrimage, he reaffirmed the sanctity of human life and property.** The teaching was delivered to a large gathered community near the end of his life.

   > “Your blood and your property are sacred to one another, like the sacredness of this day, in this month, in this town.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih Muslim 1218a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:1218a)[^3][^13][^42]

2. **When abolishing pre-Islamic usury claims, he began with a financial claim belonging to his own family.** Reform did not begin by protecting his relatives from its cost.

   > “The usury of the pre-Islamic period is abolished, and the first usury I abolish is that of Abbas ibn Abd al-Muttalib.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih Muslim 1218a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:1218a)[^3][^13][^42]

3. **He likewise abolished inherited cycles of blood revenge by beginning with a claim connected to his own clan.**

   > “The blood claims of the pre-Islamic period are abolished, and the first blood claim I abolish is one of ours.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih Muslim 1218a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:1218a)[^3][^13][^42]

4. **The final pilgrimage included a direct reminder to men about women’s rights to proper provision and treatment.**

   > “Be mindful of God concerning women... Their right over you is that you provide for them and clothe them in a fitting manner.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih Muslim 1218a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:1218a)[^3][^13][^42]

5. **He presented his task as delivering a message, not establishing his own personal immortality.** In the same sermon, he asked the people what testimony they would give about whether he had conveyed it.

   > They replied, “We bear witness that you conveyed, fulfilled, and gave sincere counsel.” He said, “O God, bear witness.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih Muslim 1218a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:1218a)[^3][^13][^42]

6. **Near his death, he privately told Fatima that he believed his end was close.** The report’s significance as evidence for prophethood is considered later. On a human level, it also preserves a father making his daughter weep with one confidence and then comforting her with another until she laughed.

   > “I think my death has drawn near, and you will be the first of my family to follow me.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 3623–3624](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3623)[^2][^12][^42]

7. **According to Aisha, his last words were directed toward God, not toward office, money, or succession.**

   > “O God, with the Highest Companion.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 4463](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:4463)[^2][^12][^42]

8. **His death was not turned into a denial of his humanity.** The Qur’an had already warned the community not to make faith depend on Muhammad’s ﷺ continued earthly life.

   > “Muhammad is only a messenger. Messengers passed away before him. If he dies or is killed, will you turn back?”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 3:144](https://quran.com/3/144)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

9. **He left no royal treasury to his descendants.** His armor remained pledged for food, and his stated rule was that what prophets leave behind becomes charity rather than a dynastic inheritance.

   > “We prophets are not inherited from. What we leave is charity.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 4240–4241](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:4240)[^2][^12][^42]

10. **His final public teaching included a warning against his followers shedding one another’s blood after his death.** Loyalty to his message was not made a license for communal violence once he was gone.

   > “Do not return after me to striking one another’s necks.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 7078](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:7078)[^2][^12][^42]

11. **Among the instructions repeated near the end of his life were prayer and responsibility toward people under one’s authority.** At a moment when a ruler might be expected to focus on preserving his house or office, the transmitted reports instead remember Muhammad ﷺ returning to worship and to the treatment of people least able to compel justice for themselves.

   > “The prayer, the prayer, and those whom your right hands possess.”
   >
   > Sources: [Sunan Abi Dawud 5156](https://sunnah.com/abudawud:5156)[^4][^14][^42]; [Sunan Ibn Majah 2698](https://sunnah.com/ibnmajah:2698)[^6][^16][^42], graded sound

### E. The society and generation he left behind

1. **The scale of change accomplished during Muhammad’s ﷺ twenty-three-year mission is part of the historical evidence that needs explaining.** He began preaching in a fragmented tribal society without a state and died leading a polity that covered most of Arabia. In between, his community developed institutions of law, worship, taxation, learning, treaty-making, public charity, and written administration, while directly challenging practices such as idolatrous worship, blood vengeance, female infanticide, and unrestricted usury. The speed of this transformation does not by itself prove revelation, but an explanation of his historical career has to account for it.

   > “He is the One who raised among the unlettered a messenger from among themselves, reciting His revelations to them, purifying them, and teaching them the Book and wisdom, though before that they were in clear error.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 62:2](https://quran.com/62/2)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

2. **Some costly reforms were adopted with striking speed despite the absence of a large enforcement bureaucracy.** Anas remembered wine being poured away when its prohibition was announced. Near the end of the mission, the financial and blood claims abolished in the Farewell Pilgrimage were made concrete by beginning with claims involving Muhammad’s ﷺ own family. This does not mean that every social vice disappeared overnight. It does show how quickly demanding norms could acquire real communal force.

   > Anas reported that when the prohibition of wine was announced, the containers in Medina were emptied.
   >
   > Sources: [Sahih al-Bukhari 2464](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2464)[^2][^12][^42]; [Sahih al-Bukhari 4620](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:4620)[^2][^12][^42]; [Sahih Muslim 1218a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:1218a)[^3][^13][^42]

3. **The mission reconciled communities whose feud had involved real bloodshed.** The Medinan tribes Aws and Khazraj had fought for generations, most recently at Bu’ath shortly before Muhammad’s ﷺ arrival. The Qur’an explicitly appealed to their own memory of once being enemies whose hearts had been brought together.

   > “Remember God’s favor upon you: you were enemies, then He joined your hearts and by His favor you became brothers.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 3:103](https://quran.com/3/103)[^1][^10][^11][^35]; early sira for the Bu’ath background[^8][^18][^27][^36][^37]

4. **The refugees from Mecca were not merely admitted to Medina; the new community deliberately created bonds of practical responsibility between households.** The emigrants had left property and protection behind. Early biography remembers pairings between emigrants and Medinan supporters, while the Qur’an praises the hosts for preferring the newcomers even when they themselves were in need.

   > “They give them preference over themselves even though they are in need.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 59:9](https://quran.com/59/9)[^1][^10][^11][^35]; early sira for the pairing of emigrants and helpers[^8][^18][^27][^36][^37]

5. **The generation formed around him did not respond to his death by making him divine.** They had been taught to distinguish the messenger from God. Early reports remember Abu Bakr calming the community by reciting the Qur’anic reminder that Muhammad ﷺ was a messenger like earlier messengers and could die. That generation then became administrators, judges, teachers, and transmitters of the religious record rather than custodians of a royal cult centered on his person.

   > “Muhammad is only a messenger. Messengers passed away before him. If he dies or is killed, will you turn back?”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 3:144](https://quran.com/3/144)[^1][^10][^11][^35]; *Sahih al-Bukhari*, report of Abu Bakr’s address after Muhammad’s ﷺ death

## IV. Why his claim to prophethood deserves serious attention

The case for Muhammad’s ﷺ prophethood is strongest when its parts are considered together. **Good character alone does not prove revelation;** a sincere person can still be mistaken. **A prediction can be ambiguous,** a miracle report is only as useful as its transmission, and **literary power is partly a matter of judgment**. The evidence therefore should not be made to carry more weight individually than it can bear. The relevant question is which overall explanation best accounts for the man, the text he delivered, the risks he accepted, the advantages he refused to exploit, the predictions and miracles attributed to him, and the responses of people who knew him closely.

### A. Character, incentives, and behavior that are difficult to fit to a cynical fraud

1. **His claim to prophethood did not emerge from a known history of deceit.** One of the most useful testimonies in Bukhari comes from Abu Sufyan while he was still Muhammad’s ﷺ opponent. The Byzantine emperor Heraclius asked whether Muhammad had ever been accused of lying before claiming prophethood. Abu Sufyan answered no.

   > Heraclius asked, “Had you ever accused him of lying before he said what he now says?” Abu Sufyan replied, “No.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 7](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:7)[^2][^12][^42]

2. **The same hostile witness said that Muhammad ﷺ was not known for breaking agreements.** Heraclius was questioning an enemy rather than a follower, and Abu Sufyan later admitted that he would have liked to say something damaging if he could.

   > Asked whether Muhammad ﷺ broke covenants, Abu Sufyan said, “No.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 7](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:7)[^2][^12][^42]

3. **Abu Sufyan described Muhammad’s early followers as largely socially weak, while saying that their numbers were growing.** This does not prove revelation, but it matters when considering motive. The message initially associated Muhammad ﷺ with vulnerable followers rather than simply winning him the existing elite.

   > Asked whether his followers were the nobles or the weak, Abu Sufyan replied, “Rather, the weak.” Asked whether they increased, he said, “They increase.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 7](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:7)[^2][^12][^42]

4. **Even his opponent’s summary of the message is morally plain rather than centered on Muhammad himself.** Abu Sufyan told Heraclius that Muhammad ﷺ called people to worship God alone, abandon ancestral idols, pray, speak truth, remain chaste, and maintain family ties.

   > “He tells us to worship God alone... and he orders us to pray, be truthful, remain chaste, and keep family ties.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 7](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:7)[^2][^12][^42]

5. **Khadija knew the private man before there was a public prophetic mission.** At the first revelation, she reassured Muhammad ﷺ by appealing to years of conduct she had already witnessed and concluded that God would not disgrace a man who lived that way.

   > “You keep family ties, support the weak, help the destitute, honor the guest, and assist those struck by calamity.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 3](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3)[^2][^12][^42]

6. **Anas provides a decade of close observation after prophethood began.** A carefully manufactured public persona is much harder to sustain in daily life with a young household servant. Anas did not remember petty domination or constant irritation.

   > “I served the Prophet ﷺ for ten years. He never said ‘uff’ to me.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 6038](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6038)[^2][^12][^42]

7. **Aisha provides another intimate test: she said that he never struck a woman or a servant.**

   > “The Messenger of God ﷺ never struck anyone with his hand, neither a woman nor a servant.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih Muslim 2328a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2328a)[^3][^13][^42]

8. **For years, the claim brought Muhammad ﷺ loss rather than political power.** The Meccan mission meant hostility, insecurity, persecution of his followers, loss of protection, rejection at al-Ta’if, and finally migration. A theory based on simple short-term personal gain fits this phase poorly.

   > “When the two were in the cave, he said to his companion, ‘Do not grieve. God is with us.’”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 9:40](https://quran.com/9/40)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

9. **When political power finally came, his household did not turn into a royal court.** By the end of the mission Muhammad ﷺ led most of Arabia, yet personally owned remarkably little and built no palace. Aisha’s memory of months without cooking fires and the report that his armor remained pledged for food are difficult to fit to a theory centered on personal enrichment.

   > “The Messenger of God ﷺ died while his iron armor was pledged to a Jewish man for thirty measures of barley.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 2916](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2916)[^2][^12][^42]

10. **He closed off obvious routes by which his family could turn prophethood into dynastic wealth.** Muhammad ﷺ taught that what prophets leave behind becomes charity rather than inherited family property, and his household was barred from receiving obligatory alms. When his young grandson Hasan put a charity date in his mouth, Muhammad ﷺ made him remove it. All of Muhammad’s sons died in childhood, and he made no arrangement to place another relative on a hereditary throne.

   > “We prophets are not inherited from. What we leave is charity.”
   >
   > Sources: [Sahih al-Bukhari 4240–4241](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:4240)[^2][^12][^42]; [Sahih al-Bukhari 1491](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:1491)[^2][^12][^42]; [Sahih Muslim 1072a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:1072a)[^3][^13][^42]

11. **He explicitly forbade his followers from exaggerating his religious status.** A founder seeking to maximize his own sacral authority would have every incentive to encourage such praise. Muhammad ﷺ instead restricted it.

   > “Do not exaggerate in praising me... I am only a servant. Say: God’s servant and His Messenger.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 3445](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3445)[^2][^12][^42]

12. **He denied possessing independent knowledge of the unseen.**

   > “If I knew the unseen, I would have acquired much good and no harm would have touched me.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 7:188](https://quran.com/7/188)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

13. **He denied possessing God’s treasures and denied being an angel.**

   > “I do not tell you that God’s treasures are with me, nor that I know the unseen, nor that I am an angel.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 6:50](https://quran.com/6/50)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

14. **He rejected an extraordinarily convenient miracle claim when others supplied it for him.** A solar eclipse occurred on the day his son Ibrahim died, and people linked the two events. For a fraudulent religious leader, the coincidence would have been exceptionally useful. Muhammad ﷺ immediately denied that the eclipse had anything to do with his son’s death.

   > “The sun and moon do not eclipse because of anyone’s death or life.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 1043](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:1043)[^2][^12][^42]

15. **At times he waited for revelation when inventing an immediate answer would have been personally useful.** When Aisha was falsely accused of sexual misconduct, the crisis struck both his household and his public credibility. Her detailed account says that roughly a month passed without revelation clearing her.

   > “No revelation concerning my case came to him for a month.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 2661](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2661)[^2][^12][^42]

16. **During that month, he did not claim certainty that he said he did not yet possess.** He told Aisha that if she was innocent God would clear her, while if she had sinned she should repent. Only afterward did the Qur’anic passage declaring her innocence arrive.

   > “If you are innocent, God will declare your innocence; and if you committed a sin, then seek God’s forgiveness and repent.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 2661](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2661)[^2][^12][^42]

17. **When his agricultural advice proved unhelpful, he distinguished prophetic teaching from ordinary technical judgment.** It would have been easy to protect his prestige by pretending expertise. Instead, he told the farmers that they knew their worldly craft better.

   > “You know better about the technical affairs of your worldly life.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih Muslim 2363](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2363)[^3][^13][^42]

18. **The Qur’an repeatedly places limits on Muhammad’s ﷺ status.** He is a messenger rather than a deity, a human rather than an angel, a recipient rather than the owner of revelation, and a mortal rather than the permanent center of the faith.

   > “Muhammad is only a messenger. Messengers passed away before him.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 3:144](https://quran.com/3/144)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

19. **He repeatedly said that he sought no payment for delivering the message.** This does not by itself prove sincerity, but it counts against explanations that treat his preaching primarily as a paid personal enterprise.

   > “I ask you no payment for it. It is only a reminder for all people.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 12:104](https://quran.com/12/104)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

20. **The Qur’an itself appeals to the long public life he had already lived before revelation.** Muhammad’s ﷺ first audience was not meeting a stranger with an unverifiable past. They had decades of ordinary conduct against which to compare his new claim.

   > “I lived among you for a lifetime before this. Will you not use reason?”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 10:16](https://quran.com/10/16)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

21. **His claim cannot easily be explained as an attempt to recover a lost family throne.** When Heraclius asked Abu Sufyan, who was still an opponent, whether any of Muhammad’s ﷺ ancestors had been a king, he answered no.

   > Heraclius asked, “Was any of his forefathers a king?” Abu Sufyan answered, “No.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 7](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:7)[^2][^12][^42]

22. **Kinship with the Prophet did not become a substitute for personal accountability before God.** Muhammad ﷺ warned his own daughter Fatima that being related to him could not save her apart from her own standing before God. That is a significant restraint for a founder supposedly constructing a privileged spiritual dynasty.

   > “Fatima, daughter of Muhammad, ask me for whatever you wish of my wealth, but I cannot save you from God.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 2753](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2753)[^2][^12][^42]

23. **He warned his followers not to turn his grave into an object of worship.** The warning came during his final illness, when a founder interested in posthumous sacral power might instead have encouraged devotion centered on his tomb. It is consistent with his earlier prohibition of exaggerated praise.

   > “God cursed the Jews and Christians for taking the graves of their prophets as places of prayer.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 1330](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:1330)[^2][^12][^42]

24. **These self-limitations matter more once the scale of his eventual authority is taken seriously.** In Medina there was no higher Arabian court forcing the head of the community to live without a palace, consult others, deny his family obligatory charity, reject worshipful praise, admit technical error, or submit to a text that rebuked him. The restraints are significant precisely because political victory had removed many ordinary external constraints.[^36][^37][^40][^41]

**What this establishes:** none of these facts logically forces the conclusion that Muhammad ﷺ was a prophet. They do, however, fit poorly with the simplest cynical explanations based on money, luxury, family dynasty, easy political advancement, self-deification, or habitual lying. A stronger skeptical account would need to involve something like sincere religious conviction, an unusual psychological experience, or another theory capable of explaining the same pattern.

### B. The Qur’an’s posture toward Muhammad ﷺ is unusual if he were simply composing it for himself

1. **The Qur’an presents Muhammad ﷺ as subject to the revelation rather than in control of it.** When opponents asked him to produce a different recitation or alter the existing one, the response said that he had no authority to change it on his own.

   > “It is not for me to change it on my own. I follow only what is revealed to me.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 10:15](https://quran.com/10/15)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

2. **It publicly corrects him for neglecting a blind man while giving attention to influential Meccans.** Abdullah ibn Umm Maktum came seeking instruction while Muhammad ﷺ was speaking to Meccan notables, and Muhammad frowned and turned away. The criticism became permanent scripture recited by his own followers.

   > “He frowned and turned away because the blind man came to him... Yet as for the one who came to you striving and in reverence, you were distracted from him.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 80:1–10](https://quran.com/80/1-10)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

3. **It corrects him for being too ready to grant certain people permission.**

   > “God pardon you. Why did you give them permission before it became clear to you who spoke truthfully?”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 9:43](https://quran.com/9/43)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

4. **It corrects him for restricting something lawful to himself in an attempt to please members of his household.**

   > “O Prophet, why do you prohibit what God has made lawful for you, seeking to please your wives?”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 66:1](https://quran.com/66/1)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

5. **It preserves a personally uncomfortable criticism in the episode involving Zayd and Zaynab.** Whatever interpretation one gives the event, a text written simply to protect Muhammad’s ﷺ image had no obvious reason to preserve the statement that he feared people’s reaction.

   > “You concealed within yourself what God would disclose, and you feared the people, while God had greater right that you fear Him.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 33:37](https://quran.com/33/37)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

6. **It threatens Muhammad ﷺ himself with severe punishment if he were to invent words and attribute them to God.** A human author could of course write such a threat, so the verse is not self-proving. But it places the supposed author under an unusually severe condemnation of the very fraud of which a skeptic would accuse him.

   > “Had he falsely attributed to Us some sayings, We would have seized him by the right hand, then severed his life-vein.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 69:44–46](https://quran.com/69/44-46)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

7. **It warns that he himself would face multiplied punishment if he yielded to pressure and falsely attributed a compromise to God.**

   > “Had We not kept you firm, you might have inclined to them a little. Then We would have made you taste double in life and double in death.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 17:74–75](https://quran.com/17/74-75)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

8. **It corrects him over speaking too confidently about what he will do in the future without acknowledging God’s will.**

   > “Never say of anything, ‘I will surely do that tomorrow,’ without adding, ‘if God wills.’”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 18:23–24](https://quran.com/18/23-24)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

9. **It tells him not to rush his recitation while revelation is being received.** The passage portrays Muhammad ﷺ trying to retain words being delivered to him and then being told that their collection and recitation are God’s responsibility.

   > “Do not move your tongue with it to hasten it. Upon Us is its collection and its recitation.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 75:16–17](https://quran.com/75/16-17)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

10. **Another passage similarly tells him not to hurry with the Qur’an before its revelation to him is complete.**

   > “Do not hasten with the Qur’an before its revelation to you is completed, and say: ‘My Lord, increase me in knowledge.’”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 20:114](https://quran.com/20/114)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

11. **It says explicitly that he did not possess this scripture before it was revealed to him.**

   > “Before this you did not recite any scripture, nor did you write it with your right hand; otherwise the falsifiers would have had grounds for doubt.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 29:48](https://quran.com/29/48)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

12. **It presents the contents of the revealed narratives as knowledge Muhammad ﷺ did not previously possess.**

   > “You did not know what the Book was, nor the faith, but We made it a light by which We guide whom We will.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 42:52](https://quran.com/42/52)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

13. **It invites readers to examine it for internal contradiction.** Issuing the challenge does not itself make the text divine, but it shows that consistency across the revelation is one of the tests the Qur’an asks readers to consider.

   > “Do they not reflect deeply on the Qur’an? Had it been from other than God, they would have found in it much inconsistency.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 4:82](https://quran.com/4/82)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

14. **It issues a literary challenge to its first Arabic audience.** The argument is not that an English translation possesses some objectively measurable magical quality. It concerns the Arabic itself: its form, rhetoric, structure, sound, compression of meaning, and the response of people competent in the language.[^33][^34][^35][^46][^47][^77][^78]

   > “If you are in doubt about what We have sent down upon Our servant, then produce a chapter like it.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 2:23](https://quran.com/2/23)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

15. **The challenge is expressed at different scales, from producing one comparable chapter to confronting the recitation as a whole.**

   > “Say: If humanity and the jinn gathered to produce the like of this Qur’an, they could not produce its like, even if they helped one another.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 17:88](https://quran.com/17/88)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

16. **God, not Muhammad ﷺ, is the center of the Qur’an.** The text repeatedly recounts earlier prophets, corrects Muhammad, limits his powers, and calls him a messenger. This does not prove divine authorship, but it does not read naturally as a work whose chief purpose is the human speaker’s self-glorification.

   > “Say: I am only a human being like you. It is revealed to me that your God is One God.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 18:110](https://quran.com/18/110)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

17. **The message places itself in continuity with Abraham, Moses, Jesus, and earlier revelation rather than introducing a new tribal deity.**

   > “Say: We believe in God and what was sent down to us, and what was sent down to Abraham, Ishmael, Isaac, Jacob... Moses, Jesus, and the prophets.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 3:84](https://quran.com/3/84)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

18. **It repeatedly treats the natural world as something to observe and reason about.** These verses are strongest when read on their own terms rather than forced into modern scientific terminology. Their clear intellectual claim is that the physical world is ordered, intelligible, and worthy of reflection.

   > “In the creation of the heavens and the earth and the alternation of night and day are signs for people of understanding.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 3:190](https://quran.com/3/190)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

19. **The Qur’an makes an explicit claim that God will preserve the revelation.**

   > “Indeed, We have sent down the Reminder, and We will surely preserve it.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 15:9](https://quran.com/15/9)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

20. **Very early manuscript evidence makes that preservation claim historically significant.** The Birmingham Qur’an leaves contain portions of chapters 18–20 in an early Arabic script. Radiocarbon dating places the parchment between 568 and 645 CE with 95.4% probability, while palaeographic assessment dates the manuscript to the mid-seventh century. The carbon test dates the parchment rather than the ink, so the evidence should not be claimed to show more than it does.[^46][^48][^49][^50][^51]

   > “We will surely preserve it.”
   >
   > Sources: [Qur’an 15:9](https://quran.com/15/9)[^1][^10][^11][^35]; [University of Birmingham, manuscript and dating details](https://www.birmingham.ac.uk/facilities/cadbury/TheBirminghamQuranManuscript)

21. **The case for Qur’anic preservation is strongest when it is stated without exaggeration.** Early manuscripts, the early standard consonantal text, mass recitation, and regulated canonical reading traditions together provide a very strong transmission picture. Canonical reading variants and early differences in orthography also exist. They belong to the history of Qur’anic transmission and should be discussed openly rather than hidden.[^46][^48][^49][^50][^51]

   > “Indeed, upon Us is its collection and its recitation.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 75:17](https://quran.com/75/17)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

22. **The Qur’an not only forbids him from changing revelation; it makes complete delivery of it an obligation.** That removes another convenient form of authorial control: withholding material when delivering it becomes personally costly.

   > “O Messenger, convey what has been sent down to you from your Lord. If you do not, then you have not conveyed His message.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 5:67](https://quran.com/5/67)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

23. **When opponents demanded spectacular signs on their own terms, the Qur’an refused to turn Muhammad ﷺ into a supernatural performer.** They asked for springs, gardens, a house of gold, angels, or a book physically brought down to them. The response was that he was a human messenger; elsewhere, the recited Book itself is presented as sufficient. He is also told to deny independent knowledge even of what will happen to him and his audience.

   > “Say: Glory be to my Lord. Am I anything but a human messenger?”
   >
   > “Is it not enough for them that We have sent down to you the Book that is recited to them?”
   >
   > “I do not know what will be done with me or with you. I only follow what is revealed to me.”
   >
   > Sources: [Qur’an 17:90–93](https://quran.com/17/90-93)[^1][^10][^11][^35]; [Qur’an 29:51](https://quran.com/29/51)[^1][^10][^11][^35]; [Qur’an 46:9](https://quran.com/46/9)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

24. **The Qur’an could place additional burdens on Muhammad ﷺ rather than simply grant him privileges.** The earliest revelations command night worship with unusual force, and classical Muslim interpretation commonly treated the night vigil as a special obligation for him while it remained voluntary for the wider community. Whatever one concludes about the exact legal classification, the text points toward additional discipline rather than easy exemption.

   > “Rise in prayer through the night, except a little...”
   >
   > “And during part of the night, keep vigil with it as an additional devotion for you.”
   >
   > Sources: [Qur’an 73:1–4](https://quran.com/73/1-4)[^1][^10][^11][^35]; [Qur’an 17:79](https://quran.com/17/79)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

25. **It also places personal limits on him during the height of his Medinan authority.** Qur’an 33:52 closes further marriages to him under the terms stated there, while an earlier passage tells his existing wives that if they prefer worldly comfort he is to release them graciously. Neither restriction is an obvious addition for a text supposedly written simply to maximize its author’s domestic options.

   > “It is not lawful for you to marry further women after this, nor to exchange them for other wives, even if their beauty impresses you...”
   >
   > “If you desire the life of this world and its adornment, then come: I will provide for you and release you graciously.”
   >
   > Sources: [Qur’an 33:52](https://quran.com/33/52)[^1][^10][^11][^35]; [Qur’an 33:28–29](https://quran.com/33/28-29)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

26. **The Qur’an sounds recognizably different from the ordinary speech attributed to Muhammad ﷺ.** Hadith preserve conversations, jokes, legal explanations, household remarks, and political instructions, while the Qur’an maintains a markedly different diction, cadence, structure, and scriptural voice. This is not a straightforward stylometric proof, especially because many hadith were transmitted by meaning. It does, however, reinforce the observation that revelation did not simply appear as ordinary speech whenever an answer was convenient. The month-long delay during Aisha’s accusation makes the same point through timing rather than style.[^33][^34][^35][^46][^47][^78]

**What this establishes:** every point above can be given a skeptical explanation in isolation. A human author can write self-criticism, issue a challenge, or claim preservation. The harder question is why the text so consistently constrains, corrects, and decenters the man who would supposedly be using it for his own interests, while maintaining a sustained theological voice across very different stages of his mission.

### C. How the hadith record was preserved and tested

Much of the evidence in this article about Muhammad’s ﷺ character, conduct, predictions, and reported miracles comes through hadith. The hadith record therefore has to be examined rather than taken for granted. The classical Muslim system for criticizing transmission is historically unusual in its scale and detail, but it does not make every report automatically true. Modern historians also debate how confidently later chains of transmission can recover events from Islam’s first century. Even with those cautions, it is inaccurate to treat the surviving material as an undifferentiated collection of anonymous legends.

1. **Hadith reports were normally accompanied by named *isnads* [chains of transmission].** The chain records who claimed to have received the report from whom, reaching back toward an earlier witness. This gave critics a stated history of transmission to investigate rather than leaving a saying as an anonymous maxim.

   > “The isnad is part of the religion. Were it not for the isnad, whoever wished could say whatever he wished.”
   >
   > Source: Abdullah ibn al-Mubarak, cited by Muslim in the introduction to *Sahih Muslim*[^3][^13][^21][^22][^42]

2. **Evaluating those chains produced a vast biographical literature on the transmitters themselves.** Hadith critics recorded dates, locations, teachers, students, memory, deterioration late in life, and reputations for honesty. Works such as al-Mizzi’s *Tahdhib al-Kamal*, Ibn Hajar’s later abridgments, and al-Dhahabi’s biographical writings preserve judgments on many thousands of transmitters.[^23][^24][^25][^32][^42]

3. **The same critical standards could be applied to teachers, relatives, and respected insiders.** A well-known example is Ali ibn al-Madini, an important teacher of al-Bukhari, judging his own father too weak to serve as a reliable hadith authority. The purpose of *al-jarh wa-l-ta‘dil* [criticism and accreditation of transmitters] was precisely to keep affection or status from rescuing a defective chain.[^21][^23][^25][^32]

4. **Reports were not treated as equally credible simply because they were called hadith.** Critics distinguished grades such as *sahih* [sound], *hasan* [good], and weak, and they compiled works devoted to reports they considered fabricated. Ibn al-Jawzi’s *al-Mawdu‘at*, for example, catalogued sayings he judged forged rather than pretending that false attributions did not exist.[^22][^26][^32][^42]

5. **Written transmission existed alongside oral transmission well before the major ninth-century compilations.** The *Sahifa* [written collection] associated with Hammam ibn Munabbih, a student of the Companion Abu Hurayra, represents an early written hadith collection transmitted into later manuscripts. Many of its reports have parallels in Bukhari and Muslim through their own chains. The surviving manuscripts are not Hammam’s autograph, so the evidence should not be overstated, but they weigh against the idea that hadith first began to be written only in al-Bukhari’s time.[^30][^31][^42][^43][^44][^45]

6. **Scholars sometimes traveled great distances simply to hear a report from a nearer source or investigate its transmission.** The practice became known as *al-rihla fi talab al-hadith* [travel in search of hadith]. It reflects a scholarly culture in which verifying or shortening a chain could be considered worth months of travel.[^21][^22][^30][^42]

7. **The canonical collections were filtered selections, not archives of everything then in circulation.** Al-Bukhari knew far more reports and transmission routes than he included in his *Sahih*. Rejected material was therefore not an accidental embarrassment outside the method. Distinguishing stronger reports from weaker ones was part of the method’s purpose.[^22][^42]

8. **Classical scholars distinguished ordinary reports from *mutawatir* [mass-transmitted] reports.** The latter category was reserved for material transmitted through enough independent routes that deliberate coordinated fabrication was judged implausible. Most individual hadith are not *mutawatir*. That is precisely why “found in a hadith book” and “mass-transmitted” should not be treated as equivalent claims.[^22][^32][^42]

This method explains how Muslim scholarship can accept some reports, weaken others, and identify statements falsely attributed to Muhammad ﷺ. It also explains why the date of a source still matters here. The major canonical collections were compiled generations after the Prophet, so a prediction fulfilled before compilation remains open to the skeptical possibility that its wording was shaped after the event. A prediction whose canonical written source demonstrably predates the event, such as the report later connected with the 1256 Hijaz eruption, has a different evidentiary advantage.

For an academic introduction to the discipline and its modern criticisms, see Jonathan A. C. Brown, *Hadith: Muhammad’s Legacy in the Medieval and Modern World* (Oneworld, 2009; later editions).

### D. Specific predictions and reported fulfillments

Predictions have evidential value only when their sources are handled carefully. A prediction is most useful when its wording is specific, there is good reason to date it before the event, the predicted outcome was outside the speaker’s control, and the match does not depend on stretching the language. Qur’anic predictions can be compared with early textual witnesses. Hadith require an additional caution because the canonical collections were compiled generations after Muhammad ﷺ, even though their compilers used named transmission chains and earlier material. A skeptic can therefore argue that an individual report was shaped after the event. The examples below should not be treated as though they all have the same degree of historical independence.

1. **The Qur’an predicted a Byzantine recovery after a devastating defeat.** The Eastern Roman, or Byzantine, Empire had suffered severe losses to the Sasanian Persians. The Qur’an nevertheless said that the Romans would reverse their defeat “within a few years.”[^62][^63]

   > “The Romans have been defeated in the nearest land. Yet after their defeat they will be victorious, within a few years.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 30:2–4](https://quran.com/30/2-4)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

2. **The historical reversal was substantial and dramatic.** After Persian advances that included the capture of Jerusalem in 614, Emperor Heraclius began a counteroffensive in 622. Byzantine victories followed, and the Battle of Nineveh in 627 broke Persian resistance. On the traditional Meccan dating of Qur’an 30, the recovery fits naturally within the prediction’s time frame. The strength of the argument still depends partly on how the passage is dated and on what stage of the recovery counts as “victory,” so it is better described as a strong historical correspondence than as an indisputable proof.[^62][^63]

   > “Yet after their defeat they will be victorious, within a few years. To God belongs the command before and after.”
   >
   > Sources: [Qur’an 30:3–4](https://quran.com/30/3-4)[^1][^10][^11][^35]; [S. E. Dean, “Battle of Nineveh,” 2016](https://www.jstor.org/stable/48578573)

3. **A Meccan Qur’anic passage said that the hostile “assembly” would be routed.** Qur’an 54:45 says the gathered force would be defeated and turn its back. Aisha’s report in Bukhari places the adjacent verse in Mecca while she was still a young girl, well before the Battle of Badr.

   > “The assembly will be defeated, and they will turn their backs.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 54:45](https://quran.com/54/45)[^1][^10][^11][^35]; Meccan setting of the adjacent verse: [Sahih al-Bukhari 4876](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:4876)[^2][^12][^42]

4. **At Badr, Muhammad ﷺ recited this prediction before facing the Meccan force, which was then defeated.** Bukhari’s account of the battle places the verse on his lips before its outcome was known.

   > He went out in armor reciting, “The assembly will be defeated, and they will turn their backs.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 4875](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:4875)[^2][^12][^42]

5. **At Hudaybiyyah, the Qur’an promised that the believers would enter the Sacred Mosque safely even though they had just been denied entry.** The wording presents the entry as future rather than as something already accomplished.

   > “God has certainly fulfilled His Messenger’s vision in truth: you will surely enter the Sacred Mosque, if God wills, in security.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 48:27](https://quran.com/48/27)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

6. **The treaty itself set the conditions for the delayed fulfillment.** Quraysh refused entry that year but agreed that the Muslims could return the following year. Bukhari’s Hudaybiyyah account records both the initial frustration and the later entry.

   > The negotiator said, in effect, “We will not allow you this year... but we will allow you next year.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 2731–2732](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2731)[^2][^12][^42]

7. **Muhammad ﷺ told Fatima that she would be the first member of his household to follow him in death.** During his final illness, he privately told her that he expected his own death and that she would be the first of his family to join him.

   > “I think my death has drawn near, and you will be the first of my family to follow me.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 3623–3624](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3623)[^2][^12][^42]

8. **The same hadith corpus separately records that Fatima survived him by only about six months.** The fulfillment is precise within the transmitted tradition, although the surviving canonical compilation postdates both events.

   > “She remained alive for six months after the death of the Prophet.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 4240–4241](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:4240)[^2][^12][^42]

9. **He predicted that his young grandson Hasan would one day reconcile two large Muslim groups.**

   > “This son of mine is a noble leader, and perhaps God will reconcile through him two great groups of Muslims.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 2704](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2704)[^2][^12][^42]

10. **The same Bukhari report narrates the later reconciliation.** In 661 Hasan reached an agreement with Muawiya and withdrew from a contest that could have prolonged a major Muslim civil war. The report is unusually self-contained because the narrator recounts both the reconciliation and the earlier saying.

   > The report describes Hasan concluding peace, then recalls Muhammad’s ﷺ words that God would “reconcile through him two great groups of Muslims.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 2704](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2704)[^2][^12][^42]

11. **He said that Ammar ibn Yasir would be killed by a rebellious group.** The statement is specific about both Ammar’s death and the moral direction of the conflict in which it would occur.

   > “Ammar will be killed by a rebellious group. Ammar will call them toward God, while they will call him toward the Fire.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 2812](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2812)[^2][^12][^42]

12. **Ammar was later killed while fighting on Ali’s side at the Battle of Siffin in 657.** The event became a major point of interpretation because the saying was remembered within the Muslim tradition. Its value as independent prophetic evidence still depends on how early the transmission of the report can be established.[^64][^65]

   > “Ammar will be killed by a rebellious group.”
   >
   > Sources: [Sahih al-Bukhari 2812](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2812)[^2][^12][^42]; historical context: [Encyclopedia.com, “Ammar Bin Yasir”](https://www.encyclopedia.com/politics/dictionaries-thesauruses-pictures-and-press-releases/ammar-bin-yasir-ammar-ibn-yaser)

13. **He called Umar and Uthman “two martyrs” while both men were still alive.** In a report set on Mount Uhud, Muhammad ﷺ was with Abu Bakr, Umar, and Uthman and identified the last two as martyrs.

   > “Be firm, Uhud. Upon you are only a prophet, a truthful one, and two martyrs.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 3675](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3675)[^2][^12][^42]

14. **Both Umar and Uthman later died violently rather than from ordinary illness.** Umar was assassinated in 644, and Uthman was killed during a siege of his home in 656. As with the report about Ammar, the critical question concerns the age of the saying’s transmission, not whether the later deaths occurred.[^64][^65]

   > “Upon you are... two martyrs.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 3675](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3675)[^2][^12][^42]

15. **He made a cluster of striking forecasts to Adi ibn Hatim: secure travel through Arabia, the opening of the Persian king’s treasures, and unusual prosperity.** Adi remembered privately thinking at the time about the bandits who made such travel dangerous.

   > “You will surely see a woman travel from Hira to circle the Kaaba, fearing none but God... and the treasures of Khosrau will be opened.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 3595](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3595)[^2][^12][^42]

16. **In the same report, Adi says that he personally witnessed the first two fulfillments.** He lived to see a woman make the journey safely and says that he himself was among those who opened the treasures of Khosrau. The narrator’s claim to have witnessed the fulfillments is notable, even though the surviving written collection is later.

   > Adi said, “I saw a woman travel from Hira... and I was among those who opened the treasures of Khosrau.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 3595](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3595)[^2][^12][^42]

17. **During the difficult Tabuk period, he named his own death, the conquest of Jerusalem, and a deadly plague in a sequence of future events.**

   > “Count six things before the Hour: my death, then the conquest of Jerusalem, then a widespread death among you like a disease among sheep...”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 3176](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3176)[^2][^12][^42]

18. **Jerusalem came under Muslim rule only a few years after his death, and the plague of Amwas struck Syria in 638–639.** The plague is especially interesting because the conquest and the epidemic are separate events within the predicted sequence. Again, the hadith’s later compilation means that this is not equivalent to a modern prediction preserved in a securely dated pre-event document.[^64][^66]

   > “My death, then the conquest of Jerusalem, then a widespread death among you...”
   >
   > Sources: [Sahih al-Bukhari 3176](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3176)[^2][^12][^42]; [Fordham sourcebook on the Plague of Amwas](https://sourcebooks.web.fordham.edu/source/dols-plagueofamwas.asp)

19. **He foretold that members of his community would undertake a sea expedition and named Umm Haram among the first participants.** At the time, Arabian Muslims were not a maritime imperial power.

   > He described people of his community “riding upon the sea” and told Umm Haram, “You will be among the first.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 2788–2789](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2788)[^2][^12][^42]

20. **The same report later describes Umm Haram sailing during Muawiya’s period and dying after she disembarked.** The prediction and its reported fulfillment are therefore preserved within the same narrative.

   > “She sailed on the sea during the caliphate of Muawiya... and after she disembarked, she fell from her riding animal and died.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 2788–2789](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2788)[^2][^12][^42]

21. **During the period of Muslim weakness in Mecca, he forecast secure travel across southern Arabia.** Khabbab’s report begins with persecuted believers asking for relief, making the promise of such security more striking in its original context.

   > “God will complete this matter until a traveler goes from Sana to Hadramawt fearing none but God, except a wolf for his sheep.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 3612](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3612)[^2][^12][^42]

22. **He described once-poor shepherds competing in tall or magnificent buildings as a sign near the end of history.** The modern skylines of the Arabian Peninsula make the saying naturally striking to many readers. It should still be handled carefully: the statement is broad, eschatological, and recorded after Muhammad’s ﷺ lifetime. The modern visual resemblance does not by itself make the report conclusive proof.

   > “You will see barefoot, destitute shepherds competing with one another in constructing magnificent buildings.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih Muslim 8a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:8a)[^3][^13][^42]

23. **A report about a later extremist faction contains a highly specific physical description.** Muhammad ﷺ is reported to have said that among the dissidents would be a man with one malformed arm resembling a breast or fleshy appendage. The report then says that, after fighting the group, Ali ordered the bodies searched and the described man was found.

   > One man among them would have “a hand like a woman’s breast or a lump of flesh.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih Muslim 1066g](https://sunnah.com/muslim:1066g)[^3][^13][^42]

   Other reports describe the same kind of violent religious faction in moral and verbal terms: they would recite the Qur’an intensely while its meaning “did not go beyond their throats” and would pass out of the religion with startling speed. Muslim historical memory identified these descriptions with the Khawarij who emerged during the first civil war. As with the physical description above, the later compilation date makes this transmitted prophetic evidence rather than a prediction preserved in a surviving pre-event manuscript.

   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 3610](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3610)[^2][^12][^42]; see also [Sahih al-Bukhari 6930](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6930)[^2][^12][^42]

24. **This example shows why the date of the surviving source matters.** A detailed match preserved only in later writing can be explained either as faithfully transmitted foreknowledge or as a tradition shaped after the event. It can contribute to a cumulative case, but it should not be presented as though a dated seventh-century manuscript of the saying had survived from before the battle.

   > Ali said during the search, “By God, I have not lied, nor was I lied to,” and the body was then found according to the report.
   >
   > Source: [Sahih Muslim 1066g](https://sunnah.com/muslim:1066g)[^3][^13][^42]

25. **The Qur’an promised a frightened and insecure believing community that it would eventually be established in security.** The promise is broader than the Byzantine prediction and is conditional on faith and righteous conduct. Even so, it is worth comparing with the community’s rapid movement from threatened survival to political security in Arabia.

   > “God has promised those among you who believe and do righteous deeds that He will surely grant them succession in the land... and will surely exchange their fear for security.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 24:55](https://quran.com/24/55)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

26. **The Qur’an also forecast the public ascendancy of the religion in a chapter revealed around Hudaybiyyah, just after the Muslims had been kept out of Mecca.** Within two years Mecca came under Muhammad’s ﷺ authority, and within the first Muslim generations Islam spread far beyond Arabia. Because the language of “prevail” can be understood in more than one way, the expansion is better treated as a strong historical correspondence than as a precisely dated prediction.

   > “He is the One who sent His Messenger with guidance and the religion of truth, to make it prevail over all religion.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 48:28](https://quran.com/48/28)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

27. **One hadith prediction has an unusual advantage because its major written sources clearly predate the event commonly identified as its fulfillment.** Muhammad ﷺ is reported to have said that before the end of history a great fire would emerge from the Hijaz, the western Arabian region containing Mecca and Medina, with light reaching as far as Busra in Syria. The prediction appears in both Bukhari and Muslim.

   > “The Hour will not come until a fire emerges from the land of the Hijaz, illuminating the necks of the camels at Busra.”
   >
   > Sources: [Sahih al-Bukhari 7118](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:7118)[^2][^12][^42]; [Sahih Muslim 2902](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2902)[^3][^13][^42]

28. **A major volcanic eruption occurred beside Medina in 1256 CE, centuries after Bukhari and Muslim were compiled.** Modern geological work confirms that the Harrat Rahat eruption produced a lava flow about 23 kilometers long and brought it within roughly 4 kilometers of Medina. Medieval Muslim accounts also describe extraordinary nighttime visibility over long distances, though those descriptions may themselves have been influenced by knowledge of the hadith. The safest independent comparison is therefore the central one: a great “fire” did emerge in the Hijaz near Medina centuries after the canonical collections containing the prediction already existed.[^67]

   > “A fire emerges from the land of the Hijaz...”
   >
   > Sources: [Sahih al-Bukhari 7118](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:7118)[^2][^12][^42]; [U.S. Geological Survey, eruptive history near Al Madinah](https://www.usgs.gov/publications/eruptive-history-within-vicinity-al-madinah-northern-harrat-rahat-kingdom-saudi-arabia); [Smithsonian Global Volcanism Program, Harrat Rahat](https://volcano.si.edu/volcano.cfm?vn=231070)

29. **The Qur’an publicly named Abu Lahab, Muhammad’s ﷺ fiercely hostile uncle, as a man headed for ruin and punishment while he still had years left to oppose the mission.** The evidential interest is that the text commits itself publicly regarding a named living opponent rather than making only anonymous future claims. A common apologetic argument says that Abu Lahab could simply have pretended to convert and falsified the verse, but that goes too far because a false profession could have been described as hypocrisy. The narrower point is firmer: the Qur’an made a public claim about a living adversary, and Abu Lahab remained an opponent until his death.

   > “May the hands of Abu Lahab perish, and may he perish. His wealth and what he earned will not benefit him. He will burn in a flaming Fire.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 111:1–3](https://quran.com/111/1-3)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

30. **A hadith also speaks in sweeping terms about the great imperial rulers surrounding Arabia, Khosrau and Caesar.** The Sasanian monarchy collapsed during the first generation of Muslim expansion. Byzantium survived for centuries but permanently lost its Near Eastern heartlands to the new polity. Because the phrase “no Caesar after him” has required interpretation, and because the hadith was canonically compiled after those early conquests, this example is less independent than the later Hijaz-fire report. It can still form part of the cumulative record, but it should be weighted accordingly.

   > “When Khosrau is ruined, there will be no Khosrau after him; and when Caesar is ruined, there will be no Caesar after him... and their treasures will be spent in God’s cause.”
   >
   > Sources: [Sahih al-Bukhari 3027](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3027)[^2][^12][^42]; [Sahih al-Bukhari 3028](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3028)[^2][^12][^42]

### E. Reported miracles, and what kind of evidence they are

The hadith collections preserve many reports of miracles. A believer who accepts the soundness of the hadith tradition will naturally regard them as direct signs of prophethood. For a skeptical historian, they remain **testimonial evidence**: transmitted claims that extraordinary events were witnessed. The testimony should not be dismissed merely because the event described is miraculous, but neither should a written report be treated like a modern recording. Its weight depends on such factors as the number and independence of transmission routes, the public or private setting, the specificity of the claim, and the reliability of those who transmitted it.[^17][^19][^20][^36][^37][^38]

1. **The Qur’an itself is presented as Muhammad’s ﷺ enduring public sign.** It was not a private vision. It was recited openly, memorized, written, challenged, and transmitted as the central content of his mission.

   > “If you are in doubt about what We sent down upon Our servant, then produce a chapter like it.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 2:23](https://quran.com/2/23)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

2. **Water was reported to flow from between his fingers in front of a large group.** Jabir’s account at Hudaybiyyah says that when the companions needed water, a small vessel was brought to Muhammad ﷺ and enough water flowed for the group to drink and wash. When asked how many people were present, Jabir said about fifteen hundred.

   > Jabir said, “I saw water flowing from between his fingers... Had we been a hundred thousand, it would have been enough for us.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 3576](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3576)[^2][^12][^42]

   Ibn Masud transmitted another water-multiplication report from a different journey, giving a separate companion route for the same broad kind of claim.

   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 3579](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3579)[^2][^12][^42]

3. **A separate Hudaybiyyah report concerns a depleted well.** Bukhari’s treaty narrative says that when the group complained of thirst, Muhammad ﷺ had an arrow placed in the well and water continued to spring until everyone had drunk. The two Hudaybiyyah water reports arise in distinct narrative contexts.

   > “The water continued springing up for them until they had quenched their thirst and departed.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 2731–2732](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2731)[^2][^12][^42]

4. **A small meal was reported to feed roughly a thousand people during the Battle of the Trench.** Jabir privately invited Muhammad ﷺ to food his household had prepared from a small goat and a measure of barley. Muhammad ﷺ invited the whole digging party. Jabir says that they ate in groups until everyone was satisfied and food still remained.

   > “They were one thousand. I swear by God, they all ate until they left it and went away, while our pot was still bubbling.”
   >
   > Sources: [Sahih al-Bukhari 4101–4102](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:4101)[^2][^12][^42]; [Sahih Muslim 2039](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2039)[^3][^13][^42]

5. **The splitting of the moon is associated explicitly with Muhammad ﷺ in both the Qur’an and hadith.** Ibn Masud’s report in Bukhari says that the moon appeared divided into two parts and that Muhammad ﷺ told those present to bear witness.

   > “The Hour has drawn near, and the moon has split.”
   >
   > Sources: [Qur’an 54:1](https://quran.com/54/1)[^1][^10][^11][^35]; [Sahih al-Bukhari 3636](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3636)[^2][^12][^42]; [Sahih al-Bukhari 3637](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3637)[^2][^12][^42], an Anas report that Meccans asked for a sign

   The next Qur’anic verse adds an important detail: it portrays opponents as seeing a “sign” and calling it magic rather than saying that nothing had happened. This matters within the Qur’an’s own public account of the dispute, but it is not independent non-Muslim confirmation of the event.

   > “Whenever they see a sign, they turn away and say, ‘Persistent magic.’”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 54:2](https://quran.com/54/2)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

6. **The moon report should be presented as transmitted miracle testimony, not as external astronomical proof.** There is no need to fill the absence of a securely established independent contemporary astronomical record with dubious stories from the internet. The actual evidential question is how much weight one gives the Qur’anic wording and the multiple Islamic reports.

   > Ibn Masud said, “The moon was split into two parts during the lifetime of the Prophet ﷺ... and the Prophet said, ‘Bear witness.’”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 3636](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3636)[^2][^12][^42]

7. **A palm trunk was reported to cry after Muhammad ﷺ stopped leaning on it during sermons.** Once a pulpit had been built, Ibn Umar said that the trunk made a crying sound until Muhammad ﷺ went to it and rubbed his hand over it.

   > “The trunk began to cry, so the Prophet ﷺ went to it and stroked it with his hand.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 3583](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3583)[^2][^12][^42]

8. **Another version compares the sound to a child crying and says that Muhammad ﷺ embraced the trunk until it became quiet.** Variant reports preserving the same central event are one reason Muslim hadith scholars regarded the episode as widely attested.

   > “The date-palm cried like a child. The Prophet ﷺ came down and embraced it while it moaned like a child being soothed.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 3584](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3584)[^2][^12][^42]

9. **Ali’s severe eye condition at Khaybar was reported to heal immediately after Muhammad ﷺ prayed for him and treated his eyes.** The incident is placed in a public military setting at the moment the standard was handed to Ali.

   > “He applied saliva to his eyes and prayed for him, and he recovered at once as though he had never had the ailment.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 3009](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3009)[^2][^12][^42]

10. **A prayer for rain is reported with an unusually concrete sequence of events.** Anas said that a man interrupted a Friday sermon to ask for rain while the sky was clear. Muhammad ﷺ prayed, clouds gathered, and heavy rain followed. The next Friday people asked for relief from excessive rain, and when he prayed for it to fall around Medina, the clouds separated.

   > “O God, around us and not upon us.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 3582](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3582)[^2][^12][^42]

11. **He reported that a stone in Mecca used to greet him before his prophetic mission.**

   > “I know a stone in Mecca that used to greet me before I was sent as a prophet. I know it even now.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih Muslim 2277](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2277)[^3][^13][^42]

12. **The Night Journey is claimed directly in the Qur’an.** The text says that God took His servant by night from the Sacred Mosque in Mecca to the “farthest mosque,” or place of worship, traditionally identified with Jerusalem, in order to show him divine signs.

   > “Glory be to the One who took His servant by night from the Sacred Mosque to the Farthest Mosque, whose surroundings We blessed, to show him some of Our signs.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 17:1](https://quran.com/17/1)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

   Hadith adds a detail about the public dispute: when Quraysh challenged his report, Muhammad ﷺ said that Jerusalem was displayed before him and that he described its features while looking at it. Early biography also portrays the claim as costly rather than conveniently persuasive, remembering some hearers recoiling from it while Abu Bakr accepted Muhammad’s word. The hadith report has stronger transmission than the later biographical detail, and neither amounts to independent archaeological verification. Together, however, they show that Muslim memory placed the claim into public dispute immediately.

   > “When Quraysh disbelieved me, I stood in the Hijr, and God displayed Jerusalem before me, and I began describing its signs to them while I was looking at it.”
   >
   > Sources: [Sahih al-Bukhari 3886](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3886)[^2][^12][^42]; [Sahih Muslim 170](https://sunnah.com/muslim:170)[^3][^13][^42]; early sira for the reactions attributed to other hearers[^8][^18][^27][^36][^37]

13. **The miracle reports are a different kind of argument from the moral portrait.** If they are historically reliable, they directly support a supernatural mission. If a reader remains uncertain about miracle testimony, the earlier evidence from Muhammad’s ﷺ character, the Qur’an’s posture toward him, and the predictions can still be assessed independently.

   > “We do not send signs except as a warning.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 17:59](https://quran.com/17/59)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

### F. What happened to people who actually encountered him

Conversion by itself proves little; false movements can also attract devoted followers. What makes this record more interesting is the range of people involved. They include a wife who knew Muhammad ﷺ before his mission, a servant who lived with him for a decade, an enemy questioned by a foreign ruler, a hostile chief held as a prisoner and then released, a political negotiator who had seen imperial courts, and former enemies who later described how their judgment of him changed.[^27][^28][^36][^37][^38][^40][^41]

1. **Khadija believed him before there was any worldly success to join.** Her first response to the revelation rested on the character she had already observed as his wife.

   > “By God, God will never disgrace you.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 3](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3)[^2][^12][^42]

2. **Waraqa ibn Nawfal, who was familiar with earlier scripture, understood Muhammad’s ﷺ first experience within the line of biblical prophecy.** According to Bukhari, he identified the messenger of revelation with the one sent to Moses and immediately predicted that Muhammad would face opposition.

   > “This is the bearer of divine secrets whom God sent to Moses. I wish I were young when your people drive you out.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 3](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3)[^2][^12][^42]

3. **Abu Sufyan gave useful testimony while he was still Muhammad’s ﷺ opponent.** He rejected the prophetic claim, but when asked about specific matters of character he acknowledged that Muhammad had not previously been accused of lying and was not known for breaking agreements.

   > Asked whether they had accused him of lying before, Abu Sufyan said, “No.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 7](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:7)[^2][^12][^42]

4. **Heraclius’s reasoning is relevant even without treating the emperor as an authority on prophethood.** He argued from ordinary human credibility: a man who had not been known to lie about people was, in his judgment, an unlikely person suddenly to invent lies about God.

   > Heraclius said in substance: “I knew that if he did not lie about people, he would not lie about God.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 7](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:7)[^2][^12][^42]

5. **Urwa ibn Masud observed Muhammad’s ﷺ community as a negotiator for the opposing side at Hudaybiyyah.** Urwa had seen the courts of Caesar, Khosrau, and the Negus. He returned to Quraysh saying that he had never seen followers respect a ruler as Muhammad’s companions respected him, and he urged Quraysh to accept the proposed agreement.

   > “I have visited kings... yet I have never seen any of them respected by his companions as Muhammad is respected by his companions.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 2731–2732](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2731)[^2][^12][^42]

6. **Thumama ibn Uthal changed sides after Muhammad ﷺ released him without extracting payment.** Thumama had been captured and held in the mosque for several days, and he offered wealth if ransom was wanted. Muhammad ﷺ ultimately ordered him released. Thumama left, washed, returned of his own choice, and declared his faith.

   > “There was no face on earth more hateful to me than yours; now your face has become the most beloved face to me.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 4372](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:4372)[^2][^12][^42]

7. **Thumama said that his feelings had changed toward the religion and the city as well as toward Muhammad ﷺ personally.** This is not miraculous proof. It is testimony to the effect of what he observed while detained and then unexpectedly released.

   > “No religion was more hateful to me than yours; now it is the most beloved religion to me.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 4372](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:4372)[^2][^12][^42]

8. **Amr ibn al-As remembered moving from wanting Muhammad ﷺ dead to loving and revering him deeply.** Near his own death, Amr described three phases of his life. During the first, no one was more hated by him than Muhammad ﷺ; after his conversion, no one was dearer.

   > “No one became dearer to me than the Messenger of God ﷺ, and no one was more majestic in my eyes.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih Muslim 121](https://sunnah.com/muslim:121)[^3][^13][^42]

9. **Safwan ibn Umayya likewise described his hostility turning to love through Muhammad’s ﷺ generosity.** The report openly acknowledges that material gifts helped produce the reconciliation. That makes the account more human and gives concrete content to the way Muhammad treated a defeated opponent.

   > “He kept giving to me until he became the dearest of people to me, though he had been the most hateful.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih Muslim 2313](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2313)[^3][^13][^42]

10. **Abdullah ibn Salam, remembered as a learned Jewish figure in Medina, accepted Muhammad’s ﷺ claim.** Bukhari reports that members of his Jewish community initially described him as among their best and most learned, then disparaged him after he announced his belief in Muhammad ﷺ.

   > They first said of Abdullah, “He is the most learned among us and the best among us.” He then came out and testified to Muhammad’s prophethood.
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 3329](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3329)[^2][^12][^42]

11. **Anas’s decade of service provides a very different kind of testimony from a dramatic conversion.** He was not merely impressed by public preaching. He remembered how Muhammad ﷺ treated him when chores were forgotten and there was no audience to persuade.

   > “He never said ‘uff’ to me.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 6038](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6038)[^2][^12][^42]

12. **Aisha’s testimony comes from the still more intimate setting of marriage.** She reports Muhammad’s ﷺ service at home, avoidance of personal revenge, gentleness, tears, humor, and refusal to strike women or servants.

   > “The Messenger of God ﷺ never struck a woman or a servant.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih Muslim 2328a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2328a)[^3][^13][^42]

13. **None of these witnesses is decisive alone, but together they observe Muhammad ﷺ from unusually different positions.** An enemy speaks to his prior honesty. Family members describe private conduct. A servant describes everyday temperament. A negotiator comments on leadership and loyalty. Former enemies describe the effect of direct encounter. This convergence says more than the bare fact that many people followed him.

   > “You are truly upon tremendous character.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 68:4](https://quran.com/68/4)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

14. **Closer access did not produce a surviving record of private disillusionment, yet insiders did not remove every uncomfortable detail.** Wives, servants, relatives, and lifelong companions remained deeply committed while transmitting reports of poverty, fatigue, household jealousy, grief, a mistaken agricultural judgment, Qur’anic corrections, and disagreements with him. They also preserved ordinary details of his food, sleep, speech, and domestic work. A later community creating an entirely frictionless saint could easily have omitted much of this material, which makes the witness record more substantial than simple praise literature.[^36][^37][^38][^40][^41]

15. **Several fierce opponents later became central participants in the community rather than merely passive subjects of Muhammad’s ﷺ rule.** Umar is remembered as having set out against him before becoming one of his closest companions. Khalid ibn al-Walid commanded Meccan cavalry against the Muslims at Uhud before becoming a Muslim commander. Abu Sufyan led years of opposition before joining the community. Conversion does not prove prophethood, but repeated reversals among people who directly experienced both sides of the conflict belong in the cumulative human evidence.[^9][^19][^24][^27][^28][^36][^40][^41]

### G. Earlier scripture and natural signs: meaningful, but easy to overstate

1. **The Qur’an claims continuity with earlier revelation and says that Muhammad ﷺ is described in the Torah and Gospel.** For a Muslim, this naturally invites comparison with earlier scripture. For a skeptic, however, the Qur’an’s statement cannot serve as independent confirmation of its own claim.

   > “Those who follow the Messenger, the unlettered prophet, whom they find written with them in the Torah and the Gospel...”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 7:157](https://quran.com/7/157)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

2. **The Qur’an presents Jesus as announcing a later messenger named Ahmad, a name from the same Arabic root as Muhammad.** This matters within the Qur’anic case, but it should not be presented as though the same sentence appears verbatim in the surviving canonical Gospels.

   > Jesus son of Mary said: “I am God’s messenger to you... giving good news of a messenger to come after me whose name is Ahmad.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 61:6](https://quran.com/61/6)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

3. **Muslim writers have long read passages such as Deuteronomy 18:18 and Isaiah 42 as possible anticipations of Muhammad ﷺ.** Deuteronomy speaks of a prophet “like Moses,” while Isaiah 42 describes a servant bringing judgment or instruction to the nations and explicitly mentions the settlements of Kedar. The parallels can be suggestive, particularly within an Abrahamic reading, but Jewish and Christian interpreters understand these passages differently. They are therefore better treated as supporting correspondences than as uncontested proof.[^68][^69][^70]

   > “We make no distinction between any of His messengers.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 2:285](https://quran.com/2/285)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

4. **Claims about identifying the Gospel’s “Paraclete” with Muhammad ﷺ need particular care.** Muslim interpreters have connected Jesus’s promised advocate in John 14–16 with Muhammad, but the surviving Greek manuscript tradition reads *paraklētos*, “advocate/helper,” not the proposed alternative term meaning “praised one.” The case for Muhammad ﷺ does not need a textual claim stronger than the manuscripts can support.[^68][^71]

   > “Do not pursue that of which you have no knowledge.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 17:36](https://quran.com/17/36)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

5. **Claims about “scientific miracles” require the same restraint.** The Qur’an repeatedly describes the heavens, earth, life, rain, animals, human development, and the regularities of nature as “signs.” Some verses have striking resonances with modern knowledge, and believers may reasonably find those resonances meaningful. But flexible premodern language should not be forced into the form of a modern scientific prediction and then presented as though no other linguistic reading were possible.[^35][^72]

   > “We will show them Our signs in the horizons and within themselves until it becomes clear to them that it is the truth.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 41:53](https://quran.com/41/53)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

6. **The Qur’an’s stronger argument from nature concerns order, intelligibility, and contingency.** It repeatedly asks people to observe, reason, compare, and draw conclusions rather than treating inquiry as a threat to faith.

   > “In the creation of the heavens and earth... are signs for people who understand.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 3:190](https://quran.com/3/190)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

7. **Restraint makes the overall case stronger.** An argument that does not depend on dubious quotations, fake NASA stories, numerology, or forced scientific readings is more credible. Muhammad ﷺ should be assessed through the strongest evidence that can genuinely be traced to his sources.

   > “Bring your proof, if you are truthful.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 2:111](https://quran.com/2/111)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

### H. Recognition and appraisal outside Muslim belief

Praise from outsiders cannot prove prophethood. A historian may admire Muhammad ﷺ while rejecting revelation, and historical influence is not inherently good. Such judgments therefore belong at the edge of the cumulative case rather than at its center. They remain worth noting because they show how some readers outside Islam have assessed the man and his career.

1. **Muhammad’s ﷺ historical stature is recognized well beyond Muslim devotional writing.** Michael H. Hart famously ranked him first in *The 100: A Ranking of the Most Influential Persons in History*, emphasizing his combined religious and secular influence. The United States Supreme Court’s courtroom frieze also includes Muhammad among historical lawgivers. Neither fact establishes that he was a prophet, but both show how difficult it is to treat his career as marginal to world history.[^73][^74]

   > Further context: [*The 100*](https://archive.org/details/the-100-michael-hart)[^73]; [U.S. Supreme Court, “Courtroom Friezes: South and North Walls”](https://www.supremecourt.gov/about/infosheets/SouthAndNorthFriezes.html)[^74]

2. **Some non-Muslim religious thinkers have found the simple “fraud” explanation inadequate and have discussed Muhammad ﷺ in genuinely prophetic terms.** The Christian theologian Keith Ward is one modern example. Such judgments are not independent tests of revelation, but they show that recognizing Muhammad’s sincerity, or even some prophetic significance in him, is not logically confined to people who already identify as Muslim.[^75]

   > Further discussion: [Keith Ward interview on Muhammad and Islam](https://www.youtube.com/live/ftRsC_2sjg8)[^75]

3. **W. Montgomery Watt, a major non-Muslim twentieth-century historian of early Islam, also rejected the simple impostor explanation as inadequate.** His conclusion was not an acceptance of Islam, but a historian’s judgment that the theory of conscious fraud creates more explanatory problems than it resolves.[^40]

   > “His readiness to undergo persecution for his beliefs, the high moral character of the men who believed in him and looked up to him as leader, and the greatness of his ultimate achievement — all argue his fundamental integrity. To suppose Muhammad an impostor raises more problems than it solves.”
   >
   > Source: W. Montgomery Watt, *Muhammad at Mecca* (Oxford: Clarendon Press, 1953), p. 52[^40]

4. **Comparisons with earlier scripture are best treated as an invitation to investigate, not as proof by collecting quotations.** Muslim readers have connected Muhammad ﷺ with biblical passages about a prophet like Moses, Kedar, Jesus’s promised successor, and expectations among Arabian Jews, while other interpreters reject those identifications. The underlying continuity claim still matters: Muhammad ﷺ did not present the God of Abraham, Moses, and Jesus as a different tribal deity, but claimed to restore the same monotheistic line.[^68][^69][^70][^71]

   > Selected comparative discussions: [Jesus and Muhammad](https://youtu.be/EGZ2RibrJtc), [Muhammad and Moses](https://youtu.be/RRlhH3_iedU), [the Abrahamic tradition](https://youtu.be/vVGiHoPZa0A), [Muhammad and the Jews of Arabia](https://youtu.be/i0vl0vAyeoo)[^68][^69][^70][^71]

### I. What the main alternative explanations must account for

A cumulative case is only useful if the main alternatives are tested against the same evidence. Four explanations recur often enough to deserve direct examination. They are not equally strong, but none should be dismissed by a label alone. Each has to account for the same life, text, and transmitted record.

1. **“He was a conscious impostor.”** This is logically possible, but the ordinary motives for such a fraud fit the record poorly. In Mecca the claim brought persecution rather than comfort, and early biography reports that offers of wealth and leadership were refused. Later authority produced neither a palace nor a hereditary treasury, worship of Muhammad ﷺ, or exemption for his family. The Qur’an corrects him, limits his claim to knowledge of the unseen, threatens him for fabrication, and sometimes leaves him waiting when an invented answer would have been useful. The eclipse on the day his son died offered an unusually convenient piece of religious theater, and he publicly rejected the connection. The fraud hypothesis therefore has to explain why a successful fraud repeatedly declined advantages that would have served him.[^36][^37][^40]

2. **“He was sincere but psychologically mistaken.”** This accounts for his willingness to suffer and much of the integrity evidence better than conscious fraud. It still leaves other features to explain: the Qur’an’s sustained literary and theological voice across twenty-three years, Muhammad’s ﷺ ability to distinguish revelation from his own mistaken agricultural judgment, the practical judgment required to lead an embattled community into a functioning polity, the striking predictions in the source record, and the reports of extraordinary events. Sincerity combined with error is a coherent hypothesis, but it does not make the rest of the evidence disappear.[^36][^40][^46][^47]

3. **“The impressive portrait is mostly later legend.”** This is the strongest naturalistic challenge to many hadith-based details and is the reason source criticism matters. The surviving canonical compilations postdate Muhammad ﷺ, so later development cannot simply be ruled out. But the legend hypothesis also has to account for named transmission networks, early written material, competing hadith grades, catalogued forgeries, and a portrait that preserves awkward details: household disagreements, poverty, grief, mistaken farming advice, limits on judicial judgment, public Qur’anic rebukes, and a ruler who died with his armor pledged for food. Later reverence undoubtedly shaped how Muhammad ﷺ was remembered. The stronger claim is that it created enough of the converging record to explain the whole portrait, and that claim itself needs evidence.[^36][^37][^38][^42][^43][^44]

4. **“The source was demonic rather than divine.”** This explanation appears especially in Christian polemic, while Meccan opponents made a different but related accusation that Muhammad ﷺ was possessed or speaking like a soothsayer. The Qur’an rejects the charge directly, saying that devils neither brought down the recitation nor were capable of doing so. More importantly for the hypothesis itself, the message relentlessly turns worship away from idols and spirits toward one God, condemns sorcery, divination, omens, murder, exploitation, and sexual wrongdoing, and repeatedly teaches people to seek refuge from Satan. Jesus’s response to the charge that he expelled demons by demonic power gives the relevant theological principle: an explanation based on a kingdom systematically working against itself is internally strained.

   > “It was not the devils who brought this down. It is neither fitting for them nor are they able.”
   >
   > “It is not the word of an accursed devil.”
   >
   > Sources: [Qur’an 26:210–212](https://quran.com/26/210-212)[^1][^10][^11][^35]; [Qur’an 81:25](https://quran.com/81/25)[^1][^10][^11][^35]; compare Matthew 12:25–28 and Mark 3:23–26

The fourth explanation also concedes something the first does not. It grants that the Qur’an and the life surrounding it call for an extraordinary source, then attributes them to the very kind of agency the message persistently teaches people to resist. Within a monotheistic worldview, that makes it a particularly strained explanation.

## V. What the whole picture amounts to

1. **The teachings form a remarkably integrated moral vision.** Worship of God is joined to truthfulness, mercy, family duty, care for orphans and the poor, commercial honesty, justice even against oneself, restraint in war, protection of non-Muslims, kindness to animals, humility, knowledge, consultation, and forgiveness. Ritual practice never makes ethical conduct disposable.

   > “Righteousness is not that you turn your faces east or west... Rather, righteousness is faith... giving beloved wealth... keeping promises, and patience in hardship.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 2:177](https://quran.com/2/177)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

2. **In the strongest Islamic sources, the private man closely resembles the public teacher.** A wife says that he served at home and did not strike women or servants. A young servant remembers ten years without petty scolding. Petitioners remember generosity, while opponents acknowledge his prior honesty. This convergence matters even before any miracle claim is considered.

   > “You are truly upon tremendous character.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 68:4](https://quran.com/68/4)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

3. **The same basic character can be followed through radically different circumstances.** Muhammad ﷺ was an orphan, worker, husband, persecuted preacher, refugee, military leader, treaty-maker, victor over his former city, and head of a growing polity. His eventual power did not end in a private palace, a dynastic treasury, or a demand that people worship him.

   > “I am only a servant, so say: God’s servant and His Messenger.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 3445](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3445)[^2][^12][^42]

4. **Several incidents fit conscious religious fraud particularly poorly.** The eclipse on the day his son died could have been used as powerful propaganda, yet he rejected the interpretation. A month-long scandal involving his wife could have been ended immediately with an invented revelation, yet the report says he waited. When his agricultural advice proved wrong, he acknowledged that the farmers knew their craft better. These incidents show how he responded when opportunities to manufacture authority presented themselves.

   > “The sun and moon do not eclipse because of anyone’s death or life.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 1043](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:1043)[^2][^12][^42]

5. **The Qur’an does not read like an unrestricted charter for Muhammad’s ﷺ personal authority.** It rebukes him, threatens him if he fabricates revelation, restricts his claims, denies him independent knowledge of the unseen, tells him not to rush revelation, and insists that he is a mortal messenger.

   > “It is not for me to change it on my own. I follow only what is revealed to me.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 10:15](https://quran.com/10/15)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

6. **The sources contain predictions that deserve serious examination.** Qur’anic examples include the Byzantine recovery and the promised return to the Sacred Mosque. The hadith tradition adds more specific forecasts concerning Fatima, Hasan, Ammar, Umar and Uthman, Persian treasures, Jerusalem and plague, maritime expeditions, and later social conditions. Their evidential weight is not identical because their source histories differ.

   > “The Romans have been defeated... Yet after their defeat they will be victorious, within a few years.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 30:2–4](https://quran.com/30/2-4)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

7. **There is also a substantial body of miracle testimony.** Major hadith collections preserve reports of multiplied water and food, the splitting of the moon, the crying palm trunk, Ali’s healing, the prayer for rain, the Night Journey, and other extraordinary events. Whether one accepts these reports depends on one’s judgment of their transmission and of the possibility of miracles in the first place.

   > “The Hour has drawn near, and the moon has split.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 54:1](https://quran.com/54/1)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

8. **The reactions of people around him are more varied than a simple story of gullible followers.** The record includes Khadija’s immediate trust, the scriptural recognition attributed to Waraqa and Abdullah ibn Salam, concrete admissions from Abu Sufyan while he was hostile, Urwa’s political observations, and striking reversals from Thumama, Amr, and Safwan. None is infallible on its own. Together they form part of the historical portrait.

   > Thumama said after being released, “There was no face more hateful to me than yours; now your face has become the most beloved.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 4372](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:4372)[^2][^12][^42]

9. **The strongest skeptical explanation is not the caricature of an obvious liar chasing wealth.** The sources fit that account badly. A more serious alternative would describe Muhammad ﷺ as a deeply sincere but mistaken religious experiencer who nonetheless produced or transmitted a remarkable scripture, exercised unusual moral authority, built an extraordinarily successful movement, and became the center of a tradition containing striking predictions and miracle reports. That hypothesis is logically possible. The question is whether it explains the whole body of evidence better than prophethood does.

   > “Say: I advise you only of one thing: stand before God, in pairs or alone, and then reflect. Your companion is not possessed by madness.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 34:46](https://quran.com/34/46)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

10. **For the believer, these different lines of evidence converge on a simpler conclusion.** The man’s integrity, the God-centered message, the Qur’an’s distinctive voice and restraints on its messenger, the cost Muhammad ﷺ bore, the way he lived after gaining power, the fulfilled predictions, and the transmitted signs fit together as the career of a genuine messenger rather than a manufactured saint.

   > “We sent you only as a mercy to all worlds.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 21:107](https://quran.com/21/107)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

11. **This is also why love for Muhammad ﷺ need not be a form of celebrity devotion.** At its best, it grows from knowing the life itself: a man who wept over his child, carried his granddaughter in prayer, listened to women, forgave personal injuries, fed enemies, restrained vengeance, helped at home, continued to live simply when he held power, and spent his last breath turning toward God.

   > “The eyes shed tears and the heart grieves... but we say only what pleases our Lord.”
   >
   > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 1303](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:1303)[^2][^12][^42]

12. **The message ultimately directs the reader beyond Muhammad ﷺ and toward God.** He did not ask to be worshipped. In his own teaching, his authority came from being God’s servant and messenger, not from possessing divinity himself.

   > “Say: I am only a human being like you. It is revealed to me that your God is One God. So whoever hopes to meet his Lord, let him do righteous work and associate no one in the worship of his Lord.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 18:110](https://quran.com/18/110)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

Taken together, these teachings were not meant simply to produce people who performed religious rites. They were meant to form people who were **truthful without cruelty, strong without arrogance, devout without extremism, just without favoritism, and merciful without moral indifference**, while remaining conscious that the vulnerable person in front of them had a claim upon them. This is why the small incidents matter beside the large claims. A moral teaching is tested most clearly by proximity, inconvenience, loss, insult, fear, money, and power.

   > “In the Messenger of God you have an excellent example for whoever hopes in God and the Last Day and remembers God often.”
   >
   > Source: [Qur’an 33:21](https://quran.com/33/21)[^1][^10][^11][^35]

The aim of this article has been to be comprehensive without pretending that every report carries the same historical weight. Strong evidence does not need weaker evidence exaggerated on its behalf. Taken on its strongest sources, the case for Muhammad ﷺ rests on a remarkable combination: **demanding monotheism, expansive mercy, disciplined justice, unusual personal humility, consistency under power, and a prophetic claim embedded in a text and life that resist simple explanations.**
