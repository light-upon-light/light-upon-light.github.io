# Muhammad ﷺ: What His Character, Teachings, and Life Say About His Prophetic Claim

If someone wants to decide whether Muhammad ﷺ was genuinely a prophet of God, his character is not a side issue. A false claimant may be able to produce an impressive speech or sustain a public image for a time. It is harder to explain a life in which the same moral pattern appears before the claim, during persecution, in private family life, in dealings with servants and strangers, and later when the claimant possesses political and military power.

None of this, by itself, mathematically proves prophethood. Moral excellence is not the same thing as revelation. But it is relevant evidence. The important question is not simply whether Muhammad taught admirable ideas. It is whether **his message, private conduct, public conduct, sacrifices, use of power, and understanding of God fit together** in a way that makes his claim credible.

This article therefore concentrates on affirmative evidence from his life and teachings. Questions about particular historical controversies deserve careful treatment in their own right and should not be evaded. But they should be considered alongside, rather than instead of, the large body of material below.

## A note on the sources

The **Qur'an** is the scripture Muhammad ﷺ presented as revelation from God. A **hadith** is a transmitted report about his words, actions, or approvals. **Sīra** means prophetic biography. The article relies most heavily on the Qur'an and on reports in Sahih al-Bukhari and Sahih Muslim, the two most highly regarded Sunni hadith collections. Reports from other hadith collections are used selectively. A small number of biographical reports are identified as sīra rather than silently treated as hadith.

The short quotations below are **concise English renderings** made for this article, rather than reproductions of one published translation. They are deliberately brief. The links allow readers to inspect the fuller context and, for hadith, the transmitted report itself.

# 1. The man behind the prophetic claim

## 1.1 His reputation and character were not invented after he became a prophet

1. **His wife's immediate reaction to his first revelation was to appeal to his established moral record.** Khadijah did not reassure a stranger she barely knew. She had lived with him for years and pointed to the kind of man he already was: loyal to family, helpful to the vulnerable, hospitable, and dependable in hardship.

    > “God will never disgrace you. You maintain family ties, support the needy, honor guests, and help people struck by hardship.”
    >
    > Source: Khadijah, [Sahih al-Bukhari 3](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3)

2. **An enemy testified that he had never known Muhammad ﷺ to lie.** Before Abu Sufyan became Muslim, the Byzantine emperor Heraclius questioned him about Muhammad. Asked whether Muhammad had been accused of lying before claiming prophethood, Abu Sufyan answered no. That hostile testimony matters precisely because it was not praise from a disciple.

    > “Had you ever accused him of telling lies before he said what he now says?” He replied, “No.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 7](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:7)

3. **The same hostile witness said Muhammad taught truthfulness, chastity, prayer, and family responsibility.** Whatever Abu Sufyan thought of the prophetic claim at that point, he described a recognizable moral program rather than a cult of Muhammad's personality.

    > “He tells us to worship God alone, pray, speak the truth, be chaste, and maintain the ties of kinship.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 7](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:7)

4. **The Qur'an itself points to his long, publicly observable life before revelation.** Muhammad did not appear from nowhere with an unverifiable past. He had lived among the people to whom he first preached.

    > “I lived among you for a lifetime before this. Will you not use reason?”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 10:16](https://quran.com/10/16)

5. **His closest household witness summarized his character by pointing to the Qur'an he preached.** When Aisha was asked what he was like, her answer was not that his public preaching differed from his private personality.

    > “The character of the Messenger of God was the Qur'an.”
    >
    > Source: Aisha, [Sahih Muslim 746a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:746a)

6. **A man who served him for ten years remembered extraordinary restraint, not habitual irritation.** Anas was around Muhammad in ordinary circumstances where impatience would have been easy to see.

    > “I served him for ten years. He never even said ‘uff’ to me, nor ‘Why did you do that?’”
    >
    > Source: Anas ibn Malik, [Sahih al-Bukhari 6038](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6038)

7. **His wife said he never struck a woman or a servant.** This is unusually relevant evidence because it concerns conduct inside the household, beyond the setting of sermons and public leadership.

    > “The Messenger of God never struck anyone with his hand, neither a woman nor a servant.”
    >
    > Source: Aisha, [Sahih Muslim 2328a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2328a)

8. **The Qur'an describes him as possessing exceptional moral character, while the hadith give concrete examples of what that meant.**

    > “You are truly of outstanding character.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 68:4](https://quran.com/68/4)

## 1.2 He refused the kinds of claims a religious charlatan could have exploited

9. **When a solar eclipse coincided with his son's death, he rejected the flattering superstition.** People connected the eclipse to Ibrahim's death. Muhammad could have accepted an apparent cosmic endorsement at a moment of enormous grief. Instead, he explicitly denied the connection.

    > “The sun and moon do not eclipse because of anyone's death or birth.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 1043](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:1043)

10. **He denied possessing God's treasures or independent knowledge of the unseen.** His authority was prophetic, not divine.

    > “I do not say that I possess God's treasures, nor that I know the unseen, nor that I am an angel.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 6:50](https://quran.com/6/50)

11. **He denied having autonomous power even over his own benefit or harm.**

    > “I have no power to benefit or harm myself except as God wills. If I knew the unseen, I would have secured much good.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 7:188](https://quran.com/7/188)

12. **He insisted that he was a human being, not a divine incarnation.** The distinction is central to the theology he taught.

    > “I am only a human being like you, to whom it is revealed that your God is one God.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 18:110](https://quran.com/18/110)

13. **He explicitly forbade his followers from exaggerating his status.** A self-serving founder might encourage increasingly divine titles. Muhammad told his followers to call him God's servant and messenger.

    > “Do not exaggerate in praising me... I am only His servant. Say: God's servant and His messenger.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 3445](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3445)

14. **He told his own daughter that family connection to him could not save her before God.** This rejects spiritual nepotism at the point where it would have benefited his own household.

    > “Fatimah, daughter of Muhammad, ask me for whatever you wish of my wealth, but I cannot save you from God.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 2753](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2753)

15. **His scripture openly states that he would die like other human beings.** His movement was not supposed to depend on the fiction of an immortal founder.

    > “Muhammad is only a messenger. Messengers passed away before him. If he dies or is killed, will you turn back?”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 3:144](https://quran.com/3/144)

16. **The Qur'an places Muhammad himself under judgment if he were to fabricate revelation.** The text does not give its messenger moral immunity.

    > “Had he falsely attributed sayings to Us, We would have seized him... then cut his life-vein.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 69:44–46](https://quran.com/69/44-46)

17. **The Qur'an preserves a public correction of Muhammad's own behavior.** When he frowned and turned away from a blind man while speaking to influential Meccans, the revelation rebuked him. A text designed merely to protect its claimant's image had no obvious need to preserve the incident.

    > “He frowned and turned away because the blind man came to him. Yet how could you know? Perhaps he might have grown in purity.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 80:1–3](https://quran.com/80/1-3)

18. **Another Qur'anic passage publicly questions a decision he made.** Again, the messenger is not presented as someone whose every personal judgment is beyond correction.

    > “May God pardon you. Why did you grant them permission before those who were truthful became clear to you?”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 9:43](https://quran.com/9/43)

19. **He distinguished revelation from ordinary technical judgment.** After commenting on date-palm pollination and seeing the agricultural result, he did not spiritualize the mistake. He told the farmers they knew their technical field better.

    > “You know better about the technical affairs of your worldly life.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih Muslim 2363](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2363)

20. **As a judge, he acknowledged that persuasive speech could affect a human ruling.** He warned litigants that winning a case through eloquence would not turn another person's property into a moral right.

    > “I am only human. You bring disputes to me, and one of you may present his argument more skillfully than another.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 2680](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2680)

## 1.3 His claim brought sacrifice rather than a life of luxury

21. **One of the hardest episodes of his mission ended with restraint rather than a demand for vengeance.** After severe rejection near Ta'if, the Angel of the Mountains was reported to have offered destruction. Muhammad refused and expressed hope for the people's descendants.

    > “No. I hope that God will bring from their descendants people who worship God alone.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 3231](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3231)

22. **Even after becoming the leader of Medina, his household sometimes went for long periods without cooked food.** Aisha described months in which their basic diet was dates and water, with occasional milk from neighbors.

    > “We would see three new moons in two months, and no fire would be lit in the houses of God's Messenger.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 6459](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6459)

23. **He bought food on credit and mortgaged his armor to a Jewish merchant.** Whatever political authority he possessed, the report is difficult to reconcile with a picture of a founder using religion to accumulate personal luxury.

    > “The Prophet bought food on credit from a Jew and mortgaged his iron armor to him.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 2509](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2509)

24. **At his death, he did not leave a personal fortune.**

    > “The Messenger of God left neither dinar nor dirham, neither male slave nor female slave.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 4461](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:4461)

25. **His private worship remained demanding even after success.** When Aisha asked why he stood in prayer until his feet swelled despite God's forgiveness, his answer was gratitude rather than anxiety about status.

    > “Should I not be a grateful servant?”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 4836](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:4836)

26. **Those around him remembered exceptional generosity, especially during Ramadan.**

    > “The Messenger of God was the most generous of people... more generous in good than a freely blowing wind.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 3554](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3554)

27. **He was remembered as personally courageous rather than protected at everyone else's expense.** When Medina was startled by a nighttime noise, Muhammad had already ridden toward the danger alone and returned to reassure everyone.

    > “We found the Prophet returning, having gone ahead of the people... He said, ‘Do not be afraid.’”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 2908](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2908)

28. **The pattern is especially striking because greater power did not produce a more luxurious or self-divinizing Muhammad.** The Qur'an kept describing him as God's servant, and his final years remained materially simple.

    > “Blessed is the One who sent down the Criterion upon His servant, so that he may be a warner to the worlds.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 25:1](https://quran.com/25/1)

# 2. Mercy in ordinary life

## 2.1 Patience, gentleness, and restraint

29. **When given a choice between two permissible courses, he generally chose the easier one.** His religion was serious without treating needless difficulty as holiness.

    > “Whenever the Prophet was given a choice between two matters, he chose the easier, so long as it was not sinful.”
    >
    > Source: Aisha, [Sahih al-Bukhari 3560](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3560)

30. **He did not use personal injury as a reason for revenge.** Aisha distinguished between violations of public moral limits and offenses against Muhammad personally.

    > “He never took revenge for himself.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 3560](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3560)

31. **When a desert Arab violently yanked his cloak hard enough to mark his shoulder and demanded money, Muhammad smiled and gave him something.** The reaction is memorable because the provocation was physical as well as rude.

    > “The Prophet turned toward him, smiled, and ordered that he be given a gift.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 5809](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5809)

32. **When an uninstructed desert Arab urinated in the mosque, he prevented the crowd from attacking him and solved the problem calmly.** He then explained the educational principle behind his response.

    > “Leave him, and pour a bucket of water over it. You were sent to make things easy, not to make them difficult.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 6128](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6128)

33. **He made ease and encouragement an explicit teaching method.**

    > “Make things easy, not difficult. Give good news, and do not drive people away.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 69](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:69)

34. **Even when Jewish visitors addressed him with a hostile wordplay, he restrained Aisha from answering harshly.**

    > “Be calm, Aisha. God loves gentleness in every matter.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 6030](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6030)

35. **He taught that gentleness improves whatever it enters.**

    > “Gentleness is not found in anything except that it beautifies it, and it is not removed except that it disfigures it.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih Muslim 2594a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2594a)

36. **When asked to curse polytheists, he described his mission in terms of mercy.**

    > “I was not sent as one who curses. I was sent only as mercy.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih Muslim 2599](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2599)

37. **When a tribe resisted his message, he prayed for their guidance rather than their destruction.**

    > “O God, guide Daws and bring them.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 4392](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:4392)

38. **He spared a man who had stood over him with an unsheathed sword.** After the immediate danger passed, the report says Muhammad did not punish him.

    > “The Prophet did not punish him.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 2910](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2910)

39. **He honored the humanity of a deceased Jew.** When a Jewish funeral passed and people questioned why he stood for it, his answer went beneath religious identity to human life itself.

    > “Is it not a soul?”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 1312](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:1312)

40. **He visited a sick Jewish boy in person.** Religious disagreement did not cancel ordinary human concern.

    > “A Jewish boy who used to serve the Prophet became ill, so the Prophet went to visit him.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 1356](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:1356)

41. **He did not turn ordinary preferences into occasions for complaint.** His household remembered that he simply ate food he liked and left what he did not.

    > “The Prophet never criticized food. If he liked it he ate it; if he disliked it he left it.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 5409](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5409)

42. **He treated self-control as a more meaningful form of strength than overpowering other people.**

    > “The strong person is not the one who defeats others in wrestling. The strong person controls himself when angry.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 6114](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6114)

43. **When a man repeatedly asked for a concise moral instruction, Muhammad kept returning to anger.**

    > “Do not become angry.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 6116](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6116)

44. **He rejected vulgarity as a mark of good character.**

    > “The best among you are those with the best character.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 6029](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6029)

## 2.2 What he was like as a husband and family member

45. **He did household work.** Asked what Muhammad did at home, Aisha did not describe a ruler waiting to be served.

    > “He used to work for his family; when prayer time came, he went out to pray.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 5363](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5363)

46. **He made treatment of one's wife a measure of moral excellence.**

    > “The best of you is the best to his family, and I am the best of you to my family.”
    >
    > Source: [Jami at-Tirmidhi 3895](https://sunnah.com/tirmidhi:3895)

47. **He taught that spending on one's own household can itself be charity.** Family responsibility was not spiritually inferior to public religious acts.

    > “When a Muslim spends on his family seeking God's reward, it is charity for him.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 5351](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5351)

48. **He remained loyal to the memory of Khadijah long after her death.** Aisha recalled that he often mentioned her and sent portions of meat to Khadijah's old friends.

    > “Whenever he slaughtered a sheep, he would send a good share to Khadijah's friends.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 3818](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3818)

49. **He gave mothers an extraordinary claim to good companionship.** When asked who deserved the best treatment, he named the mother three times before the father.

    > “Your mother.” “Then who?” “Your mother.” “Then who?” “Your mother.” “Then your father.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 5971](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5971)

50. **He invalidated a marriage a woman said her father had imposed on her.** That is a concrete application of consent, not merely a general slogan.

    > “Her father married her while she disliked it, so the Messenger of God annulled the marriage.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 5138](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5138)

51. **He explicitly taught that a woman's permission matters in marriage.**

    > “A previously married woman should not be married until consulted, and a virgin until her permission is sought.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 5136](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5136)

52. **He could make a personal request to a woman and accept her refusal.** When he encouraged Barirah to return to her former husband, she asked whether he was commanding her. He said he was only interceding. She declined, and the matter ended there.

    > “Are you ordering me?” He said, “No, I am only interceding.” She said, “I have no need of him.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 5283](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5283)

53. **He created dedicated teaching time for women when they said men were taking most of his availability.**

    > “The women said, ‘The men have taken all your time, so appoint a day for us.’ He appointed a day and taught them.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 101](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:101)

54. **In his Farewell Pilgrimage, he publicly reminded men that women had enforceable claims on them.**

    > “Be mindful of God concerning women... Their right over you is provision and clothing in a fitting manner.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih Muslim 1218a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:1218a)

55. **The Qur'an commands husbands to live with wives honorably, including when feelings are difficult.**

    > “Live with them in kindness. If you dislike something in them, perhaps you dislike something in which God has placed much good.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 4:19](https://quran.com/4/19)

56. **The Qur'an describes marriage in terms of tranquility, affection, and mercy rather than ownership alone.**

    > “He created spouses for you from among yourselves so that you may find tranquility in them, and He placed affection and mercy between you.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 30:21](https://quran.com/30/21)

## 2.3 His treatment of children and emotional vulnerability

57. **He openly kissed and showed affection to children.** When a man boasted that he never kissed his ten children, Muhammad treated that as a lack, not as toughness.

    > “Whoever does not show mercy will not be shown mercy.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 5997](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5997)

58. **He carried his young granddaughter while leading prayer.** He put her down when he prostrated and picked her up again when he stood.

    > “He prayed while carrying Umamah... when he bowed he put her down, and when he stood he carried her.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 516](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:516)

59. **He shortened congregational prayer when he heard a baby cry because he knew the mother would be distressed.** The needs of a parent and child altered his own preferred length of worship.

    > “I hear a child crying, so I shorten the prayer because I know how deeply his crying troubles his mother.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 707](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:707)

60. **He greeted children rather than treating them as beneath notice.**

    > “Anas passed by a group of boys and greeted them, then said: ‘The Prophet used to do so.’”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 6247](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6247)

61. **He wept when his son Ibrahim died and presented grief as compatible with faith.** He neither performed emotional invulnerability nor treated sorrow as rebellion against God.

    > “The eyes shed tears and the heart grieves, but we say only what pleases our Lord.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 1303](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:1303)

62. **When a dying child was placed before him, his tears were described as mercy.**

    > “This is mercy God has placed in the hearts of His servants, and God shows mercy to the merciful.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 7377](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:7377)

# 3. Human dignity and care for the vulnerable

## 3.1 The poor, widowed, orphaned, indebted, and socially weak

63. **He placed care for an orphan beside companionship with himself in Paradise.** The teaching gives ordinary long-term caregiving exceptional spiritual weight.

    > “I and the person who cares for an orphan will be in Paradise like this,” and he held two fingers close together.
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 6005](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6005)

64. **He compared sustained care for a widow or poor person to the most demanding forms of worship.**

    > “The one who works for a widow or a poor person is like one who strives in God's cause, or one who prays at night and fasts by day.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 5353](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5353)

65. **The Qur'an treats neglect of an orphan as a symptom of religious falseness.** Piety is not allowed to become a set of rituals detached from vulnerable people.

    > “Have you seen the one who denies the Judgment? That is the one who pushes away the orphan and does not urge feeding the poor.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 107:1–3](https://quran.com/107/1-3)

66. **Misappropriating an orphan's wealth is condemned in unusually severe terms.**

    > “Those who consume the property of orphans unjustly are only consuming fire into their bellies.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 4:10](https://quran.com/4/10)

67. **Mandatory charity was directed to identifiable social needs, not merely religious institutions.** The Qur'an names the poor, needy, indebted, travelers, and people seeking freedom among its beneficiaries.

    > “Charities are for the poor, the needy... those in bondage, those in debt, for God's cause, and the stranded traveler.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 9:60](https://quran.com/9/60)

68. **The Qur'an calls for giving valuable property away even when one loves it.** Its model of righteousness joins belief and worship to costly generosity.

    > “Righteousness is... to give wealth, despite loving it, to relatives, orphans, the needy, the traveler, those who ask, and for freeing people.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 2:177](https://quran.com/2/177)

69. **A debtor in genuine hardship is to be given time rather than crushed.** Forgiving the debt altogether is presented as better still.

    > “If someone is in hardship, then grant respite until ease; and if you remit it as charity, that is better for you.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 2:280](https://quran.com/2/280)

70. **Muhammad praised a creditor who routinely made life easier for struggling debtors.**

    > “He would say to his servant: ‘If you come to someone in difficulty, overlook it.’ So God overlooked his faults.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 2078](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2078)

71. **He made simple, practical help part of everyday charity.** Social goodness was not restricted to donating money.

    > “Helping a person onto his mount is charity; a good word is charity; and removing harm from the road is charity.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih Muslim 1009](https://sunnah.com/muslim:1009)

72. **He taught that even small acts of social warmth matter.**

    > “Your smile to your brother is charity.”
    >
    > Source: [Jami at-Tirmidhi 1956](https://sunnah.com/tirmidhi:1956)

## 3.2 Enslaved people and the movement toward emancipation

Slavery was an existing institution throughout the societies Muhammad lived among. It would be inaccurate to claim that he abolished it in a single decree. What the sources do show is a sustained moral pressure toward **emancipation, human kinship, equal basic treatment, and consequences for abuse**.

73. **He confronted racialized abuse directly.** When Abu Dharr insulted a man through his mother's ancestry, Muhammad told Abu Dharr that pre-Islamic ignorance still remained in him.

    > “You insulted him because of his mother? You are a man in whom there is still ignorance.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 30](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:30)

74. **He called enslaved workers ‘your brothers’ and required shared standards of food and clothing.**

    > “Those under your authority are your brothers. Feed them from what you eat and clothe them from what you wear.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 30](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:30)

75. **He forbade overburdening people under one's authority and required help when work was too heavy.**

    > “Do not burden them with what overwhelms them; if you burden them, then help them.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 30](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:30)

76. **He made freeing an enslaved person the required expiation for physical abuse in a striking report.**

    > “Whoever slaps or beats an enslaved person under his authority, the expiation is to set that person free.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih Muslim 1657a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:1657a)

77. **The Qur'an places freeing a person from bondage on the ‘steep path’ of moral excellence.**

    > “What will make you understand the steep path? It is freeing a person from bondage, or feeding on a day of hunger.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 90:12–14](https://quran.com/90/12-14)

78. **The Qur'an directs owners to facilitate contracts by which enslaved people can purchase their freedom and to help them financially.**

    > “If those under your authority seek a contract for freedom, make such a contract if you know good in them, and give them from God's wealth.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 24:33](https://quran.com/24/33)

79. **The same verse forbids coercing enslaved women into sexual exploitation for profit.**

    > “Do not compel your slave women into prostitution, if they desire chastity, seeking the temporary gains of worldly life.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 24:33](https://quran.com/24/33)

80. **He named selling a free person into slavery and withholding a worker's earned wage among wrongs for which God Himself will be the opponent.**

    > “I will be the opponent of... one who sells a free person and consumes the price, and one who hires a worker, takes his full labor, then withholds his wage.”
    >
    > Source: Sacred hadith, [Sahih al-Bukhari 2270](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2270)

## 3.3 Equality across race, tribe, status, and religion

81. **The Qur'an rejects race and tribe as measures of human worth.** Human diversity is for recognition and relationship; moral worth before God depends on character and God-consciousness.

    > “We made you peoples and tribes so that you may know one another. The most honored of you before God is the most God-conscious.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 49:13](https://quran.com/49/13)

82. **Human beings are given a basic dignity as children of Adam.**

    > “We have certainly honored the children of Adam.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 17:70](https://quran.com/17/70)

83. **Mockery, humiliating nicknames, and social contempt are prohibited.**

    > “Let no people ridicule another people, for they may be better than them... and do not insult one another or call one another by offensive names.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 49:11](https://quran.com/49/11)

84. **Suspicion, spying, and backbiting are treated as moral violations, not harmless social habits.**

    > “Avoid much suspicion... do not spy, nor backbite one another.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 49:12](https://quran.com/49/12)

85. **A neighbor's safety is treated as a matter of faith.**

    > “By God, he does not believe... the one whose neighbor is not safe from his harm.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 6016](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6016)

86. **Muhammad said Gabriel emphasized the neighbor's rights so persistently that he wondered whether neighbors would even receive inheritance rights.**

    > “Gabriel continued to urge me concerning the neighbor until I thought he would make the neighbor an heir.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 6014](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6014)

87. **Faith is linked to hospitality and disciplined speech.**

    > “Whoever believes in God and the Last Day should not harm his neighbor, should honor his guest, and should speak good or remain silent.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 6018](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6018)

88. **The life of a non-Muslim living under a Muslim covenant is treated as inviolable.** Muhammad attached an extraordinary warning to murdering such a person.

    > “Whoever kills a person under covenant will not smell the fragrance of Paradise.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 6914](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6914)

89. **The Qur'an explicitly permits kindness and justice toward people of other religions who are not fighting Muslims.**

    > “God does not forbid you from being kind and just toward those who did not fight you because of religion or drive you from your homes.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 60:8](https://quran.com/60/8)

90. **Religious belief itself is not to be produced by coercion.**

    > “There is no compulsion in religion. Truth has become distinct from error.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 2:256](https://quran.com/2/256)

91. **Even an enemy polytheist seeking protection is to receive asylum and safe escort.** The Qur'anic instruction is notable because it appears in a chapter dealing heavily with conflict.

    > “If any of the polytheists asks you for protection, grant it so he may hear God's word; then escort him to a place of safety.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 9:6](https://quran.com/9/6)

92. **Muhammad's Medina included ordinary economic dealings with Jews.** The report of his armor being mortgaged to a Jewish merchant is also evidence of day-to-day commercial coexistence.

    > “He purchased food on credit from a Jew and left his armor as security.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 2509](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2509)

## 3.4 He tried to repair social relationships, not merely identify wrongdoers

93. **He taught that helping an oppressor means stopping the oppression.** Loyalty is not permission to defend one's own side when it is wrong.

    > “Help your brother whether he is an oppressor or oppressed... Help the oppressor by preventing him from oppressing.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 2444](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2444)

94. **When people at Quba fought, Muhammad personally went to reconcile them.**

    > “Let us go and make peace between them.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 2693](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2693)

95. **He taught reciprocity at the level of moral desire.**

    > “None of you has complete faith until he loves for his brother what he loves for himself.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 13](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:13)

96. **The Qur'an tells believers to answer evil with something better when that can transform hostility.**

    > “Good and evil are not equal. Repel evil with what is better, and the one between whom and you was hostility may become like a close friend.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 41:34](https://quran.com/41/34)

97. **A single Qur'anic verse joins worship of God to concrete duties toward almost every vulnerable social circle around a person.**

    > “Worship God alone... and be good to parents, relatives, orphans, the needy, the near neighbor, the distant neighbor, the companion, the traveler, and those under your authority.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 4:36](https://quran.com/4/36)

# 4. A moral and spiritual program for ordinary human beings

## 4.1 Inner motives, truthfulness, repentance, and self-mastery

98. **He placed intention at the center of moral life.** An outwardly impressive deed is not spiritually equivalent to the same act done for a sincere reason.

    > “Actions are according to intentions, and each person will have what he intended.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 1](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:1)

99. **He taught that truthfulness forms character over time, while habitual lying deforms it.**

    > “Truthfulness leads to righteousness, and righteousness leads to Paradise... lying leads to wickedness.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 6094](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6094)

100. **The Qur'an does not let a person's hatred become an excuse for injustice.**

    > “Do not let the hatred of a people cause you to depart from justice. Be just; that is nearer to God-consciousness.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 5:8](https://quran.com/5/8)

101. **It likewise commands justice even when the truth is personally costly.**

    > “Stand firmly for justice, as witnesses for God, even against yourselves, your parents, or close relatives.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 4:135](https://quran.com/4/135)

102. **Repentance is kept open even to people who have seriously wronged themselves.** Despair is not presented as humility.

    > “My servants who have transgressed against themselves, do not despair of God's mercy. God forgives all sins.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 39:53](https://quran.com/39/53)

103. **Muhammad described God's mercy through the most instinctively compassionate human bond his listeners could see.**

    > “God is more merciful to His servants than this mother is to her child.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 5999](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5999)

104. **He refused to turn salvation into self-congratulation about one's good deeds.** Even when asked about himself, he said God's mercy remained decisive.

    > “Not even I, unless God covers me with His mercy.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 6463](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6463)

105. **He taught disciplined moral effort without spiritual perfectionism.**

    > “Aim to do right, come as close as you can, and follow a moderate, regular course.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 6463](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6463)

106. **He explicitly warned against making religion so burdensome that a person cannot sustain it.**

    > “Religion is easy, and no one makes the religion excessively hard on himself except that it overwhelms him.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 39](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:39)

107. **He affirmed a balance among worship, personal wellbeing, and family obligations.** When Salman told Abu al-Darda that God, the self, and the family each have rights, Muhammad endorsed the principle.

    > “Your Lord has a right over you, your self has a right over you, and your family has a right over you. Give each its right.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 1968](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:1968)

108. **He rejected religious extremism expressed through permanent fasting, all-night prayer, and celibacy.** His own model included worship, sleep, fasting, eating, and marriage.

    > “I fast and I break my fast; I pray and I sleep; and I marry. Whoever turns away from my way is not of me.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 5063](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5063)

109. **He taught that small sustainable acts can be more beloved to God than dramatic bursts a person cannot maintain.**

    > “The deeds most loved by God are those done regularly, even if they are few.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 6465](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6465)

110. **The Qur'an explicitly teaches that God does not demand the impossible from a person.**

    > “God does not burden a soul beyond its capacity.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 2:286](https://quran.com/2/286)

## 4.2 A God-centered worldview without a priestly barrier

111. **The theology Muhammad preached is radically centered on one God.** God is not one member of a pantheon and Muhammad is not part of God.

    > “Say: He is God, One; God, the One all depend upon. He neither begets nor is born, and none is comparable to Him.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 112:1–4](https://quran.com/112)

112. **The Qur'an presents God as directly near to the person who calls on Him.** No human intermediary is required for a private prayer to reach God.

    > “When My servants ask you about Me, I am near. I answer the call of the caller when he calls upon Me.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 2:186](https://quran.com/2/186)

113. **Each person is morally responsible for his or her own choices.** No soul inherits another person's moral guilt.

    > “No bearer of burdens will bear the burden of another.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 6:164](https://quran.com/6/164)

114. **Righteousness is not reduced to religious symbolism.** The Qur'an itself says that facing a particular direction is not the essence of goodness, then names faith, generosity, worship, promise-keeping, and patience.

    > “Righteousness is not that you turn your faces east or west... but to believe... give wealth despite loving it... fulfill promises, and remain patient in hardship.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 2:177](https://quran.com/2/177)

115. **One of the Qur'an's broadest moral summaries joins justice to generosity and prohibits aggression.**

    > “God commands justice, excellence, and giving to relatives, and forbids indecency, wrongdoing, and transgression.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 16:90](https://quran.com/16/90)

116. **The stated purpose of Muhammad's mission is mercy extending beyond his own tribe.**

    > “We have sent you only as a mercy to the worlds.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 21:107](https://quran.com/21/107)

117. **He presented his message as continuity with earlier revelation, not as the discovery of a tribal Arabian god.**

    > “He has ordained for you the faith He enjoined upon Noah, and what We revealed to you, and what We enjoined upon Abraham, Moses, and Jesus.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 42:13](https://quran.com/42/13)

118. **Muslims are told to affirm the revelations associated with Abraham, Moses, and Jesus rather than build faith on contempt for earlier prophets.**

    > “We believe in God, and what was revealed to us, and what was given to Abraham... Moses and Jesus... We make no distinction between them.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 2:136](https://quran.com/2/136)

119. **Men and women are placed side by side in the Qur'an's list of spiritual virtues and reward.**

    > “The Muslim men and Muslim women, believing men and believing women... truthful men and truthful women... God has prepared for them forgiveness and a great reward.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 33:35](https://quran.com/33/35)

120. **The same principle is stated in terms of deeds: moral worth is not made dependent on being male.**

    > “Whoever does righteous deeds, whether male or female, while believing, We will give them a good life and reward them according to the best of what they did.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 16:97](https://quran.com/16/97)

121. **Care for elderly parents is expressed in unusually tender language.**

    > “Do not even say ‘uff’ to them... lower to them the wing of humility out of mercy, and say: ‘My Lord, have mercy on them as they raised me when I was small.’”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 17:23–24](https://quran.com/17/23-24)

122. **The Qur'an asks people who have been personally wronged to consider forgiveness rather than use every legitimate grievance to retaliate.**

    > “Let them pardon and overlook. Would you not love that God should forgive you?”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 24:22](https://quran.com/24/22)

123. **Charity is not supposed to humiliate the recipient.** A kind word can be morally better than a donation followed by injury.

    > “Kind words and forgiveness are better than charity followed by hurt.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 2:263](https://quran.com/2/263)

124. **Private charity is praised because helping another person need not become a performance.**

    > “If you give charity openly, that is good; but if you conceal it and give it to the poor, that is better for you.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 2:271](https://quran.com/2/271)

125. **Believing men and women are described as moral allies of one another.**

    > “The believing men and believing women are allies of one another. They encourage what is right and restrain what is wrong.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 9:71](https://quran.com/9/71)

## 4.3 Knowledge, evidence, and intellectual honesty

126. **The first revealed command begins with reading and immediately mentions teaching by the pen.**

    > “Read in the name of your Lord who created... Read, and your Lord is most generous, who taught by the pen, taught the human being what he did not know.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 96:1–5](https://quran.com/96/1-5)

127. **Seeking knowledge is given a direct spiritual value.**

    > “Whoever travels a path seeking knowledge, God makes easy for him a path to Paradise.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih Muslim 2699a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2699a)

128. **Collective reading and study are described as acts surrounded by tranquility and mercy.**

    > “No people gather in one of God's houses, reciting God's Book and studying it together, except that tranquility descends upon them.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih Muslim 2699a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2699a)

129. **The same report denies that ancestry can substitute for personal moral effort.**

    > “Whoever is slowed by his deeds will not be accelerated by his lineage.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih Muslim 2699a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2699a)

130. **The Qur'an instructs believers to verify consequential reports before acting on them.**

    > “If an unreliable person brings you news, verify it, lest you harm people out of ignorance and then regret what you have done.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 49:6](https://quran.com/49/6)

131. **It prohibits confident claims where a person lacks knowledge and makes the senses and mind morally accountable.**

    > “Do not follow what you have no knowledge of. Indeed, the hearing, sight, and heart will all be questioned.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 17:36](https://quran.com/17/36)

132. **Muhammad issued a severe warning against inventing sayings and attributing them to him.** That warning helped create an early religious motive to distinguish transmission from fabrication.

    > “Whoever deliberately lies about me, let him take his place in the Fire.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 107](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:107)

133. **He rejected omen-based superstition.** He did not teach his followers to treat birds, directions, or random events as secret messages controlling their lives.

    > “There is no evil omen. I like a good word.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 5754](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5754)

134. **His eclipse response is a particularly strong example of epistemic restraint because the false interpretation would have glorified him personally.**

    > “The sun and moon are two signs of God; they do not eclipse for anyone's death or birth.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 1043](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:1043)

135. **His correction after the date-palm episode recognizes genuine human expertise in technical affairs.**

    > “You are more knowledgeable about the affairs of your worldly life.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih Muslim 2363](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2363)

## 4.4 Health, hygiene, and sustainable living

136. **He encouraged medical treatment rather than treating illness as something that must simply be endured without intervention.**

    > “Seek treatment, for God has not made a disease without making a treatment for it, except old age.”
    >
    > Source: [Sunan Abi Dawud 3855](https://sunnah.com/abudawud:3855)

137. **He taught an elementary quarantine rule for plague.** The instruction limits movement into and out of an outbreak area.

    > “If you hear of plague in a land, do not enter it; and if it occurs where you are, do not leave it fleeing from it.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 5728](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5728)

138. **He strongly encouraged oral hygiene while explicitly avoiding an unbearable obligation.**

    > “Were it not that I would cause hardship to my community, I would have commanded them to clean their teeth before every prayer.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 887](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:887)

139. **He taught moderation in eating rather than indulgence as a normal ideal.**

    > “No human fills a container worse than his stomach... if more is necessary, then a third for food, a third for drink, and a third for breath.”
    >
    > Source: [Jami at-Tirmidhi 2380](https://sunnah.com/tirmidhi:2380)

140. **Ritual life repeatedly incorporates washing and bodily cleanliness.**

    > “When you rise for prayer, wash your faces and your hands to the elbows, wipe your heads, and wash your feet to the ankles.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 5:6](https://quran.com/5/6)

# 5. Justice, leadership, wealth, and the use of power

## 5.1 Justice was supposed to bind the powerful too

141. **He explicitly rejected a two-tier justice system in which elites escape consequences imposed on weaker people.** When people tried to intercede for a well-connected woman convicted of theft, he gave the example of his own daughter.

    > “Those before you were ruined because they left the noble person when he stole but punished the weak... If Fatimah daughter of Muhammad stole, I would apply the law to her.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 6788](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6788)

142. **His warning to litigants recognizes that a legal victory can still be a moral theft.**

    > “If I award someone his brother's right because of his argument, I am only giving him a piece of the Fire.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 2680](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2680)

143. **The Qur'an commands public leadership to combine gentleness, pardon, consultation, and resolve.**

    > “It was by God's mercy that you were gentle with them... pardon them, ask forgiveness for them, and consult them in affairs.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 3:159](https://quran.com/3/159)

144. **Consultation is also described as a general quality of the believing community, not merely a concession from one ruler.**

    > “Their affairs are conducted by mutual consultation.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 42:38](https://quran.com/42/38)

145. **He warned people not to seek political authority out of personal ambition.**

    > “Do not ask for authority. If you are given it because you asked, you will be left to it; if given it without asking, you will be helped in it.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 7146](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:7146)

146. **He refused to appoint a person simply because that person was eager for the office.**

    > “We do not appoint to this work anyone who asks for it or is eager for it.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 7149](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:7149)

147. **He described public authority as a trust that could become a source of disgrace if its duties were not fulfilled.**

    > “Authority is a trust, and on the Day of Resurrection it will be a cause of humiliation and regret except for one who takes it rightly and fulfills its duties.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih Muslim 1825](https://sunnah.com/muslim:1825)

148. **He condemned gifts that public officials received because of their office.** His test was simple: would the person have received the gift if he had stayed at his parents' home instead of holding public authority?

    > “Why did he not sit in his father's or mother's house and see whether gifts would be given to him?”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 7174](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:7174)

149. **Before prophethood, he participated in a Meccan pact to defend people who had been wronged, and later praised that pact.** The sīra remembers the alliance as a commitment to stand with the oppressed even when tribal interest would point elsewhere.

    > “They pledged that they would stand with any wronged person in Mecca until his right was restored.”
    >
    > Source: Sīra report, [Ibn Hisham, al-Sirah al-Nabawiyyah 1/133](https://www.abuaminaelias.com/dailyhadithonline/2012/08/06/prophet-universal-justice/)

150. **The Qur'an's standard of justice applies even to people one strongly dislikes.** This matters most precisely when impartiality is hardest.

    > “Do not let hatred of a people cause you to be unjust. Be just; that is nearer to God-consciousness.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 5:8](https://quran.com/5/8)

## 5.2 Economic ethics: honest trade, fair wages, debt relief, and limits on exploitation

151. **He linked commercial blessing to honesty and disclosure.** A seller was not merely required to avoid an outright lie; known defects had to be disclosed.

    > “If buyer and seller speak truthfully and disclose defects, their transaction is blessed; if they lie and conceal, its blessing is erased.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 2079](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2079)

152. **He treated concealed defects in goods as a serious moral breach.** When he found wet grain hidden beneath dry grain, he ordered that it be made visible.

    > “Why did you not put the wet part on top so people could see it? Whoever cheats us is not of us.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih Muslim 102](https://sunnah.com/muslim:102)

153. **He prohibited manipulative bidding designed only to drive up someone else's price.**

    > “The Prophet forbade artificial bidding.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 2140](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2140)

154. **The Qur'an makes mutual consent a basic condition of legitimate exchange.**

    > “Do not consume one another's wealth unjustly, but only through trade by mutual consent.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 4:29](https://quran.com/4/29)

155. **Shortchanging people in weights and measures is condemned as a moral offense.**

    > “Woe to those who give less than due: when they take a measure from people they demand it in full, but when they measure for others they give less.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 83:1–3](https://quran.com/83/1-3)

156. **The Qur'an encourages written debt contracts and witnesses.** The longest verse of the Qur'an is largely about recording financial obligations so that rights are clear and disputes are reduced.

    > “When you contract a debt for a fixed period, write it down... let a scribe write between you with justice.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 2:282](https://quran.com/2/282)

157. **He praised earning through one's own work.**

    > “No one has eaten food better than that earned by the work of his own hands.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 2072](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2072)

158. **Withholding a laborer's pay after taking the full benefit of the work is singled out for severe condemnation.**

    > “I will be the opponent of... one who hires a worker, receives the full work from him, then does not give him his wage.”
    >
    > Source: Sacred hadith, [Sahih al-Bukhari 2270](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2270)

159. **He condemned deliberate hoarding of needed goods for exploitative gain.**

    > “No one hoards except a sinner.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih Muslim 1605b](https://sunnah.com/muslim:1605b)

160. **He praised leniency toward people who genuinely could not repay a debt on time.**

    > “Whoever gives respite to a person in difficulty or reduces his debt, God will shade him under His shade.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih Muslim 3006](https://sunnah.com/muslim:3006)

161. **Wastefulness is criticized even where the wealth technically belongs to the spender.**

    > “God has disliked for you... wasting wealth.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 2408](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2408)

162. **The Qur'an condemns interest-based exploitation and tells creditors to relinquish outstanding usurious increase.**

    > “Be mindful of God and give up what remains of usury, if you are believers.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 2:278](https://quran.com/2/278)

163. **When Muhammad publicly abolished pre-Islamic usury, he began with a financial claim belonging to his own family.** Reform was not supposed to protect the founder's relatives from its cost.

    > “The usury of the pre-Islamic period is abolished, and the first of our usury I abolish is that of Abbas ibn Abd al-Muttalib.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih Muslim 1218a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:1218a)

164. **His Farewell Pilgrimage sermon made property and life inviolable in the most public setting of his final year.**

    > “Your blood and your property are sacred and inviolable, like the sacredness of this day, in this month, in this town.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih Muslim 1218a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:1218a)

## 5.3 War was permitted, but it was not morally unbounded

Muhammad ﷺ was not a pacifist. He led a community that fought wars. The morally relevant question is therefore not whether conflict ever occurred, but **what limits his teaching placed on it, how he treated peace and treaties, and whether enmity suspended ordinary standards of justice**.

165. **He told his followers not to desire war.** Courage in battle was required when battle came, but craving conflict was not treated as piety.

    > “Do not wish to meet the enemy. Ask God for wellbeing. But if you meet them, be steadfast.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 3026](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3026)

166. **The Qur'an commands accepting a genuine offer of peace.**

    > “If they incline to peace, then incline to it, and put your trust in God.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 8:61](https://quran.com/8/61)

167. **Treaties with opponents are to be honored when the other party honors them.**

    > “As for the polytheists with whom you made a treaty and who have not failed you in anything... fulfill your treaty with them to its term.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 9:4](https://quran.com/9/4)

168. **He prohibited killing women and children in war.** The rule appears in response to a woman found dead after a battle.

    > “The Messenger of God disapproved of the killing of women and children.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 3015](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3015)

169. **His instructions to commanders forbade betrayal, mutilation, embezzlement, and killing children.**

    > “Do not embezzle, do not break pledges, do not mutilate, and do not kill children.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih Muslim 1731a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:1731a)

170. **He retracted an order to burn two enemy individuals before it was carried out, saying human beings should not punish with fire.**

    > “I had ordered you to burn so-and-so and so-and-so, but none should punish with fire except God.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 3016](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3016)

171. **The Qur'an permits fighting while explicitly forbidding transgression.**

    > “Fight in God's cause those who fight you, but do not transgress. God does not love transgressors.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 2:190](https://quran.com/2/190)

172. **The Qur'an praises feeding captives even when the giver has little food to spare.**

    > “They give food, despite loving it, to the poor, the orphan, and the captive.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 76:8](https://quran.com/76/8)

173. **A hostile person asking for temporary protection is to be brought to safety, not exploited while vulnerable.**

    > “Grant him protection... then escort him to his place of safety.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 9:6](https://quran.com/9/6)

174. **At Hudaybiyyah, Muhammad accepted treaty wording that omitted his prophetic title in order to secure an agreement.** He agreed to be written simply as “Muhammad son of Abdullah” when the Meccan negotiator rejected “Messenger of God.”

    > “Write: ‘Muhammad son of Abdullah’... The Prophet accepted their condition.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 2731](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2731)

175. **The Hudaybiyyah treaty also shows willingness to accept terms many followers initially found difficult for the sake of a truce.** The Qur'an subsequently called the outcome a clear victory.

    > “Indeed, We have granted you a clear victory.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 48:1](https://quran.com/48/1)

176. **In his final pilgrimage, he abolished inherited blood-feuds and began with a claim connected to his own clan.**

    > “The blood-revenges of the pre-Islamic period are abolished, and the first blood-revenge I abolish is one of ours.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih Muslim 1218a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:1218a)

177. **The earliest Qur'anic permission to fight is framed around people who had been wronged and expelled.**

    > “Permission to fight is given to those who are fought because they have been wronged... those driven from their homes only for saying, ‘Our Lord is God.’”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 22:39–40](https://quran.com/22/39-40)

178. **That same passage names monasteries, churches, synagogues, and mosques when explaining why aggression sometimes has to be resisted.**

    > “Had God not repelled some people by means of others, monasteries, churches, synagogues, and mosques where God's name is mentioned often would have been destroyed.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 22:40](https://quran.com/22/40)

179. **The Qur'an repeatedly makes covenant-keeping a religious duty rather than a matter of convenience.**

    > “Fulfill God's covenant when you have made it, and do not break oaths after confirming them.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 16:91](https://quran.com/16/91)

180. **At the conquest of Mecca, he announced routes to safety instead of treating the whole defeated city as a legitimate target.** A canonical report says people could enter Abu Sufyan's house, lay down their weapons, or close their own doors to be safe.

    > “Whoever enters the house of Abu Sufyan is safe; whoever lays down arms is safe; whoever closes his door is safe.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih Muslim 1780a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:1780a)

# 6. The breadth of the ethic

## 6.1 Animals and the natural world

181. **He taught that compassion toward an animal can be morally momentous.** A man who climbed down a well to bring water to a desperately thirsty dog was forgiven by God.

    > “He gave the dog water, so God thanked him and forgave him.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 2363](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2363)

182. **When asked whether kindness to animals is rewarded, Muhammad generalized the principle.**

    > “In every living creature there is a reward.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 2363](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2363)

183. **Cruel neglect of an animal is treated as serious sin.** A woman was condemned for confining a cat without feeding it or allowing it to find food.

    > “She neither fed it nor gave it water when she confined it, nor let it eat from the creatures of the earth.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 3318](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3318)

184. **He required minimizing suffering even when an animal was being slaughtered for food.**

    > “God has prescribed excellence in everything... when you slaughter, slaughter well; sharpen the blade and spare the animal suffering.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih Muslim 1955a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:1955a)

185. **He forbade using living animals as target practice.**

    > “Do not take anything in which there is a soul as a target.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih Muslim 1957a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:1957a)

186. **He prohibited striking or branding animals on the face.**

    > “The Messenger of God forbade striking the face and branding on the face.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih Muslim 2116a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2116a)

187. **Seeing an emaciated camel, he turned animal care into a matter of accountability before God.**

    > “Fear God regarding these animals that cannot speak. Ride them when they are fit, and eat them when they are fit.”
    >
    > Source: [Sunan Abi Dawud 2548](https://sunnah.com/abudawud:2548)

188. **Planting food that later benefits a person, bird, or animal is counted as charity.**

    > “Whenever a Muslim plants a tree and a person or animal eats from it, it is charity for him.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 2320](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2320)

## 6.2 Reforms that changed the moral position of vulnerable groups

189. **The Qur'an condemns female infanticide by imagining the murdered girl herself called to testify.**

    > “When the girl buried alive is asked for what sin she was killed.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 81:8–9](https://quran.com/81/8-9)

190. **Muhammad also named burying daughters alive among practices God had forbidden.**

    > “God has forbidden for you... burying daughters alive.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 2408](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2408)

191. **Women were given defined inheritance shares in a society where inheritance had often followed male power.**

    > “For men is a share of what parents and relatives leave, and for women is a share of what parents and relatives leave, whether little or much.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 4:7](https://quran.com/4/7)

192. **A wife's marriage gift is assigned directly to her.**

    > “Give women their marriage gifts graciously.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 4:4](https://quran.com/4/4)

193. **Spiritual reward is stated without a male monopoly.**

    > “Whoever does righteous deeds, male or female, while believing, will enter Paradise and will not be wronged in the least.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 4:124](https://quran.com/4/124)

194. **Men are addressed first in the Qur'an's command to control the sexual gaze.** Modesty is not assigned to women alone.

    > “Tell the believing men to lower their gaze and guard their chastity; that is purer for them.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 24:30](https://quran.com/24/30)

195. **Serious sexual accusations require evidence, protecting people from casual destruction of reputation.**

    > “Those who accuse chaste women and then do not produce four witnesses... do not accept their testimony thereafter.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 24:4](https://quran.com/24/4)

196. **Intoxicants and gambling are rejected partly because of the social hostility and impaired worship they produce.**

    > “Intoxicants and gambling... are filth from Satan's work, so avoid them... Satan only seeks to create hostility and hatred between you through them.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 5:90–91](https://quran.com/5/90-91)

197. **The social ethic repeatedly moves responsibility away from inherited status and toward conduct.** Race, tribe, sex, wealth, and lineage are not sufficient measures of standing before God.

    > “The most honored of you before God is the most God-conscious of you.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 49:13](https://quran.com/49/13)

198. **He noticed the disappearance of a Black person whose work others had treated as insignificant.** When told that the mosque cleaner had died and that people had not thought the death important enough to tell him about, Muhammad asked to be shown the grave and prayed there.

    > “Why did you not inform me? Show me the grave.” Then he went to the grave and prayed for that person.
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 458](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:458)

## 6.3 Extraordinary signs reported in the Islamic sources

Character and ethics are one part of the case for prophethood. The Islamic sources also report extraordinary signs. A careful reader should distinguish two questions: **what the sources claim happened**, and **how much historical weight the reader gives those transmitted reports**. The following points are therefore presented as traditional evidence, not as a demand that a skeptical reader assume the reports are true before evaluating their transmission.

199. **Muhammad presented the Qur'an itself as a public sign and issued a literary challenge to those who regarded it as human composition.**

    > “If you are in doubt about what We have sent down upon Our servant, then produce a chapter like it.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 2:23](https://quran.com/2/23)

200. **The Qur'an links Muhammad's mission with the reported splitting of the moon, and multiple canonical hadith preserve eyewitness reports of the event.**

    > “The Hour has drawn near, and the moon has split.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 54:1](https://quran.com/54/1); see also [Sahih al-Bukhari 3637](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3637)

201. **Companions reported water flowing abundantly from between his fingers at Hudaybiyyah when the group lacked enough water.**

    > “He placed his hand in the vessel, and water began flowing from between his fingers like springs.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 3576](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3576)

202. **A canonical report records Muhammad predicting that his grandson Hasan would reconcile two large Muslim factions.** The same hadith's narrative then describes Hasan's later peace agreement.

    > “This son of mine is a noble leader, and perhaps God will reconcile through him two great groups of Muslims.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 2704](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2704)

203. **A canonical report records him saying that Ammar ibn Yasir would be killed by a rebellious group, decades before Ammar died in the first Muslim civil war.**

    > “May God have mercy on Ammar. He will be killed by a rebellious, aggressive group.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 2812](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2812)

204. **The Qur'an presents the Night Journey as a divine sign shown to Muhammad.**

    > “Glory be to the One who took His servant by night from the Sacred Mosque to the Farthest Mosque... so that We might show him some of Our signs.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 17:1](https://quran.com/17/1)

205. **The Qur'an made a striking near-term prediction about the defeated Byzantines returning to victory within a few years.** Evaluating it historically requires a separate discussion of chronology, but the claim itself is clear in the text.

    > “The Romans have been defeated in a nearby land. Yet after their defeat they will triumph within a few years.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 30:2–4](https://quran.com/30/2-4)

# 7. What the pattern adds up to

No single item above forces a conclusion. A kind person need not be a prophet. A courageous leader need not be a prophet. A reported miracle must be evaluated through the sources that transmit it. A scripture's claim about itself still has to be judged rather than merely repeated.

The case becomes more interesting when the features are considered **together**.

206. **His prophetic claim did not begin with a known reputation for lying.** His wife appealed to his established goodness, and a then-enemy told a foreign ruler that he had never known Muhammad to lie.

    > “Had you ever accused him of lying before this?” He said, “No.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 7](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:7)

207. **He did not use his religious status to claim divinity, omniscience, or supernatural control.**

    > “I am only a human being like you, to whom it is revealed that your God is one God.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 18:110](https://quran.com/18/110)

208. **He rejected an apparently miraculous coincidence when accepting it would have elevated him.**

    > “The sun and moon do not eclipse because of anyone's death or birth.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 1043](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:1043)

209. **The scripture he presented as revelation contains corrections of him rather than functioning as uninterrupted self-praise.**

    > “He frowned and turned away because the blind man came to him.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 80:1–2](https://quran.com/80/1-2)

210. **His private life supplied unusually strong witnesses to gentleness.** A wife said he never struck a woman or servant, and a man who served him for a decade remembered no verbal expression of contempt.

    > “I served him for ten years. He never said ‘uff’ to me.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 6038](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6038)

211. **Power did not make him a theological object of worship or a wealthy dynast living in obvious luxury.**

    > “Do not exaggerate in praising me... I am only His servant.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 3445](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3445)

212. **His ethical teaching reaches from interior motives to institutions.** It addresses intention, anger, truth, family, debt, commerce, rulers, courts, war, religious difference, animals, and the poor.

    > “God commands justice, excellence, and giving to relatives, and forbids indecency, wrongdoing, and transgression.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 16:90](https://quran.com/16/90)

213. **The demands of justice were repeatedly applied against the interests of his own side.** The Qur'an requires justice even against oneself and relatives, while Muhammad abolished his family's usury claim and rejected legal favoritism for elites.

    > “Stand firmly for justice... even against yourselves, your parents, or close relatives.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 4:135](https://quran.com/4/135)

214. **His mercy is most revealing where retaliation would have been understandable.** He prayed for hostile people, refused destruction after Ta'if, and spared a man who had stood over him with a sword.

    > “No. I hope that God will bring from their descendants people who worship God alone.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 3231](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3231)

215. **His theology persistently points away from Muhammad and toward God.** Prayer goes to God, forgiveness comes from God, salvation depends on God's mercy, and even Muhammad's family cannot rely on kinship with him.

    > “When My servants ask you about Me, I am near. I answer the call of the caller when he calls upon Me.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 2:186](https://quran.com/2/186)

216. **His moral message presents itself as continuity with Abraham, Moses, Jesus, and the earlier prophetic tradition rather than as worship of a new founder.**

    > “We make no distinction between any of them, and to Him we submit.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 2:136](https://quran.com/2/136)

217. **The central test remains open to investigation rather than personality alone.** Muhammad's character is evidence about whether he plausibly believed and lived his claim; the Qur'an he brought is the claim's central content and must also be examined on its own terms.

    > “Will they not reflect deeply on the Qur'an?”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 4:82](https://quran.com/4/82)

## A fair conclusion

The most careful conclusion is not that “Muhammad ﷺ was good, therefore he must have been a prophet.” That inference is too quick. The stronger point is that **many common explanations for a false prophetic claimant fit the recorded life poorly**.

The sources do not show a man building toward his own divinity. They show a man repeatedly telling people he was only God's servant. They do not show someone seizing every coincidence as proof of supernatural favor. They preserve him rejecting the eclipse story that would have glorified him. They do not hide all criticism of him. The Qur'an preserves correction. They do not show his private household remembering a different moral personality from the one in his sermons. Aisha and Anas describe unusual gentleness at close range. They do not show material success culminating in extravagant personal wealth. His household remained simple, and he died without a private treasury.

At the same time, the message he preached was not morally thin. It connected worship of one God with truthfulness, intention, justice, mercy, family responsibility, women's consent and property rights, care for orphans and the poor, emancipation, fair trade, written debts, honest government, restraint in war, protection of noncombatants and covenants, learning, medical treatment, animal welfare, and personal humility. It also confronted his own community's interests when justice demanded it.

A skeptical reader can reasonably ask further questions about the transmission of particular hadith, the interpretation of difficult historical episodes, the Qur'an's origin, or the evidential force of miracle reports. Those questions should be asked carefully. But they should be asked after taking the **whole pattern** seriously.

For someone deciding whether Muhammad ﷺ was a genuine prophet of God, that pattern is at least substantial evidence: **a long reputation for truthfulness, a costly mission, remarkable consistency between public and private life, restraint when revenge was available, simplicity when wealth was available, humility when self-deification was available, moral seriousness when favoritism was available, and a message that relentlessly directs worship away from himself and toward one God**.

That is not a trivial profile to explain away. It is one of the central reasons Muslims across centuries have regarded his life, not only his words, as evidence for the truth of his mission.
