> **Part of:** [Prophet Muhammad ﷺ: Evidence for His Prophethood](/messenger)  
> This is an in-depth evidence page. Return to the main argument at any time.

Moral teaching does not prove revelation by itself. Its relevance to the prophethood question is narrower: it establishes what Prophet Muhammad ﷺ publicly taught, so those teachings can be compared with his private conduct, his treatment of vulnerable people and opponents, and his behavior after acquiring authority. The material below preserves the detailed evidence from the original page.

## Work, trade, wealth, charity, and market integrity

- Prophet Muhammad ﷺ taught that the best food a person eats is what he earns through his own work, citing the prophet David as an example.

   > “No one has eaten food better than that earned by the work of his own hands.” — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 2072[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- Prophet Muhammad ﷺ taught buyers and sellers to be truthful and disclose defects, warning that lying and concealment remove the blessing from a transaction.

   > “If the buyer and seller speak truthfully and disclose defects, their transaction is blessed; if they lie and conceal, its blessing is erased.” — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 2079[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- The Qur’an condemns cheating people by taking full measure for oneself while giving others less.

   > "Woe to the defrauders. Those who take full measure ˹when they buy˺ from people, but give less when they measure or weigh for buyers." (**Sūrat al-Muṭaffifīn 83:1-3**), Dr. Mustafa Khattab, *The Clear Quran*[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}
   >
   > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿وَيْلٌ لِّلْمُطَفِّفِينَ ۝١ ٱلَّذِينَ إِذَا ٱكْتَالُوا۟ عَلَى ٱلنَّاسِ يَسْتَوْفُونَ ۝٢ وَإِذَا كَالُوهُمْ أَو وَّزَنُوهُمْ يُخْسِرُونَ ۝٣﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ المُطَفِّفِينَ</span></div>
   {: .quote }

- In a hadith reporting words from God, withholding a worker’s earned wages is named among the wrongs for which God will be the offender’s adversary on the Day of Judgment.

   > God says: “I will be the opponent on the Day of Resurrection of... a man who hires a worker, receives the full work from him, then does not pay his wage.” — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 2270[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- Prophet Muhammad ﷺ taught that a wealthy person who deliberately delays paying a debt is acting unjustly.

   > “Delay in payment by a wealthy person is injustice.” — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 2287[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- Prophet Muhammad ﷺ prayed for mercy on people who are easygoing when buying, selling, and collecting what they are owed.

   > “May God have mercy on a person who is easygoing when he sells, when he buys, and when he asks for what is owed.” — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 2076[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- The Qur’an forbids taking other people’s wealth wrongfully and using bribery to obtain what belongs to them.

   > "Do not consume one another's wealth unjustly, nor deliberately bribe authorities in order to devour a portion of others' property, knowing that it is a sin." (**Sūrat al-Baqarah 2:188**), Dr. Mustafa Khattab, *The Clear Quran*[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}
   >
   > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿وَلَا تَأْكُلُوٓا۟ أَمْوَٰلَكُم بَيْنَكُم بِٱلْبَـٰطِلِ وَتُدْلُوا۟ بِهَآ إِلَى ٱلْحُكَّامِ لِتَأْكُلُوا۟ فَرِيقًا مِّنْ أَمْوَٰلِ ٱلنَّاسِ بِٱلْإِثْمِ وَأَنتُمْ تَعْلَمُونَ ۝١٨٨﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ البَقَرَةِ</span></div>
   {: .quote }

- Qur’an 9:60 directs mandatory alms to specified groups including the poor, the needy, people in bondage, debtors, and travelers, making support for vulnerable people part of public finance.

   > "Alms-tax is only for the poor and the needy, for those employed to administer it, for those whose hearts are attracted ˹to the faith˺, for ˹freeing˺ slaves, for those in debt, for Allah's cause, and for ˹needy˺ travellers. ˹This is˺ an obligation from Allah. And Allah is All-Knowing, All-Wise." (**Sūrat al-Tawbah 9:60**), Dr. Mustafa Khattab, *The Clear Quran*[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}
   >
   > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿۞ إِنَّمَا ٱلصَّدَقَـٰتُ لِلْفُقَرَآءِ وَٱلْمَسَـٰكِينِ وَٱلْعَـٰمِلِينَ عَلَيْهَا وَٱلْمُؤَلَّفَةِ قُلُوبُهُمْ وَفِى ٱلرِّقَابِ وَٱلْغَـٰرِمِينَ وَفِى سَبِيلِ ٱللَّهِ وَٱبْنِ ٱلسَّبِيلِ ۖ فَرِيضَةً مِّنَ ٱللَّهِ ۗ وَٱللَّهُ عَلِيمٌ حَكِيمٌ ۝٦٠﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ التَّوۡبَةِ</span></div>
   {: .quote }

- Prophet Muhammad ﷺ taught that true wealth is contentment of the soul, not simply having many possessions.

   > “Richness is not having many possessions. True richness is richness of the soul.” — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 6446[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- When Prophet Muhammad ﷺ found wet grain concealed beneath dry grain in the market, he told the seller to expose the defect so buyers could see it and condemned deception.

   > “Why did you not put it on top so people could see it? Whoever deceives us is not of us.” — *Sahih Muslim* 102[3](/messenger/sources#ref-3){: .footnote}[14](/messenger/sources#ref-14){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- The Qur’an requires mutual consent in lawful trade and forbids consuming one another’s wealth wrongfully.

   > "O believers! Do not devour one another's wealth illegally, but rather trade by mutual consent. And do not kill ˹each other or˺ yourselves. Surely Allah is ever Merciful to you." (**Sūrat al-Nisāʾ 4:29**), Dr. Mustafa Khattab, *The Clear Quran*[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}
   >
   > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿يَـٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ لَا تَأْكُلُوٓا۟ أَمْوَٰلَكُم بَيْنَكُم بِٱلْبَـٰطِلِ إِلَّآ أَن تَكُونَ تِجَـٰرَةً عَن تَرَاضٍ مِّنكُمْ ۚ وَلَا تَقْتُلُوٓا۟ أَنفُسَكُمْ ۚ إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ كَانَ بِكُمْ رَحِيمًا ۝٢٩﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ النِّسَاءِ</span></div>
   {: .quote }

- The Qur’an prohibits *riba* [prohibited increase], distinguishes it from ordinary trade, and tells creditors who repent to keep only their principal so neither side is wronged.

   > "O believers! Fear Allah, and give up outstanding interest if you are ˹true˺ believers. If you do not, then beware of a war with Allah and His Messenger! But if you repent, you may retain your principal—neither inflicting nor suffering harm." (**Sūrat al-Baqarah 2:278-279**), Dr. Mustafa Khattab, *The Clear Quran*[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}
   >
   > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿يَـٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ ٱتَّقُوا۟ ٱللَّهَ وَذَرُوا۟ مَا بَقِىَ مِنَ ٱلرِّبَوٰٓا۟ إِن كُنتُم مُّؤْمِنِينَ ۝٢٧٨ فَإِن لَّمْ تَفْعَلُوا۟ فَأْذَنُوا۟ بِحَرْبٍ مِّنَ ٱللَّهِ وَرَسُولِهِۦ ۖ وَإِن تُبْتُمْ فَلَكُمْ رُءُوسُ أَمْوَٰلِكُمْ لَا تَظْلِمُونَ وَلَا تُظْلَمُونَ ۝٢٧٩﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ البَقَرَةِ</span></div>
   {: .quote }

- Prophet Muhammad ﷺ condemned deliberately hoarding goods to profit from scarcity.

   > “No one hoards except a sinner.” — *Sahih Muslim* 1605a[3](/messenger/sources#ref-3){: .footnote}[14](/messenger/sources#ref-14){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- Prophet Muhammad ﷺ prohibited sham bidding used only to drive up another buyer’s price.

   > “The Prophet ﷺ forbade artificial bidding.” — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 2140[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- When Umar acquired valuable land, Prophet Muhammad ﷺ advised him to preserve the property itself and dedicate its yield to the poor, relatives, travelers, and other beneficiaries.

   > “Retain the property itself and give its produce in charity.” — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 2737, 2764; *Sahih Muslim* 1632[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[3](/messenger/sources#ref-3){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[14](/messenger/sources#ref-14){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}[61](/messenger/sources#ref-61){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

## Knowledge, reflection, expertise, and responsible teaching

- The Qur’an teaches Prophet Muhammad ﷺ to ask God for an increase in knowledge.

   > "So high [above all] is Allah, the Sovereign, the Truth. And, [O Muhammad], do not hasten with [recitation of] the Qur'an before its revelation is completed to you, and say, 'My Lord, increase me in knowledge.'" (**Sūrat Ṭāhā 20:114**), Saheeh International[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}
   >
   > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿فَتَعَـٰلَى ٱللَّهُ ٱلْمَلِكُ ٱلْحَقُّ ۗ وَلَا تَعْجَلْ بِٱلْقُرْءَانِ مِن قَبْلِ أَن يُقْضَىٰٓ إِلَيْكَ وَحْيُهُۥ ۖ وَقُل رَّبِّ زِدْنِى عِلْمًا ۝١١٤﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ طه</span></div>
   {: .quote }

- The opening revelation begins with a command to read or recite in God’s name and immediately speaks of teaching and the pen.

   > "Read, ˹O Prophet,˺ in the Name of your Lord Who created— created humans from a clinging clot. Read! And your Lord is the Most Generous, Who taught by the pen— taught humanity what they knew not." (**Sūrat al-ʿAlaq 96:1-5**), Dr. Mustafa Khattab, *The Clear Quran*[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}
   >
   > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿ٱقْرَأْ بِٱسْمِ رَبِّكَ ٱلَّذِى خَلَقَ ۝١ خَلَقَ ٱلْإِنسَـٰنَ مِنْ عَلَقٍ ۝٢ ٱقْرَأْ وَرَبُّكَ ٱلْأَكْرَمُ ۝٣ ٱلَّذِى عَلَّمَ بِٱلْقَلَمِ ۝٤ عَلَّمَ ٱلْإِنسَـٰنَ مَا لَمْ يَعْلَمْ ۝٥﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ العَلَقِ</span></div>
   {: .quote }

- Prophet Muhammad ﷺ taught that seeking knowledge is a path that God makes easy toward Paradise.

   > “Whoever travels a path seeking knowledge, God makes easy for him a path to Paradise.” — *Sahih Muslim* 2699a[3](/messenger/sources#ref-3){: .footnote}[14](/messenger/sources#ref-14){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- The Qur’an repeatedly tells people to reflect on the heavens, the earth, and the patterns of creation as signs for people who reason.

   > "Indeed, in the creation of the heavens and the earth and the alternation of the day and night there are signs for people of reason." (**Sūrat Āli ʿImrān 3:190**), Dr. Mustafa Khattab, *The Clear Quran*[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}
   >
   > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿إِنَّ فِى خَلْقِ ٱلسَّمَـٰوَٰتِ وَٱلْأَرْضِ وَٱخْتِلَـٰفِ ٱلَّيْلِ وَٱلنَّهَارِ لَـَٔايَـٰتٍ لِّأُو۟لِى ٱلْأَلْبَـٰبِ ۝١٩٠﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ آلِ عِمۡرَانَ</span></div>
   {: .quote }

- After his comments about date-palm pollination proved unhelpful, Prophet Muhammad ﷺ acknowledged that people knew the technical affairs of their worldly work better than he did.

   > “You know better about the technical affairs of your worldly life.” — *Sahih Muslim* 2363[3](/messenger/sources#ref-3){: .footnote}[14](/messenger/sources#ref-14){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- Prophet Muhammad ﷺ praised people who learn the Qur’an and then teach it to others.

   > “The best of you are those who learn the Qur’an and teach it.” — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 5027[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- Prophet Muhammad ﷺ taught that when God intends good for someone, He gives that person understanding of the religion.

   > “When God intends good for someone, He gives him understanding of the religion.” — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 71[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}; *Sahih Muslim* 1037[3](/messenger/sources#ref-3){: .footnote}[14](/messenger/sources#ref-14){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- Prophet Muhammad ﷺ taught that people who gather to recite and study God’s Book together are surrounded by tranquility and mercy.

   > “No people gather in one of God’s houses, reciting God’s Book and studying it together, except that tranquility descends upon them.” — *Sahih Muslim* 2699a[3](/messenger/sources#ref-3){: .footnote}[14](/messenger/sources#ref-14){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- Ibn Masud remembered that Prophet Muhammad ﷺ chose suitable times for teaching because he did not want listeners to become bored or exhausted.

   > “He used to choose suitable times to teach us, fearing that we would become bored.” — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 68, 70[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- The hadith literature preserves people asking Prophet Muhammad ﷺ direct questions about belief, doubt, intimacy, grief, worship, money, family problems, and death, including the famous questions in the hadith of Gabriel.

   > “Tell me about Islam... Tell me about faith... Tell me about excellence.” — *Sahih Muslim* 8[3](/messenger/sources#ref-3){: .footnote}[14](/messenger/sources#ref-14){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- Prophet Muhammad ﷺ warned severely against deliberately inventing teachings and attributing them to him.

   > “Whoever deliberately lies about me, let him take his place in the Fire.” — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 107[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

## Consultation and public leadership

- The Qur’an describes believers as conducting their collective affairs through mutual consultation.

   > "Who respond to their Lord, establish prayer, conduct their affairs by mutual consultation, and donate from what We have provided for them." (**Sūrat al-Shūrā 42:38**), Dr. Mustafa Khattab, *The Clear Quran*[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}
   >
   > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿وَٱلَّذِينَ ٱسْتَجَابُوا۟ لِرَبِّهِمْ وَأَقَامُوا۟ ٱلصَّلَوٰةَ وَأَمْرُهُمْ شُورَىٰ بَيْنَهُمْ وَمِمَّا رَزَقْنَـٰهُمْ يُنفِقُونَ ۝٣٨﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ الشُّورَىٰ</span></div>
   {: .quote }

- After the defeat at Uhud, the Qur’an told Prophet Muhammad ﷺ to remain gentle, pardon his followers, and continue consulting them.

   > "It is out of Allah's mercy that you ˹O Prophet˺ have been lenient with them. Had you been cruel or hard-hearted, they would have certainly abandoned you. So pardon them, ask Allah's forgiveness for them, and consult with them in ˹conducting˺ matters. Once you make a decision, put your trust in Allah. Surely Allah loves those who trust in Him." (**Sūrat Āli ʿImrān 3:159**), Dr. Mustafa Khattab, *The Clear Quran*[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}
   >
   > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿فَبِمَا رَحْمَةٍ مِّنَ ٱللَّهِ لِنتَ لَهُمْ ۖ وَلَوْ كُنتَ فَظًّا غَلِيظَ ٱلْقَلْبِ لَٱنفَضُّوا۟ مِنْ حَوْلِكَ ۖ فَٱعْفُ عَنْهُمْ وَٱسْتَغْفِرْ لَهُمْ وَشَاوِرْهُمْ فِى ٱلْأَمْرِ ۖ فَإِذَا عَزَمْتَ فَتَوَكَّلْ عَلَى ٱللَّهِ ۚ إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ يُحِبُّ ٱلْمُتَوَكِّلِينَ ۝١٥٩﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ آلِ عِمۡرَانَ</span></div>
   {: .quote }

   The early biography also records him accepting ideas that were not his own, including Salman al-Farisi’s unfamiliar proposal to defend Medina with a trench. At Hudaybiyyah, the canonical hadith record likewise preserves him acting on Umm Salama’s practical advice when the camp reached an impasse. **Consultation was something he practiced as well as taught.**

   > “Each of you is a shepherd, and each of you is responsible for those under his care.” — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 7138[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}

## Public office, contracts, and moral responsibility

- The Qur’an’s longest verse instructs believers to document deferred debts and use witnesses as safeguards against dispute and exploitation.

   > "O believers! When you contract a loan for a fixed period of time, commit it to writing. Let the scribe maintain justice between the parties. The scribe should not refuse to write as Allah has taught them to write. They will write what the debtor dictates, bearing Allah in mind and not defrauding the debt. If the debtor is incompetent, weak, or unable to dictate, let their guardian dictate for them with justice. Call upon two of your men to witness. If two men cannot be found, then one man and two women of your choice will witness—so if one of the women forgets the other may remind her. The witnesses must not refuse when they are summoned. You must not be against writing ˹contracts˺ for a fixed period—whether the sum is small or great. This is more just ˹for you˺ in the sight of Allah, and more convenient to establish evidence and remove doubts. However, if you conduct an immediate transaction among yourselves, then there is no need for you to record it, but call upon witnesses when a deal is finalized. Let no harm come to the scribe or witnesses. If you do, then you have gravely exceeded ˹your limits˺. Be mindful of Allah, for Allah ˹is the One Who˺ teaches you. And Allah has ˹perfect˺ knowledge of all things." (**Sūrat al-Baqarah 2:282**), Dr. Mustafa Khattab, *The Clear Quran*[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}
   >
   > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿يَـٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوٓا۟ إِذَا تَدَايَنتُم بِدَيْنٍ إِلَىٰٓ أَجَلٍ مُّسَمًّى فَٱكْتُبُوهُ ۚ وَلْيَكْتُب بَّيْنَكُمْ كَاتِبٌۢ بِٱلْعَدْلِ ۚ وَلَا يَأْبَ كَاتِبٌ أَن يَكْتُبَ كَمَا عَلَّمَهُ ٱللَّهُ ۚ فَلْيَكْتُبْ وَلْيُمْلِلِ ٱلَّذِى عَلَيْهِ ٱلْحَقُّ وَلْيَتَّقِ ٱللَّهَ رَبَّهُۥ وَلَا يَبْخَسْ مِنْهُ شَيْـًٔا ۚ فَإِن كَانَ ٱلَّذِى عَلَيْهِ ٱلْحَقُّ سَفِيهًا أَوْ ضَعِيفًا أَوْ لَا يَسْتَطِيعُ أَن يُمِلَّ هُوَ فَلْيُمْلِلْ وَلِيُّهُۥ بِٱلْعَدْلِ ۚ وَٱسْتَشْهِدُوا۟ شَهِيدَيْنِ مِن رِّجَالِكُمْ ۖ فَإِن لَّمْ يَكُونَا رَجُلَيْنِ فَرَجُلٌ وَٱمْرَأَتَانِ مِمَّن تَرْضَوْنَ مِنَ ٱلشُّهَدَآءِ أَن تَضِلَّ إِحْدَىٰهُمَا فَتُذَكِّرَ إِحْدَىٰهُمَا ٱلْأُخْرَىٰ ۚ وَلَا يَأْبَ ٱلشُّهَدَآءُ إِذَا مَا دُعُوا۟ ۚ وَلَا تَسْـَٔمُوٓا۟ أَن تَكْتُبُوهُ صَغِيرًا أَوْ كَبِيرًا إِلَىٰٓ أَجَلِهِۦ ۚ ذَٰلِكُمْ أَقْسَطُ عِندَ ٱللَّهِ وَأَقْوَمُ لِلشَّهَـٰدَةِ وَأَدْنَىٰٓ أَلَّا تَرْتَابُوٓا۟ ۖ إِلَّآ أَن تَكُونَ تِجَـٰرَةً حَاضِرَةً تُدِيرُونَهَا بَيْنَكُمْ فَلَيْسَ عَلَيْكُمْ جُنَاحٌ أَلَّا تَكْتُبُوهَا ۗ وَأَشْهِدُوٓا۟ إِذَا تَبَايَعْتُمْ ۚ وَلَا يُضَآرَّ كَاتِبٌ وَلَا شَهِيدٌ ۚ وَإِن تَفْعَلُوا۟ فَإِنَّهُۥ فُسُوقٌۢ بِكُمْ ۗ وَٱتَّقُوا۟ ٱللَّهَ ۖ وَيُعَلِّمُكُمُ ٱللَّهُ ۗ وَٱللَّهُ بِكُلِّ شَىْءٍ عَلِيمٌ ۝٢٨٢﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ البَقَرَةِ</span></div>
   {: .quote }

- When Abu Dharr asked for authority, Prophet Muhammad ﷺ refused to appoint him and explained that public office is a trust that requires the ability to fulfill its duties.

   > “Abu Dharr, you are weak, and authority is a trust... except for the one who takes it with its right and fulfills its duties.” — *Sahih Muslim* 1825[3](/messenger/sources#ref-3){: .footnote}[14](/messenger/sources#ref-14){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- Prophet Muhammad ﷺ warned people not to seek authority merely because they wanted to hold power.

   > “Do not seek authority. If it is given to you because you asked for it, you will be left to bear it; if it is given without your asking, you will be helped in it.” — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 7146[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- Prophet Muhammad ﷺ told prayer leaders to consider the elderly, the sick, and the weak by not making communal prayer unnecessarily long.

   > “When one of you leads the people in prayer, let him shorten it, for among them are the weak, the sick, and the elderly.” — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 703[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- The Qur’an tells believers to honor their contracts and obligations.

   > "O believers! Honour your obligations. All grazing livestock has been made lawful to you—except what is hereby announced to you and hunting while on pilgrimage. Indeed, Allah commands what He wills." (**Sūrat al-Māʾidah 5:1**), Dr. Mustafa Khattab, *The Clear Quran*[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}
   >
   > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿يَـٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوٓا۟ أَوْفُوا۟ بِٱلْعُقُودِ ۚ أُحِلَّتْ لَكُم بَهِيمَةُ ٱلْأَنْعَـٰمِ إِلَّا مَا يُتْلَىٰ عَلَيْكُمْ غَيْرَ مُحِلِّى ٱلصَّيْدِ وَأَنتُمْ حُرُمٌ ۗ إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ يَحْكُمُ مَا يُرِيدُ ۝١﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ المَائـِدَةِ</span></div>
   {: .quote }

- When a tax collector claimed that some property had been given to him personally, Prophet Muhammad ﷺ asked whether he would have received those gifts if he had stayed at home, treating gifts generated by public office as a conflict of interest.

   > “Why did he not sit in his father’s or mother’s house and see whether gifts would be given to him?” — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 7174[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- Prophet Muhammad ﷺ prayed that God deal harshly with leaders who make life hard for people and gently with leaders who treat them gently.

   > “O God, whoever is put in charge of my community and is hard on them, be hard on him; whoever is gentle with them, be gentle with him.” — *Sahih Muslim* 1828a[3](/messenger/sources#ref-3){: .footnote}[14](/messenger/sources#ref-14){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- Hisham ibn Hakim later invoked Prophet Muhammad’s ﷺ warning against tormenting people when he saw non-Muslim taxpayers being abused over the *jizya* [tax historically levied on certain non-Muslim subjects].

   > “God will punish those who torment people in this world.” — *Sahih Muslim* 2613b[3](/messenger/sources#ref-3){: .footnote}[14](/messenger/sources#ref-14){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- Prophet Muhammad ﷺ taught that public leadership should assume responsibility for people who die leaving debts or unsupported dependents, while their private property still passes to their heirs.

   > “Whoever leaves property, it belongs to his heirs; whoever leaves debt or dependents, we will take care of them.” — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 6763[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- Prophet Muhammad ﷺ told those making appointments not to give authority to someone merely because that person asked for it or was eager to rule.

   > “We do not appoint to this work anyone who asks for it or is eager for it.” — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 7149[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- Prophet Muhammad’s ﷺ treatment of Abu Dharr shows that personal goodness did not automatically qualify someone for every responsibility; he loved Abu Dharr but still advised him not to take charge of others or administer an orphan’s property.

   > “I love for you what I love for myself. Do not be in charge of even two people, and do not administer an orphan’s property.” — *Sahih Muslim* 1826[3](/messenger/sources#ref-3){: .footnote}[14](/messenger/sources#ref-14){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}

## Health, moderation, shared resources, animals, and creation

- The Qur’an tells people to eat and drink without waste, and a reported teaching advises moderation rather than routine overfilling.

   > "O Children of Adam! Dress properly whenever you are at worship. Eat and drink, but do not waste. Surely He does not like the wasteful." (**Sūrat al-Aʿrāf 7:31**), Dr. Mustafa Khattab, *The Clear Quran*[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}
   >
   > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿۞ يَـٰبَنِىٓ ءَادَمَ خُذُوا۟ زِينَتَكُمْ عِندَ كُلِّ مَسْجِدٍ وَكُلُوا۟ وَٱشْرَبُوا۟ وَلَا تُسْرِفُوٓا۟ ۚ إِنَّهُۥ لَا يُحِبُّ ٱلْمُسْرِفِينَ ۝٣١﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ الأَعۡرَافِ</span></div>
   {: .quote }

   > “If more is necessary, then a third for food, a third for drink, and a third for breath.” — *Jami at-Tirmidhi* 2380[5](/messenger/sources#ref-5){: .footnote}[16](/messenger/sources#ref-16){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- Prophet Muhammad ﷺ taught that planting a tree or crop can count as charity whenever a person, bird, or animal benefits from it.[61](/messenger/sources#ref-61){: .footnote}[62](/messenger/sources#ref-62){: .footnote}

   > “No Muslim plants a tree or sows a crop from which a person, bird, or animal eats except that it is charity for him.” — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 2320[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- Prophet Muhammad ﷺ told of a man whom God forgave for giving water to a desperately thirsty dog and taught that kindness to living creatures is rewarded.[62](/messenger/sources#ref-62){: .footnote}

   > “In every living creature there is a reward.” — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 2363[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- Prophet Muhammad ﷺ told of a woman punished for confining a cat until it died without feeding it or letting it find food.[62](/messenger/sources#ref-62){: .footnote}

   > “A woman was punished because of a cat she confined until it died.” — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 3318[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- Prophet Muhammad ﷺ taught that even lawful slaughter should be done carefully, with a sharp blade and the least possible suffering to the animal.

   > “God has prescribed excellence in all things... when you slaughter, slaughter well. Let one of you sharpen his blade and spare the animal suffering.” — *Sahih Muslim* 1955a[3](/messenger/sources#ref-3){: .footnote}[14](/messenger/sources#ref-14){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- Prophet Muhammad ﷺ forbade using living creatures as targets for sport.

   > “The Messenger of God ﷺ cursed whoever takes a living creature as a target.” — *Sahih Muslim* 1957a[3](/messenger/sources#ref-3){: .footnote}[14](/messenger/sources#ref-14){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- Prophet Muhammad ﷺ described purity as half of faith.

   > “Purity is half of faith.” — *Sahih Muslim* 223[3](/messenger/sources#ref-3){: .footnote}[14](/messenger/sources#ref-14){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- Prophet Muhammad ﷺ taught that illness should be treated, saying that God has made a cure for disease.

   > “God has not sent down a disease except that He has also sent down its cure.” — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 5678[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- Prophet Muhammad ﷺ told people not to enter a land where plague was occurring and not to flee a place after plague had broken out there.[67](/messenger/sources#ref-67){: .footnote}

   > “If you hear of plague in a land, do not enter it; and if it occurs in a land while you are there, do not leave it.” — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 5728[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- When Prophet Muhammad ﷺ saw a severely emaciated camel, he rebuked its neglect and told people to feed and use working animals while they were in good condition.

   > “Fear God regarding these animals. Ride them while they are in good condition, and feed them while they are in good condition.” — *Sunan Abi Dawud* 2548, graded *sahih* [sound][4](/messenger/sources#ref-4){: .footnote}[15](/messenger/sources#ref-15){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- When companions took baby birds from their mother, Prophet Muhammad ﷺ ordered them returned after seeing her distress. The same report records him objecting to the burning of an ant colony.

   > “Who has distressed this bird by taking her young? Return her young to her.” — *Sunan Abi Dawud* 2675, graded *sahih* [sound][4](/messenger/sources#ref-4){: .footnote}[15](/messenger/sources#ref-15){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- Prophet Muhammad ﷺ described health and free time as two blessings that people often fail to appreciate.

   > “There are two blessings in which many people are deceived: health and free time.” — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 6412[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- Prophet Muhammad ﷺ forbade urinating in shared stagnant water and then bathing in it.

   > “None of you should urinate in stagnant water that does not flow and then bathe in it.” — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 239[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- Prophet Muhammad ﷺ taught that the earth itself could serve as a place of prayer and a means of purification, so worship was not confined to special buildings.

   > “The earth has been made for me a place of prayer and a means of purification.” — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 335[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- Prophet Muhammad ﷺ forbade striking animals in the face.

   > “The Messenger of God ﷺ forbade striking on the face.” — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 5541[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}

- Prophet Muhammad ﷺ also condemned mutilating animals.

   > “The Prophet ﷺ cursed the one who mutilates an animal.” — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 5515[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}

   > “Muslims are partners in three: water, pasture, and fire.” — *Sunan Abi Dawud* 3477, graded sound[4](/messenger/sources#ref-4){: .footnote}[15](/messenger/sources#ref-15){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}

## Judicial caution and limits on authority

- When people asked Prophet Muhammad ﷺ to fix rising prices, he declined because he feared meeting God responsible for an injustice concerning another person’s property or life. The incident presents authority as something that should not be exercised merely because the power exists.

   > “I hope to meet God with no one having a claim against me for an injustice concerning blood or property.” — *Sunan Abi Dawud* 3451, graded sound[4](/messenger/sources#ref-4){: .footnote}[15](/messenger/sources#ref-15){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- When a young man openly asked permission to commit adultery, Prophet Muhammad ﷺ called him close and asked whether he would accept the same act involving his own mother, daughter, sister, or aunt. The young man answered no each time, after which Prophet Muhammad ﷺ prayed for him.

   > The young man was asked whether he would accept the act for his own female relatives, and each time answered that he would not. — *Musnad Ahmad* 22211, graded sound by Shuayb al-Arnaut[10](/messenger/sources#ref-10){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- When Ma’iz repeatedly confessed to adultery, Prophet Muhammad ﷺ turned him away several times and investigated whether he understood what he was saying and whether the legal conditions applied before accepting the confession.

   > The report describes Ma’iz returning repeatedly before the confession was finally accepted. — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 6825[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- Prophet Muhammad ﷺ openly acknowledged that a court can reach the wrong result if one litigant presents a more persuasive case, and he warned that a favorable ruling does not make another person’s property morally lawful.

   > “I am only a human being. You bring me your disputes, and one of you may be more persuasive than another... If I award him something from his brother’s right, let him not take it.” — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 2680[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }
