> This page preserves the detailed evidence behind one part of the main cumulative case: the moral vision Prophet Muhammad ﷺ taught concerning vulnerable people and the home. Individual teachings do not prove prophethood by themselves; their significance comes from the larger pattern of message and life.

Prophet Muhammad ﷺ’s teachings do not separate worship from ordinary conduct. Duties to God repeatedly appear beside duties to family, neighbors, the poor, strangers, workers, opponents, animals, and the wider community. The result is a moral vision in which devotion is tested by how people actually treat one another. Because this section gathers many examples, they are grouped by theme so that the larger pattern remains visible.[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[3](/messenger/sources#ref-3){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[14](/messenger/sources#ref-14){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}

## The vulnerable, neighbors, servants, and human dignity
{: #the-poor-the-vulnerable-neighbors-servants-and-social-dignity }

### Poverty, orphans, debt, and practical care

- The Qur’an presents righteousness as including faith, prayer, keeping promises, patience, and giving cherished wealth to relatives, orphans, the poor, stranded travelers, people who ask for help, and people seeking freedom from bondage.[56](/messenger/sources#ref-56){: .footnote}[61](/messenger/sources#ref-61){: .footnote}

   > "Righteousness is not in turning your faces towards the east or the west. Rather, the righteous are those who believe in Allah, the Last Day, the angels, the Books, and the prophets; who give charity out of their cherished wealth to relatives, orphans, the poor, ˹needy˺ travellers, beggars, and for freeing captives; who establish prayer, pay alms-tax, and keep the pledges they make; and who are patient in times of suffering, adversity, and in ˹the heat of˺ battle. It is they who are true ˹in faith˺, and it is they who are mindful ˹of Allah˺." (**Sūrat al-Baqarah 2:177**), Dr. Mustafa Khattab, *The Clear Quran*[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}
   >
   > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿۞ لَّيْسَ ٱلْبِرَّ أَن تُوَلُّوا۟ وُجُوهَكُمْ قِبَلَ ٱلْمَشْرِقِ وَٱلْمَغْرِبِ وَلَـٰكِنَّ ٱلْبِرَّ مَنْ ءَامَنَ بِٱللَّهِ وَٱلْيَوْمِ ٱلْـَٔاخِرِ وَٱلْمَلَـٰٓئِكَةِ وَٱلْكِتَـٰبِ وَٱلنَّبِيِّـۧنَ وَءَاتَى ٱلْمَالَ عَلَىٰ حُبِّهِۦ ذَوِى ٱلْقُرْبَىٰ وَٱلْيَتَـٰمَىٰ وَٱلْمَسَـٰكِينَ وَٱبْنَ ٱلسَّبِيلِ وَٱلسَّآئِلِينَ وَفِى ٱلرِّقَابِ وَأَقَامَ ٱلصَّلَوٰةَ وَءَاتَى ٱلزَّكَوٰةَ وَٱلْمُوفُونَ بِعَهْدِهِمْ إِذَا عَـٰهَدُوا۟ ۖ وَٱلصَّـٰبِرِينَ فِى ٱلْبَأْسَآءِ وَٱلضَّرَّآءِ وَحِينَ ٱلْبَأْسِ ۗ أُو۟لَـٰٓئِكَ ٱلَّذِينَ صَدَقُوا۟ ۖ وَأُو۟لَـٰٓئِكَ هُمُ ٱلْمُتَّقُونَ ۝١٧٧﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ البَقَرَةِ</span></div>
   {: .quote }

- The Qur’an condemns people who repel orphans and do not encourage feeding the poor.

   > "Have you seen the one who denies the ˹final˺ Judgment? That is the one who repulses the orphan and does not encourage the feeding of the poor." (**Sūrat al-Māʿūn 107:1-3**), Dr. Mustafa Khattab, *The Clear Quran*[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}
   >
   > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿ أَرَءَيْتَ ٱلَّذِى يُكَذِّبُ بِٱلدِّينِ ۝١ فَذَٰلِكَ ٱلَّذِى يَدُعُّ ٱلْيَتِيمَ ۝٢ وَلَا يَحُضُّ عَلَىٰ طَعَامِ ٱلْمِسْكِينِ ۝٣﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ المَاعُونِ</span></div>
   {: .quote }

- The Qur’an praises freeing an enslaved person and feeding an orphaned relative or a poor person during hunger as difficult forms of goodness.[54](/messenger/sources#ref-54){: .footnote}[56](/messenger/sources#ref-56){: .footnote}

   > "And what will make you realize what ˹attempting˺ the challenging path is? It is to free a slave, or to give food in times of famine, to an orphaned relative, or to a poor person in distress." (**Sūrat al-Balad 90:12-16**), Dr. Mustafa Khattab, *The Clear Quran*[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}
   >
   > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿وَمَآ أَدْرَىٰكَ مَا ٱلْعَقَبَةُ ۝١٢ فَكُّ رَقَبَةٍ ۝١٣ أَوْ إِطْعَـٰمٌ فِى يَوْمٍ ذِى مَسْغَبَةٍ ۝١٤ يَتِيمًا ذَا مَقْرَبَةٍ ۝١٥ أَوْ مِسْكِينًا ذَا مَتْرَبَةٍ ۝١٦﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ البَلَدِ</span></div>
   {: .quote }

- Prophet Muhammad ﷺ compared caring for widows and the poor to striving in God’s cause, worshipping through the night, or fasting through the day.

   > "The one who works for the widow and the poor is like one striving in God's cause, or like one who prays through the night and fasts through the day." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 5353[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- Prophet Muhammad ﷺ said that a person who cares for an orphan would be close to him in Paradise.

   > "I and the caretaker of an orphan will be in Paradise like this," and he brought his index and middle fingers together. — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 6005[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- Prophet Muhammad ﷺ taught that relieving another person’s hardship, easing a debtor’s difficulty, and protecting another person’s dignity are acts for which God gives reward.

   > "Whoever relieves a believer of a worldly hardship, God will relieve him of a hardship on the Day of Resurrection." — *Sahih Muslim* 2699a[3](/messenger/sources#ref-3){: .footnote}[14](/messenger/sources#ref-14){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- The Qur’an requires a creditor to give more time to a debtor who genuinely cannot repay and encourages forgiving the debt as charity.

   > "If it is difficult for someone to repay a debt, postpone it until a time of ease. And if you waive it as an act of charity, it will be better for you, if only you knew." (**Sūrat al-Baqarah 2:280**), Dr. Mustafa Khattab, *The Clear Quran*[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}
   >
   > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿وَإِن كَانَ ذُو عُسْرَةٍ فَنَظِرَةٌ إِلَىٰ مَيْسَرَةٍ ۚ وَأَن تَصَدَّقُوا۟ خَيْرٌ لَّكُمْ ۖ إِن كُنتُمْ تَعْلَمُونَ ۝٢٨٠﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ البَقَرَةِ</span></div>
   {: .quote }

- Prophet Muhammad ﷺ told of a creditor whom God forgave because he gave people time and was lenient with those in financial hardship.

   > "Give time to one in ease, and overlook one in hardship." Then God overlooked him. — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 2077[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- Prophet Muhammad ﷺ taught that sincerely spending on one’s own family counts as charity.

   > "When a Muslim spends on his family seeking reward, it is charity for him." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 5351[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- Prophet Muhammad ﷺ taught that it is better to leave one’s heirs financially secure than to leave them poor and dependent on others.

   > "To leave your heirs well provided for is better than leaving them poor, begging from people." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 5354[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

### Neighbors, hospitality, and communal responsibility

- Prophet Muhammad ﷺ taught that a believer must not harm his neighbor.

   > "Whoever believes in God and the Last Day must not harm his neighbor." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 6018[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- Prophet Muhammad ﷺ said Gabriel instructed him so repeatedly about neighbors that he thought neighbors might eventually be given inheritance rights.

   > "Gabriel kept instructing me concerning the neighbor until I thought he would make the neighbor an heir." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 6014[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- Prophet Muhammad ﷺ taught believers to honor their guests, protect their neighbors, and discipline their speech.

   > "Whoever believes in God and the Last Day should honor his guest." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 6018[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- Prophet Muhammad ﷺ encouraged people to feed others and greet both those they knew and those they did not know.

   > A man asked, "What part of Islam is best?" He said: "Feed people, and greet those you know and those you do not know." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 12[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- Prophet Muhammad ﷺ compared believers in their mutual affection and compassion to one body that shares the pain of any injured part.

   > "The believers in their mutual affection, mercy, and compassion are like one body: when one limb suffers, the whole body responds with sleeplessness and fever." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 6011[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

### Human dignity, social status, and freedom from abuse

- After Abu Dharr insulted another man through his mother, Prophet Muhammad ﷺ rebuked him and described the attitude as a remnant of pre-Islamic ignorance.

   > "You are a man in whom there remains ignorance." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 30[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- In the same rebuke, Prophet Muhammad ﷺ required masters to feed and clothe enslaved people to the same standard as themselves.

   > "Your servants are your brothers whom God placed under your authority. Feed them from what you eat and clothe them from what you wear." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 30[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- Prophet Muhammad ﷺ also taught that if a servant was given work too heavy to manage, the master had to help with it.

   > "Do not burden them with what overwhelms them; and if you burden them, then help them." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 30[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- The Qur’an repeatedly prescribes freeing enslaved people as a way to repair serious wrongs, and Prophet Muhammad ﷺ explicitly praised setting people free.

   > "Whoever frees a Muslim slave, God will save from the Fire a limb of his for every limb he freed." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 2517[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- The Qur’an teaches that all human beings come from a common origin and that racial or tribal background does not determine a person’s worth before God.

   > "O humanity! Indeed, We created you from a male and a female, and made you into peoples and tribes so that you may ˹get to˺ know one another. Surely the most noble of you in the sight of Allah is the most righteous among you. Allah is truly All-Knowing, All-Aware." (**Sūrat al-Ḥujurāt 49:13**), Dr. Mustafa Khattab, *The Clear Quran*[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}
   >
   > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿يَـٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلنَّاسُ إِنَّا خَلَقْنَـٰكُم مِّن ذَكَرٍ وَأُنثَىٰ وَجَعَلْنَـٰكُمْ شُعُوبًا وَقَبَآئِلَ لِتَعَارَفُوٓا۟ ۚ إِنَّ أَكْرَمَكُمْ عِندَ ٱللَّهِ أَتْقَىٰكُمْ ۚ إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ عَلِيمٌ خَبِيرٌ ۝١٣﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ الحُجُرَاتِ</span></div>
   {: .quote }

- The Qur’an states that God has honored the children of Adam as human beings.

   > "Indeed, We have dignified the children of Adam, carried them on land and sea, granted them good and lawful provisions, and privileged them far above many of Our creatures." (**Sūrat al-Isrāʾ 17:70**), Dr. Mustafa Khattab, *The Clear Quran*[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}
   >
   > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿۞ وَلَقَدْ كَرَّمْنَا بَنِىٓ ءَادَمَ وَحَمَلْنَـٰهُمْ فِى ٱلْبَرِّ وَٱلْبَحْرِ وَرَزَقْنَـٰهُم مِّنَ ٱلطَّيِّبَـٰتِ وَفَضَّلْنَـٰهُمْ عَلَىٰ كَثِيرٍ مِّمَّنْ خَلَقْنَا تَفْضِيلًا ۝٧٠﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ الإِسۡرَاءِ</span></div>
   {: .quote }

- Prophet Muhammad ﷺ taught that wrongfully striking or beating an enslaved person could require setting that person free.

   > "Whoever slaps or beats his slave, the expiation for it is to set him free." — *Sahih Muslim* 1657b[3](/messenger/sources#ref-3){: .footnote}[14](/messenger/sources#ref-14){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- In a hadith reporting words from God, selling a free person into slavery and consuming the price is named among the wrongs for which God will be the offender’s adversary on the Day of Judgment.

   > "I will be the opponent of... a man who sells a free person and consumes the price." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 2270[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- Bilal, a formerly enslaved Abyssinian Muslim, became the best-known caller to prayer in Prophet Muhammad’s ﷺ community, and Prophet Muhammad ﷺ told him that he had heard his footsteps ahead of him in Paradise.

   > "Tell me of the deed you have done in Islam for which you most hope, for I heard your footsteps before me in Paradise." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 1149[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- The Qur’an expressly forbids killing children out of fear of poverty.

   > "Say, ˹O Prophet,˺ 'Come! Let me recite to you what your Lord has forbidden to you: do not associate others with Him ˹in worship˺. ˹Do not fail to˺ honour your parents. Do not kill your children for fear of poverty. We provide for you and for them. Do not come near indecencies, openly or secretly. Do not take a ˹human˺ life—made sacred by Allah—except with ˹legal˺ right. This is what He has commanded you, so perhaps you will understand.'" (**Sūrat al-Anʿām 6:151**), Dr. Mustafa Khattab, *The Clear Quran*[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}
   >
   > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿۞ قُلْ تَعَالَوْا۟ أَتْلُ مَا حَرَّمَ رَبُّكُمْ عَلَيْكُمْ ۖ أَلَّا تُشْرِكُوا۟ بِهِۦ شَيْـًٔا ۖ وَبِٱلْوَٰلِدَيْنِ إِحْسَـٰنًا ۖ وَلَا تَقْتُلُوٓا۟ أَوْلَـٰدَكُم مِّنْ إِمْلَـٰقٍ ۖ نَّحْنُ نَرْزُقُكُمْ وَإِيَّاهُمْ ۖ وَلَا تَقْرَبُوا۟ ٱلْفَوَٰحِشَ مَا ظَهَرَ مِنْهَا وَمَا بَطَنَ ۖ وَلَا تَقْتُلُوا۟ ٱلنَّفْسَ ٱلَّتِى حَرَّمَ ٱللَّهُ إِلَّا بِٱلْحَقِّ ۚ ذَٰلِكُمْ وَصَّىٰكُم بِهِۦ لَعَلَّكُمْ تَعْقِلُونَ ۝١٥١﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ الأَنۡعَامِ</span></div>
   {: .quote }

- The Qur’an treats unjustly consuming an orphan’s property as a grave offense, not merely neglect of the orphan’s physical needs.

   > "Indeed, those who unjustly consume orphans' wealth ˹in fact˺ consume nothing but fire into their bellies. And they will be burned in a blazing Hell." (**Sūrat al-Nisāʾ 4:10**), Dr. Mustafa Khattab, *The Clear Quran*[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}
   >
   > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿إِنَّ ٱلَّذِينَ يَأْكُلُونَ أَمْوَٰلَ ٱلْيَتَـٰمَىٰ ظُلْمًا إِنَّمَا يَأْكُلُونَ فِى بُطُونِهِمْ نَارًا ۖ وَسَيَصْلَوْنَ سَعِيرًا ۝١٠﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ النِّسَاءِ</span></div>
   {: .quote }

### Charity, shared responsibility, and dignity in giving

- Prophet Muhammad ﷺ taught that moral good is within everyone’s reach: even a small gift, a good word, a cheerful face, removing an obstacle, or helping another person can count as charity.

   > "Do not belittle any good deed, even meeting your brother with a cheerful face." — *Sahih Muslim* 2626[3](/messenger/sources#ref-3){: .footnote}[14](/messenger/sources#ref-14){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- Prophet Muhammad ﷺ taught that giving charity does not diminish a person’s wealth.

   > "Charity does not diminish wealth." — *Sahih Muslim* 2588[3](/messenger/sources#ref-3){: .footnote}[14](/messenger/sources#ref-14){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- In recounting a command given to the Children of Israel, the Qur’an compares unjustly killing one person with killing all humanity and saving one life with saving all humanity.

   > "That is why We ordained for the Children of Israel that whoever takes a life—unless as a punishment for murder or mischief in the land—it will be as if they killed all of humanity; and whoever saves a life, it will be as if they saved all of humanity. ˹Although˺ Our messengers already came to them with clear proofs, many of them still transgressed afterwards through the land." (**Sūrat al-Māʾidah 5:32**), Dr. Mustafa Khattab, *The Clear Quran*[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}
   >

## Parents, spouses, women, children, and the home

### Parents, dignity, and fairness within the family

- The Qur’an places kindness to parents immediately after worship of God and forbids even a small expression of contempt toward them in old age.

   > "For your Lord has decreed that you worship none but Him. And honour your parents. If one or both of them reach old age in your care, never say to them ˹even˺ 'ugh,' nor yell at them. Rather, address them respectfully." (**Sūrat al-Isrāʾ 17:23**), Dr. Mustafa Khattab, *The Clear Quran*[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}
   >
   > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿وَقَضَىٰ رَبُّكَ أَلَّا تَعْبُدُوٓا۟ إِلَّآ إِيَّاهُ وَبِٱلْوَٰلِدَيْنِ إِحْسَـٰنًا ۚ إِمَّا يَبْلُغَنَّ عِندَكَ ٱلْكِبَرَ أَحَدُهُمَآ أَوْ كِلَاهُمَا فَلَا تَقُل لَّهُمَآ أُفٍّ وَلَا تَنْهَرْهُمَا وَقُل لَّهُمَا قَوْلًا كَرِيمًا ۝٢٣﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ الإِسۡرَاءِ</span></div>
   {: .quote }

- When asked who most deserved a person’s good companionship, Prophet Muhammad ﷺ answered “your mother” three times before saying “your father.”

   > "Your mother." He asked, "Then who?" He said, "Your mother." He asked, "Then who?" He said, "Your mother." Then, "Your father." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 5971[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- The Qur’an explicitly acknowledges the physical burden a mother bears in pregnancy, childbirth, and nursing.

   > "And We have enjoined upon man [care] for his parents. His mother carried him, [increasing her] in weakness upon weakness, and his weaning is in two years. Be grateful to Me and to your parents; to Me is the [final] destination." (**Sūrat Luqmān 31:14**), Saheeh International[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}
   >
   > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿وَوَصَّيْنَا ٱلْإِنسَـٰنَ بِوَٰلِدَيْهِ حَمَلَتْهُ أُمُّهُۥ وَهْنًا عَلَىٰ وَهْنٍ وَفِصَـٰلُهُۥ فِى عَامَيْنِ أَنِ ٱشْكُرْ لِى وَلِوَٰلِدَيْكَ إِلَىَّ ٱلْمَصِيرُ ۝١٤﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ لُقۡمَانَ</span></div>
   {: .quote }

- When Safiyya was insulted because of her Jewish ancestry, Prophet Muhammad ﷺ comforted her, affirmed her dignity through the prophetic lineage of her people, and rebuked the insult.

   > "You are the daughter of a prophet, your uncle is a prophet, and you are married to a prophet. So what can she boast over you about?" — *Jami at-Tirmidhi* 3894[5](/messenger/sources#ref-5){: .footnote}[16](/messenger/sources#ref-16){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- When a man sought permission for voluntary warfare, Prophet Muhammad ﷺ redirected him to caring for his living parents instead.

   > "Are your parents alive?" He said, "Yes." He replied, "Then strive in their service." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 5972[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- When women complained that their husbands had beaten them, Prophet Muhammad ﷺ said that those men were not the best Muslims. The report preserves a difficult legal sequence, but his moral judgment on the conduct is explicit.

   > "Many women have gone around Muhammad's family complaining of their husbands. Those men are not the best among you." — *Sunan Abi Dawud* 2146[4](/messenger/sources#ref-4){: .footnote}[15](/messenger/sources#ref-15){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}

### Consent, property, and legal standing in marriage

- Prophet Muhammad ﷺ taught that a woman’s consent must be sought before marriage; a previously married woman must be consulted and a virgin’s permission must also be obtained.[54](/messenger/sources#ref-54){: .footnote}[55](/messenger/sources#ref-55){: .footnote}

   > "A previously married woman is not to be married until consulted, and a virgin is not to be married until her permission is sought." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 5136[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- When Khansa bint Khidam told Prophet Muhammad ﷺ that her father had married her against her wishes, he invalidated the marriage.

   > "Her father married her while she disliked it, so the Messenger of God ﷺ annulled that marriage." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 5138[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- The Qur’an forbids treating women as property that can be inherited against their will.

   > "O you who have believed, it is not lawful for you to inherit women by compulsion. And do not make difficulties for them in order to take [back] part of what you gave them unless they commit a clear immorality. And live with them in kindness. For if you dislike them - perhaps you dislike a thing and Allah makes therein much good." (**Sūrat al-Nisāʾ 4:19**), Saheeh International[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}

### Marriage: affection, good treatment, rights, and separation

- The Qur’an describes marriage as a relationship of tranquility, affection, and mercy.

   > "And of His signs is that He created for you from yourselves mates that you may find tranquillity in them; and He placed between you affection and mercy. Indeed in that are signs for a people who give thought." Thus does Allah make clear His ordinances to the people that they may become righteous." ### Women as moral agents, learners, and members of the community

- The Qur’an repeatedly addresses believing women and men together as responsible moral agents.

   > "The believing men and believing women are allies of one another. They enjoin what is right and forbid what is wrong and establish prayer and give zakah and obey Allah and His Messenger. Those - Allah will have mercy upon them. Indeed, Allah is Exalted in Might and Wise." (**Sūrat al-Tawbah 9:71**), Saheeh International[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}

### Children, tenderness, and consideration

- Prophet Muhammad ﷺ openly kissed and showed affection to children and treated a man’s lack of tenderness toward them as a moral problem.

   > "Whoever does not show mercy will not be shown mercy." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 5997[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- Prophet Muhammad ﷺ shortened a public prayer when he heard a baby crying because he knew the child’s mother would be distressed.

   > "I begin the prayer intending to lengthen it, then I hear a child crying, so I shorten it because I know how his mother suffers from his crying." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 710[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- Prophet Muhammad ﷺ carried his granddaughter Umama while leading prayer, holding her while standing and putting her down for bowing and prostration.

   > "The Messenger of God ﷺ prayed while carrying Umama... when he stood he carried her, and when he prostrated he put her down." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 516[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

   Children could interrupt him even in his most public and authoritative settings. On another occasion, he stopped a sermon, came down from the pulpit, picked up his stumbling grandsons Hasan and Husayn, and then resumed with them.
