> This page focuses on the kind of inner and everyday life the message seeks to form: direct worship of God, repentance, steadiness, balanced devotion, self-control, truthful speech, forgiveness, privacy, reconciliation, and ordinary acts of goodness.

## God, worship, hope, repentance, and the inner life
{: #god-worship-hope-repentance-and-the-inner-life }

### God, direct worship, and servanthood

- Prophet Muhammad ﷺ called people to worship one God alone and did not ask them to worship him, his family, saints, tribes, natural forces, or intermediaries.

   > "Say, ‘He is Allah, [who is] One,’ Allah, the Eternal Refuge. He neither begets nor is born, nor is there to Him any equivalent." (**Sūrat al-Ikhlāṣ 112:1-4**), Saheeh International[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}
   >
   > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿قُلْ هُوَ ٱللَّهُ أَحَدٌ ۝١ ٱللَّهُ ٱلصَّمَدُ ۝٢ لَمْ يَلِدْ وَلَمْ يُولَدْ ۝٣ وَلَمْ يَكُن لَّهُۥ كُفُوًا أَحَدٌۢ ۝٤﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ الإِخۡلَاصِ</span></div>
   {: .quote }

- The Qur’an says that God is near and responds directly to the sincere prayer of the person who calls on Him.

   > "When My servants ask you, [O Muhammad], concerning Me - indeed I am near. I respond to the invocation of the supplicant when he calls upon Me. So let them respond to Me [by obedience] and believe in Me that they may be [rightly] guided." (**Sūrat al-Baqarah 2:186**), Saheeh International[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}
   >
   > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿وَإِذَا سَأَلَكَ عِبَادِى عَنِّى فَإِنِّى قَرِيبٌ ۖ أُجِيبُ دَعْوَةَ ٱلدَّاعِ إِذَا دَعَانِ ۖ فَلْيَسْتَجِيبُوا۟ لِى وَلْيُؤْمِنُوا۟ بِى لَعَلَّهُمْ يَرْشُدُونَ ۝١٨٦﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ البَقَرَةِ</span></div>
   {: .quote }

- In the Qur’an’s account of the Night Journey, Prophet Muhammad ﷺ is called God’s servant. The extraordinary event does not turn him into a divine figure, and authenticated reports connect the journey with the establishment of the five daily prayers.

   > "Glory be to the One Who took His servant ˹Muḥammad˺ by night from the Sacred Mosque to the Farthest Mosque whose surroundings We have blessed, so that We may show him some of Our signs. Indeed, He alone is the All-Hearing, All-Seeing." (**Sūrat al-Isrāʾ 17:1**), Dr. Mustafa Khattab, *The Clear Quran*[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}
   >
   > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿سُبْحَـٰنَ ٱلَّذِىٓ أَسْرَىٰ بِعَبْدِهِۦ لَيْلًا مِّنَ ٱلْمَسْجِدِ ٱلْحَرَامِ إِلَى ٱلْمَسْجِدِ ٱلْأَقْصَا ٱلَّذِى بَـٰرَكْنَا حَوْلَهُۥ لِنُرِيَهُۥ مِنْ ءَايَـٰتِنَآ ۚ إِنَّهُۥ هُوَ ٱلسَّمِيعُ ٱلْبَصِيرُ ۝١﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ الإِسۡرَاءِ</span></div>
   {: .quote }

   The Night Journey is also reported in the hadith collections *Sahih al-Bukhari* and *Sahih Muslim*.

- Prophet Muhammad ﷺ taught that a servant is especially near to God while prostrating and should increase supplication then.

   > "The closest a servant is to his Lord is while prostrating, so increase your supplication." — *Sahih Muslim* 482[3](/messenger/sources#ref-3){: .footnote}[14](/messenger/sources#ref-14){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- Prophet Muhammad ﷺ compared the five daily prayers to bathing repeatedly in a river, presenting them as a repeated return to God throughout the day.

   > "If a river were at the door of one of you and he bathed in it five times a day, would any dirt remain?" — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 528; *Sahih Muslim* 667[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[3](/messenger/sources#ref-3){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[14](/messenger/sources#ref-14){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- Prophet Muhammad ﷺ taught that fasting is not only abstaining from food and drink; lying and acting on falsehood undermine its ethical purpose.

   > "Whoever does not leave false speech and acting upon it, God has no need of his leaving food and drink." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 1903, 6057[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- The Qur’an describes Prophet Muhammad’s ﷺ mission as a mercy to the worlds, a claim that can be read alongside the concrete teachings on children, servants, debtors, captives, non-Muslims, defeated enemies, and animals presented here.

   > "And We have not sent you, [O Muhammad], except as a mercy to the worlds." (**Sūrat al-Anbiyāʾ 21:107**), Saheeh International[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}
   >
   > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿وَمَآ أَرْسَلْنَـٰكَ إِلَّا رَحْمَةً لِّلْعَـٰلَمِينَ ۝١٠٧﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ الأَنبِيَاءِ</span></div>
   {: .quote }

### Repentance and hope

- The Qur’an tells people who have seriously sinned not to despair of God’s mercy.

   > "Say, ‘O My servants who have transgressed against themselves [by sinning], do not despair of the mercy of Allah. Indeed, Allah forgives all sins. Indeed, it is He who is the Forgiving, the Merciful.’" (**Sūrat al-Zumar 39:53**), Saheeh International[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}
   >
   > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿۞ قُلْ يَـٰعِبَادِىَ ٱلَّذِينَ أَسْرَفُوا۟ عَلَىٰٓ أَنفُسِهِمْ لَا تَقْنَطُوا۟ مِن رَّحْمَةِ ٱللَّهِ ۚ إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ يَغْفِرُ ٱلذُّنُوبَ جَمِيعًا ۚ إِنَّهُۥ هُوَ ٱلْغَفُورُ ٱلرَّحِيمُ ۝٥٣﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ الزُّمَرِ</span></div>
   {: .quote }

- The Qur’an teaches that sincere repentance, faith, and reform can transform a person’s moral record.

   > "Except for those who repent, believe and do righteous work. For them Allah will replace their evil deeds with good. And ever is Allah Forgiving and Merciful." (**Sūrat al-Furqān 25:70**), Saheeh International[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}
   >
   > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿إِلَّا مَن تَابَ وَءَامَنَ وَعَمِلَ عَمَلًا صَـٰلِحًا فَأُو۟لَـٰٓئِكَ يُبَدِّلُ ٱللَّهُ سَيِّـَٔاتِهِمْ حَسَنَـٰتٍ ۗ وَكَانَ ٱللَّهُ غَفُورًا رَّحِيمًا ۝٧٠﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ الفُرۡقَانِ</span></div>
   {: .quote }

- Prophet Muhammad ﷺ compared God’s joy at a servant’s repentance to the relief of a traveler in a deadly desert who unexpectedly recovers the animal carrying his food and water, saying God’s joy is greater still.

   > "God is more joyful at the repentance of His servant than one of you who finds his lost camel in a barren desert." — *Sahih Muslim* 2747a[3](/messenger/sources#ref-3){: .footnote}[14](/messenger/sources#ref-14){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- In a story Prophet Muhammad ﷺ told, even a man who had murdered one hundred people was told that repentance remained open to him and that he should leave the corrupt environment that had shaped him.

   > "Who can stand between you and repentance? Go to such-and-such a land, for there are people there who worship God." — *Sahih Muslim* 2766a[3](/messenger/sources#ref-3){: .footnote}[14](/messenger/sources#ref-14){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

### Hardship, trust, and inner steadiness

- The Qur’an does not promise a life without hardship; it repeats that ease accompanies hardship.

   > "So, surely with hardship comes ease. Surely with ˹that˺ hardship comes ˹more˺ ease." (**Sūrat al-Sharḥ 94:5-6**), Dr. Mustafa Khattab, *The Clear Quran*[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}
   >
   > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿فَإِنَّ مَعَ ٱلْعُسْرِ يُسْرًا ۝٥ إِنَّ مَعَ ٱلْعُسْرِ يُسْرًا ۝٦﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ الشَّرۡحِ</span></div>
   {: .quote }

- The Qur’an states that God does not burden a soul beyond its capacity.

   > "Allah does not charge a soul except [with that within] its capacity. It will have [the consequence of] what [good] it has gained, and it will bear [the consequence of] what [evil] it has earned. ‘Our Lord, do not impose blame upon us if we have forgotten or erred. Our Lord, and lay not upon us a burden like that which You laid upon those before us. Our Lord, and burden us not with that which we have no ability to bear. And pardon us; and forgive us; and have mercy upon us. You are our protector, so give us victory over the disbelieving people.’" (**Sūrat al-Baqarah 2:286**), Saheeh International[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}
   >
   > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿لَا يُكَلِّفُ ٱللَّهُ نَفْسًا إِلَّا وُسْعَهَا ۚ لَهَا مَا كَسَبَتْ وَعَلَيْهَا مَا ٱكْتَسَبَتْ ۗ رَبَّنَا لَا تُؤَاخِذْنَآ إِن نَّسِينَآ أَوْ أَخْطَأْنَا ۚ رَبَّنَا وَلَا تَحْمِلْ عَلَيْنَآ إِصْرًا كَمَا حَمَلْتَهُۥ عَلَى ٱلَّذِينَ مِن قَبْلِنَا ۚ رَبَّنَا وَلَا تُحَمِّلْنَا مَا لَا طَاقَةَ لَنَا بِهِۦ ۖ وَٱعْفُ عَنَّا وَٱغْفِرْ لَنَا وَٱرْحَمْنَآ ۚ أَنتَ مَوْلَىٰنَا فَٱنصُرْنَا عَلَى ٱلْقَوْمِ ٱلْكَـٰفِرِينَ ۝٢٨٦﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ البَقَرَةِ</span></div>
   {: .quote }

- The Qur’an presents remembrance of God as a source of inward peace for the heart.

   > "Those who have believed and whose hearts are assured by the remembrance of Allah. Unquestionably, by the remembrance of Allah hearts are assured." (**Sūrat al-Raʿd 13:28**), Saheeh International[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}
   >
   > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ وَتَطْمَئِنُّ قُلُوبُهُم بِذِكْرِ ٱللَّهِ ۗ أَلَا بِذِكْرِ ٱللَّهِ تَطْمَئِنُّ ٱلْقُلُوبُ ۝٢٨﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ الرَّعۡدِ</span></div>
   {: .quote }

- The Qur’an tells believers that when they remember God, God remembers them.

   > "So remember Me; I will remember you. And be grateful to Me and do not deny Me." (**Sūrat al-Baqarah 2:152**), Saheeh International[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}
   >
   > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿فَٱذْكُرُونِىٓ أَذْكُرْكُمْ وَٱشْكُرُوا۟ لِى وَلَا تَكْفُرُونِ ۝١٥٢﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ البَقَرَةِ</span></div>
   {: .quote }

- Prophet Muhammad ﷺ told believers to pursue what benefits them, seek God’s help, and not give in to helplessness.

   > "Be eager for what benefits you, seek God’s help, and do not give up." — *Sahih Muslim* 2664[3](/messenger/sources#ref-3){: .footnote}[14](/messenger/sources#ref-14){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- Prophet Muhammad ﷺ told believers not to torment themselves with “if only” after a loss, but to accept what has happened as God’s decree.

   > "If something befalls you, do not say, ‘If only I had done such-and-such.’ Say: ‘God decreed, and He did what He willed.’" — *Sahih Muslim* 2664[3](/messenger/sources#ref-3){: .footnote}[14](/messenger/sources#ref-14){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- Prophet Muhammad ﷺ taught that fatigue, illness, grief, hurt, distress, and even the prick of a thorn can become occasions through which sins are erased.

   > "No fatigue, illness, sorrow, hurt, or distress afflicts a Muslim, even a thorn that pricks him, except that God removes sins through it." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 5641-5642[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- The Qur’an teaches gratitude in times of ease and warns against ingratitude.

   > "And [remember] when your Lord proclaimed, ‘If you are grateful, I will surely increase you [in favor]; but if you deny, indeed, My punishment is severe.’" (**Sūrat Ibrāhīm 14:7**), Saheeh International[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}
   >
   > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿وَإِذْ تَأَذَّنَ رَبُّكُمْ لَئِن شَكَرْتُمْ لَأَزِيدَنَّكُمْ ۖ وَلَئِن كَفَرْتُمْ إِنَّ عَذَابِى لَشَدِيدٌ ۝٧﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ إِبۡرَاهِيمَ</span></div>
   {: .quote }

### Intention, balance, humility, and accountability

- Prophet Muhammad ﷺ taught that the moral value of an action depends on the intention behind it.

   > "Actions are only by intentions, and each person will have only what he intended." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 1[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- Prophet Muhammad ﷺ warned against making religion so difficult through excessive devotion that a person becomes overwhelmed.

   > "Religion is ease. No one makes the religion excessively hard upon himself except that it overwhelms him. So aim rightly and come close." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 39[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- When three men proposed perpetual fasting, all-night prayer, and celibacy as higher forms of devotion, Prophet Muhammad ﷺ pointed instead to his own balanced practice of fasting and eating, praying and sleeping, and marrying.

   > "I fast and I break my fast; I pray and I sleep; and I marry women. Whoever turns away from my way is not of me." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 5063[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- Prophet Muhammad ﷺ approved the principle that a person owes rights to God, to his own self, and to his family, and should give each its due.

   > "Your Lord has a right over you, your self has a right over you, and your family has a right over you. Give each its right." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 1968[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- Prophet Muhammad ﷺ taught that the deeds most loved by God are those done consistently, even when they are small.

   > "The deeds most loved by God are those done most consistently, even if they are small." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 6465[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- Prophet Muhammad ﷺ said that he himself sought God’s forgiveness and repented repeatedly every day.

   > "By God, I seek God’s forgiveness and turn to Him in repentance more than seventy times in a day." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 6307[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- Prophet Muhammad ﷺ taught that the mercy visible throughout creation is only a small part of God’s mercy.

   > "God made mercy into one hundred parts. He sent down one part among creation and retained ninety-nine parts." — *Sahih Muslim* 2752a[3](/messenger/sources#ref-3){: .footnote}[14](/messenger/sources#ref-14){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- After seeing a mother desperately search for her child and then nurse him, Prophet Muhammad ﷺ said that God is even more merciful to His servants than that mother is to her child.

   > "God is more merciful to His servants than this woman is to her child." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 5999[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- Prophet Muhammad ﷺ taught that each person will stand directly before God without an interpreter between them.

   > "Each of you will meet his Lord with no interpreter between him and God." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 3595[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- Prophet Muhammad ﷺ told people not to wish for death because of suffering, but to leave the timing of life and death to God.

   > "Do not wish for death because of harm that has befallen you. Say: ‘O God, keep me alive while life is better for me.’" — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 5671[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- Prophet Muhammad ﷺ rejected belief in evil omens and said that he preferred a hopeful good word.

   > "There is no evil omen, and I like a good omen." They asked what that was. He said, "A good word." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 5756[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- When Aisha asked why Prophet Muhammad ﷺ stood in night prayer until his feet swelled, he said that he wanted to be a grateful servant.

   > "Should I not be a grateful servant?" — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 4836[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- Prophet Muhammad ﷺ taught that no one earns Paradise as a wage owed for deeds, including himself, but enters through God’s mercy.

   > "No one’s deeds will admit him to Paradise." They asked, "Not even you?" He said, "Not even me, unless God envelops me in mercy." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 5673[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- Prophet Muhammad ﷺ told of a woman known for sexual sin who was forgiven after giving water to a thirsty dog, joining mercy to animals with the possibility of repentance from a stigmatized past.

   > "She gave the dog water, so God thanked her for it and forgave her." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 3321[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

## Character, speech, anger, forgiveness, and everyday goodness
{: #character-speech-anger-forgiveness-and-everyday-goodness }

### Self-control, forgiveness, and gentleness

- The Qur’an describes Prophet Muhammad ﷺ as possessing great moral character.

   > "And indeed, you are of a great moral character." (**Sūrat al-Qalam 68:4**), Saheeh International[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}
   >
   > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿وَإِنَّكَ لَعَلَىٰ خُلُقٍ عَظِيمٍ ۝٤﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ القَلَمِ</span></div>
   {: .quote }

- Prophet Muhammad ﷺ defined real strength as controlling oneself when angry rather than overpowering other people physically.

   > "The strong person is not the one who overcomes people in wrestling. The strong person is the one who controls himself when angry." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 6114[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- The Qur’an praises people who control their anger and pardon others.

   > "˹They are˺ those who donate in prosperity and adversity, control their anger, and pardon others. And Allah loves the good-doers." (**Sūrat Āli ʿImrān 3:134**), Dr. Mustafa Khattab, *The Clear Quran*[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}
   >
   > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿ٱلَّذِينَ يُنفِقُونَ فِى ٱلسَّرَّآءِ وَٱلضَّرَّآءِ وَٱلْكَـٰظِمِينَ ٱلْغَيْظَ وَٱلْعَافِينَ عَنِ ٱلنَّاسِ ۗ وَٱللَّهُ يُحِبُّ ٱلْمُحْسِنِينَ ۝١٣٤﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ آلِ عِمۡرَانَ</span></div>
   {: .quote }

- Prophet Muhammad ﷺ taught that forgiving another person can increase one’s honor rather than diminish it.

   > "No one forgives another except that God increases him in honor, and no one humbles himself for God except that God raises him." — *Sahih Muslim* 2588[3](/messenger/sources#ref-3){: .footnote}[14](/messenger/sources#ref-14){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- The Qur’an permits proportionate redress for a wrong but presents pardon and reconciliation as better.

   > "And the retribution for an evil act is an evil one like it, but whoever pardons and makes reconciliation - his reward is [due] from Allah. Indeed, He does not like wrongdoers." (**Sūrat al-Shūrā 42:40**), Saheeh International[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}
   >
   > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿وَجَزَٰٓؤُا۟ سَيِّئَةٍ سَيِّئَةٌ مِّثْلُهَا ۖ فَمَنْ عَفَا وَأَصْلَحَ فَأَجْرُهُۥ عَلَى ٱللَّهِ ۚ إِنَّهُۥ لَا يُحِبُّ ٱلظَّـٰلِمِينَ ۝٤٠﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ الشُّورَىٰ</span></div>
   {: .quote }

- The Qur’an tells believers to respond to evil with what is better where possible, with reconciliation as the hoped-for result.

   > "Good and evil cannot be equal. Respond ˹to evil˺ with what is best, then the one you are in a feud with will be like a close friend." (**Sūrat Fuṣṣilat 41:34**), Dr. Mustafa Khattab, *The Clear Quran*[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}
   >
   > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿وَلَا تَسْتَوِى ٱلْحَسَنَةُ وَلَا ٱلسَّيِّئَةُ ۚ ٱدْفَعْ بِٱلَّتِى هِىَ أَحْسَنُ فَإِذَا ٱلَّذِى بَيْنَكَ وَبَيْنَهُۥ عَدَٰوَةٌ كَأَنَّهُۥ وَلِىٌّ حَمِيمٌ ۝٣٤﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ فُصِّلَتۡ</span></div>
   {: .quote }

- Prophet Muhammad ﷺ taught that gentleness beautifies what it enters and that harshness disfigures what it enters.

   > "Gentleness is not present in anything except that it beautifies it, and it is not removed from anything except that it disfigures it." — *Sahih Muslim* 2594a[3](/messenger/sources#ref-3){: .footnote}[14](/messenger/sources#ref-14){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- Prophet Muhammad ﷺ told teachers and leaders to make things easy, give people good news, and avoid driving them away.

   > "Make things easy; do not make them hard. Give good news; do not drive people away." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 69[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- After Mistah helped spread the slander against Aisha, Abu Bakr swore to stop supporting him financially. The Qur’an called Abu Bakr back to forgiveness, and he resumed helping him.

   > "And let not those of virtue among you and wealth swear not to give [aid] to their relatives and the needy and the emigrants for the cause of Allah, and let them pardon and overlook. Would you not like that Allah should forgive you? And Allah is Forgiving and Merciful." (**Sūrat al-Nūr 24:22**), Saheeh International[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}; the occasion is recorded in *Sahih al-Bukhari* 4750[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   >
   > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿وَلَا يَأْتَلِ أُو۟لُوا۟ ٱلْفَضْلِ مِنكُمْ وَٱلسَّعَةِ أَن يُؤْتُوٓا۟ أُو۟لِى ٱلْقُرْبَىٰ وَٱلْمَسَـٰكِينَ وَٱلْمُهَـٰجِرِينَ فِى سَبِيلِ ٱللَّهِ ۖ وَلْيَعْفُوا۟ وَلْيَصْفَحُوٓا۟ ۗ أَلَا تُحِبُّونَ أَن يَغْفِرَ ٱللَّهُ لَكُمْ ۗ وَٱللَّهُ غَفُورٌ رَّحِيمٌ ۝٢٢﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ النُّورِ</span></div>
   {: .quote }

- Prophet Muhammad ﷺ taught that part of being a good Muslim is making other people safe from harm caused by one’s tongue and hand.

   > "A Muslim is one from whose tongue and hand the Muslims are safe." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 10[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- When a man repeatedly asked Prophet Muhammad ﷺ for advice, he kept giving the same instruction: do not give yourself over to anger.

   > "Do not become angry." He repeated it several times: "Do not become angry." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 6116[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- Prophet Muhammad ﷺ defined backbiting as saying something true about a person behind his back that he would dislike; if the statement is false, it becomes slander as well.

   > "Mentioning about your brother what he dislikes." They asked, "What if it is true?" He replied, "If true, you backbit him; if false, you slandered him." — *Sahih Muslim* 2589[3](/messenger/sources#ref-3){: .footnote}[14](/messenger/sources#ref-14){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- The Qur’an tells believers to repair broken relationships rather than be content with the mere absence of open fighting.

   > "The believers are but brothers, so make settlement between your brothers. And fear Allah that you may receive mercy." (**Sūrat al-Ḥujurāt 49:10**), Saheeh International[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}
   >

   {: .quote }

- Prophet Muhammad ﷺ taught that maintaining family ties means continuing the relationship even when relatives cut it off.

   > "The one who truly maintains kinship is the one who, when his relatives cut him off, maintains relations with them." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 5991[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- Prophet Muhammad ﷺ taught that helping an oppressor means stopping that person from committing oppression.

   > "Help your brother whether he is an oppressor or oppressed." ... "You help the oppressor by preventing him from oppression." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 2444[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}

### Speech, dignity, and teaching without humiliation

- Prophet Muhammad ﷺ taught that a believer should speak what is good or remain silent.

   > "Whoever believes in God and the Last Day should speak good or remain silent." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 6018[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- The Qur’an forbids mocking people, insulting them, and using degrading nicknames.

   > "O believers! Do not let some ˹men˺ ridicule others, they may be better than them, nor let ˹some˺ women ridicule other women, they may be better than them. Do not defame one another, nor call each other by offensive nicknames. How evil it is to act rebelliously after having faith! And whoever does not repent, it is they who are the ˹true˺ wrongdoers." (**Sūrat al-Ḥujurāt 49:11**), Dr. Mustafa Khattab, *The Clear Quran*[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}
   >
   > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿يَـٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ لَا يَسْخَرْ قَوْمٌ مِّن قَوْمٍ عَسَىٰٓ أَن يَكُونُوا۟ خَيْرًا مِّنْهُمْ وَلَا نِسَآءٌ مِّن نِّسَآءٍ عَسَىٰٓ أَن يَكُنَّ خَيْرًا مِّنْهُنَّ ۖ وَلَا تَلْمِزُوٓا۟ أَنفُسَكُمْ وَلَا تَنَابَزُوا۟ بِٱلْأَلْقَـٰبِ ۖ بِئْسَ ٱلِٱسْمُ ٱلْفُسُوقُ بَعْدَ ٱلْإِيمَـٰنِ ۚ وَمَن لَّمْ يَتُبْ فَأُو۟لَـٰٓئِكَ هُمُ ٱلظَّـٰلِمُونَ ۝١١﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ الحُجُرَاتِ</span></div>
   {: .quote }

   > "O believers! Avoid many suspicions, ˹for˺ indeed, some suspicions are sinful. And do not spy, nor backbite one another. Would any of you like to eat the flesh of their dead brother? You would despise that! And fear Allah. Surely Allah is ˹the˺ Accepter of Repentance, Most Merciful." (**Sūrat al-Ḥujurāt 49:12**), Dr. Mustafa Khattab, *The Clear Quran*[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}

   {: .quote }

- The Qur’an tells believers to choose the best way of speaking, not merely words that are technically permissible.

   > "And tell My servants to say that which is best. Indeed, Satan induces [dissension] among them. Indeed Satan is ever, to mankind, a clear enemy." (**Sūrat al-Isrāʾ 17:53**), Saheeh International[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}

   > "And do not turn your cheek [in contempt] toward people and do not walk through the earth exultantly. Indeed, Allah does not like everyone self-deluded and boastful. And be moderate in your pace and lower your voice; indeed, the most disagreeable of sounds is the voice of donkeys."    > "And the servants of the Most Merciful are those who walk upon the earth easily, and when the ignorant address them [harshly], they say [words of] peace." - Prophet Muhammad ﷺ taught that habitual truthfulness leads toward righteousness and Paradise, while habitual lying leads toward wickedness and punishment.

   > "Truthfulness leads to righteousness, and righteousness leads to Paradise... Lying leads to wickedness, and wickedness leads to the Fire." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 6094[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- Prophet Muhammad ﷺ taught believers to want good for others as sincerely as they want it for themselves.

   > "None of you truly believes until he loves for his brother what he loves for himself." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 13[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- Prophet Muhammad ﷺ taught that simply despising another Muslim is itself a serious moral wrong.

   > "It is enough evil for a person to despise his Muslim brother." — *Sahih Muslim* 2564a[3](/messenger/sources#ref-3){: .footnote}[14](/messenger/sources#ref-14){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- Prophet Muhammad ﷺ defined arrogance as rejecting truth and looking down on other people, not as wearing good clothes.

   > "Arrogance is rejecting the truth and looking down on people." — *Sahih Muslim* 91a[3](/messenger/sources#ref-3){: .footnote}[14](/messenger/sources#ref-14){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- A man who accidentally spoke during prayer said Prophet Muhammad ﷺ neither struck, scolded, nor insulted him afterward, but simply explained the rule; he remembered him as the best teacher he had known.

   > "I never saw a teacher before or after him better in teaching than he was." — *Sahih Muslim* 537a[3](/messenger/sources#ref-3){: .footnote}[14](/messenger/sources#ref-14){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}

   {: .quote }

- When a Bedouin promised to fulfill the obligatory prayers, fasting, and charity without adding voluntary practices, Prophet Muhammad ﷺ said he would succeed if he was truthful.

   > "He will succeed if he is truthful." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 46[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

### Everyday goodness, privacy, and responsible information

- Prophet Muhammad ﷺ taught that even small acts—a good word, half a date, a smile, useful guidance, or helping someone with poor sight—can carry moral value.

   > "Protect yourself from the Fire even with half a date; if you cannot find that, then with a good word." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 1417[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

   > "Your smile in your brother’s face is charity... and guiding a person with poor sight is charity for you." — *Jami at-Tirmidhi* 1956[5](/messenger/sources#ref-5){: .footnote}[16](/messenger/sources#ref-16){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- Prophet Muhammad ﷺ counted reconciling people, helping someone with a mount, speaking a good word, walking to prayer, and removing harm from a road as forms of charity.

   > "Every good word is charity... and removing something harmful from the road is charity." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 2989[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- The Qur’an forbids entering another person’s home without first seeking permission and greeting its occupants.

   > "O believers! Do not enter any house other than your own until you have asked for permission and greeted its occupants. This is best for you, so perhaps you will be mindful." ### Trust, social peace, and protection from harm

- Prophet Muhammad ﷺ identified lying, breaking promises, and betraying trusts as signs of hypocrisy.

   > "The signs of a hypocrite are three: when he speaks he lies, when he promises he breaks it, and when entrusted he betrays." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 33[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- Prophet Muhammad ﷺ told believers not to hate, abandon, despise, or oppress one another.

   > "Do not hate one another or turn away from one another... A Muslim does not oppress, abandon, or despise his brother." — *Sahih Muslim* 2564a[3](/messenger/sources#ref-3){: .footnote}[14](/messenger/sources#ref-14){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- Prophet Muhammad ﷺ taught that a person’s life, property, and honor are inviolable and may not be attacked casually.

   > "All of a Muslim is inviolable to another Muslim: his blood, his wealth, and his honor." — *Sahih Muslim* 2564a[3](/messenger/sources#ref-3){: .footnote}[14](/messenger/sources#ref-14){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- The Qur’an requires sexual modesty from men as well as women, first telling believing men to lower their gaze and guard their chastity before addressing women.

   > "˹O Prophet!˺ Tell the believing men to lower their gaze and guard their chastity. That is purer for them. Surely Allah is All-Aware of what they do. And tell the believing women to lower their gaze and guard their chastity, and not to reveal their adornments except what normally appears. Let them draw their veils over their chests, and not reveal their ˹hidden˺ adornments except to their husbands, their fathers, their fathers-in-law, their sons, their stepsons, their brothers, their brothers’ sons or sisters’ sons, their fellow women, those ˹bondwomen˺ in their possession, male attendants with no desire, or children who are still unaware of women’s nakedness. Let them not stomp their feet, drawing attention to their hidden adornments. Turn to Allah in repentance all together, O believers, so that you may be successful." (**Sūrat al-Nūr 24:30-31**), Dr. Mustafa Khattab, *The Clear Quran*[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}
   >
   > <div dir="rtl" lang="ar" class="quran-arabic">﴿قُل لِّلْمُؤْمِنِينَ يَغُضُّوا۟ مِنْ أَبْصَـٰرِهِمْ وَيَحْفَظُوا۟ فُرُوجَهُمْ ۚ ذَٰلِكَ أَزْكَىٰ لَهُمْ ۗ إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ خَبِيرٌۢ بِمَا يَصْنَعُونَ ۝٣٠ وَقُل لِّلْمُؤْمِنَـٰتِ يَغْضُضْنَ مِنْ أَبْصَـٰرِهِنَّ وَيَحْفَظْنَ فُرُوجَهُنَّ وَلَا يُبْدِينَ زِينَتَهُنَّ إِلَّا مَا ظَهَرَ مِنْهَا ۖ وَلْيَضْرِبْنَ بِخُمُرِهِنَّ عَلَىٰ جُيُوبِهِنَّ ۖ وَلَا يُبْدِينَ زِينَتَهُنَّ إِلَّا لِبُعُولَتِهِنَّ أَوْ ءَابَآئِهِنَّ أَوْ ءَابَآءِ بُعُولَتِهِنَّ أَوْ أَبْنَآئِهِنَّ أَوْ أَبْنَآءِ بُعُولَتِهِنَّ أَوْ إِخْوَٰنِهِنَّ أَوْ بَنِىٓ إِخْوَٰنِهِنَّ أَوْ بَنِىٓ أَخَوَٰتِهِنَّ أَوْ نِسَآئِهِنَّ أَوْ مَا مَلَكَتْ أَيْمَـٰنُهُنَّ أَوِ ٱلتَّـٰبِعِينَ غَيْرِ أُو۟لِى ٱلْإِرْبَةِ مِنَ ٱلرِّجَالِ أَوِ ٱلطِّفْلِ ٱلَّذِينَ لَمْ يَظْهَرُوا۟ عَلَىٰ عَوْرَٰتِ ٱلنِّسَآءِ ۖ وَلَا يَضْرِبْنَ بِأَرْجُلِهِنَّ لِيُعْلَمَ مَا يُخْفِينَ مِن زِينَتِهِنَّ ۚ وَتُوبُوٓا۟ إِلَى ٱللَّهِ جَمِيعًا أَيُّهَ ٱلْمُؤْمِنُونَ لَعَلَّكُمْ تُفْلِحُونَ ۝٣١﴾ <span class="ayah-ref">سُورَةُ النُّورِ</span></div>

And those are the defiantly disobedient." - The Qur’an describes intoxicants and gambling as sources of hostility that also distract people from remembrance of God and prayer.

   > "O believers! Intoxicants, gambling, idols, and drawing lots for decisions are all evil of Satan’s handiwork. So shun them so you may be successful. Satan’s plan is to stir up hostility and hatred between you with intoxicants and gambling and to prevent you from remembering Allah and praying. Will you not then abstain?" - The Qur’an warns against excess, and Prophet Muhammad ﷺ also condemned wasting wealth.

   > "God dislikes for you gossip, needless questioning, and wasting wealth." — *Sahih Muslim* 1715a[3](/messenger/sources#ref-3){: .footnote}[14](/messenger/sources#ref-14){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- When Muadh led such a long communal prayer that a working man left, Prophet Muhammad ﷺ took the complaint seriously and rebuked him for making worship unnecessarily difficult.

   > "Are you putting people to trial, Muadh?" — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 705[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}

- Prophet Muhammad ﷺ told of a person whom God forgave for removing something harmful from a public road.

   > "A man saw a thorny branch in the road and removed it, so God appreciated his act and forgave him." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 652[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}

- Prophet Muhammad ﷺ linked salvation to self-control in both speech and sexual conduct.

   > "Whoever guarantees for me what is between his jaws and what is between his legs, I guarantee Paradise for him." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 6474[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}

   {: .quote }

- Prophet Muhammad ﷺ taught that when two people are estranged, the better one is the person who first reopens the relationship with a greeting.

   > "The better of the two is the one who begins with peace." — *Sahih al-Bukhari* 6077; *Sahih Muslim* 2560[2](/messenger/sources#ref-2){: .footnote}[3](/messenger/sources#ref-3){: .footnote}[13](/messenger/sources#ref-13){: .footnote}[14](/messenger/sources#ref-14){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}

### Character as the measure of religious life

- Prophet Muhammad ﷺ said that the people nearest to him in the next life would be those with the best character, not those with the greatest wealth or status.

   > "Nothing is heavier on the believer’s scale on the Day of Resurrection than good character." — *Jami at-Tirmidhi* 2002[5](/messenger/sources#ref-5){: .footnote}[16](/messenger/sources#ref-16){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

   > "The most beloved of you to me and nearest to me on the Day of Resurrection are those best in character." — *Jami at-Tirmidhi* 2018[5](/messenger/sources#ref-5){: .footnote}[16](/messenger/sources#ref-16){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- An early report summarizes Prophet Muhammad’s ﷺ mission as including the refinement of good character, a theme reflected in the many concrete moral teachings presented here.

   > "I was sent to complete good character." — *Al-Adab al-Mufrad* 273[7](/messenger/sources#ref-7){: .footnote}[18](/messenger/sources#ref-18){: .footnote}[43](/messenger/sources#ref-43){: .footnote}
   {: .quote }

- The Qur’an describes other people’s hardship as weighing heavily on Prophet Muhammad ﷺ and portrays him as deeply concerned for them.

   > "There has certainly come to you a Messenger from among yourselves. Grievous to him is what you suffer; [he is] concerned over you and to the believers is kind and merciful." (**Sūrat al-Tawbah 9:128**), Saheeh International[1](/messenger/sources#ref-1){: .footnote}[11](/messenger/sources#ref-11){: .footnote}[12](/messenger/sources#ref-12){: .footnote}[36](/messenger/sources#ref-36){: .footnote}
