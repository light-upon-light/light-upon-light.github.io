# Muhammad ﷺ: What His Life, Character, and Teachings Say About His Prophetic Claim

*A cumulative portrait of the Prophet of Islam from the Qur'an, hadith [transmitted reports], and sīra [Prophetic biography]*

Muhammad ﷺ is not presented in Islam merely as a teacher of doctrines. The Qur'an presents him as **a lived example** whose worship, family life, private habits, leadership, restraint, courage, mercy, and judgment can all be examined. His followers preserved how he prayed, governed, joked, grieved, ate, forgave, listened, fought, made peace, treated children, and behaved when no public performance was required.

For someone asking whether he was genuinely a prophet of God, that life is not a side issue. Moral excellence alone does not mathematically prove revelation. But character is relevant evidence about a prophetic claimant, especially when the same pattern appears **before the claim, during years of persecution, in private family life, and later when the claimant possesses political and military power**.

The case below is therefore cumulative. No one incident explains why his companions loved him so deeply or why Muslims have continued to love him. The force lies in the coherence of the whole: the man known for truthfulness before revelation did not become dishonest after it; the man who preached mercy when weak practiced restraint when powerful; the man who demanded justice denied special exemption to his own family; and the man who led a state still served in his home and died without accumulated personal wealth.

Questions about particular historical controversies deserve careful treatment and should not be evaded. But they should be considered alongside, rather than instead of, this wider record.

## A note on the sources

The **Qur'an** is the scripture Muhammad ﷺ presented as revelation from God. A **hadith [transmitted report]** records something he said, did, or approved. **Sīra [Prophetic biography]** is the historical literature about his life. The strongest evidence below comes first from the Qur'an and then from rigorously authenticated reports, especially *Sahih al-Bukhari* and *Sahih Muslim*. Reports from other hadith collections are identified, and biographical reports are not silently treated as authenticated hadith.

The English quotations are concise renderings for readability. References are supplied so that readers can inspect the fuller wording and context.

# 1. Before prophethood: the man his society already knew

## 1.1 His life did not begin as a project of worldly power

1. **He began life without the protections normally associated with worldly privilege.** His father died before he was born, his mother died while he was young, and he was then raised by his grandfather and uncle. Care for the vulnerable was therefore part of his own biography before it became part of his public teaching.

    > “Did He not find you an orphan and give you shelter?”
    >
    > Source: Qur'an 93:6

2. **He did not build his claim on inherited kingship.** He came from a respected clan, but no recent ancestor had been a king. Even Abu Sufyan, then an enemy, admitted this when the Byzantine emperor Heraclius questioned him. That weakens the theory that Muhammad was simply using religion to recover a family throne.

    > Heraclius asked, “Was any of his forefathers a king?” Abu Sufyan answered, “No.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 7](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:7)

3. **He worked instead of living as an idle spiritual aristocrat.** He tended sheep and later worked in trade. His later teaching would likewise honor lawful work and earning by one's own effort.

    > “God did not send any prophet except that he tended sheep.”
    >
    > Source: *Sahih al-Bukhari* 2262

4. **Before prophethood, he took part in a Meccan alliance formed to restore the rights of people who had been wronged.** He later remembered the pact positively. The report matters because concern for justice appears in his biography before he possessed religious or political authority.

    > “They pledged that they would stand with any wronged person in Mecca until his right was restored.”
    >
    > Source: Sīra report, Ibn Hisham, *al-Sirah al-Nabawiyyah* 1/133; [online excerpt](https://www.abuaminaelias.com/dailyhadithonline/2012/08/06/prophet-universal-justice/)

5. **His marriage to Khadijah was marked by lasting loyalty rather than opportunism.** She was his only wife throughout their long marriage, supported him at the beginning of revelation, endured the years of persecution with him, and remained so deeply loved that he continued honoring her friends after her death.

    > “The Messenger of God did not marry another woman until Khadijah died.”
    >
    > Source: *Sahih Muslim* 2436; see also [*Sahih al-Bukhari* 3818](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3818)

## 1.2 His reputation was established before the prophetic claim

6. **His wife's immediate response to the first revelation was to appeal to his already established moral record.** Khadijah did not reassure him with vague affection. She pointed to the man she had known for years: loyal to family, truthful, helpful to the weak, hospitable, and dependable when people were struck by hardship.

    > “God will never disgrace you. You maintain family ties, support the needy, honor guests, and help people struck by hardship.”
    >
    > Source: Khadijah, [Sahih al-Bukhari 3](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3)

7. **An enemy testified that he had never known Muhammad ﷺ to lie.** Before Abu Sufyan became Muslim, Heraclius asked whether Muhammad had ever been accused of lying before he announced prophethood. Abu Sufyan answered no. Hostile testimony matters precisely because it is not praise from a disciple.

    > “Had you ever accused him of telling lies before he said what he now says?” He replied, “No.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 7](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:7)

8. **The same hostile witness described a recognizable moral program rather than a cult of Muhammad's personality.** Abu Sufyan said Muhammad taught worship of God alone, prayer, truthfulness, chastity, and family responsibility.

    > “He tells us to worship God alone, pray, speak the truth, be chaste, and maintain the ties of kinship.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 7](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:7)

9. **The Qur'an itself appeals to his long, publicly observable life before revelation.** Muhammad ﷺ had lived among the people to whom he first preached for decades. His past was available for examination rather than hidden behind an unverifiable origin story.

    > “I lived among you for a lifetime before this. Will you not use reason?”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 10:16](https://quran.com/10/16)

10. **The Qur'an described his moral character as exceptional before political success could have manufactured that reputation.** The verse belongs to an early Meccan chapter, long before he governed Medina.

    > “You are truly of outstanding character.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 68:4](https://quran.com/68/4)

## 1.3 The beginning of revelation looks unlike a staged seizure of authority

11. **He sought solitude before he sought authority.** Before revelation he withdrew to the cave of Hira for worship and reflection. The public mission emerged from an experience that overwhelmed him, not from a ceremonial announcement of political ambition.

    > “He used to withdraw to the cave of Hira and worship there for a number of nights.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 3](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3)

12. **The first revealed command centered reading, creation, and teaching rather than domination.** The opening revelation directed the human being toward the Creator and immediately mentioned knowledge and the pen.

    > “Read in the name of your Lord who created... Read, and your Lord is most generous, who taught by the pen.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 96:1–5](https://quran.com/96/1-5)

13. **His first reaction was fear, not self-congratulation.** He returned trembling and asked to be covered. He did not emerge from the cave presenting himself as a master of the unseen; Khadijah had to reassure him and take him to someone knowledgeable about earlier revelation.

    > “He returned with his heart trembling and said, ‘Cover me, cover me.’”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 3](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3)

# 2. Why ordinary explanations for a false claimant fit the record poorly

## 2.1 He refused claims that could have magnified him

14. **His central message directed worship to God alone, not to himself, his family, his tribe, or an image.**

    > “Say: He is God, One; God, the One all depend upon. He neither begets nor is born, and none is comparable to Him.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 112:1–4](https://quran.com/112)

15. **He insisted that he was a human messenger, not a divine incarnation.** His greatness was located in faithful servanthood to God, not in sharing God's divinity.

    > “I am only a human being like you, to whom it is revealed that your God is one God.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 18:110](https://quran.com/18/110)

16. **He denied possessing God's treasures or independent knowledge of the unseen.** His authority was prophetic, not divine.

    > “I do not say that I possess God's treasures, nor that I know the unseen, nor that I am an angel.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 6:50](https://quran.com/6/50)

17. **He denied having autonomous power even over his own benefit or harm.** This is a remarkable limitation for a supposed charlatan to place on his own supernatural claims.

    > “I have no power to benefit or harm myself except as God wills. If I knew the unseen, I would have secured much good.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 7:188](https://quran.com/7/188)

18. **He explicitly forbade his followers from exaggerating his status.** A self-serving founder could have welcomed increasingly divine titles. Muhammad ﷺ told followers to call him God's servant and messenger.

    > “Do not exaggerate in praising me... I am only His servant. Say: God's servant and His messenger.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 3445](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3445)

19. **He told his own daughter that kinship with him could not save her before God.** His prophetic status did not become spiritual nepotism for his household.

    > “Fatimah, daughter of Muhammad, ask me for whatever you wish of my wealth, but I cannot save you from God.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 2753](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2753)

20. **The Qur'an states openly that he would die like other human beings.** His movement was not made to depend on an immortal founder.

    > “Muhammad is only a messenger. Messengers passed away before him. If he dies or is killed, will you turn back?”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 3:144](https://quran.com/3/144)

21. **The Qur'an places Muhammad himself under terrible judgment if he were to fabricate revelation.** The text does not grant its messenger immunity from the truth-claim he makes.

    > “Had he falsely attributed sayings to Us, We would have seized him... then cut his life-vein.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 69:44–46](https://quran.com/69/44-46)

## 2.2 He rejected flattering falsehoods and preserved correction

22. **When a solar eclipse coincided with the death of his son Ibrahim, he rejected the flattering superstition.** He could have accepted what looked like cosmic confirmation at a moment of grief. Instead he explicitly denied that the eclipse had occurred because of his son's death.

    > “The sun and moon do not eclipse because of anyone's death or birth.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 1043](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:1043)

23. **The Qur'an publicly corrected him for turning away from a blind seeker while speaking to influential Meccans.** A scripture designed merely to protect its claimant's image had no obvious need to preserve the rebuke.

    > “He frowned and turned away because the blind man came to him. Yet how could you know? Perhaps he might have grown in purity.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 80:1–3](https://quran.com/80/1-3)

24. **Another Qur'anic passage publicly questioned a decision he made.** Again, revelation does not function as uninterrupted self-praise.

    > “May God pardon you. Why did you grant them permission before those who were truthful became clear to you?”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 9:43](https://quran.com/9/43)

25. **The Qur'an also corrected him for making something lawful difficult for himself in an effort to please members of his household.** The broader point is not that every personal judgment of Muhammad was revelation, but that the scripture he recited preserved correction rather than erasing it.

    > “O Prophet, why do you prohibit what God has made lawful for you, seeking to please your wives?”
    >
    > Source: Qur'an 66:1

26. **He distinguished revelation from ordinary technical judgment.** After commenting on date-palm pollination and seeing the agricultural result, he did not spiritualize the mistake. He acknowledged the farmers' expertise.

    > “You are more knowledgeable about the affairs of your worldly life.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih Muslim 2363](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2363)

27. **As a judge, he acknowledged that persuasive speech could influence a human ruling.** A favorable judgment based on available evidence could not transform someone else's right into morally lawful property.

    > “I am only human. You bring disputes to me, and one of you may present his argument more skillfully than another.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 2680](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2680)

## 2.3 The Meccan mission brought suffering rather than easy advantage

28. **He refused religious compromise even when compromise could have relieved the pressure on him.** Peaceful coexistence did not require pretending that contradictory objects of worship were equally true.

    > “I do not worship what you worship... For you is your religion, and for me is mine.”
    >
    > Source: Qur'an 109:2, 6

29. **He continued preaching through ridicule, social pressure, and violence without authorizing private revenge.** The early Muslims were weak, and the Meccan revelations repeatedly taught patience and principled restraint.

    > “Be patient over what they say, and leave them with a dignified withdrawal.”
    >
    > Source: Qur'an 73:10

30. **He cared about the socially weak before they could offer him political advantage.** Many early followers were poor, enslaved, young, or unprotected. The Qur'an forbade him from dismissing sincere low-status believers in order to attract elites.

    > “Do not drive away those who call upon their Lord morning and evening, seeking His face.”
    >
    > Source: Qur'an 6:52

31. **He sent persecuted followers to a Christian ruler because that ruler was known for justice.** Before Muslims had a state, Muhammad ﷺ trusted that a just Christian king in Abyssinia would not allow people to be wronged. Justice could be recognized across a religious boundary.

    > “There is a king there under whom no one is wronged.”
    >
    > Source: Report of the first migration in the sīra of Ibn Ishaq, preserved by Ibn Hisham

32. **He endured a clan boycott without abandoning or diluting his message.** Social and economic isolation did not turn the movement into a campaign for personal revenge.

    > “Do people think they will be left to say, ‘We believe,’ without being tested?”
    >
    > Source: Qur'an 29:2

33. **The deaths of Khadijah and his uncle Abu Talib removed his closest emotional support and his principal tribal protection in the same period.** Yet the mission continued. Persistence after those losses fits poorly with an enterprise sustained merely by comfort or easy success.

    > “Surely with hardship comes ease; surely with hardship comes ease.”
    >
    > Source: Qur'an 94:5–6

34. **At Ta'if, one of the hardest episodes of his mission ended with hope for his attackers rather than a demand for vengeance.** After severe rejection and injury, he was offered the destruction of the town. He declined and hoped that future generations would worship God.

    > “No. I hope that God will bring from their descendants people who worship God alone.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 3231](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3231)

35. **The Night Journey placed servanthood and prayer, not wealth or territory, at the center of his spiritual life.** The Qur'an calls him God's servant in describing the journey, and the authenticated reports connect it with the establishment of the five daily prayers.

    > “Glory be to the One who took His servant by night from the Sacred Mosque to the Farthest Mosque.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 17:1](https://quran.com/17/1)

## 2.4 Greater power did not turn into personal luxury

36. **Even after he became the leader of Medina, his household sometimes passed long periods without cooked food.** Aisha described months in which dates and water were the staple, with milk occasionally supplied by neighbors.

    > “We would see three new moons in two months, and no fire would be lit in the houses of God's Messenger.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 6459](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6459)

37. **He bought food on credit and mortgaged his armor to a Jewish merchant, and he died while armor was still pledged for food.** Whatever political authority he possessed, this is difficult to reconcile with the picture of a founder using religion to accumulate personal luxury.

    > “The Prophet bought food on credit from a Jew and mortgaged his iron armor to him.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 2509](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2509); see also *Sahih al-Bukhari* 2916, 4467

38. **At his death, he did not leave a personal fortune.**

    > “The Messenger of God left neither dinar nor dirham, neither male slave nor female slave.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 4461](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:4461)

39. **His private worship remained demanding after success.** Aisha asked why he stood in prayer until his feet swelled despite God's forgiveness. His answer was gratitude, not anxiety about his public status.

    > “Should I not be a grateful servant?”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 4836](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:4836)

40. **Those around him remembered extraordinary generosity rather than hoarding.** Ibn Abbas described him as especially generous during Ramadan.

    > “The Messenger of God was the most generous of people... more generous in good than a freely blowing wind.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 3554](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3554); *Sahih Muslim* 2308

41. **He was personally courageous rather than protected at everyone else's expense.** When Medina was startled by a nighttime noise, Muhammad ﷺ had already ridden toward the danger alone and returned to reassure the people.

    > “We found the Prophet returning, having gone ahead of the people... He said, ‘Do not be afraid.’”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 2908](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2908)

42. **Greater power produced neither a self-divinizing ruler nor a hereditary dynast.** He died as a servant-messenger, without a palace, accumulated treasury, or dynasty passed to a son.

    > “Blessed is the One who sent down the Criterion upon His servant, so that he may be a warner to the worlds.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 25:1](https://quran.com/25/1)

# 3. From persecution to public leadership

43. **He planned the migration to Medina carefully while trusting God.** Routes, supplies, intelligence, concealment, and a skilled guide were arranged. Trust in God did not mean refusing practical preparation.

    > “Do not grieve; God is with us.”
    >
    > Source: Qur'an 9:40

44. **He accepted skilled help from a non-Muslim guide.** Professional trustworthiness and competence were recognized even across a religious boundary.

    > “The Prophet and Abu Bakr hired a skilled guide... who followed the religion of Quraysh.”
    >
    > Source: *Sahih al-Bukhari* 2264

45. **One of his first major communal projects in Medina was a mosque, and he helped build it with his own hands.** The space became a center of worship, teaching, consultation, shelter, and public life.

    > “The Prophet began carrying bricks with them.”
    >
    > Source: *Sahih al-Bukhari* 428

46. **He joined the migrants and the residents of Medina in bonds of mutual responsibility.** The Qur'an praised the local believers for preferring displaced people even when they themselves were in need.

    > “They give them preference over themselves, even though they are in need.”
    >
    > Source: Qur'an 59:9

47. **The early Medinan agreement established a plural civic order rather than erasing religious difference.** Distinct religious communities retained their religion while being bound by shared public obligations and mutual defense.

    > “The Jews have their religion and the Muslims have theirs... and between them is mutual help against whoever attacks the people of this document.”
    >
    > Source: Constitution of Medina, preserved in the sīra of Ibn Ishaq

48. **The earliest Qur'anic permission to fight was framed around persecution and expulsion.** War did not open Muhammad's mission. Armed defense came after believers had been wronged and driven from their homes.

    > “Permission to fight is given to those who are fought because they have been wronged... those driven from their homes only for saying, ‘Our Lord is God.’”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 22:39–40](https://quran.com/22/39-40)

49. **The same passage names monasteries, churches, synagogues, and mosques as places whose destruction unchecked aggression can cause.** The rationale for resisting aggression is not framed as protection for mosques alone.

    > “Had God not repelled some people by means of others, monasteries, churches, synagogues, and mosques where God's name is mentioned often would have been destroyed.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 22:40](https://quran.com/22/40)

50. **After the disaster at Uhud, he was told to pardon and continue consulting the same followers whose mistakes had contributed to the defeat.** Military failure did not become a pretext for authoritarian rule.

    > “Pardon them, ask forgiveness for them, and consult them in affairs.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 3:159](https://quran.com/3/159)

51. **At the trench, he worked beside his companions and endured their hunger and danger.** Leadership did not exempt him from physical labor.

    > “We saw the Prophet carrying earth until the dust covered his abdomen.”
    >
    > Source: *Sahih al-Bukhari* 4104

52. **At Hudaybiyyah, he accepted treaty wording that omitted his prophetic title and terms many followers initially found painful.** Securing peace mattered more than insisting that an opponent acknowledge an honorific.

    > “Write: ‘Muhammad son of Abdullah’... The Prophet accepted their condition.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 2731](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2731)

53. **He honored the Hudaybiyyah agreement even when doing so was emotionally painful.** Abu Jandal arrived in chains asking the Muslims for protection just as the agreement required his return. Muhammad ﷺ kept the covenant while assuring him that God would provide relief.

    > “Be patient and seek God's reward. God will make for you and those with you a relief and a way out.”
    >
    > Source: Hudaybiyyah report in *Sahih al-Bukhari* 2731–2732 and the sīra

54. **The Qur'an later called the apparently difficult truce a clear victory.** The agreement opened space for peaceful contact rather than treating military maximalism as the only form of success.

    > “Indeed, We have granted you a clear victory.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 48:1](https://quran.com/48/1)

55. **At the conquest of Mecca, he announced multiple routes to safety rather than treating the defeated city as a free target.** People could enter Abu Sufyan's house, lay down their weapons, or close their own doors.

    > “Whoever enters the house of Abu Sufyan is safe; whoever lays down arms is safe; whoever closes his door is safe.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih Muslim 1780a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:1780a)

56. **His response to victory was worship and accountability rather than self-glorification.** The Qur'an instructed him to praise God and seek forgiveness when victory came.

    > “When God's help and victory come... glorify your Lord with praise and seek His forgiveness.”
    >
    > Source: Qur'an 110:1–3

57. **His final public teaching emphasized the sanctity of life and property rather than celebrating conquest.**

    > “Your blood and your property are sacred and inviolable, like the sacredness of this day, in this month, in this town.”
    >
    > Source: *Sahih al-Bukhari* 1739, 1741; [Sahih Muslim 1218a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:1218a); see also *Sahih Muslim* 1679

# 4. His relationship with God and the spiritual vision he taught

## 4.1 God at the center, Muhammad as servant

58. **He understood the highest human rank as servanthood to God.** Even when describing the Night Journey, the Qur'an calls him God's servant. Submission to God frees the person from idols, ego, fear of people, and the need to dominate others.

    > “Glory be to the One who took His servant by night...”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 17:1](https://quran.com/17/1)

59. **The theology he preached is radically centered on one God.** God is not one member of a pantheon, and Muhammad is not part of God.

    > “Say: He is God, One; God, the One all depend upon.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 112:1–4](https://quran.com/112)

60. **God is presented as directly near to the person who calls upon Him.** No priestly barrier is required for an ordinary person's private prayer to reach God.

    > “When My servants ask you about Me, I am near. I answer the call of the caller when he calls upon Me.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 2:186](https://quran.com/2/186)

61. **Each person is morally responsible for his or her own choices.** No soul inherits another person's moral guilt.

    > “No bearer of burdens will bear the burden of another.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 6:164](https://quran.com/6/164)

62. **He presented his message as continuity with earlier revelation rather than the invention of a tribal Arabian god.**

    > “He has ordained for you the faith He enjoined upon Noah, and what We revealed to you, and what We enjoined upon Abraham, Moses, and Jesus.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 42:13](https://quran.com/42/13)

63. **Muslims are told to affirm Abraham, Moses, Jesus, and the earlier prophets rather than build their faith on contempt for them.**

    > “We believe in God, and what was revealed to us... and what was given to Moses and Jesus... We make no distinction between them.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 2:136](https://quran.com/2/136)

## 4.2 Worship that changes character

64. **His own worship was driven by gratitude.** He stood in night prayer until his feet swelled, yet described the motive as grateful servanthood.

    > “Should I not be a grateful servant?”
    >
    > Source: *Sahih al-Bukhari* 1130, [4836](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:4836)

65. **He placed intention at the center of moral life.** The same outward action can be sincere worship, vanity, exploitation, or courage depending on its purpose.

    > “Actions are according to intentions, and each person will have what he intended.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 1](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:1); *Sahih Muslim* 1907

66. **His prayer was marked by concentration, tenderness, and awe.** His own worship could be long, while his leadership of others remained considerate.

    > “The closest a servant is to his Lord is while prostrating, so increase your supplication.”
    >
    > Source: *Sahih Muslim* 482

67. **The five daily prayers make worship a repeated return to God rather than a weekly performance.** He compared their effect to bathing repeatedly in a river.

    > “If a river were at the door of one of you and he bathed in it five times a day, would any dirt remain?”
    >
    > Source: *Sahih al-Bukhari* 528; *Sahih Muslim* 667

68. **He connected fasting to character rather than hunger alone.** Abstaining from food while continuing to lie and harm people misses the moral purpose of fasting.

    > “Whoever does not leave false speech and acting upon it, God has no need of his leaving food and drink.”
    >
    > Source: *Sahih al-Bukhari* 1903, 6057

69. **He taught that truthfulness forms a person over time, while habitual lying deforms character.**

    > “Truthfulness leads to righteousness, and righteousness leads to Paradise... lying leads to wickedness.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 6094](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6094)

70. **He taught repentance without despair, even for people who have seriously wronged themselves.**

    > “My servants who have transgressed against themselves, do not despair of God's mercy. God forgives all sins.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 39:53](https://quran.com/39/53)

71. **He described God's joy at repentance through the relief of a traveler who unexpectedly recovers what he needs to survive.** Repentance was not presented as returning to a reluctant God.

    > “God is more joyful at the repentance of His servant than one who finds his lost camel in a barren land.”
    >
    > Source: *Sahih Muslim* 2747

72. **He described God's mercy through the most instinctively compassionate human relationship his listeners could see.**

    > “God is more merciful to His servants than this mother is to her child.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 5999](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5999)

73. **He himself repeatedly sought God's forgiveness.** Prophethood did not produce spiritual complacency.

    > “By God, I seek God's forgiveness and repent to Him more than seventy times a day.”
    >
    > Source: *Sahih al-Bukhari* 6307

74. **He refused to turn salvation into self-congratulation about one's deeds.** Even when asked about himself, he made God's mercy decisive.

    > “Not even I, unless God covers me with His mercy.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 6463](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6463)

75. **He combined trust in God with responsible action.** His migration, treaties, military preparation, appointments, and household decisions all involved planning. Reliance meant doing what was required and then entrusting outcomes to God.

    > “When you have decided, then place your trust in God.”
    >
    > Source: Qur'an 3:159

## 4.3 A demanding religion without self-destructive extremism

76. **He explicitly rejected religious excess expressed through permanent fasting, all-night prayer, and celibacy.** Spiritual seriousness was not supposed to destroy the body, family, or ordinary human life.

    > “I fast and I break my fast; I pray and I sleep; and I marry. Whoever turns away from my way is not of me.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 5063](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5063); *Sahih Muslim* 1401

77. **He affirmed a balance among worship, personal wellbeing, and family obligations.**

    > “Your Lord has a right over you, your self has a right over you, and your family has a right over you. Give each its right.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 1968](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:1968)

78. **He taught disciplined effort without perfectionism.** The believer is told to aim rightly, come as close as possible, and continue on a moderate course.

    > “Aim to do right, come as close as you can, and follow a moderate, regular course.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 6463](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6463)

79. **He warned against making religion so burdensome that a person cannot sustain it.**

    > “Religion is easy, and no one makes the religion excessively hard on himself except that it overwhelms him.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 39](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:39)

80. **He valued small, consistent good more than impressive bursts that collapse.**

    > “The deeds most loved by God are those done regularly, even if they are few.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 6465](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6465); *Sahih Muslim* 783

81. **The Qur'an explicitly states that God does not demand the impossible from a person.**

    > “God does not burden a soul beyond its capacity.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 2:286](https://quran.com/2/286)

## 4.4 Righteousness is larger than ritual symbolism

82. **The Qur'an refuses to reduce righteousness to religious symbolism.** It joins belief and worship to generosity, promise-keeping, and patience.

    > “Righteousness is not that you turn your faces east or west... but to believe... give wealth despite loving it... fulfill promises, and remain patient in hardship.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 2:177](https://quran.com/2/177)

83. **One of the Qur'an's broadest moral summaries joins justice, generosity, and family care while prohibiting indecency and aggression.**

    > “God commands justice, excellence, and giving to relatives, and forbids indecency, wrongdoing, and transgression.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 16:90](https://quran.com/16/90)

84. **The stated purpose of Muhammad's mission is mercy extending beyond his own tribe.**

    > “We have sent you only as a mercy to the worlds.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 21:107](https://quran.com/21/107)

# 5. Mercy and character in ordinary life

## 5.1 The private witnesses matter

85. **Aisha summarized his character as the Qur'an lived out.** The mercy, discipline, patience, justice, and worship of his message were not, in her testimony, a public persona detached from the man at home.

    > “The character of the Messenger of God was the Qur'an.”
    >
    > Source: Aisha, [Sahih Muslim 746a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:746a)

86. **Anas served him for ten years and remembered extraordinary restraint rather than habitual irritation.** Ordinary service is precisely where contempt and impatience are difficult to hide.

    > “I served him for ten years. He never even said ‘uff’ to me, nor ‘Why did you do that?’”
    >
    > Source: Anas ibn Malik, [Sahih al-Bukhari 6038](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6038); *Sahih Muslim* 2309

87. **Aisha said he never struck a woman or a servant.** This evidence concerns the household, beyond sermons and public leadership.

    > “The Messenger of God never struck anyone with his hand, neither a woman nor a servant.”
    >
    > Source: Aisha, [Sahih Muslim 2328a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2328a)

88. **The combination of these witnesses matters more than any one anecdote.** A wife and a servant who knew him at close range describe the same gentleness his public teaching commended. The usual split between public virtue and private contempt is notably difficult to find in these reports.

    > “I served him for ten years. He never even said ‘uff’ to me.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 6038](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6038); see also Aisha's testimony in [Sahih Muslim 2328a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2328a)

## 5.2 Patience, gentleness, and restraint

89. **When given a choice between two permissible courses, he generally chose the easier one.** His religion was serious without treating needless difficulty as holiness.

    > “Whenever the Prophet was given a choice between two matters, he chose the easier, so long as it was not sinful.”
    >
    > Source: Aisha, [Sahih al-Bukhari 3560](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3560)

90. **He did not use personal injury as a reason for personal revenge.** Aisha distinguished offenses against Muhammad himself from matters involving public rights and justice.

    > “He never took revenge for himself.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 3560](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3560); *Sahih Muslim* 2328

91. **When a desert Arab yanked his cloak so violently that it marked his shoulder and then demanded money, Muhammad ﷺ smiled and gave him something.** The provocation was physical as well as rude.

    > “The Prophet turned toward him, smiled, and ordered that he be given a gift.”
    >
    > Source: *Sahih al-Bukhari* 3149, [5809](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5809)

92. **When an uninstructed desert Arab urinated in the mosque, he stopped the crowd from attacking him and dealt with the problem calmly.** He corrected ignorance without turning correction into humiliation.

    > “Leave him, and pour a bucket of water over it. You were sent to make things easy, not to make them difficult.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 6128](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6128)

93. **He made ease and encouragement an explicit principle for teachers and leaders.**

    > “Make things easy, not difficult. Give good news, and do not drive people away.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 69](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:69); *Sahih Muslim* 1734

94. **Even when Jewish visitors addressed him with hostile wordplay, he restrained Aisha from answering harshly.** Gentleness was not reserved for friendly audiences.

    > “Be calm, Aisha. God loves gentleness in every matter.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 6030](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6030)

95. **He taught that gentleness improves whatever it enters.** It was a governing principle in teaching, family life, correction, leadership, and invitation to faith.

    > “Gentleness is not found in anything except that it beautifies it, and it is not removed except that it disfigures it.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih Muslim 2594a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2594a)

96. **When asked to curse polytheists, he described his mission in terms of mercy instead.**

    > “I was not sent as one who curses. I was sent only as mercy.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih Muslim 2599](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2599)

97. **When the tribe of Daws resisted his message, he prayed for their guidance rather than their destruction.**

    > “O God, guide Daws and bring them.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 4392](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:4392)

98. **He spared a man who had stood over him with an unsheathed sword.** Once the immediate danger passed, the canonical report says Muhammad ﷺ did not punish him.

    > “The Prophet did not punish him.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 2910](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2910)

99. **He did not make ordinary preferences into occasions for complaint.** His household remembered that he never criticized food. If he wanted it, he ate; if not, he left it.

    > “The Prophet never criticized food. If he liked it he ate it; if he disliked it he left it.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 5409](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5409); *Sahih Muslim* 2064

100. **He redefined strength as mastery of the self rather than domination of others.** In a culture that admired physical prowess, he made control during anger the more meaningful victory.

    > “The strong person is not the one who defeats others in wrestling. The strong person controls himself when angry.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 6114](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6114); *Sahih Muslim* 2609

101. **When a man repeatedly asked for concise moral advice, Muhammad ﷺ kept returning to anger.**

    > “Do not become angry.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 6116](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6116)

102. **He made good character itself a measure of excellence and rejected vulgarity as moral greatness.**

    > “The best among you are those with the best character.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 6029](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6029)

## 5.3 Humility, generosity, courage, humor, and grief

103. **He sat among his companions so simply that a stranger could enter and ask which man was Muhammad.** His authority did not require a throne or conspicuous special seat.

    > “Which of you is Muhammad?”
    >
    > Source: *Sahih al-Bukhari* 63

104. **He disliked ceremonial self-exaltation.** Companions did not routinely stand on seeing him because they knew he disliked that form of reverence.

    > “They would not stand when they saw him because they knew that he disliked that.”
    >
    > Source: *Jami al-Tirmidhi* 2754, graded *hasan* [sound]

105. **His generosity could transform hostility into affection.** Safwan ibn Umayyah said Muhammad ﷺ continued giving him until the man he had hated most became the person he loved most.

    > “He kept giving me until he became the most beloved of people to me.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih Muslim 2313](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2313)

106. **At Hunayn, his courage was clearest when part of his army scattered.** He stayed in place and identified himself openly rather than disappearing into the retreat.

    > “I am the Prophet, this is no lie. I am the son of Abd al-Muttalib.”
    >
    > Source: *Sahih al-Bukhari* 2864, 4315; *Sahih Muslim* 1776

107. **He shared physical danger with his followers.** He dug at the trench, was wounded at Uhud, and experienced hunger and fatigue on campaign. He did not ask others to bear risks from which he was institutionally insulated.

    > “On that day, the Prophet's face was wounded and one of his teeth was broken.”
    >
    > Source: *Sahih al-Bukhari* 2911; *Sahih Muslim* 1790

108. **He was deeply modest.** Companions said that when he disliked something, they often knew it from his face rather than from humiliating speech.

    > “The Prophet was more modest than a virgin in her private room.”
    >
    > Source: *Sahih al-Bukhari* 3562, 6102; *Sahih Muslim* 2320

109. **He smiled frequently without becoming frivolous.** Jarir ibn Abdullah remembered being consistently admitted and met with a smile after becoming Muslim.

    > “He never saw me after I accepted Islam except that he smiled at me.”
    >
    > Source: *Sahih al-Bukhari* 3035; *Sahih Muslim* 2475

110. **His humor remained truthful.** He joked with companions and children without making falsehood the price of being entertaining.

    > “I do not say anything except the truth.”
    >
    > Source: *Jami al-Tirmidhi* 1990, graded *hasan* [sound]

111. **He wept when his infant son Ibrahim died and presented grief as compatible with faith.** Emotional honesty was not treated as rebellion against God.

    > “The eyes shed tears and the heart grieves, but we say only what pleases our Lord.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 1303](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:1303)

112. **When a dying child was placed before him, his tears were described as mercy rather than weakness.**

    > “This is mercy God has placed in the hearts of His servants, and God shows mercy to the merciful.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 7377](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:7377)

113. **He remembered goodness for years.** Long after Khadijah's death, he spoke of her warmly and sent gifts to her friends. Loyalty did not expire when the relationship could no longer benefit him.

    > “Whenever he slaughtered a sheep, he would send a good share to Khadijah's friends.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 3818](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3818); *Sahih Muslim* 2435

# 6. What he was like with family, women, and children

## 6.1 Marriage and family life

114. **He made treatment of one's family a test of moral quality.** Public religiosity could not compensate for cruelty at home, where a false public persona is hardest to sustain.

    > “The best of you is the best to his family, and I am the best of you to my family.”
    >
    > Source: [Jami at-Tirmidhi 3895](https://sunnah.com/tirmidhi:3895); *Sunan Ibn Majah* 1977

115. **He did household work himself.** Leadership of a community did not turn domestic service into work beneath him.

    > “He used to work for his family; when prayer time came, he went out to pray.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 5363](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5363)

116. **He taught that spending responsibly on one's household can itself be charity.** Family duty was not spiritually inferior to public generosity.

    > “When a Muslim spends on his family seeking God's reward, it is charity for him.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 5351](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5351)

117. **He expressed marital affection openly.** Aisha remembered him deliberately drinking from the same place on a cup where she had drunk. Tenderness was not treated as incompatible with masculinity or religious seriousness.

    > “I would drink while menstruating, then hand it to the Prophet, and he would place his mouth where mine had been.”
    >
    > Source: *Sahih Muslim* 300

118. **He made room for play in marriage.** Aisha described racing with him on journeys and his later joke when the result was reversed.

    > “He said, ‘This makes up for that earlier race.’”
    >
    > Source: *Sunan Abi Dawud* 2578

119. **The Qur'an commands husbands to live with wives honorably even when feelings are difficult.**

    > “Live with them in kindness. If you dislike something in them, perhaps you dislike something in which God has placed much good.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 4:19](https://quran.com/4/19)

120. **The Qur'an describes marriage through tranquility, affection, and mercy rather than ownership alone.**

    > “He created spouses for you from among yourselves so that you may find tranquility in them, and He placed affection and mercy between you.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 30:21](https://quran.com/30/21)

## 6.2 Women were moral agents with voices and rights

121. **He gave mothers an extraordinary claim to good companionship.** When asked who most deserved a man's good treatment, he named the mother three times before the father.

    > “Your mother.” “Then who?” “Your mother.” “Then who?” “Your mother.” “Then your father.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 5971](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5971); *Sahih Muslim* 2548

122. **He invalidated a marriage that a woman said her father had imposed on her.** This is a concrete application of consent rather than an abstract slogan.

    > “Her father married her while she disliked it, so the Messenger of God annulled the marriage.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 5138](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5138)

123. **He explicitly taught that a woman's permission matters in marriage.**

    > “A previously married woman should not be married until consulted, and a virgin until her permission is sought.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 5136](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5136); see also *Sahih Muslim* 1421

124. **He could make a personal request to a woman and accept her refusal.** When he encouraged Barirah to return to her former husband, she asked whether he was commanding her. He said he was only interceding. She declined, and the matter ended there.

    > “Are you ordering me?” He said, “No, I am only interceding.” She said, “I have no need of him.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 5283](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5283)

125. **He created dedicated teaching time for women when they said men were taking most of his availability.** They questioned him directly and were not told serious religious knowledge belonged only to men.

    > “The women said, ‘The men have taken all your time, so appoint a day for us.’ He appointed a day and taught them.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 101](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:101)

126. **He listened to his wife's political judgment at a moment of crisis.** At Hudaybiyyah, Umm Salama advised him to perform the ritual sacrifice and shaving himself rather than continue debating with distressed companions. He followed her advice, and the crisis ended.

    > “Go out, speak to no one, sacrifice your animal, and shave your head.”
    >
    > Source: Report of Umm Salama's counsel in *Sahih al-Bukhari* 2731–2732

127. **He honored his daughter Fatimah visibly.** When she entered, he stood, welcomed her, kissed her, and seated her in his place.

    > “When she entered upon him, he stood, took her hand, kissed her, and seated her in his place.”
    >
    > Source: *Sunan Abi Dawud* 5217; *Jami al-Tirmidhi* 3872

128. **During the Farewell Pilgrimage, he publicly reminded men that women had claims on them.**

    > “Be mindful of God concerning women... Their right over you is provision and clothing in a fitting manner.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih Muslim 1218a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:1218a)

129. **The Qur'an places men and women side by side in its catalogue of spiritual virtues and reward.**

    > “The Muslim men and Muslim women, believing men and believing women... truthful men and truthful women... God has prepared for them forgiveness and a great reward.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 33:35](https://quran.com/33/35)

130. **The same principle is stated in terms of deeds: moral worth before God is not made dependent on being male.**

    > “Whoever does righteous deeds, whether male or female, while believing, We will give them a good life.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 16:97](https://quran.com/16/97)

131. **Believing men and women are described as moral allies of one another.**

    > “The believing men and believing women are allies of one another. They encourage what is right and restrain what is wrong.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 9:71](https://quran.com/9/71)

## 6.3 Children and emotional tenderness

132. **He openly kissed and embraced children.** When a man boasted that he had ten children and had never kissed any of them, Muhammad ﷺ treated that as a lack of mercy, not toughness.

    > “Whoever does not show mercy will not be shown mercy.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 5997](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5997); *Sahih Muslim* 2318

133. **He carried his young granddaughter while leading prayer.** He put her down when he prostrated and picked her up when he stood. A child's presence was not treated as contamination of sacred space.

    > “He prayed while carrying Umamah... when he bowed he put her down, and when he stood he carried her.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 516](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:516); *Sahih Muslim* 543

134. **He shortened congregational prayer when he heard a baby cry because he knew the mother would be distressed.** The parent's anguish altered his own preferred length of worship.

    > “I hear a child crying, so I shorten the prayer because I know how deeply his crying troubles his mother.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 707](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:707)

135. **He greeted children instead of treating them as beneath notice.**

    > “Anas passed by a group of boys and greeted them, then said: ‘The Prophet used to do so.’”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 6247](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6247)

136. **He entered a child's world.** He noticed a small boy's grief over a pet bird and addressed him with playful affection. The companions considered even this small act worth preserving.

    > “O Abu Umayr, what happened to the little bird?”
    >
    > Source: *Sahih al-Bukhari* 6129; *Sahih Muslim* 2150

# 7. Human dignity and care for the vulnerable

## 7.1 Orphans, widows, the poor, debtors, and people easily overlooked

137. **He placed the person who cares for an orphan beside himself in Paradise.** Ordinary, long-term caregiving was given exceptional spiritual weight.

    > “I and the person who cares for an orphan will be in Paradise like this,” and he held two fingers close together.
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 6005](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6005)

138. **He compared sustained care for a widow or poor person to some of the most demanding forms of worship.**

    > “The one who works for a widow or a poor person is like one who strives in God's cause, or one who prays at night and fasts by day.”
    >
    > Source: [*Sahih al-Bukhari* 5353](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5353), 6006; *Sahih Muslim* 2982

139. **The Qur'an treats neglect of an orphan as a symptom of religious falseness.** Piety cannot be reduced to ritual while vulnerable people are pushed aside.

    > “Have you seen the one who denies the Judgment? That is the one who pushes away the orphan and does not urge feeding the poor.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 107:1–3](https://quran.com/107/1-3)

140. **Misappropriating an orphan's property is condemned in unusually severe language.**

    > “Those who consume the property of orphans unjustly are only consuming fire into their bellies.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 4:10](https://quran.com/4/10)

141. **Mandatory charity was directed to identifiable social needs, not merely to religious institutions.** The poor, needy, indebted, travelers, and people seeking freedom are explicitly named among beneficiaries.

    > “Charities are for the poor, the needy... those in bondage, those in debt... and the stranded traveler.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 9:60](https://quran.com/9/60)

142. **The Qur'an calls for costly generosity, not only giving away what one no longer values.**

    > “Righteousness is... to give wealth, despite loving it, to relatives, orphans, the needy, the traveler, those who ask, and for freeing people.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 2:177](https://quran.com/2/177)

143. **A debtor in genuine hardship is to be given time rather than crushed, and remission of the debt is praised as better still.**

    > “If someone is in hardship, then grant respite until ease; and if you remit it as charity, that is better for you.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 2:280](https://quran.com/2/280)

144. **Muhammad ﷺ praised a creditor who routinely made life easier for struggling debtors.**

    > “He would say to his servant: ‘If you come to someone in difficulty, overlook it.’ So God overlooked his faults.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 2078](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2078); *Sahih Muslim* 1562

145. **He made ordinary practical help part of everyday charity.** Social goodness was not restricted to donating money.

    > “Helping a person onto his mount is charity; a good word is charity; and removing harm from the road is charity.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih Muslim 1009](https://sunnah.com/muslim:1009)

146. **He taught that even small acts of social warmth matter.**

    > “Your smile to your brother is charity.”
    >
    > Source: [Jami at-Tirmidhi 1956](https://sunnah.com/tirmidhi:1956)

147. **He made himself available to people without status.** A woman whose mental condition could easily have led others to dismiss her could ask him to walk aside and listen until her need was addressed.

    > “Choose whichever road you wish, and I will go with you until I meet your need.”
    >
    > Source: *Sahih Muslim* 2326; see also *Sahih al-Bukhari* 6072

148. **He noticed the disappearance of a Black person whose work others had treated as insignificant.** When told the mosque cleaner had died and people had not thought the death important enough to report, he asked to be shown the grave and prayed there.

    > “Why did you not inform me? Show me the grave.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 458](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:458)

## 7.2 Enslaved people and sustained pressure toward emancipation

Slavery was an existing institution throughout the societies Muhammad ﷺ lived among. It would be inaccurate to claim he abolished it in a single decree. The sources instead show repeated moral pressure toward **human kinship, humane treatment, emancipation, routes to freedom, and consequences for abuse**.

149. **He confronted racialized abuse directly.** When Abu Dharr insulted a man through his mother's ancestry, Muhammad ﷺ told Abu Dharr that pre-Islamic ignorance still remained in him.

    > “You insulted him because of his mother? You are a man in whom there is still ignorance.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 30](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:30); *Sahih Muslim* 1661

150. **He called enslaved and dependent workers ‘your brothers’ and required shared basic standards of food and clothing.**

    > “Those under your authority are your brothers. Feed them from what you eat and clothe them from what you wear.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 30](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:30)

151. **He forbade overburdening people under one's authority and required help if difficult work was imposed.**

    > “Do not burden them with what overwhelms them; if you burden them, then help them.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 30](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:30)

152. **He made emancipation the expiation for physical abuse in a striking report.**

    > “Whoever slaps or beats an enslaved person under his authority, the expiation is to set that person free.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih Muslim 1657a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:1657a)

153. **The Qur'an places freeing a person from bondage on the ‘steep path’ of moral excellence.**

    > “What will make you understand the steep path? It is freeing a person from bondage, or feeding on a day of hunger.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 90:12–14](https://quran.com/90/12-14)

154. **The Qur'an directs owners to facilitate freedom contracts for enslaved people who seek them and to assist them financially.**

    > “If those under your authority seek a contract for freedom, make such a contract if you know good in them, and give them from God's wealth.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 24:33](https://quran.com/24/33)

155. **The same verse forbids forcing enslaved women into sexual exploitation for profit.**

    > “Do not compel your slave women into prostitution, if they desire chastity, seeking the temporary gains of worldly life.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 24:33](https://quran.com/24/33)

156. **Selling a free person into slavery is placed among wrongs for which God Himself will be the opponent.** The same report condemns taking a worker's full labor and withholding the wage.

    > “I will be the opponent of... one who sells a free person and consumes the price, and one who hires a worker, takes his full labor, then withholds his wage.”
    >
    > Source: Sacred hadith, [Sahih al-Bukhari 2270](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2270)

## 7.3 Equality, social dignity, and the rights of neighbors

157. **The Qur'an rejects race and tribe as measures of human worth.** Human diversity exists for recognition and relationship; moral standing before God turns on character and God-consciousness.

    > “We made you peoples and tribes so that you may know one another. The most honored of you before God is the most God-conscious.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 49:13](https://quran.com/49/13)

158. **Human beings are given a basic dignity as children of Adam.**

    > “We have certainly honored the children of Adam.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 17:70](https://quran.com/17/70)

159. **Mockery, humiliating nicknames, and social contempt are prohibited.**

    > “Let no people ridicule another people, for they may be better than them... and do not insult one another or call one another by offensive names.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 49:11](https://quran.com/49/11)

160. **Suspicion, spying, and backbiting are treated as moral violations rather than harmless social habits.**

    > “Avoid much suspicion... do not spy, nor backbite one another.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 49:12](https://quran.com/49/12); see also *Sahih al-Bukhari* 6064 and *Sahih Muslim* 2563

161. **A neighbor's safety is made a matter of faith.**

    > “By God, he does not believe... the one whose neighbor is not safe from his harm.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 6016](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6016)

162. **Muhammad ﷺ said Gabriel emphasized the neighbor's rights so persistently that he wondered whether neighbors would even receive inheritance rights.**

    > “Gabriel continued to urge me concerning the neighbor until I thought he would make the neighbor an heir.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 6014](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6014)

163. **Faith is linked to hospitality and disciplined speech.**

    > “Whoever believes in God and the Last Day should not harm his neighbor, should honor his guest, and should speak good or remain silent.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 6018](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6018); *Sahih Muslim* 47

164. **He connected religious identity with being safe from a person's tongue and hand.** Ritual belonging without control of harm was morally incomplete.

    > “The Muslim is the one from whose tongue and hand the Muslims are safe.”
    >
    > Source: *Sahih al-Bukhari* 10; *Sahih Muslim* 40

165. **He made control of speech and sexual conduct a path to salvation.**

    > “Whoever guarantees for me what is between his jaws and what is between his legs, I guarantee Paradise for him.”
    >
    > Source: *Sahih al-Bukhari* 6474

## 7.4 Religious difference did not erase ordinary human obligations

166. **The life of a non-Muslim living under Muslim covenant was treated as inviolable.** Muhammad ﷺ attached an extraordinary warning to murdering such a person.

    > “Whoever kills a person under covenant will not smell the fragrance of Paradise.”
    >
    > Source: *Sahih al-Bukhari* [3166](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3166), [6914](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6914)

167. **The Qur'an explicitly permits kindness and justice toward people of other religions who are not fighting Muslims.**

    > “God does not forbid you from being kind and just toward those who did not fight you because of religion or drive you from your homes.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 60:8](https://quran.com/60/8)

168. **Religious belief is not to be produced through coercion.**

    > “There is no compulsion in religion. Truth has become distinct from error.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 2:256](https://quran.com/2/256)

169. **Even an enemy polytheist requesting temporary protection is to receive asylum and safe escort.** The instruction is notable precisely because it appears amid passages dealing with conflict.

    > “If any of the polytheists asks you for protection, grant it so he may hear God's word; then escort him to a place of safety.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 9:6](https://quran.com/9/6)

170. **He stood for the funeral procession of a Jewish man and answered an objection by invoking shared humanity.**

    > “Was he not a human soul?”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 1312](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:1312); *Sahih Muslim* 961

171. **He visited a sick Jewish boy in person.** Religious disagreement did not cancel ordinary human concern.

    > “A Jewish boy who used to serve the Prophet became ill, so the Prophet went to visit him.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 1356](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:1356)

172. **His ordinary economic dealings in Medina crossed religious lines.** The same report that demonstrates his material simplicity also shows him buying food on credit from a Jewish merchant and leaving armor as security.

    > “He purchased food on credit from a Jew and left his armor as security.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 2509](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2509)

## 7.5 He tried to repair relationships, not merely identify wrongdoers

173. **He taught that helping an oppressor means stopping the oppression.** Group loyalty did not permit defending one's own side when it was wrong.

    > “Help your brother whether he is an oppressor or oppressed... Help the oppressor by preventing him from oppressing.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 2444](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2444)

174. **When people at Quba fought, Muhammad ﷺ personally went to reconcile them.**

    > “Let us go and make peace between them.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 2693](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2693)

175. **He made love of good for another person part of faith.**

    > “None of you has complete faith until he loves for his brother what he loves for himself.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 13](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:13); *Sahih Muslim* 45

176. **The Qur'an tells believers to answer evil with something better when that can transform hostility.**

    > “Repel evil with what is better, and the one between whom and you was hostility may become like a close friend.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 41:34](https://quran.com/41/34)

177. **He discouraged ordinary estrangement from hardening into a permanent feud.** The better of two estranged people was the one who began the greeting of peace.

    > “The better of the two is the one who begins with peace.”
    >
    > Source: *Sahih al-Bukhari* 6077; *Sahih Muslim* 2560

178. **A single Qur'anic verse joins worship of God to duties toward parents, relatives, orphans, the poor, neighbors, companions, travelers, and people under one's authority.**

    > “Worship God alone... and be good to parents, relatives, orphans, the needy, the near neighbor, the distant neighbor, the companion, the traveler, and those under your authority.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 4:36](https://quran.com/4/36)

179. **Care for elderly parents is expressed in unusually tender language.**

    > “Do not even say ‘uff’ to them... lower to them the wing of humility out of mercy, and say: ‘My Lord, have mercy on them as they raised me when I was small.’”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 17:23–24](https://quran.com/17/23-24)

180. **The Qur'an asks people who have genuinely been wronged to consider forgiveness rather than use every legitimate grievance to retaliate.**

    > “Let them pardon and overlook. Would you not love that God should forgive you?”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 24:22](https://quran.com/24/22)

181. **Charity is not supposed to humiliate the recipient.** A kind word can be morally better than a donation followed by injury.

    > “Kind words and forgiveness are better than charity followed by hurt.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 2:263](https://quran.com/2/263)

182. **Private charity is praised because helping another person need not become a performance.**

    > “If you give charity openly, that is good; but if you conceal it and give it to the poor, that is better for you.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 2:271](https://quran.com/2/271)

# 8. Knowledge, evidence, health, and sustainable living

## 8.1 Knowledge was a religious good

183. **The first revealed command begins with reading and immediately mentions teaching by the pen.** Knowledge appears at the opening of the revelation Muhammad ﷺ brought.

    > “Read in the name of your Lord who created... who taught by the pen, taught the human being what he did not know.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 96:1–5](https://quran.com/96/1-5)

184. **He made understanding a sign of divine good.** Knowledge was not merely the accumulation of information, but insight that produces responsible worship and judgment.

    > “When God intends good for someone, He gives him understanding of the religion.”
    >
    > Source: *Sahih al-Bukhari* 71; *Sahih Muslim* 1037

185. **Seeking knowledge was given direct spiritual value.**

    > “Whoever travels a path seeking knowledge, God makes easy for him a path to Paradise.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih Muslim 2699a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2699a)

186. **Collective reading and study were described as acts surrounded by tranquility and mercy.**

    > “No people gather in one of God's houses, reciting God's Book and studying it together, except that tranquility descends upon them.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih Muslim 2699a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2699a)

187. **The same report denies that ancestry can substitute for personal moral effort.**

    > “Whoever is slowed by his deeds will not be accelerated by his lineage.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih Muslim 2699a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2699a)

188. **He taught in ways designed for human attention.** Ibn Masud said Muhammad ﷺ deliberately chose appropriate times rather than speaking so constantly that people became bored.

    > “He used to choose suitable times to teach us, fearing that we would become bored.”
    >
    > Source: *Sahih al-Bukhari* 68, 70

189. **He taught through questions and made room for inquiry.** The hadith collections preserve questions about faith, intimacy, doubt, grief, family conflict, worship, money, and death. The famous report of Gabriel teaches the foundations of religion through direct questions and answers.

    > “Tell me about Islam... Tell me about faith... Tell me about excellence.”
    >
    > Source: The hadith of Gabriel, *Sahih Muslim* 8

## 8.2 Intellectual honesty and epistemic restraint

190. **The Qur'an tells believers to verify consequential reports before acting on them.** The concern is practical: false information can lead people to harm others and regret it afterward.

    > “If an unreliable person brings you news, verify it, lest you harm people out of ignorance and then regret what you have done.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 49:6](https://quran.com/49/6)

191. **It prohibits confident claims where a person lacks knowledge and makes the senses and mind morally accountable.**

    > “Do not follow what you have no knowledge of. Indeed, the hearing, sight, and heart will all be questioned.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 17:36](https://quran.com/17/36)

192. **Muhammad ﷺ issued a severe warning against inventing sayings and attributing them to him.** The warning created a direct religious motive to distinguish genuine transmission from fabrication.

    > “Whoever deliberately lies about me, let him take his place in the Fire.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 107](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:107)

193. **He rejected omen-based superstition.** Random birds, directions, and occurrences were not to become imagined secret messages governing a person's life.

    > “There is no evil omen. I like a good word.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 5754](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5754)

194. **His response to the eclipse is an unusually strong example of epistemic restraint because the false interpretation would have glorified him personally.** Instead of exploiting the coincidence of the eclipse and his son's death, he denied the connection.

    > “The sun and moon are two signs of God; they do not eclipse for anyone's death or birth.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 1043](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:1043)

195. **His response to the date-palm episode recognized genuine human expertise in technical affairs.** Religious authority was not made into a claim of infallible expertise in agriculture.

    > “You are more knowledgeable about the affairs of your worldly life.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih Muslim 2363](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2363)

## 8.3 Health, hygiene, and moderation

196. **He encouraged medical treatment rather than treating illness as something that must simply be endured without intervention.**

    > “Seek treatment, for God has not made a disease without making a treatment for it, except old age.”
    >
    > Source: [Sunan Abi Dawud 3855](https://sunnah.com/abudawud:3855)

197. **He taught a quarantine principle for plague.** People were not to enter an outbreak area, nor flee from one in which they already were.

    > “If you hear of plague in a land, do not enter it; and if it occurs where you are, do not leave it fleeing from it.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 5728](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5728); *Sahih Muslim* 2219

198. **He strongly encouraged oral hygiene while explicitly avoiding an unbearable obligation.**

    > “Were it not that I would cause hardship to my community, I would have commanded them to clean their teeth before every prayer.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 887](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:887)

199. **He taught moderation in eating rather than habitual excess.**

    > “No human fills a container worse than his stomach... if more is necessary, then a third for food, a third for drink, and a third for breath.”
    >
    > Source: [Jami at-Tirmidhi 2380](https://sunnah.com/tirmidhi:2380)

200. **Ritual life repeatedly incorporates bodily washing and cleanliness.**

    > “When you rise for prayer, wash your faces and your hands to the elbows, wipe your heads, and wash your feet to the ankles.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 5:6](https://quran.com/5/6)

201. **He described purity itself as half of faith.** Cleanliness was integrated into spiritual life rather than treated as unrelated to it.

    > “Purity is half of faith.”
    >
    > Source: *Sahih Muslim* 223

# 9. Justice, leadership, wealth, and economic ethics

## 9.1 Justice was supposed to bind the powerful too

202. **He explicitly rejected a two-tier justice system in which elites escape consequences imposed on weaker people.** When prominent people tried to intercede for a well-connected woman convicted of theft, he gave the example of his own daughter.

    > “Those before you were ruined because they left the noble person when he stole but punished the weak... If Fatimah daughter of Muhammad stole, I would apply the law to her.”
    >
    > Source: *Sahih al-Bukhari* 3475, [6788](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6788); *Sahih Muslim* 1688

203. **He warned litigants that a legal victory can still be a moral theft.** Eloquence does not transform someone else's right into one's own.

    > “If I award someone his brother's right because of his argument, I am only giving him a piece of the Fire.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 2680](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2680); *Sahih Muslim* 1713

204. **The Qur'an commands justice even when the truth is personally costly.** Family, wealth, and self-interest do not override truthful judgment.

    > “Stand firmly for justice, as witnesses for God, even against yourselves, your parents, or close relatives.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 4:135](https://quran.com/4/135)

205. **Hatred is explicitly forbidden from becoming an excuse for injustice.** Moral standards do not end at the boundary of friendship.

    > “Do not let the hatred of a people cause you to be unjust. Be just; that is nearer to God-consciousness.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 5:8](https://quran.com/5/8)

## 9.2 Leadership as consultation and trust rather than personal entitlement

206. **The Qur'an commands public leadership to combine gentleness, pardon, consultation, and resolve.** This is especially striking in the verse revealed in connection with the painful aftermath of Uhud.

    > “It was by God's mercy that you were gentle with them... pardon them, ask forgiveness for them, and consult them in affairs.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 3:159](https://quran.com/3/159)

207. **Consultation is also described as a quality of the believing community generally, not merely a concession from one ruler.**

    > “Their affairs are conducted by mutual consultation.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 42:38](https://quran.com/42/38)

208. **He warned people not to seek political authority out of personal ambition.**

    > “Do not ask for authority. If you are given it because you asked, you will be left to it; if given it without asking, you will be helped in it.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 7146](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:7146)

209. **He refused to appoint someone merely because that person was eager for office.**

    > “We do not appoint to this work anyone who asks for it or is eager for it.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 7149](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:7149)

210. **He described public authority as a trust that could become a source of disgrace if its duties were not fulfilled.**

    > “Authority is a trust, and on the Day of Resurrection it will be a cause of humiliation and regret except for one who takes it rightly and fulfills its duties.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih Muslim 1825](https://sunnah.com/muslim:1825)

211. **He condemned gifts that public officials received because of their office.** His test exposed the conflict of interest: would the gift have arrived if the official had stayed at home without the position?

    > “Why did he not sit in his father's or mother's house and see whether gifts would be given to him?”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 7174](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:7174); *Sahih Muslim* 1832

212. **His pre-prophetic participation in the Meccan pact for the wronged fits the same later pattern.** Justice did not first become attractive when he gained authority; the biography preserves an earlier commitment to restoring the rights of people without protection.

    > “They pledged that they would stand with any wronged person in Mecca until his right was restored.”
    >
    > Source: Sīra report, Ibn Hisham, *al-Sirah al-Nabawiyyah* 1/133

## 9.3 Honest trade, work, wages, and contracts

213. **He honored work and self-sufficiency.**

    > “No one has eaten food better than that earned by the work of his own hands.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 2072](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2072)

214. **He linked commercial blessing to truthfulness and disclosure.** Avoiding a direct lie was not enough; known defects were to be made clear.

    > “If buyer and seller speak truthfully and disclose defects, their transaction is blessed; if they lie and conceal, its blessing is erased.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 2079](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2079)

215. **He treated concealed defects as a serious moral breach.** When he found wet grain hidden beneath dry grain, he required that the defect be visible.

    > “Why did you not put the wet part on top so people could see it? Whoever cheats us is not of us.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih Muslim 102](https://sunnah.com/muslim:102)

216. **He prohibited manipulative bidding designed only to drive up someone else's price.**

    > “The Prophet forbade artificial bidding.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 2140](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2140)

217. **The Qur'an makes mutual consent a basic condition of legitimate exchange.**

    > “Do not consume one another's wealth unjustly, but only through trade by mutual consent.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 4:29](https://quran.com/4/29)

218. **Shortchanging people in weights and measures is condemned as a moral offense.**

    > “Woe to those who give less than due: when they take a measure from people they demand it in full, but when they measure for others they give less.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 83:1–3](https://quran.com/83/1-3)

219. **The Qur'an encourages written debt contracts and witnesses.** Its longest verse is largely concerned with recording financial obligations so that rights are clear and disputes are reduced.

    > “When you contract a debt for a fixed period, write it down... let a scribe write between you with justice.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 2:282](https://quran.com/2/282)

220. **Withholding a laborer's earned wage after taking the full benefit of the work is singled out for severe condemnation.**

    > “I will be the opponent of... one who hires a worker, receives the full work from him, then does not give him his wage.”
    >
    > Source: Sacred hadith, [Sahih al-Bukhari 2270](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2270)

221. **He condemned deliberate hoarding of needed goods for exploitative gain.**

    > “No one hoards except a sinner.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih Muslim 1605b](https://sunnah.com/muslim:1605b)

222. **Wastefulness is criticized even where the wealth technically belongs to the spender.**

    > “God has disliked for you... wasting wealth.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 2408](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2408)

## 9.4 Debt relief, redistribution, and limits on exploitation

223. **He repeatedly encouraged leniency toward debtors in genuine hardship.**

    > “Whoever gives respite to a person in difficulty or reduces his debt, God will shade him under His shade.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih Muslim 3006](https://sunnah.com/muslim:3006)

224. **The Qur'an condemns interest-based exploitation and orders creditors to relinquish outstanding usurious increase.**

    > “Be mindful of God and give up what remains of usury, if you are believers.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 2:278](https://quran.com/2/278)

225. **When Muhammad ﷺ publicly abolished pre-Islamic usury, he began with a financial claim belonging to his own family.** Reform was not designed to preserve the founder's relatives from its cost.

    > “The usury of the pre-Islamic period is abolished, and the first of our usury I abolish is that of Abbas ibn Abd al-Muttalib.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih Muslim 1218a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:1218a)

226. **Required almsgiving turned wealth into a social obligation rather than leaving redistribution entirely to exceptional generosity.**

    > “It is taken from their rich and returned to their poor.”
    >
    > Source: *Sahih al-Bukhari* 1395; *Sahih Muslim* 19

227. **He encouraged lasting charitable institutions.** When Umar obtained valuable land, Muhammad ﷺ advised him to preserve the asset while dedicating its yield to the poor, relatives, travelers, and public benefit. This became a foundation for charitable endowments.

    > “Retain the property itself and give its produce in charity.”
    >
    > Source: *Sahih al-Bukhari* 2737, 2764; *Sahih Muslim* 1632

228. **His Farewell Pilgrimage placed the inviolability of property beside the inviolability of life.**

    > “Your blood and your property are sacred and inviolable.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih Muslim 1218a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:1218a)

# 10. War, peace, treaties, and restraint under conflict

Muhammad ﷺ was not a pacifist. He led a community that fought wars. The relevant question is therefore not whether conflict ever occurred, but **what moral limits his teaching placed on it, how he treated peace and covenants, and whether enmity suspended ordinary standards of justice**.

229. **He told his followers not to desire war.** Courage was required when battle came, but craving conflict was not treated as piety.

    > “Do not wish to meet the enemy. Ask God for wellbeing. But if you meet them, be steadfast.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 3026](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3026)

230. **The Qur'an commands accepting a genuine offer of peace.**

    > “If they incline to peace, then incline to it, and put your trust in God.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 8:61](https://quran.com/8/61)

231. **Treaties with opponents are to be honored when the other party honors them.**

    > “As for the polytheists with whom you made a treaty and who have not failed you in anything... fulfill your treaty with them to its term.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 9:4](https://quran.com/9/4)

232. **The Qur'an repeatedly makes covenant-keeping a religious duty rather than a matter of convenience.**

    > “Fulfill God's covenant when you have made it, and do not break oaths after confirming them.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 16:91](https://quran.com/16/91)

233. **The Qur'an permits fighting while explicitly forbidding transgression.** Armed force does not become morally self-justifying merely because a war has begun.

    > “Fight in God's cause those who fight you, but do not transgress. God does not love transgressors.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 2:190](https://quran.com/2/190)

234. **He prohibited killing women and children in war.** The rule appears in response to a woman found dead after battle.

    > “The Messenger of God disapproved of the killing of women and children.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 3015](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3015); *Sahih Muslim* 1744

235. **His instructions to commanders forbade betrayal, embezzlement, mutilation, and killing children.** Ethical limits remained even against enemies.

    > “Do not embezzle, do not break pledges, do not mutilate, and do not kill children.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih Muslim 1731a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:1731a)

236. **He retracted an order to burn two enemy individuals before it was carried out, saying human beings should not punish with fire.**

    > “I had ordered you to burn so-and-so and so-and-so, but none should punish with fire except God.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 3016](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3016)

237. **The Qur'an praises feeding captives even when the giver values and needs the food.**

    > “They give food, despite loving it, to the poor, the orphan, and the captive.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 76:8](https://quran.com/76/8)

238. **A hostile person seeking temporary protection is to be brought to safety rather than exploited while vulnerable.**

    > “Grant him protection... then escort him to his place of safety.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 9:6](https://quran.com/9/6)

239. **Hudaybiyyah showed that treaty obligations could bind even when immediate sympathy pulled the other way.** The return of Abu Jandal was painful, but Muhammad ﷺ kept the newly made agreement.

    > “Be patient and seek God's reward. God will make for you and those with you a relief and a way out.”
    >
    > Source: *Sahih al-Bukhari* 2731–2732 and the sīra

240. **Hudaybiyyah also showed willingness to accept wording and terms many followers disliked for the sake of a truce.** The Qur'an subsequently called the outcome a clear victory.

    > “Indeed, We have granted you a clear victory.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 48:1](https://quran.com/48/1)

241. **In his final pilgrimage, he abolished inherited blood-feuds and began with a claim connected to his own clan.** Reform was again applied to his own side first.

    > “The blood-revenges of the pre-Islamic period are abolished, and the first blood-revenge I abolish is one of ours.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih Muslim 1218a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:1218a)

242. **The earliest permission to fight was tied to wrong and expulsion, not to religious difference alone.**

    > “Permission to fight is given to those who are fought because they have been wronged.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 22:39–40](https://quran.com/22/39-40)

243. **The same passage expressly mentions monasteries, churches, synagogues, and mosques when describing what unchecked aggression destroys.**

    > “Monasteries, churches, synagogues, and mosques where God's name is mentioned often would have been destroyed.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 22:40](https://quran.com/22/40)

244. **At the conquest of Mecca, he announced routes to safety instead of treating the defeated population as a legitimate target.**

    > “Whoever enters the house of Abu Sufyan is safe; whoever lays down arms is safe; whoever closes his door is safe.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih Muslim 1780a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:1780a)

# 11. Animals and the natural world

245. **He taught that compassion toward an animal can be morally momentous.** One report describes a man forgiven after climbing into a well to bring water to a desperately thirsty dog.

    > “He gave the dog water, so God thanked him and forgave him.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 2363](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2363)

246. **Another report makes the point even more striking by describing a woman known for serious sin forgiven for giving water to a dog.** Mercy toward an animal could matter even in the moral life of someone with a deeply compromised past.

    > “God thanked her for it and forgave her.”
    >
    > Source: *Sahih al-Bukhari* 3321

247. **When asked whether kindness to animals is rewarded generally, Muhammad ﷺ extended the principle to living creatures as such.**

    > “In every living creature there is a reward.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 2363](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2363)

248. **Cruel neglect of an animal is treated as serious sin.** A woman was condemned for confining a cat without feeding it or allowing it to find food.

    > “She neither fed it nor gave it water when she confined it, nor let it eat from the creatures of the earth.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 3318](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3318)

249. **He required minimizing suffering even when an animal was being slaughtered for food.**

    > “God has prescribed excellence in everything... when you slaughter, slaughter well; sharpen the blade and spare the animal suffering.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih Muslim 1955a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:1955a)

250. **He forbade using living animals as target practice.**

    > “Do not take anything in which there is a soul as a target.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih Muslim 1957a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:1957a)

251. **He prohibited striking or branding animals on the face.**

    > “The Messenger of God forbade striking the face and branding on the face.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih Muslim 2116a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2116a)

252. **Seeing an emaciated camel, he turned animal care into accountability before God.**

    > “Fear God regarding these animals that cannot speak. Ride them when they are fit, and eat them when they are fit.”
    >
    > Source: [Sunan Abi Dawud 2548](https://sunnah.com/abudawud:2548)

253. **Planting something that later feeds a person, bird, or animal is counted as continuing charity.**

    > “Whenever a Muslim plants a tree and a person or animal eats from it, it is charity for him.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 2320](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2320); *Sahih Muslim* 1553

254. **Even removing a source of harm from a road could carry immense spiritual value.** Care for shared space was made part of ordinary moral life, not dismissed as too small to matter.

    > “Removing harm from the road is charity.”
    >
    > Source: *Sahih Muslim* 1009; see also reports in *Sahih Muslim* on forgiveness for removing a harmful branch

---

# 12. Reforms that changed the moral position of vulnerable groups

255. **The Qur'an condemns female infanticide by imagining the murdered girl herself called to testify.** The victim, rather than the family that killed her, is placed at the moral center of the scene.

    > “When the girl buried alive is asked for what sin she was killed.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 81:8–9](https://quran.com/81/8-9)

256. **Muhammad ﷺ also named burying daughters alive among practices God had forbidden.**

    > “God has forbidden for you... burying daughters alive.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 2408](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2408)

257. **Women were given defined inheritance shares in a society where inheritance had often followed male power.**

    > “For men is a share of what parents and relatives leave, and for women is a share of what parents and relatives leave, whether little or much.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 4:7](https://quran.com/4/7)

258. **A wife's marriage gift is assigned directly to her.**

    > “Give women their marriage gifts graciously.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 4:4](https://quran.com/4/4)

259. **Spiritual reward is stated without a male monopoly.**

    > “Whoever does righteous deeds, male or female, while believing, will enter Paradise and will not be wronged in the least.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 4:124](https://quran.com/4/124)

260. **Men are addressed first in the Qur'an's command to control the sexual gaze.** Modesty is not assigned to women alone.

    > “Tell the believing men to lower their gaze and guard their chastity; that is purer for them.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 24:30](https://quran.com/24/30)

261. **Serious sexual accusations require evidence, protecting people from casual destruction of reputation.**

    > “Those who accuse chaste women and then do not produce four witnesses... do not accept their testimony thereafter.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 24:4](https://quran.com/24/4)

262. **Intoxicants and gambling are rejected in part because of the social hostility and impaired worship they produce.**

    > “Intoxicants and gambling... are filth from Satan's work, so avoid them... Satan only seeks to create hostility and hatred between you through them.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 5:90–91](https://quran.com/5/90-91)

263. **Across these reforms, inherited status repeatedly gives way to moral responsibility.** Race, tribe, sex, wealth, and lineage are not sufficient measures of standing before God.

    > “The most honored of you before God is the most God-conscious of you.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 49:13](https://quran.com/49/13)

264. **The same pattern appears in his treatment of people whom society barely noticed.** His insistence on being shown the grave of the Black mosque cleaner made the person's quiet service and death matter even when others had considered it too minor to report.

    > “Why did you not inform me? Show me the grave.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 458](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:458)

# 13. Extraordinary signs reported in the Islamic sources

Character and ethics are one part of the case for prophethood. The Islamic sources also report extraordinary signs. A careful reader should distinguish two questions: **what the sources claim happened** and **how much historical weight those transmitted reports deserve**. The following are therefore presented as traditional evidence to be evaluated, not as an assumption that a skeptical reader must grant before examining the sources.

265. **Muhammad ﷺ presented the Qur'an itself as a public sign and issued a literary challenge to those who regarded it as human composition.** Unlike a private vision available only on his assertion, the recited text was placed before friend and opponent alike.

    > “If you are in doubt about what We have sent down upon Our servant, then produce a chapter like it.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 2:23](https://quran.com/2/23)

266. **The Qur'an links his mission with the splitting of the moon, and multiple canonical hadith preserve reports of the event.**

    > “The Hour has drawn near, and the moon has split.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 54:1](https://quran.com/54/1); see also [Sahih al-Bukhari 3637](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3637)

267. **Companions reported water flowing abundantly from between his fingers at Hudaybiyyah when the group lacked enough water.**

    > “He placed his hand in the vessel, and water began flowing from between his fingers like springs.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 3576](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3576)

268. **A canonical report records Muhammad ﷺ predicting that his grandson Hasan would reconcile two large Muslim factions.** The later history of Hasan's peace agreement is the event to which the prediction is traditionally connected.

    > “This son of mine is a noble leader, and perhaps God will reconcile through him two great groups of Muslims.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 2704](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2704)

269. **A canonical report records him saying that Ammar ibn Yasir would be killed by a rebellious group, decades before Ammar died in the first Muslim civil war.**

    > “May God have mercy on Ammar. He will be killed by a rebellious, aggressive group.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 2812](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2812)

270. **The Qur'an presents the Night Journey as a divine sign shown to Muhammad ﷺ.** The same episode also reinforces one of the recurring themes of his life: at the height of an extraordinary experience, he is still called God's servant.

    > “Glory be to the One who took His servant by night from the Sacred Mosque to the Farthest Mosque... so that We might show him some of Our signs.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 17:1](https://quran.com/17/1)

271. **The Qur'an made a striking near-term prediction that the defeated Byzantines would return to victory within a few years.** Its chronology and historical force can be evaluated separately, but the prediction itself is explicit in the text.

    > “The Romans have been defeated in a nearby land. Yet after their defeat they will triumph within a few years.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 30:2–4](https://quran.com/30/2-4)

# 14. What the whole pattern adds up to

No single item above forces the conclusion that Muhammad ﷺ was a prophet. A kind person need not be a prophet. A courageous leader need not be a prophet. A reported miracle has to be evaluated through the sources that transmit it. A scripture's claim about itself still has to be judged rather than merely repeated.

The cumulative case is stronger because **many ordinary explanations for a false prophetic claimant fit the recorded life poorly**.

272. **His claim did not begin with a reputation for lying or an unknown past.** His wife appealed to his established goodness, a then-enemy told a foreign ruler that he had never known Muhammad ﷺ to lie, and the Qur'an appealed to the decades he had already lived among his audience.

    > “Had you ever accused him of lying before this?” He said, “No.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 7](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:7)

273. **He did not use religious authority to claim divinity, omniscience, or autonomous supernatural control.** He repeatedly redirected attention from himself to God and explicitly restrained exaggerated praise.

    > “I am only a human being like you, to whom it is revealed that your God is one God.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 18:110](https://quran.com/18/110)

274. **He rejected an apparently miraculous coincidence when accepting it would have elevated him.** The eclipse at his son's death is difficult to explain as the behavior of a man eager to exploit every apparent supernatural endorsement.

    > “The sun and moon do not eclipse because of anyone's death or birth.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 1043](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:1043)

275. **The scripture he presented as revelation contains corrections of him rather than functioning as uninterrupted self-praise.**

    > “He frowned and turned away because the blind man came to him.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 80:1–2](https://quran.com/80/1-2)

276. **His private life supplied unusually strong witnesses to gentleness.** A wife said he never struck a woman or servant, and a man who served him for a decade remembered no routine expression of contempt.

    > “I served him for ten years. He never said ‘uff’ to me.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 6038](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6038)

277. **Power did not turn him into a theological object of worship or a wealthy dynast living in obvious luxury.** His household remained materially simple, his armor was mortgaged for food, and his followers were forbidden from turning him into a divine figure.

    > “Do not exaggerate in praising me... I am only His servant.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 3445](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3445)

278. **His ethical teaching reaches from interior motives to institutions.** Intention, anger, truthfulness, repentance, family, debt, commerce, rulers, courts, war, religious difference, knowledge, animals, and care for the poor all fall inside the moral vision.

    > “God commands justice, excellence, and giving to relatives, and forbids indecency, wrongdoing, and transgression.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 16:90](https://quran.com/16/90)

279. **The demands of justice were repeatedly applied against the interests of his own side.** The Qur'an requires justice even against oneself and relatives; Muhammad ﷺ rejected legal favoritism, condemned official corruption, abolished his family's usury claim, and began the abolition of blood-feuds with a claim connected to his own clan.

    > “Stand firmly for justice... even against yourselves, your parents, or close relatives.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 4:135](https://quran.com/4/135)

280. **His mercy is most revealing where retaliation would have been understandable.** He refused destruction after Ta'if, prayed for hostile people, spared a man who had stood over him with a sword, and accepted a difficult truce rather than treating maximal retaliation as faithfulness.

    > “No. I hope that God will bring from their descendants people who worship God alone.”
    >
    > Source: [Sahih al-Bukhari 3231](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3231)

281. **His theology persistently points away from Muhammad and toward God.** Prayer goes to God, forgiveness comes from God, salvation rests on God's mercy, and even Muhammad's own family cannot rely on kinship with him.

    > “When My servants ask you about Me, I am near. I answer the call of the caller when he calls upon Me.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 2:186](https://quran.com/2/186)

282. **His moral message presents itself as continuity with Abraham, Moses, Jesus, and the earlier prophetic tradition rather than as worship of a new founder.**

    > “We make no distinction between any of them, and to Him we submit.”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 2:136](https://quran.com/2/136)

283. **The central test remains open to investigation rather than personality alone.** Character helps answer whether Muhammad plausibly believed and lived his claim. The Qur'an he brought is the central content of that claim and must also be examined on its own terms.

    > “Will they not reflect deeply on the Qur'an?”
    >
    > Source: [Qur'an 4:82](https://quran.com/4/82)

## Conclusion: the coherence of the man

The strongest conclusion is not the simplistic argument, “Muhammad ﷺ was good, therefore he must have been a prophet.” The more serious point is that a large set of familiar alternatives fit the recorded life badly when the evidence is considered together.

The sources do not show a man building toward his own divinity. They show a man repeatedly calling himself God's servant. They do not show someone seizing every coincidence as supernatural proof. They preserve him rejecting the eclipse story that would have glorified him. They do not hide every correction. The Qur'an preserves rebuke. They do not show a private household remembering a different moral personality from the one in his sermons. Aisha and Anas describe unusual gentleness at close range. They do not show political success culminating in extravagant personal wealth. His household remained simple, and he died without a private treasury.

Nor was his message morally thin. It joined worship of one God with truthfulness, intention, repentance, justice, mercy, family responsibility, women's consent and property rights, care for orphans and the poor, emancipation, fair trade, written debts, honest government, restraint in war, protection of noncombatants and covenants, learning, medical treatment, animal welfare, and humility. It confronted his own community's interests when justice required it.

The consistency is what gives the portrait its force. **When he lacked power, he preached without authorizing private revenge. When he gained power, he remained personally unpretentious. He demanded justice but denied privilege to his family. He worshipped intensely but rejected religious excess. He could command armies yet shorten a prayer for a crying child. He received public revenue yet died with his armor pledged for food. He was obeyed by a growing civilization yet still worked in his home, listened to women, joked with children, sat among ordinary people, and accepted correction.**

A skeptical reader can reasonably ask further questions about particular hadith, disputed historical episodes, the origin of the Qur'an, or the evidential weight of miracle reports. Those questions should be asked carefully. But they should be asked after taking the whole pattern seriously: a long reputation for truthfulness, a costly mission, unusual consistency between public and private life, restraint when revenge was available, simplicity when wealth was available, humility when self-deification was available, justice when favoritism was available, and a message that relentlessly directs worship away from himself and toward God.

> “In the Messenger of God you have an excellent example for whoever hopes in God and the Last Day and remembers God often.”
>
> Source: Qur'an 33:21
